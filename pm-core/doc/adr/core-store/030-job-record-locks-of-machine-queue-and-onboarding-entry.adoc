= ADR 030: Job Records of the Machine and of an Onboarding Entry Are Written Under Two New Lock Keys
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-17

== Context

A job record is written at fixed points (queued, write-ahead, post-spawn, fate), each an atomic replace under the lock
of the scope that owns the job, and the id of a new job is allocated by a check-then-act on `jobs/` under the same lock
(PM-EXEC-4, PM-IMPL-7). For a job of a project the lock exists: the plan, epic or workspace lock, and the queue lock of
the project for a worker. Two homes of job records had no lock: the machine scope (`machine/jobs/`: its probes and the
steward's workers) and an onboarding entry of a project before its enrolment (`onboardings/<entry_id>/jobs/`: the
probes of its checkout). Every lock must have a place in the one total lock order that `LockTransaction.acquire`
asserts (ADR 011).

A write point also replaces a record that may have been written by an earlier point of the same job; nothing in the
atomic replace itself keeps a later point from being overwritten by an earlier one.

== Decision

* `LockKey.machineQueue(base)` is the queue lock of the machine scope, `machine/locks/queue.lock`, at the level `QUEUE`
  with the id `machine/queue`. It guards the machine's task queue and the records of the jobs of the machine scope. The
  id orders it among the queue locks of the projects, whose id is the project id; it cannot equal one, because `/` is no
  character of an identifier.
* `LockKey.onboardingEntry(base, entryId)` is the lock of an onboarding entry, `onboardings/locks/<entry_id>.lock`, at
  the level `WORKSPACE` of the scope the entry becomes at its enrolment, with the entry id as its id. It guards the
  records of the entry's probe jobs.
* `JobRecordStore.lockKey(scope)` maps every scope of the job record to its lock: `plan` and `epic` to their lock,
  `workspace` to the workspace lock, `project` to the project's queue lock, `machine` to the machine queue lock, `entry`
  to the entry lock. Every write and the allocation acquire it in the caller's transaction.
* Each write point reads the record it replaces under that lock and refuses a record that does not follow it: the
  queued record is the first, the write-ahead record replaces nothing or the queued record, the post-spawn record
  replaces the write-ahead record once, the fate replaces a queued or running record or nothing. A replacement keeps
  the job's scope and execution.

== Alternatives Considered

Alternatives not recorded. The levels follow the scopes the locks stand for, as the Javadoc of `LockKey` states: the
machine queue lock guards the machine's task queue as the queue lock of a project guards the project's, and the entry
lock stands at the level of the workspace scope the entry becomes at its enrolment.

== Consequences

* A probe of the machine and a probe of an entry are allocated and recorded under a lock of the total order, as every
  job of a project is.
* A transaction that holds a project's queue lock may take the machine queue lock after it only where the ids order
  so; the order assertion refuses the rest with `InternalFaultException`.
* A record moves only forward through its points: a settled record is never replaced, a post-spawn record never twice.
