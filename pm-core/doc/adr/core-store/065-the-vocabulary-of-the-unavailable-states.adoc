= ADR 065: A Refused Lesson Transition Reports One Vocabulary of Unavailable States
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-11-02

== Context

Every transition of the lesson store acts only on a lesson that resolves `found` and is in the state the transition
needs: `claim` refuses a lesson another plan holds and a retired one; `release` and the end of a claim are authorized by
the durable `claim.plan_id`; `retire` refuses a claimed lesson; `revise` refuses a retired one. The specification reports
such a refusal as `lesson_unavailable`, "naming the state and, for a claim, the claiming plan" (Store Schemas § Lesson
Record, Claim), and the premise state of `source-lesson` acts on it.

The resolver's states (`found`, `absent`, `present_unreadable`, `empty_shell`, `store_tampered`, `deleted_out_of_band`,
`untracked`) are not enough: "claimed" and "retired" are states of a lesson resolved `found`, and a release of a lesson
without a claim has no resolver state at all.

== Decision

`LessonResolution.Refusal` is the one vocabulary: the six non-found resolution states (the mapping is `Refusal.of`), plus
`claimed` (carrying the claiming plan), `retired` and `unclaimed`. A transition that does not act returns
`LessonResolution.Unavailable(lessonId, refusal, claimingPlan)`; a transition that acts returns its own outcome
(`Claimed`, `Released`, `Retired`, `Revised`). A refusal is a value, never an exception.

== Alternatives Considered

* Throw a `StoreRefusal` for every refusal: `StoreRefusal` is the substrate's refusal of an unreadable or absent
  document, and a refused transition is a state of a well-read lesson, not a defective store. Throwing would also make
  the recovery of a plan's end handle exceptions where it needs to read a state.
* Reuse `LessonResolution.State` alone: it has no word for "claimed", "retired" or "unclaimed" without overloading
  `found`, and the caller could not tell "found and eligible" from "found and refused".
* Return a boolean and a separate state: two values a caller can contradict.

== Consequences

* One switch over the vocabulary serves every caller; `source-lesson` maps `claimed` and `retired` to its
  `lesson_unavailable` with the claiming plan beside it.
* `Refusal.of` refuses `found`: a found lesson is never a refusal on its own, and a transition that refuses a found
  lesson says which kind of unavailability it means.
* The three integrity states travel as refusals too, so the caller of a transition never has to re-read the store to
  learn why nothing happened.
