== Purpose
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-6[PM-ARCH-6], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-4[PM-ARCH-4], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-7[PM-WF-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-CLIENT-1[PM-CLIENT-1], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-CLIENT-3[PM-CLIENT-3], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-EXEC-4[PM-EXEC-4], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7]_

This guide covers the stores of a scope that `pm-core` adds on top of the store substrate, and the engine ports:

* the workflow state store `mcp/status.json` in `de.planmarshall.core.store`, and the engine ports in
  `de.planmarshall.core.spi` through which the workflow engine of `pm-workflow` causes and observes what lies outside
  that store. Every run state of a plan, an epic or the workspace lives in the status document, so a killed run resumes
  where it stopped;
* the delete mode of the transaction journal, and on it the pending entry record `pending/<entry_id>.json` of the
  workspace scope, kept for the start; a generation belongs to a worker and is its job id, issued with its job token;
* the machine's audit log `logs/audit.jsonl` as a hash chain, the formats of its head and its index, the seam through
  which the runtime anchors it, and the production audit sink; every audit record a scope transaction owes carries a
  `transaction_id` of its own (<<_audit_log>>). The keyed head, the rotation and the verification live in
  `runtime.audit` of `pm-runtime`
  (link:../../../pm-runtime/doc/developer/runtime-audit.adoc[Audit Trail (`runtime.audit`)]);
* the job record `jobs/<job_id>.json` of a project, of the machine scope and of an onboarding entry, its identifier and
  its store, and the two lock keys of the machine queue and of an onboarding entry (<<_job_records>>). The runner that
  writes the record and the recovery that reads it live in `runtime.job` and `runtime.start` of `pm-runtime`;
* the waiver and waiver consumption logs of a plan, the typed waiver requests of its status, the question log of every
  asking scope, the per-project machine store with its identity record, and the Project Resolution Rule
  (<<_operator_and_question_stores>>);
* the store of the machine model slots, the rows of a `metrics.json` with their three-valued usage figures, the learned
  budgets of a project, the change ledger and the writer of the operator's qualification settings
  (<<_slots_metrics_budgets_ledger>>).

The store substrate these types build on (transaction journal, `ScopeTransaction`, `StoreSnapshot`, `StrictJson`,
digests, recovery) is described in <<_the_store_substrate>> below, together with the enrolment store and the confinement
types that this package also holds; this guide describes what the delete mode and the create target change in the
substrate. The schemas of the documents are in
link:{spec}/store-schemas/02-workflow-state-store.adoc[Store Schemas § Workflow State Store],
link:{spec}/store-schemas/01-format-and-session-log.adoc#pending-entry-record[Store Schemas § Pending Entry] and the
sections linked from the type tables below.

== The Workflow State Store
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-6[PM-ARCH-6], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-4[PM-ARCH-4], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16]_

[horizontal]
`WorkflowStatus`:: The immutable record of one scope's status document. The members the engine owns are typed: the
position (`scope_type`, `scope_id`, `phase`, `state`, `call_stack` with its `CallFrame` records and `BoundArg` values),
the loop-back accounting (`loop_back_iteration`, `outline_loop_backs`, `policy_counters`), the retry counts
(`retry_counters`), the bindings of the current state (`active_job`, `active_jobs`, `active_tasks`), the condition
overlay (`pending_condition`), the waiver requests of a plan (`waiver_requests`, see <<_waiver_requests>>) and
`updated_at`. Every other member of the closed schema is kept in `retained()` as the raw JSON text it was read with. The engine updates the record through its `with…` and `toBuilder()` copies; the
constructor checks every closed value, so a copy with a value outside the schema cannot be built.
`RetryCounters`:: The typed value of the member `retry_counters` (see <<_retry_counters>>).
`WorkflowStatusCodec`:: Reads and writes the document over `StrictJson`. It checks `format_version` first
(`FORMAT_VERSION` 1), reads the typed members strictly, each with its one JSON type and its closed values, and
captures every other declared member verbatim. It writes every member in the order the schema declares it: the typed
members in one canonical form, the retained members as the bytes they were read with. A document in that form
round-trips byte for byte. Its readers and writers of a call frame and a condition overlay, and its capture of a raw
member, are package-private, because the pending entry record holds members of the same shapes
(<<_pending_entry_record>>).
`WorkflowStatusStore`:: The store of one `JournalScope` (`mcp/status.json` of a plan, `status.json` of an epic,
`workspace/mcp/status.json` of the workspace, `mcp/status.json` of the machine scope, which is
`<PM_MCP_BASE>/machine/mcp/status.json`). `read()` takes no lock and reads through `StoreSnapshot`; a store that
does not exist is reported as `Optional.empty()`, never as an empty status. `write(ScopeTransaction, WorkflowStatus)`
adds the whole document as one replace target to the caller's transaction and never commits on its own.

[source]
----
WorkflowStatus ──write──► WorkflowStatusCodec ──bytes──► ScopeTransaction.replace("mcp/status.json")
      ▲                                                        │ commit (journal, apply, digests)
      └──read── WorkflowStatusCodec ◄──bytes── StoreSnapshot ◄─┘
----

=== Invariants

* *Format version first*: a document of another `format_version` is refused before any member is read.
* *Strict typed members*: a typed member with another JSON type, a value outside its closed set (phase, job kind and
  status, task state and outcome, condition kind, policy class, retry rule), a negative counter, more than five call
  frames, a `scope_id` for the workspace or the machine or none for a plan or epic, or a binding of another state than
  the current one is refused.
* *Verbatim remainder*: a member the schema declares but the engine does not own is re-emitted unchanged; the engine
  never rewrites what another component owns.
* *Closed schema*: a member the schema does not declare, or a member twice, is refused.
* *Journal-only writes*: the status is written only as a target of a `ScopeTransaction` of its scope, so a state change
  and the writes of the other stores of the same transaction are one journaled write.
* *Absent members are `Optional` at the reader*: `StrictJson.nullableString` and `StrictJson.nullableInstant` answer
  `Optional` for a JSON `null`, and a codec assigns the `orElse(null)` of that answer to a member its record declares
  `@Nullable`. A method that answers absence returns `Optional`; a field, a parameter and a record component that may be
  absent are `@Nullable`.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|A document violates the schema or has another format version
|`read()` throws a `StoreRefusal` naming the file and the member; the file is not touched.

|The store does not exist
|`read()` returns `Optional.empty()`.

|A status of another scope is written
|`write` throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is added to the transaction.

|A value outside the schema is put into a `WorkflowStatus`
|The record's constructor throws `IllegalArgumentException`; no such status exists to be written.

|A write stops at any step
|`JournalRecovery` of the scope completes or discards the transaction: the store holds the previous or the new document,
never a mix.
|===

=== Adding a Typed Member

The codec knows every member name of the closed schema (`WorkflowStatusCodec.MEMBERS`); the ones the engine owns are
`TYPED_MEMBERS`, every other one is retained. A later package that needs to read or write a member types it without a
format change:

. add the component to `WorkflowStatus` with its check in the compact constructor;
. add the member to `TYPED_MEMBERS` and its read and write to the codec, in its schema position;
. remove it from the retained members of existing test documents and extend `WorkflowStatusCodecTest` with its
  round trip and its refusals.

A document written before the change still reads, because the member was declared and kept verbatim all along.

=== The Machine Scope

One machine scope exists per machine, beside the plan, epic and workspace scopes: `JournalScope.machine(base)` is its
scope with the type `JournalScopeType.MACHINE`, no scope id, the path base `<PM_MCP_BASE>/machine/`, the journal
`machine/mcp/transaction-journal.json`, the sidecar `machine/mcp/digests.json` and the scope lock
`machine/locks/machine.lock` (`LockKey.machineScope`, at the level of the workspace lock). Its workflow state is
`machine/mcp/status.json` (`WorkflowStatusStore`), the status of the resident unit `onboarding.pmwf`, whose phase is
`machine`; the status carries no plan id. The change ledger, the metrics store and the question log of the scope lie
directly below `machine/`; the machine's task queue and job records are the separate machine stores.

[#_the_call_stack]
== The Call Stack
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16]_

`call_stack` holds the active sub-workflow call frames of a scope, bottom to top, at most five. A frame is the record
`WorkflowStatus.CallFrame`: `callerWorkflowId`, `callerStateId`, `subworkflowId`, `entry`, `boundArgs` (the `BoundArg`
values by parameter name, in their order) and `pushedAt`. A frame carries no caller phase: the phase is derived from the
phase sub-workflow on the stack (link:../adr/core-store/050-five-call-frames-and-no-caller-phase.adoc[ADR 050]).

`entry` is the entry of the called sub-workflow the call went through: the entry the call names, or the callee's sole
entry when the call names none; for a phase sub-workflow the point at which the phase is entered. The engine resolves
it when it pushes the frame (link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-engine.adoc#_entering_a_callee_through_its_entry[Workflow
Engine § Entering a Callee Through Its Entry]) and writes it with the status in the transaction of the call.

* *Required and non-blank*: the record's constructor refuses a blank entry with `IllegalArgumentException`, as it does
  a blank identifier.
* *Strict read*: `WorkflowStatusCodec` reads `entry` as a declared member of the frame with one JSON type, a string;
  a frame without it, or with it as another type, is a `StoreRefusal` naming the file, like every other required frame
  member.
* *Schema order*: the codec writes `entry` after `subworkflow_id` and before `bound_args`, the order of the frame's
  properties in the schema, so a frame round-trips byte for byte.
* *Format version 1 unchanged*: the member is added to format 1 in place. There is no format bump, no reader of an
  earlier frame shape and no migration; a frame without `entry` is refused
  (link:../adr/core-store/029-call-frame-records-its-entry.adoc[ADR 029]).

The pending entry record holds its bootstrap's call frames in the same shape and reads and writes them through the same
package-private readers and writers of the codec (<<_pending_entry_record>>), so its frames carry the entry without a
change of `PendingEntryRecord` or `PendingEntryCodec`.

Tested by `WorkflowStatusCodecTest` (a frame with an entry round-trips byte for byte; a frame without the entry, or
with a number, `null` or a blank as entry, is refused) and `PendingEntryCodecTest` (the entry of a bootstrap's frame
is read, and a frame without it is refused).

[#_retry_counters]
== Retry Counters
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-7[PM-WF-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-4[PM-ARCH-4]_

`retry_counters` persists the attempts of the server-rule retries and of the compiled condition budgets counted
outside a condition, so a runtime restart, a drain or a re-read of the store never resets a budget. Its keys are the
closed enum `RetryCounters.RetryRule`:

[cols="2,4"]
|===
|Key |Counted by

|`sync_failed` |a failed fetch or push of `finalize.merge-sync`
|`merge_barrier_indeterminate` |`finalize.merge-barrier` when the findings store could not be evaluated
|`merge_refused_re_enter` |`finalize.merge-refused`, a refusal the merge gate is entered again for
|`merge_queue_re_enter` |`finalize.merge-queue-exit`, an ejected pull request
|`push_failed` |`git.push-retry-wait`, a push failure other than a rejection
|`open_pr` |the provider call of `finalize.open-pr`
|`retry_once` |the conservative retry once per unit instance of a remediation unit
|`condition_budget` |a compiled condition budget counted outside a condition (`finalize.adopt-blocked`,
`finalize.merge-lease-interrupted`, `finalize.archive-plan`)
|===

Each key holds at most one entry `{state_id, attempts}`, bound to the state whose primitive counts the retry.

* `attempt(rule, stateId)` counts one attempt in the same status write as the retry it records; an entry of the rule
  bound to another state is replaced and starts at one.
* `attemptsOf(rule, stateId)` reads 0 when the rule has no entry or its entry is bound to another state.
* `clearedFor(stateId)` drops every entry bound to a state.

Who lowers a count is fixed: the state that counts a retry clears its own count when the retry ends (the retried
operation succeeded, or the budget's spent outcome is taken), and the workflow engine clears the counts of every state
of a sub-workflow when it pops or unwinds that sub-workflow's call frame. Nothing else lowers a count: entering and
leaving states within a frame keeps them, and a restart reads the same values from the store
(link:../adr/core-store/005-retry-count-clearing-rule.adoc[ADR 005]). The engine counts no retry itself; the retrying
primitives of later packages count through `RetryCounters`.

[#_delete_mode]
== The Delete Mode of the Transaction Journal
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

A target of a `ScopeTransaction` replaces a store, appends to a JSONL store, or deletes a store. The delete is what
removes a store of a scope with the same guarantees as every other write: journaled, applied once, rolled forward
after a crash, and reflected in the digest sidecar, so a removed store is never mistaken for an edit outside a
transaction. The pending entry record is the first store removed this way
(link:../adr/core-store/007-delete-mode-of-the-transaction-journal.adoc[ADR 007]).

[horizontal]
`ScopeTransaction.delete(relativePath)`:: Collects a delete target under the same checks as `replace` and `append`: a
store of the scope, at most one target per store, so a delete and a replace of the same store in one transaction are
refused. Deleting a store that does not exist is no error.
`TransactionJournal.Mode.DELETE`:: The wire name `delete`. A delete target is `{"path", "mode"}` alone: it stages no
file and leaves no store whose digest could be recorded, so `staged_path`, `size_before`, `new_size` and `new_sha256`
are absent. A journal that gives a delete target one of them is refused as unreadable.
`TransactionJournal.Target.delete(path)`:: The one way to build a delete target; the record constructor refuses a
delete with a staged path, a size before or a new digest.
`DigestSidecar.without(path)`:: The sidecar without the entry of a deleted store, every other digest unchanged.
`ScopeIntegrity.Absent`:: The verification of a store that has neither a file nor a sidecar entry. That is the state a
completed delete leaves, and it is consistent: only a sidecar entry that outlives its file is a `Mismatch`.

The journal is written in `format_version` 1 (`TransactionJournal.FORMAT_VERSION`), the only version: it carries the owed
audit records and the delete target. A journal of another version is refused as outdated or newer; there is no reader
for another shape.

=== The Five Steps for a Delete

[cols="1,4"]
|===
|Step |What a delete target does

|Stage |Nothing is staged. `TransactionId.stagedName` refuses the delete mode, so no staged name can be derived for it.
|Journal |The target is written into the journal like every other; from here on the delete is completed by the recovery.
|Apply |The store is removed if it exists (`Files.deleteIfExists`) and its directory is forced to disk.
|Digests |The entry of the store is dropped from the sidecar; the digests of the other stores stay as they are.
|Close |The owed audit records are handed over and the journal is deleted, as for every transaction.
|===

=== Recovery

`JournalRecovery` decides a delete target idempotently. It is *applied* when its store is absent, and it is always
*appliable*: a store still present is removed. A delete target is therefore never reported as an invariant violation,
and applying it a second time changes nothing. The check for links below the path base covers only the store path of a
delete, since it has no staged path. The digests step of the recovery drops the entry as the commit does.

=== Invariants

* *One lock*: a delete is a check-then-act on the store and on its sidecar entry. It runs only inside a
  `ScopeTransaction` whose `LockTransaction` holds the scope lock, the same serialization every other target relies on.
* *Absent and untracked agree*: after a completed delete the store has neither file nor sidecar entry, and
  `ScopeIntegrity` verifies it `Absent`; a store whose file is gone while its entry remains is still a `Mismatch`, and a
  file without an entry is still `Untracked`.
* *No shim*: a journal of another format version is refused as outdated or newer; the drain rule of the specification
  applies to an upgrade.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|A crash after the stage step of a transaction with a delete target
|The journal was not written: the store and its digest are unchanged, and the recovery has nothing to complete for the
delete.

|A crash after the journal, apply or digests step
|`JournalRecovery` removes the store if it is still present and drops its sidecar entry; the owed audit records are
handed over again.

|A journal of another format version is found
|It is refused as outdated or newer (`format_outdated` or `format_newer`); nothing is applied.

|A delete target in a journal carries a staged path, a size before or a digest
|The journal is refused as unreadable.
|===

Tested by `TransactionJournalTest` (round trip of a delete target, another version refused, a delete target with staged
members refused), `ScopeTransactionTest` (a delete stopped after each step through the step hook, the delete of an
absent store, a delete and a replace of one store refused), `JournalRecoveryTest` (a half-applied delete with the store
present and absent, a torn journal of a delete, a repeated recovery), `DigestSidecarTest` and `ScopeIntegrityTest`.

[#_create_target]
== The Create Target of a Scope Transaction
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

A store that is written once and never rewritten, such as a version file of a plan instance, needs a write that
refuses a store that exists. A check made while the targets are collected is not enough: the scope lock serializes
lock transactions, not the `ScopeTransaction` objects opened under one of them, so two transactions of one lock
transaction can both collect a target of the same absent store, and the apply step of the second moves its staged file
over the store the first one wrote. The check therefore belongs in the commit
(link:../adr/core-store/028-create-target-refused-at-commit-when-the-store-exists.adoc[ADR 028]).

[horizontal]
`ScopeTransaction.create(relativePath, content)`:: Collects a replace target that must not exist when the transaction
commits. The same path rules as for `replace` apply, and a store is a target of a transaction once, whatever its mode.
`ScopeTransaction.commit()`:: After it has checked that the transaction still holds the scope lock and that the scope
has no journal to recover, and before anything is staged, it refuses the transaction with `IllegalStateException`
naming the store when a create target exists (checked without following a link). Nothing is staged, journaled or
applied, the sidecar is untouched, and the transaction stays uncommitted, like the other refusals of the commit.

A create target is a `TransactionJournal.Mode.REPLACE` target in the journal: the staged name, the journal format, the
apply step and `JournalRecovery` are those of a replace. The check is made once, before the journal is written; a
commit stopped after its journal is completed by the recovery as any replace.

=== Invariants

* *Checked under the lock where it is written*: the create check runs in the commit, while the lock transaction holds
  the scope lock, so no transaction of another lock transaction writes the store between the check and the apply; a
  transaction of the same lock transaction that committed first is seen.
* *All or nothing*: a refused create refuses the whole transaction; no other target of it is written.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|The store of a create target exists when the transaction commits
|`commit()` throws `IllegalStateException` naming the store; nothing is written, the transaction is not committed.

|A store is named twice in one transaction, by a create and any other target
|The second target throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is added.
|===

Tested by `ScopeTransactionTest` (a create of an absent store, the refused commit of an existing store with nothing
written and the transaction left open, two transactions of one lock transaction creating the same store: the first
commits, the second is refused, the first content stays).

[#_pending_entry_record]
== The Pending Entry Record
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-CLIENT-1[PM-CLIENT-1], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7]_

A client opens an entry for a plan or an epic that does not exist yet, and the server records what the client named in
a pending entry record `pending/<entry_id>.json` of the workspace scope. The record is the state store of that entry's
bootstrap until a terminal of the bootstrap, or the pending-record sweep, removes it.

[horizontal]
`EntryId`:: `ent-` and 8 to 32 lower-case hexadecimal digits; a new id has 16 random digits. `ofFileName` parses the
name of a record; a name that does not parse is never an entry id.
`PendingEntryRecord`:: The typed envelope: id, kind (`plan` or `epic`), `requested_id` and `hint` as given, the parent
epic of a server-started plan, and the creation and update instants. `createdBefore(instant)` is the age the sweep
compares.
`PendingEntryRecord.Bootstrap`:: The bootstrap state. The members the engine owns are typed in the shapes of the
workflow state store: the state, the call frames and the condition overlay, and the reason of the last refused
re-check. `facts`, `source` and `options` are kept as the raw JSON text they were read with
(link:../adr/core-store/008-pending-entry-record-engine-owned-members-typed.adoc[ADR 008]).
`PendingEntryCodec`:: Reads and writes the record over `StrictJson` in `format_version` 1; the raw members are written
back byte for byte. It reuses the call-frame, condition and raw-member helpers of `WorkflowStatusCodec`.
`PendingEntryStore`:: The records of one workspace scope: `allocate`, `create`, `replace` and `remove` as targets of
the caller's workspace `ScopeTransaction`, `read` and `entryIds` without a lock.

=== Invariants

* *Journaled under the workspace lock*: a record is created or replaced as a replace target and removed as a delete
  target (<<_delete_mode>>), so it is covered by the workspace's digest sidecar and a removed record verifies `Absent`.
* *A fresh id per entry*: `allocate` draws an id and redraws while it names a record present or a record the same
  transaction already writes; the caller creates the record in that transaction under the same lock, so two
  concurrent entries never share a record.
* *Only entry ids are entries*: `entryIds()` lists the regular files of `pending/` whose name parses as
  `<entry_id>.json`; a staged file, a foreign file or a link is never listed, and no sweep selects it.
* *`create` and `replace` know the record*: `create` refuses an id whose record exists, `replace` one whose record does
  not; `remove` of an absent record is no error and reports that it was absent.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|A record is unreadable or of another format version
|`read` throws a `StoreRefusal` naming the file; the record is not touched.

|The directory `pending/` does not exist
|`entryIds()` is empty.

|A transaction of another scope
|Every writing method throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is added.
|===

Tested by `PendingEntryCodecTest` (round trip with byte-identical raw members, refusals), `PendingEntryStoreTest`
(create, replace, read and remove, the removal of an absent record, two concurrent entries with two distinct records,
names that do not parse never listed) and `PendingEntrySchemaParityTest`.

[#_audit_log]
== The Audit Log
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

Every record a component hands to the `AuditSink` of `core.service` ends up as one line of `<base>/logs/audit.jsonl`.
Each line names the SHA-256 of the line before it, so no complete record can be removed, reordered or edited without
breaking the chain. This package holds the formats, the chain and the sink; what anchors the chain beyond itself (the
keyed head, the rotation, the index) is behind the seam `AuditAnchor` and lives in the runtime, because the head is keyed
by the enrolment integrity key, which `pm-core` does not hold
(link:../adr/core-store/026-audit-formats-in-core-store-keyed-anchor-in-the-runtime.adoc[ADR 026]).

=== The Three Formats

[horizontal]
`AuditRecord`:: One line of the log in `format_version` 1: `event_id` (a lower-case UUID version 7), `prev_hash`,
`timestamp`, `category` and `outcome` (closed sets), `event`, `scope_type` and `scope_id` (nullable), `actor` and
`details`. `EVENT_CHECKPOINT` names the record that closes a log before a rotation.
`AuditHeadRecord`:: The head `logs/audit.head`: `file` (always `audit.jsonl`), `record_count`, `last_event_id` (`null`
for an empty log), `head_hash`, `index_head_hash` and `hmac`.
`AuditIndexEntry`:: One line of `logs/audit-index.jsonl`: `prev_hash`, `file` (`audit-<first_event_id>.jsonl`),
`first_event_id`, `last_event_id` (the checkpoint), `record_count` (at least 1) and `checkpoint_hash`.
`AuditCodec`:: Reads the three shapes strictly over `StrictJson`: `format_version` first, every field at most once,
only the declared fields, the closed sets and the forms of ids and hashes; whatever does not parse is a `StoreRefusal`
naming the file. It writes every field in schema order without whitespace, the details sorted by name. The canonical
line of a record is its exact UTF-8 bytes in the log without the newline, and `sha256Hex` hashes those bytes as they
stand in the file, never a re-encoding. `signedContent` is the canonical JSON of the head without `hmac`, the bytes the
runtime's HMAC covers. `GENESIS_HASH` is 64 `0` characters.

=== The Chain and the Predecessor Rule

`AuditLog` is the chain on `logs/audit.jsonl`. One rule decides what every line written into the log chains to:

. the SHA-256 of the last complete line of the log;
. when the log holds no complete line, `AuditAnchor.emptyLogPredecessor()` when present: the `checkpoint_hash` of the
  last index entry, that is the checkpoint line that closed the rotated log;
. `GENESIS_HASH` only when the log is empty and no rotation ever happened.

So the first record after a rotation links to the checkpoint line of the log before it, and the chain runs across
every rotated file.

[source]
----
caller: LockTransaction holding LockKey.auditLog(base)   (leaf of the lock order)
AuditLog.append(transaction, template)
  1. JsonlStoreFile.repairTail(transaction, inLogSink)   a torn tail is cut, its record written into the log
  2. AuditAnchor.beforeAppend(transaction, log)          rotation if due; the log is empty afterwards
  3. prev_hash by the predecessor rule
  4. JsonlStoreFile.append(transaction, line, inLogSink)
  5. AuditAnchor.afterAppend(transaction, log, appended, lineHash)   the head over the new last line
----

The read of the last line, the append and the anchor calls run in the one transaction that holds the audit lock, so no
second appender comes between the hash and the line that carries it. The append goes through the one append path of a
JSONL store, `JsonlStoreFile`, unchanged. It closes its channel after every append, so only the in-process lock of the
key holds the append; the runtime lock (`RuntimeLock`) excludes a second runtime on the same base
(link:../adr/core-store/024-audit-log-appended-through-the-jsonl-store-file.adoc[ADR 024]).

*The write paths.* Every record a producer hands over goes through `append`, which calls the anchor. A rotation, which
runs inside that call, writes through exactly two members that never call the anchor, so a rotation cannot recurse:

* `AuditLog.appendCheckpoint(transaction, checkpoint)` closes the log before the rename: it repairs a torn tail, chains
  the checkpoint by the same rule, appends it and returns the SHA-256 of its line. It refuses a record that is not a
  checkpoint (`IllegalArgumentException`).
* `AuditLog.appendOwedRepair(transaction, owed)` hands an owed `jsonl_tail_repaired` record of another file of the
  trail, the audit index, into the new log after the rename: it forms the record by the mapping of `AuditLogSink`,
  repairs a torn tail, chains it by the same rule and appends it. It refuses another event, and a category or an
  outcome that is not one of the audit log, as the sink does (`IllegalArgumentException`).

Both refuse a transaction without the audit lock (`IllegalStateException`). The in-log sink of the log's own torn tail
(<<_the_torn_tail_of_the_log_itself>>) is the only other write; there is no further path into the log. `logFile()`
names the file a rotation renames, `recordCount(transaction)` counts its complete lines under the lock, and
`snapshot()` reads it without a lock.

The directory `logs/` must exist before the first append, and the caller that builds the log creates it: the audit
lock is a leaf key whose lock file is the log itself, and the lock manager creates that file where it is absent but
never its directory. In the daemon the assembly `pm-mcp-server` has to create it before it builds the log.

=== The Torn Tail of the Log Itself

A stop in the middle of an append leaves a line without its newline. The next append cuts it through the unchanged
`JsonlStoreFile.repairTail`, which owes the `jsonl_tail_repaired` record in the intent file of the log and hands it to
the sink it is given. For the log itself that sink is the private in-log sink of `AuditLog`: before it returns, it maps
the record to an `AuditRecord` (its own `transaction_id` kept), chains it by the predecessor rule and writes the line
directly with `SyncedFiles.append`, forced to disk, never through `JsonlStoreFile.append`, which would repair the tail
again while the intent still exists. The intent is deleted only after the sink returned
(link:../adr/core-store/027-torn-tail-of-the-audit-log-recorded-in-the-log.adoc[ADR 027]):

* a stop before the in-log write leaves the intent, and the next append hands the record over again;
* a stop after the write and before the intent is deleted writes the record once more, with the same
  `transaction_id`;
* a write that fails throws `UncheckedIOException`; the intent stays, and the append is not carried out.

=== The Anchor Seam

`AuditAnchor` is what the writer calls under the audit lock, in every append:

[horizontal]
`beforeAppend(transaction, log)`:: Rotates the log when a rotation is due, writing the checkpoint through
`AuditLog.appendCheckpoint` on the handed log, renaming `logFile()` and handing owed index repairs into the new log
through `AuditLog.appendOwedRepair`; or completes a rotation pending after its checkpoint.
`emptyLogPredecessor()`:: The hash a line chains to when the log holds no complete line; empty when no rotation ever
happened.
`afterAppend(transaction, log, appended, lineHash)`:: Writes the head from the new last line.

An implementation takes no `LockKey` of its own, since the audit lock is the leaf of the total lock order
(link:../adr/core-store/011-one-total-lock-order-asserted-at-acquisition.adoc[ADR 011]), and never throws for a head it
cannot write: the record stays appended, and the next verification reports the gap. The one failure it throws is a
rotation that stays pending after its checkpoint: no record may follow a checkpoint, so the append is not carried out.
`AuditAnchor.NONE`
anchors nothing and serves the tests of the chain alone. The implementation of the daemon is `AuditHead` of
`runtime.audit`.

=== The Production Sink

`AuditLogSink` implements `AuditSink`. It gives a record what the producer does not know: a fresh `event_id` and the
`timestamp` from the clock of the log, and the OS principal of the runtime as `actor`. A record whose details name a
scope (`scope_type` with a type of the audit log, and `scope_id`) carries that scope; every other one is a
machine-level record without a scope id. The details are kept as given, `transaction_id` included. Each record is
appended in a transaction of its own that acquires the audit lock; two sinks of one runtime serialize on its
in-process lock. An append that fails throws `UncheckedIOException`, as the `AuditSink` contract states, and the
producer of a store mutation keeps its intent.

=== One Transaction Id per Record

Every record a `ScopeTransaction` owes carries a `transaction_id` of its own, issued by `ScopeTransaction.recordId()`:
distinct from `transactionId()` and from the id of every other record the transaction owes. The id travels with the
owed record into the journal, so a record the recovery hands over again keeps it. The transaction's own id stays the id
of its journal, its staged files and its `transaction_rolled_forward` record. `OrderedWrite` owes
`cross_scope_reconciled` with such an id, and so does the pending-record sweep of `runtime.start` for
`pending_entry_removed`
(link:../adr/core-store/025-one-transaction-id-per-audit-record.adoc[ADR 025]).

Delivery of an owed record is at least once. The `transaction_id` is the deduplication key: a reader of the audit log
drops a record whose id it has seen. `AuditLogSink` appends what it is handed and drops nothing; a record handed over
twice is two lines with the same id. `PM_CORE-100` (`AUDIT_RECORD_DEFERRED`) names the record by that id.

=== Invariants

* *One lock for the check and the act*: the read of the last line, the append and the anchor calls run under the audit
  lock held by the caller's transaction; an append without it is refused before anything is written.
* *One predecessor rule*: every line, the in-log repair record, the checkpoint and an owed index repair included,
  chains by the same rule, so
  the first record after a rotation links to the checkpoint line.
* *Never rewritten*: a complete line of the log is never changed or removed; only a torn tail is cut, and its record is
  written into the log.
* *The anchor takes no lock*: `AuditAnchor` runs under the leaf of the lock order and acquires nothing.
* *One id per record*: no record a scope transaction owes carries `transactionId()` of the transaction.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|An append without the audit lock
|`append` throws `InternalFaultException`, `appendCheckpoint` and `appendOwedRepair` `IllegalStateException`; nothing
is written.

|The intent file of the log is unreadable
|`append` throws `StoreRefusal`; nothing is written.

|The log cannot be repaired or appended to, or the record of a repair cannot be written into the log
|`UncheckedIOException`; the record is not appended, and the intent of a repair stays for the next append.

|A stop in the middle of an append
|A torn tail, reported by `snapshot()` apart from the records; the next append cuts it and writes its record.

|The anchor cannot write the head, or a rotation fails before its checkpoint
|The record stays appended; the anchor logs it, and the next verification reports the head that does not match.

|A rotation stays pending after its checkpoint
|The anchor throws; the record is not appended, and the next append completes the rotation.

|A record whose category or outcome is not one of the audit log
|`AuditLogSink.append` throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is written.
|===

[#_job_records]
== Job Records
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-EXEC-4[PM-EXEC-4], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

The job record `jobs/<job_id>.json` is the write-ahead journal of one job: every job the runtime starts, a worker and a
probe included, has one. The recovery after a runtime start reads the records of a project's store
(`JobRecordStore.ofProject`) to settle what a previous instance left behind; the records of the machine store and of
an onboarding entry, where probes are recorded, are not read by the recovery. This package holds the identifier, the record, its codec and its store; the runner that writes the record and
the recovery that reads it live in `runtime.job` and `runtime.start` of `pm-runtime`.

[horizontal]
`JobId`:: The `job_id`: the prefix of its kind, `-` and 16 lower-case hexadecimal digits, `^[bcglpv]-[0-9a-f]{16}$`.
`JobId.Kind` owns the full kind vocabulary of the job records: `build` (`b`), `ci` (`c`), `git` (`g`),
`project_script` (`p`), `llm` (`l`, a worker) and `probe` (`v`). `random(kind)` draws 16 digits from a `SecureRandom`;
whether the id is free is decided by the store. `ofRecordFileName` parses the name of a record; a name that does not
parse is never a job.
`JobRecord`:: The record in `format_version` 1. Every member this package writes is typed; `worker` (the block a
supervisor writes) and `gate_facts` (the facts a gate writes) are kept as the raw JSON text they were read with
(link:../adr/core-store/030-job-record-typed-members-worker-and-gate-facts-verbatim.adoc[ADR 030]). The record parses
each raw member at construction and refuses a text that is not exactly one JSON value of its type (an object for `worker`
and `gate_facts`, a number or a string for a `ClampedValue`'s `configured`; trailing content such as
`{"a":1},"x":{}` is refused), because a member that only starts and ends like a value would be written and then refuse
the whole store on read. The record carries
its own vocabularies: `Execution` (`process`, `in_process`), `Status` (`queued`, `refused`, `running`, `ended`),
`Fate`, `Confinement`, the owning `Scope` (`plan`, `epic`, `workspace`, `project`, `machine`, `entry`), the
`BudgetDerivation`, a `SelfReportContradiction` and the `ClampedValue` rows. `isSpawnRecorded()` is true once the record
carries the PID: only from then on does the job count as running.
`JobRecordCodec`:: Reads and writes the record over `StrictJson`: `format_version` first, every member once, only the
declared members, every required member, one JSON type per member, the closed values; the `kind` must be the kind the
`job_id` names, and `worker` and `gate_facts` must be objects or `null`. It writes the members in schema order, the two
raw members as the bytes they were read with, so a supervisor's or a gate's block is re-emitted byte for byte.
`JobRecordStore`:: The records of one store: `ofProject(base, projectId)` (`projects/<project_id>/jobs/`),
`ofMachine(base)` (`machine/jobs/`) and `ofEntry(base, entryId)` (`onboardings/<entry_id>/jobs/`). The raw log
`<job_id>.log` lies beside its record. A project store holds the jobs of the scopes `plan`, `epic`, `workspace` and
`project`, the machine store those of `machine`, an entry store those of its own `entry`; a job of another scope is
refused before anything is written.

=== The Write Points and Their Locks

A record is written at four fixed points, each one atomic replace (`AtomicStoreFile`) under the lock of the scope that
owns the job, which the method acquires in the caller's `LockTransaction`. Each point reads the record it replaces
under that lock and refuses a record that does not follow it, so a record moves only forward:

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Point |Writes |Replaces

|`writeQueued` |`queued`, not spawned, budget not started |nothing: it is the first record of the job
|`writeAhead` |`running` with neither PID nor process start, before the process or the in-process operation starts
|nothing, or the job's `queued` record
|`writePostSpawn` |the PID, the process start and the spawn instant of a process job |the job's write-ahead record,
once
|`writeFate` |the settled record, `refused` or `ended` |a `queued` or `running` record, or nothing, as for a job
refused before anything was written
|===

A replacement must keep the job's scope and execution. The lock of the owning scope is
(link:../adr/core-store/031-job-record-locks-of-machine-queue-and-onboarding-entry.adoc[ADR 031]):

[cols="1,2"]
|===
|Scope |Lock

|`plan`, `epic` |the plan or epic lock of the project
|`workspace` |the workspace lock of the project
|`project` (a worker) |the queue lock of the project, `LockKey.queue`
|`machine` |the machine queue lock `machine/locks/queue.lock`, `LockKey.machineQueue`, at the level `QUEUE` with the id
`machine/queue`
|`entry` |the entry lock `onboardings/locks/<entry_id>.lock`, `LockKey.onboardingEntry`, at the level `WORKSPACE`
with the entry id
|===

`allocate(transaction, kind, scope)` is a check-then-act on `jobs/`: it acquires the owning lock in the caller's
transaction, draws an id and draws again while the id names a record or a log present. The lock stays held, so the
caller writes the job's first record in the same transaction and no second writer of that scope comes between the
check and the write.

A read takes no lock: `read(jobId)` returns the record or `Optional.empty()`, `jobIds()` lists the regular files of
`jobs/` whose name parses as `<job_id>.json` (a log, a staged file or any other name is not a job), and `records()`
reads them all, as the recovery does. The directory `jobs/` is created by the first write.

=== Invariants

* *The rules of the schema at construction*: `fate` and `outcome_row` are set exactly for a settled job, the status
  `refused` goes with the fate `refused` and `refusal_reason` exactly with it; a worker carries its `worker` block, runs
  as a process and belongs to a project or the machine; a probe runs as a process and belongs to the machine or an
  entry; an in-process operation has no process field and the confinement `not_applicable`; `pid` and
  `process_started_at` are set together; a queued or refused job was never spawned; `child_wall_ms` is recorded only for
  an ended process job and never exceeds its wall time from `spawned_at` to `ended_at`. A record outside these rules
  cannot be built, so it is never written.
* *Forward only*: each write point checks the record it replaces under the owning lock; a settled record is never
  replaced, and a post-spawn record is never written twice.
* *Verbatim remainder*: `worker` and `gate_facts` are re-emitted unchanged; this package never rewrites what the
  supervisor or a gate owns.
* *Only job ids are jobs*: a listing never names a file whose name does not parse as `<job_id>.json`.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|A record is unreadable, of another format version, or states another `job_id` than its file name
|`read` and `records` throw a `StoreRefusal` naming the file.

|A write point does not follow the record it replaces, or the record is not of its status
|The write throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is written.

|A job of a scope of another store
|`lockKey`, `allocate` and every write throw `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is acquired or written.

|The transaction may not take the owning lock after the locks it holds
|`InternalFaultException` from the lock manager; nothing is written.

|The directory `jobs/` does not exist
|`jobIds()` and `records()` are empty, `read` is empty.
|===

Tested by `JobIdTest`, `JobRecordCodecTest` (round trip byte for byte, schema order, `worker` and `gate_facts` verbatim
with their whitespace, the refusals, the rules of the record), `JobRecordStoreTest` (paths and locks per home, the four
write points in order, a refused job's only record, the refused out-of-order writes, the redraw on a collision under the
owning lock, the listing and the refused foreign `job_id`), `LockKeyTest` (the two new keys and their place in the order)
and `JobRecordSchemaParityTest`, which compares the codec with the schema of the job record: the members and their order,
the required members, the values of every enumeration, the confinement and `job_id` patterns, the format version and the
range of `outcome_row`.

[#_lesson_record_and_file_format]
== Lesson Record and File Format
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

This section covers the record of a project lesson and the format of its file; the store itself is § The Lesson Store.
The project lesson store holds one Markdown file `<lesson_id>.md` per lesson; its front matter is structured and its body
is the narrative (`PM-IMPL-2`: the narrative stays Markdown, only the front matter is structured).

`LessonId` is the identifier of a lesson: the UTC date and hour of creation plus a per-hour sequence, at least three
digits (`{spec}/store-schemas/10-job-and-lesson-records.adoc#lesson-record`). The store holds `<lesson_id>.md` per
lesson, `fileName()` and `fromFileName(...)` convert between the two, and a file name that does not parse is no lesson.
`hourPrefix()` is the UTC hour a new identifier is allocated against. **A lesson that is listed must be addressable by
the id it is listed under** — a headerless legacy lesson still parses as an identifier and is never `absent`.

`LessonFrontMatter` is the record of the front matter, its members in the order of the schema, with the closed
enumerations of the record as nested types and the typed objects `Source`, `Remedy`, `Promotion`, `Revision`,
`MergedFrom`, `Claim` and `Retired`. Its compact constructor states the rules the specification states beside the schema
and refuses a violation with an `IllegalArgumentException`: a `wire_check` remedy carries its `check` and no other kind
does (`remedy_check_mismatch`); `retired` is set exactly when the status is `retired`; a `rule` is set exactly for the
category `arch-constraint`, and `recurrence_count` and `last_seen` only for it; `sources` and `revisions` are never
empty; and `revision` is the `n` of the last `revisions` entry. `derived_from_untrusted` is a primitive the record only
carries — the server sets it and it never turns back to `false` — and `Lesson` is the front matter with its Markdown
body.

`LessonCodec` reads and writes one file: the line `---`, the front matter, the line `---`, the body verbatim. The front
matter is written as one JSON object in the order of the schema — a JSON object is a YAML 1.2 flow mapping — and read
through a YAML library with the YAML 1.2 core schema (ADR core-store/061). The writer escapes every non-ASCII character,
so a title, a statement or a source that carries a YAML line break (U+0085, U+2028, U+2029), a C1 control or a
noncharacter reads back unchanged instead of being folded or refused by the YAML reader.
The reader refuses an anchor, an alias, a tag, a second document, a duplicate or unknown
member, a missing member and a mistyped member; it refuses a `format_version` other than 1, and an instant only in the
shape `Instant.toString` writes — RFC 3339, UTC, suffix `Z` — so what is read is written again byte for byte; a
`date-time` with an offset or another fraction spelling is refused. The member lists are package-private constants in the
order of the schema, and `LessonFrontMatterSchemaParityTest` compares them with the schema block of the specification.

A file that does not decode is not an exception: `decode` returns a sealed `Reading`, either `Readable(Lesson)` or
`Unreadable(reason, detail)` with the reason `no_opening_fence`, `no_closing_fence`, `not_an_object`, `strict_reader` or
`rule_violation`. The store resolves such a file to `present_unreadable`, never to `absent` (§ The Lesson Store). A write
with missing or invalid front matter is rejected: `Lesson` cannot be built without a front matter, and every rule of the
record is refused by its constructor before the codec sees it.

=== Invariants

* The front matter of the record is the schema of the specification, member by member and in order; the schema block is
  test input and stays byte-stable unless the codec changes with it (R10).
* The canonical bytes are the writer's: `encode(decode(b))` is `b` for canonical bytes, and a block-style file is
  normalized once when it is written again.
* A lesson id is the file name and the file name is the id; a name that does not parse is no lesson.
* The current revision is the last entry of `revisions`, and the body's history is never empty.
* A retirement lives in the front matter of the same file, and a `derived_from_untrusted` mark never turns back.

=== Failure Modes

|===
|Symptom |Cause |What to do
|`Unreadable` with `no_opening_fence` |the file does not start with the line `---` |the file is no lesson file; the
store lists it `present_unreadable` with its path
|`Unreadable` with `no_closing_fence` |no second line `---` closes the front matter |the file is no lesson file; the
store lists it `present_unreadable` with its path
|`Unreadable` with `not_an_object` |the front matter is a sequence or a scalar, not a mapping |read the detail; the
front matter is an object, never a list
|`Unreadable` with `strict_reader` |an anchor, an alias, a tag, a second document, a duplicate or unknown member, a
missing or mistyped member, another `format_version`, or a file that is not UTF-8 |read the detail; fix the front matter, never the reader
|`Unreadable` with `rule_violation` |a rule of the record or of its schema, for example a `wire_check` without its
check or a value outside a pattern or a bound |read the detail, which names the rule
|A block-style file is normalized on the next write |the writer emits the canonical JSON object |intended: the reader
accepts the hand-edited file, the writer one shape
|===

Tested by `LessonIdTest` (the grammar, the length bound, the file name, the hour prefix, the watch fixtures),
`LessonFrontMatterTest` (every rule with a refusing case and a passing control, the bounds of the members),
`LessonCodecTest` (the canonical bytes, the round trip both ways, a body with fence lines, a block-style file, a CRLF
file, one refusing case per reason, a file that is not UTF-8, an anchor, an alias and a tag) and `LessonFrontMatterSchemaParityTest` (the title,
the members and their order, the required sets, the enumerations, the `lesson_id` pattern at its three places, the
format version, the bounds and the absence of a dependent-required pair).

[#_the_lesson_store]
== The Lesson Store
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7]_

`LessonStore` is the store of the project's lesson records: one file `<lesson_id>.md` per lesson below `lessons/` of the
per-project machine store, with the journal `lessons/.transaction-journal.json` and the digest sidecar
`lessons/.digests.json` of the lesson scope (`JournalScope.lessons`). It is built from the scope and takes no lock and
opens no transaction of its own: every write is a target of the caller's `ScopeTransaction` of that scope, which holds
the lesson store lock `locks/lessons.lock`. A transition that also writes outside the lesson store — the claim after the
new plan scope, the end after the plan's terminal transaction — is ordered by its caller (Store Schemas § Transaction
Journal, Writes outside the scope).

**Allocation and creation.** `allocate(transaction, clock)` draws the UTC hour of the clock and the per-hour sequence that
follows every lesson file of that hour, every sequence the sidecar records (an out-of-band delete does not free an id)
and every lesson the transaction already holds as a target, at least three digits and at most 50. A draw is free for the
transaction that creates its lesson in the same step; two draws before the first create return the same id, because
neither is a target yet. `create` adds a create target (`mustBeAbsent`), refuses an id the sidecar records already and
refuses an id whose hour is not the UTC hour of the clock; its server-set members are derived by the store:
`format_version` 1, the id, `created_at`, the status (`upstream_pending` for the audience `upstream`, `active` otherwise),
revision 1 with its first `revisions` entry and the SHA-256 of the UTF-8 body, and the null claim and retirement. The
caller gives the title, component, category, bundle, rule, remedy, provenance, audience, `derived_from_untrusted`,
`tool_version`, the non-empty sources and the body.

**The two read flavours.** `list()` and `get(LessonId)` take no lock and read through `ScopeIntegrity.snapshot`: a file
that differs from its sidecar entry is `found` with `StoreIntegrity.DIGEST_UNVERIFIED`, and `get(transaction, LessonId)`
recovers the journal of the scope and decides through `ScopeIntegrity.verify`, so the same file is `store_tampered` there.
The locked read raises `store_tampered` and `untracked`; the lock-free read decides nothing about a digest. Its one
integrity state is `deleted_out_of_band`: a sidecar entry whose file is gone is an observed absence, never a digest
verdict, and never `absent` (ADR core-store/066). The locked read hashes the bytes it decodes, so a file replaced between
the verification and the read is `store_tampered` as well. `LessonResolution` is the one sealed result: `found`, `absent`,
`present_unreadable` (listed with its path, never omitted), `empty_shell` (a body without a non-whitespace character),
`store_tampered`, `deleted_out_of_band` and `untracked`. The listing names only files whose name parses as
`<lesson_id>.md`; a staged file `<lesson_id>.md.tmp.<transaction id>` and the sidecar are never lessons. `unclaimed()` is
the candidate list of `source-lesson.choose` — `active`, `claim` null, resolved `found` with a `verified` digest, newest
id first, the `derived_from_untrusted` mark travelling in the front matter — and `claims()` and `claimedBy(planId)` are
the read of the plan's end, which lists an unreadable lesson separately by id, because no one can read or release its
claim.

**The transitions.** `revise` replaces the body wholesale as the next revision and never turns `derived_from_untrusted`
back; a revision whose sources name a lesson of the kind `lesson` that is marked becomes marked itself.
`retire` writes `retired` and `status: retired` in one transaction and leaves the file where it is; it refuses a
retirement without its verdict's members (`completely_covered` needs a non-blank clause and input, `merged` and
`superseded` need `into`, no other verdict takes one) and a retirement of anything but a found, unclaimed lesson. `claim`
sets `claim{plan_id, claimed_at}`; `release` clears it and does not retire the lesson; `endClaimApplied` retires it with
the verdict `applied` and clears the claim, so the plan stays the author in `retired.by`, which is what reach is computed
from (ADR core-store/064). The release and the end are authorized by the durable `claim.plan_id` alone, never by their
argument (R14 (2)); a transition that does not act returns `Unavailable` with the state it found — the resolution states,
`claimed` with the claiming plan, `retired` and `unclaimed` (ADR core-store/065) — and a repeated end reports the
retirement without writing.

=== Invariants per crash window

Every write is one `ScopeTransaction` commit in five steps. A write stopped at any of them leaves exactly what a killed
process leaves, and `JournalRecovery` brings the store to one of the two stable states (R14 (8)):

|===
|Stopped after |What is on disk |What the recovery makes of it
|stage |the staged file and no journal |the staged file is deleted; the lesson is the old one (or absent)
|journal |the journal, the staged content and the old lesson |the journal is rolled forward; the lesson is the new one
|apply |the new lesson, the old sidecar entry and the journal |the journal is rolled forward; the sidecar matches the new lesson
|digests |the new lesson, its sidecar entry and the journal |the journal is rolled forward and deleted; nothing is rewritten
|===

* A lesson is always the old or the new one, never a torn file: a write replaces it by atomic rename.
* The sidecar entry of a lesson matches its file after every recovery, and an entry is never written for a file that is
  not there.
* The staged namespace `<lesson_id>.md.tmp.<transaction id>` is disjoint from the legal names, so a listing never names a
  staged file as a lesson (R14 (6)).
* A test that asserts a file location derives the expected path from `LockKey.projectStore(base, projectId)` and
  `lessons/`, never from the store object under test (R14 (9)).

=== Failure Modes

|===
|Symptom |Cause |What to do
|`found` with `DIGEST_UNVERIFIED` |the lock-free read saw a difference from the sidecar, or a journal of the scope is in flight |decide under the lock; the locked read answers `store_tampered` or `verified`
|`store_tampered` |the file was edited outside a transaction |the operator's `accept-edit` re-baselines it; no store verb touches it
|`deleted_out_of_band` |the file is gone but its sidecar entry remains |the lesson is not `absent`, and nothing retries it
|`untracked` |a file exists without a sidecar entry |no transaction wrote it; the store does not act on it
|`present_unreadable` |the front matter is missing or invalid |the lesson is listed with its path and is never a false absence
|`IllegalStateException` on a commit |a `create` target exists already, or the transaction has no target |a create over an existing id is refused; a transition that changes nothing adds no target and must not be committed
|===

Tested by `LessonStoreTest` (allocation, creation, the transition table of create, revise and retire), `LessonResolverTest`
(the fifteen ported fixtures, the four states, the three integrity states, the listing, the candidates, a property test),
`LessonRevisionTest`, `LessonRetirementTest`, `LessonClaimTest` and `LessonStoreCrashTest` (the five steps of every write,
two concurrent claims, a claim racing a retirement, the repeated release and end, the staged namespace).

[#_waiver_logs]
=== The Waiver Logs
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-3[PM-SEC-3], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7]_

[horizontal]
`WaiverRecord`:: One operator act on a waiver of a plan: `waived` (a gate declared `met`, or a remediation budget
raised to the ceiling `value`) or `revoked`, with the waiver id (`W-` and digits), the reason and its class, the
request it answers (`WR-` and digits), the operator with the channel and device, and the instant. A `waived` line names
its gate, mode and reason class, and only a `raise` carries a `value`.
`WaiverRecordCodec`:: Reads and writes one line strictly; a refused line names its file and line.
`WaiverLog`:: `plans/<plan_id>/waivers.jsonl`; only a plan has one. `append(transaction, waiver)` adds one line as an
append target to the plan's transaction. `read()` takes no lock; `Lines.current(waiverId)` is the last readable line
of the id and `currentWaivers()` the current state of every waiver. A second line with the id of a waiver the log
already holds is legal and is how a waiver is revoked or raised again: the line before it stays, and the last readable
line is the current state of the id, taken whole.
`WaiverConsumptionRecord`:: One evaluation of a waivable gate that admitted a failure because a `met` waiver was set:
the waiver, the gate, the state, the commit (`head_sha`), the instant and what it admitted (`covered`).
`WaiverConsumptionCodec`, `WaiverConsumptionLog`:: The codec of a consumption line and the log
`plans/<plan_id>/waiver-consumptions.jsonl`, appended like the waiver log; two evaluations that relied on one waiver
are two lines.

The two logs are separate files of the plan scope, appended through the plan's transaction and covered by its digest
sidecar (link:../adr/core-store/034-waiver-logs-appended-through-the-scope-transaction.adoc[ADR 034]). That the
operator's act is the only writer of `waivers.jsonl` and `waiver.lookup` the only writer of `waiver-consumptions.jsonl`
is the rule of the callers that run them; the store offers no other write than the append.

[#_waiver_requests]
=== The Waiver Requests of a Plan
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-3[PM-SEC-3], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-6[PM-ARCH-6]_

A waiver request is no store of its own: it is an element of the typed member `waiver_requests[]` of the plan's status
document (link:../adr/core-store/035-waiver-requests-typed-in-the-status-document.adoc[ADR 035]).

[horizontal]
`WaiverRequest`:: The request id (`WR-` and digits), the gate, the remediation unit that filed it, the rationale (1 to
2000 characters), the evidence, the instant it was filed, the outcome, the waiver that answered it, the operator's act
that answered it (`Answered`: channel, device, reason of a decline, instant) and the id of its pull request comment.
`WaiverRequestOutcome`:: The closed outcomes `waived`, `raised`, `declined` and `cancelled`. There is no `expired`: a
request has no time limit and stays pending, without an outcome, until the operator answers it or the plan ends.
`WorkflowStatus.waiverRequests()`:: The requests in the order of the document. The member is kept for plan scopes
only; a request in the status of an epic or the workspace is refused, and a `null` list is refused. An absent member
reads as no request, and the codec always writes the member, as `[]` when it is empty. Each request id occurs at most
once, and at most one request is pending at a time: a document that breaks either is refused as unreadable naming the
member, and a status built with either is refused by `WorkflowStatus` (`IllegalArgumentException`) before it can be
written (link:../adr/core-store/041-a-waiver-request-is-held-once-and-one-is-pending.adoc[ADR 041]).

[#_question_log]
=== The Question Log
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-12[PM-WF-12], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7]_

[horizontal]
`QuestionRecord`:: One event of a question (`QuestionEvent`: `asked`, `revised`, `adjudicated`, `delivered`,
`answered`, `withdrawn`, `cancelled`) with the question id (`Q-` and 3 to 6 digits), the set it belongs to (named by
its first question), the revision of the decision, and the members of its event, the channel of an answer
(`QuestionChannel`) among them. `closes()` is true for `answered`, `withdrawn` and `cancelled`.
`QuestionRecordCodec`:: Reads and writes one line strictly against the closed record schema.
`QuestionLog`:: `questions.jsonl` of a plan, an epic or the machine scope, `workspace/questions.jsonl` of the workspace,
and `pending/<entry_id>/questions.jsonl` of a pending entry, which belongs to the workspace scope (`ofEntry`); the lesson
store has none. `ask`, `revise`, `adjudicate`, `deliver`, `answer`, `withdraw` and `cancel` check the event of their
lines and append them through `append(transaction, lines)`; `read()` takes no lock, and `Lines.current(questionId)`,
`isClosed` and `currentRevision` answer from the readable lines.
`QuestionRefusal`:: The two invariants the store holds, as values: `AlreadyAnswered(questionId)` and
`Conflict(questionId, currentRevision)`; and `Unreadable(questionId, refusedLine)` (code `unreadable`) when the log
holds a refused line, so the invariants cannot be decided.

The log lives in `core.store`. The engine reads it through the port `QuestionRecordReader` of `core.spi`, which an
adapter of `pm-runtime` implements over `QuestionLog`; whether an answer fits its part stays the engine's check in
`pm-workflow` (link:../adr/core-store/032-question-store-in-core-store-read-through-a-runtime-adapter.adoc[ADR 032]).

[source]
----
caller: LockTransaction (scope lock) ──► ScopeTransaction.open
QuestionLog.answer(transaction, lines)
   ├─ read() under the held lock ──► a refused line? ──► Unreadable                       (nothing added)
   │                                 else the state of every question: set, revision, closed
   ├─ each line in order: closed? ──► AlreadyAnswered     other revision? ──► Conflict     (nothing added)
   └─ all pass ──► QuestionRecordCodec.write + "\n" ──► transaction.append("questions.jsonl")
caller: transaction.commit()
----

*Invariants.*

* *Exactly one closing line*: a line about a closed question, a second closing line among the appended ones included,
  is `AlreadyAnswered`, and nothing of the append is added. A revision is never a way to reopen a closed question.
* *Asked whole*: a decision is asked as one set, and its parts are fixed at that moment. A line that asks a further
  part into a set the log already holds is a fault of the caller: a change of the decision is a revision of the
  questions that are open, never a part added to the set
  (link:../adr/core-store/040-a-question-set-is-asked-whole.adoc[ADR 040]).
* *The current revision*: an answer, an adjudication and a delivery name the revision of the last `asked` or `revised`
  line of their question; another one is a `Conflict` carrying the current revision. A revision revises every question
  of its set that is open when it begins, at its first `revised` line within the append, each with exactly the next
  revision, the one after its current revision. A question of the set the same append answers or withdraws after
  revising it stays covered by the revision; a question closed before the revision begins is not part of it. The
  `decision` of the set is carried on the line of its first question and only there, as on an `asked` line; once that
  question is closed the set carries no changed decision, so a changed decision then needs a new decision.
* *Decided under the lock*: the log is read while the caller's transaction holds the scope lock, so no other append
  comes between the check and the write
  (link:../adr/core-store/033-question-invariants-held-by-the-store-as-refusal-values.adoc[ADR 033]).
* *Fail closed on a refused line*: a line refused when read (unreadable, or of another format version) names no
  question the log can read and may be the closing line or the revision of any of them, so the state of the questions
  is unknown. While the log holds one, every append is `Unreadable` naming the first refused line, an `asked` line of a
  new question included, and nothing is added. The read accessors of the log (`current`, `isClosed`,
  `currentRevision`) count readable lines only, so a caller consults `refused()` first and reports a question unreadable
  when the log holds a refused line; a refused line is never taken for an open question by the append gate
  (link:../adr/core-store/039-question-append-refused-while-the-log-holds-a-refused-line.adoc[ADR 039]).
* *A malformed append is the caller's fault*: a question asked twice, a part asked into a set the log holds, a part
  whose `question_set` is no first question of a set, a line about a question the log does not have, a revised line
  that does not name the next revision, a set revised in part, a decision on a revised line of another question than
  the first, an empty append or a line of another event than the method's is an `IllegalArgumentException`, not a
  refusal value.

*The move of an entry's questions.* A client opens an entry for a scope that does not exist yet, and its questions go
into the entry's log. When the bootstrap creates the plan or the epic, `QuestionLog.moveEntryQuestions(workspace,
entryId, target)` adds the complete lines of the entry's log unchanged as a create target of `questions.jsonl` to the
transaction of the new scope, and the delete of the entry's log to the transaction of the workspace. A scope's
transaction writes only the stores of its scope, so the move is two transactions held by one caller: the new scope is
committed first, then the workspace. A stop between the two commits leaves the entry's log in place, and repeating the
move is refused by the create target instead of appending the lines twice; a torn tail is not moved
(link:../adr/core-store/036-entry-question-file-moved-by-the-store.adoc[ADR 036]). `PendingEntryStore` is unchanged:
the record `pending/<entry_id>.json` stays a flat file beside the directory of the entry's log.

[#_project_store]
=== The Per-Project Machine Store
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-1[PM-ARCH-1], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

[horizontal]
`ProjectStore`:: `projects/<project_id>/` below the base directory of the runtime, resolved by
`LockKey.projectStore`, the one place of the server-owned state of a project, the same for its main checkout and all
its worktrees. `directory(Directory)` names `plans`, `archive`, `workspace`, `epics`, `pending`, `locks`, `queue` and
`jobs`; `identityFile()` names `store.json`.
`ProjectStore.create(transaction, identity)`:: Acquires the project's workspace lock in the caller's transaction,
creates the directories and writes the identity record when it does not exist. It returns `CREATED` or `EXISTED`; an
existing `store.json` is never rewritten, whatever it holds, an unreadable one included.
`ProjectStore.readIdentity()`:: Reads without a lock and answers `Present(identity)`, `Absent(file)` or
`Unreadable(refusal)`; neither a missing nor a refused record is ever an empty identity. A record whose `project_id` is
not the project of the store directory, a `store.json` of another project restored there, is `Unreadable` with a
refusal naming the mismatch, as the enrolment store refuses the same mismatch for its files.
`ProjectStoreIdentity`, `ProjectStoreIdentityCodec`:: The identity record: the project id, the project root (checked by
the one check of an enrolment, `ProjectEnrolment.requireAbsolutePath`, which is package-private for this reuse), the
display name (1 to 128 code points, never an identifier), the creation and the unenrolment instant.

The store and its identity live in `core.store` beside the enrolment; `core.config` is not touched, and nothing with
state lies below the repository of the project, `.marshall/local/` included
(link:../adr/core-store/038-per-project-machine-store-with-its-identity-record.adoc[ADR 038]).

[#_project_resolution]
=== The Project Resolution
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-2[PM-SEC-2], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-1[PM-ARCH-1]_

`ProjectResolver.resolve(path)` maps an absolute checkout path to the enrolled project it belongs to and to the main
checkout or the worktree of that project it lies in. It reads the plain pointer files git writes and runs no git
process (link:../adr/core-store/037-project-resolution-file-based-from-the-pointer-files.adoc[ADR 037]):

. The path is canonicalised; a path that cannot be is `PathRefused` with the reason `canonicalization_failed` of the
  confinement. Its toplevel is the nearest ancestor, the path itself included, that holds a `.git`.
. A `.git` directory makes the toplevel a main checkout. A `.git` file is followed by
  `PathConfinement.linkedMainCheckout`, the recognition of a linked worktree the confinement uses as well: its
  `gitdir:`, the `commondir` of that git directory, which must be a directory named `.git` whose `worktrees` directory
  holds the git directory, and the `gitdir` record that names the `.git` file back. A symbolic link, a pointer file that
  names no path, and every failure to read is no main checkout.
. The main checkout is matched against the `project_root` of every enrolment file, read without a lock. Exactly one
  readable match resolves only when every enrolment file was read; a file that was refused makes the match
  unprovable, because it may name the same root, and the resolution is `EnrolmentRefused` with that file's refusal.
. The toplevel is the main checkout, or a worktree directly below `<project_root>/.marshall/local/worktrees/` whose
  name is a plan id or `_epics`.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Result |When

|`MainCheckout(projectId)` |the toplevel is the main checkout of exactly one readable enrolment, and every enrolment
file was read
|`Worktree(projectId, worktree)` |the toplevel is a plan worktree or the shared epic worktree of that project
|`NotEnrolled(NO_WORK_TREE)` |no ancestor holds a `.git`
|`NotEnrolled(UNRECOGNISED_GIT)` |the `.git` is neither a directory nor a `.git` file that passes the checks, a
worktree moved by hand with its stale `gitdir` record among them
|`NotEnrolled(NO_ENROLMENT)` |no enrolment names the main checkout: an unenrolled repository, a nested repository
inside the root, a main checkout moved away from its `project_root`
|`NotEnrolled(BASELINE_WORKTREE)` |the toplevel is a baseline scratch worktree `baseline-<plan_id>-<sha12>`: any
name below the worktrees with the prefix `baseline-`, refused by the prefix alone, so that no copy of the plan id
grammar decides it and no such name is ever taken for a plan worktree
|`NotEnrolled(FOREIGN_TOPLEVEL)` |a worktree of the project elsewhere, or below the worktrees with another name
|`RootConflict(projectIds)` |more than one readable enrolment names the main checkout (`enrolment_root_conflict`)
|`EnrolmentRefused(refusal)` |an enrolment file was refused or could not be read: the match cannot be shown to be
the only one, because the refused file may name the main checkout too. Two readable matches are `RootConflict`
first
|===

Every `NotEnrolled` carries the outcome code `project_not_enrolled`; its reason is a diagnostic. No result holds the
path: what a request records is the project id. The integrity of an enrolment file against its sidecar is the
runtime's per-use check: `pm-core` holds no key, and the caller checks the HMAC of the enrolment the resolution names.

=== Pitfalls

* *A waiver rewritten*: a revocation is a new `revoked` line; editing or removing the line it revokes destroys the
  operator's record, and the digest sidecar reports the edit. Never write either log other than by `append`.
* *A decision ended by time*: neither a waiver request nor a question has a time limit. A request without an outcome is
  pending, not expired; a question is closed only by its closing line. Code that ends either by a clock invents a
  decision the operator never took.
* *A stored path bypassing the resolver*: a request records the project id, never a checkout path. A path kept and
  compared later skips the resolution, the enrolment match and the confinement; resolve the path the client names on
  each request.
* *Missing read as empty*: an absent store identity, an unreadable enrolment file and an unreadable line are outcomes
  of their own (`Absent`, `EnrolmentRefused`, a refused entry), never an empty record, no enrolment or no line.
* *A match read as unique*: an enrolment file that was refused may name the same project root as the readable one; the
  resolver never names a project while such a file exists, because that would decide a root conflict it did not read.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|A line of a waiver, consumption or question log is unreadable or of another format version
|`read()` reports it as a refused entry with its line number; the other lines are read, and a waiver or question keeps
the last readable line as its current one.

|An append stops at any step
|The recovery of the scope completes or discards the lines; a torn tail is reported by `read()` and repaired before
the next append.

|A transaction of another scope, or a log already a target of the transaction
|The append throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is added.

|A question line breaks an invariant
|`AlreadyAnswered` or `Conflict` is returned; nothing of the append is added.

|The question log holds a refused line when an append is checked
|`Unreadable` naming the first refused line is returned; nothing of the append is added. The store has no act that
repairs the log, so every later append is refused too, `withdraw` and `cancel` included.

|The move of an entry's questions finds a log in the new scope
|The commit of the new scope's transaction is refused (<<_create_target>>); the entry's log stays.

|`store.json` exists, readable or not, when a store is created
|`create` returns `EXISTED` and leaves it as it is.

|An enrolment file is refused when it is decoded, or exists and cannot be read
|`EnrolmentRefused` with that file's refusal (a read failure as `unreadable`, naming the file), also when one readable
enrolment names the main checkout: the match is not shown to be the only one. Two readable matches are `RootConflict`.

|The enrolment directory cannot be listed
|`resolve` throws `UncheckedIOException`.
|===

[#_slots_metrics_budgets_ledger]
== The Model-Slot, Metrics, Learned-Budget and Ledger Stores
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-EXEC-3[PM-EXEC-3], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7]_

This section covers the stores of the machine model slots, of the metrics rows, of the
learned budgets and the change ledger, and the writer of the operator's qualification settings. Each is read and
written strictly in `format_version` 1 over `StrictJson` and refuses what it cannot read as a `StoreRefusal` naming the
file. A store that owns its write transaction writes in one short transaction on the lock of its store; `MetricsStore`
and `ChangeLedger` instead stage their write in the caller's locked `ScopeTransaction`, so the scope's journal and
digest sidecar cover them. The schemas are in
link:{spec}/store-schemas/13-leases-integrity-testing.adoc#machine-lease-stores[Store Schemas § Machine Lease Stores],
link:{spec}/store-schemas/05-metrics-and-quality-report.adoc#metrics-store[Store Schemas § Metrics & Quality Report],
link:{spec}/store-schemas/09-derived-facts-and-build-timings.adoc#build-timings-store[Store Schemas § Build Timings
Store], link:{spec}/store-schemas/04-epic-and-change-ledger.adoc#_change_ledger_record_change_ledger_jsonl[Store Schemas
§ Change Ledger Record] and link:{spec}/store-schemas/08-project-configuration-local.adoc#local-config[Store Schemas §
Operator Local Configuration].

[horizontal]
`ModelSlotStore`, `ModelSlotCodec`:: The machine model slots `state/model-slots.json`: one lease per running worker,
warm or fresh, and the FIFO of the workers waiting for one. `update` runs one read, change and write under the lock
`LockKey.modelSlots`; the store decides nothing itself. Admission — the cap, the FIFO, the one-writer rule and the
reaping of dead runtimes — is `ModelSlotScheduler` of `runtime.launch`
(link:../../../pm-runtime/doc/developer/runtime-launch.adoc[Worker Launch]).
`Measurement`, `HarnessUsage`, `UsagePointers`:: The usage of a harness output. A figure is measured, `unmeasured` or
`not_applicable`; a profile's `headless.usage` pointers are read onto one parsed turn, and `total_tokens` is derived
from the five token components, never parsed
(link:../adr/core-store/042-metrics-row-three-valued-figures-and-verbatim-foreign-rows.adoc[ADR 042]).
`MetricsRow`, `WorkerIdleRow`, `MetricsCodec`, `MetricsStore`:: The task row and the worker-idle row of a
`metrics.json`, their codec and the store. The codec keeps a row of another kind (`wait`, `phase_close`, `served_read`)
as the raw JSON text it was read with and re-emits it token by token in the same shape (the task rows first, then the
idle rows, then the foreign ones); the store refuses a second row for the same task and
attempt, or a second idle row for the same worker. The store takes the `JournalScope` of its scope and writes through
`ScopeTransaction.replace`, so `metrics.json` is journaled and covered by the scope's digest sidecar; its row partition
follows the scope: a plan's file carries task rows with their `phase`, the epic and workspace files only task rows with
`phase: null`, and only the workspace file the worker-idle rows of the project's warm workers (ADR 044).
`BuildTimings`, `BuildTimingsCodec`, `BuildTimingsStore`:: The learned budgets `build-timings.json` of a project: per
budget key the clean, poisoned and cold baselines, the one-shot escalation and the instant of the last observation.
`observe` feeds the max-biased blend `floor(0.8 * max(L, d) + 0.2 * min(L, d))` into the baseline of the run's kind and
clears the escalation; `escalate` sets `E = max(t, min(2 * t, C))` for a timed-out run, which never feeds a baseline.
`WorkerBudget`:: The allocation of one worker's budget over the key `llm:<role>`: escalation, else operator, else the
learned value scaled by `1.25`, else the role floor — never below the role's `budget_floor_seconds`, whose compiled
minimum is `60`. The fixed probe budgets are unchanged (`runtime.job.ProbeBudget`).
`ChangeLedgerRecord`, `ChangeLedgerCodec`, `ChangeLedger`:: One line of a `change-ledger.jsonl`: a
`worktree_sha`-stamped build, change, job, task-diff or baseline-red row. The ledger takes the `JournalScope` of its
scope and appends through `ScopeTransaction.append`, which repairs a torn tail and draws the digest entry of the scope,
so a line is journaled with the other stores of the same decision and never rewritten; a digest of `unavailable`
carries how the git invocation failed and never satisfies a freshness gate (ADR 044).
`QualificationSettings`, `QualificationSettingsCodec`, `QualificationSettingsStore`:: The operator's
`settings.qualification` of `local-config.json` — the agreement level, the allowed warm-fresh gap and the two skip flags
— with the compiled defaults. The store reads the settings with the document's `revision`; a write names the revision
the caller read, replaces that one member, copies every other member through, increments the document's revision and
refuses an absent document or a revision the document has left behind with `StoreRefusal` outcome `conflict` (ADR 045,
link:../adr/core-store/043-qualification-settings-replaced-in-place.adoc[ADR 043]).

=== Invariants

* *One transaction per decision*: an admission and an observation each read and write their store in one transaction
  on its lock. A metrics row and a ledger append are staged in the caller-owned `ScopeTransaction`, which holds the
  scope lock, so no second writer comes between the check and the write.
* *One row partition per scope*: a task row of an epic or the workspace carries no `phase`, a worker-idle row is written
  only to the workspace, and the store refuses a row of another partition.
* *A stale revision is refused*: a write of the operator's qualification settings names the revision it read, and a
  document that has moved on is a `conflict` refusal, never an overwrite.
* *Unmeasured is never zero*: a usage figure the harness did not report is `unmeasured` with its reason, and the derived
  total of a row with an unmeasured applicable component is `unmeasured`.
* *Append-only ledgers*: the change ledger only grows; a torn tail is repaired by the JSONL store before the next
  append and the repair is handed to the audit sink.
* *A foreign member stays*: the metrics codec keeps a row kind it does not own, and the qualification codec keeps every
  member of the operator's document it does not own.
* *The learned blend is named*: a budget derived from a learned value carries the observation count it rests on, and a
  timed-out run never feeds a baseline.

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|A store is of another format version or unreadable
|`StoreRefusal` naming the file; the file is not touched.

|A second row for the same task and attempt, or a second idle row for a worker
|`MetricsStore` throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is written.

|A row outside the partition of the store's scope, or a worker-idle row outside the workspace
|`MetricsStore` throws `IllegalArgumentException`; nothing is written.

|A stored `total_tokens` disagrees with its components

|A baseline has observations but no value, or a value but no observation

|The qualification settings are written while `local-config.json` is absent
|`StoreRefusal.absent` naming the file; the store creates nothing.

|The qualification settings are written against a revision the document has left behind
|`StoreRefusal` with outcome `conflict` naming the file; nothing is written.

|An unavailable digest without its failure, or a `baseline` on another kind

|===

== Derived Facts Store
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-EXT-6[PM-EXT-6], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

This section covers the per-project cache `derived-facts.json`
(`<PM_MCP_BASE>/projects/<project_id>/derived-facts.json`,
link:{spec}/store-schemas/09-derived-facts-and-build-timings.adoc#derived-facts[Store Schemas § Derived Facts Store]):
the entry records, the strict codec, the registry through which a consumer binds a fact kind to its value schema, and
the store with the rules that decide when an entry is derived again. The derivations of the concrete kinds are not
here — each belongs to the package that consumes the kind.

[horizontal]
`DerivedFactKind`:: The closed enumeration of the sixteen kinds of the schema's `kind` enum. Each constant carries its
wire name (`fromWireName` reads it back) and `operatorOnly`, true for `AGENT_IDENTITY` alone: the identity of the agent
credential is shown to the operator and used server-side, and it never appears in a model-facing representation,
resource or tool result.
`DerivedFactKey`:: The key of an entry: a kind and an optional qualifier. Its wire form is `<kind>` or
`<kind>:<qualifier>` (for example `domain_activation:java`) and matches the schema's `propertyNames` pattern
`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,47}(:[A-Za-z0-9_.-]{1,128})?$`. A key whose kind part names no `DerivedFactKind` is refused. The
qualifier is opaque to the store; whether a kind admits one is declared by its `DerivedFactKindSpec`.
`DerivedFact`:: One entry: the kind, the value as a `JsonValue` tree or `null`, the `Source` (the derivation and its
class, `local` or `network`), the `inputDigest` (64 lower-case hexadecimal digits), the instant `derivedAt`, the
`State` (`current`, `stale` or `failed`) and, for a failed derivation, the `Failure` (a reason of at most 500 code
points and the instant it failed). The compact constructor holds the state shapes: `current` carries a value and no
failure, `stale` carries the last value and a failure, `failed` carries no value and a failure.
`DerivedFacts`:: The document: the entries by key in document order, `FORMAT_VERSION` 1, `empty()` and `fact(key)`.
The compact constructor refuses an entry filed under a key of another kind.
`DerivedFactsCodec`:: Reads and writes `derived-facts.json` over `StrictJson` as `BuildTimingsCodec` does: only the
current format version, only the declared members, each at most once, every required member, the closed values of
`kind`, `source.class` and `state`, the key grammar, the digest grammar, an instant in the one shape
`Instant.toString` writes, and the value and failure shapes of `DerivedFact`. Anything else is a `StoreRefusal`
naming the file, never an empty store. The value is read with `JsonValue.read` and written by a private generator over
the tree, which keeps the member order; an integer is written as its plain digits, and a fraction in the canonical form
the writer produces, so an exponent-spelled number or a negative zero is written in that form once and
`DerivedFactsCodecTest` states it. A current entry is written with `"failure": null`, and the reader accepts the member
absent or `null` there.
`DerivedFactKindSpec`:: What a consumer registers for a kind: the value schema as a JSON object, the `source.class`
values the kind admits (at least one), whether a key of the kind may carry a qualifier, and the description of the
derivation inputs. A schema that is not a JSON object and an empty class set are refused.
`DerivedFactKinds`:: The registry, an instance and never a static. `register(kind, spec)` binds a kind once — a second
registration is refused — and `spec(kind)` answers the specification or `Optional.empty()` for a kind never
registered, never a default. `validate(kind, value)` returns the violations of `SchemaSubset.validate` of the
registered schema and refuses a kind with no registration. `isOperatorOnly(kind)` answers the kind's flag whether it
is registered or not.
`DerivedFactReading`:: What a read of one fact can say, a sealed interface of four variants: `Value(fact)` for a
`current` entry inside its validity period, `Indeterminate(reason)` for a `stale` or `failed` entry (the reason is the
stored failure reason with the kind), `Expired()` for a `current` network entry past its validity period, and
`Absent()`. `couldNotEvaluate()` is empty only for `Value`, so a consumer that maps a reading onto a three-valued guard
result fails closed.
`DerivedFactDerivation`:: The function a consumer hands to a refresh, and the sealed outcome it returns: `Derived(value,
derivation, sourceClass)` or `Failed(reason)`. A failed derivation is a returned value, never an exception; an exception
escaping the function is a programming fault of the consumer and is not turned into a stored failure.
`DerivedFactStore`:: The reader and writer of `derived-facts.json`. Its constructor refuses a store file whose key is
not a `DERIVED_FACTS` lock, as `BuildTimingsStore` does. `snapshot()` reads without a lock and answers the empty
document when the file is absent; `fact(key)` answers one entry; `store()` answers the file. `reading(key, now)` answers
a `DerivedFactReading` at the instant passed in, and `refresh(key, inputDigest, derivation, now)` applies the decision
table below and returns the reading after the attempt. `modelFacing()` and `operatorFacts()` are the two projections
of the snapshot.

=== Registering a Kind

A consumer registers its kind once, with the value schema of the specification part that defines it, before the store
is read:

[source,java]
----
var kinds = new DerivedFactKinds();
kinds.register(DerivedFactKind.MODULE_DISCOVERY, DerivedFactKindSpec.builder()
        .schema(JsonValue.parse(moduleDiscoverySchema))
        .classes(Set.of(DerivedFact.DerivationClass.LOCAL))
        .qualifierAllowed(false)
        .inputs("the descriptor tree hash of the worktree")
        .build());
----

No concrete kind is registered by the data side, in production code or in a default registry: `module_discovery`
belongs to module discovery, `ci_provider` and `base_branch_default` to the CI contract, the onboarding facts to the
onboarding plans and `cost_calibration` to the recalibration, each registering its kind when it lands
(link:../adr/core-store/053-the-fact-kinds-are-an-enumeration-bound-by-a-registry.adoc[ADR 053],
link:../adr/core-store/054-the-fact-value-is-the-jsonvalue-tree.adoc[ADR 054]).

=== Refreshing a Fact

`DerivedFactStore.refresh(key, inputDigest, derivation, now)` applies the decision table of the specification in this
order; the first matching row derives again, and the last row returns the stored entry without calling the derivation:

[cols="3,2"]
|===
|Condition |Outcome

|No entry exists for the key
|Derive.

|The entry's `input_digest` differs from the digest passed, for a `local` and a `network` kind alike
|Derive.

|The entry's `source.class` is `network` and `now` is not before `derived_at` plus `86400` seconds
|Derive.

|The entry's `state` is `stale` or `failed`
|Derive. A failed derivation is retried by the next call that needs the fact; no back-off is invented.

|otherwise
|Return the stored entry; the derivation is not called.
|===

The validity period is the compiled constant `DerivedFactStore.NETWORK_VALIDITY`
(`Duration.ofSeconds(86400)`), not a setting: the specification says *fixed*. A local fact never expires by time; its
digest decides. The instant is always the caller's `now`; the store reads no wall clock.

A derived value is validated before it is written: the value against the schema registered for its kind through
`DerivedFactKinds.validate`, the qualifier against `DerivedFactKindSpec.qualifierAllowed`, and the source class against
the classes the kind admits. A value that violates any of them, a kind that is not registered, a qualifier a kind does
not admit and a source class a kind does not admit are a failed derivation with that reason, never a `current` entry.
The reason of a violation of an operator-only kind names the kind and the number of violations and never the value or a
violation text, so an `agent_identity` value never reaches a model-facing guard reason; a consumer's own `Failed` reason
is the consumer's text and must not carry an operator-only value
(link:../adr/core-store/064-the-operator-only-value-never-enters-a-reason.adoc[ADR 064]).

=== The Lock and Its Order

The derivation runs outside the lock: a network derivation is a provider call, and holding the lock across it would
make every writer of the file wait behind a provider. The lock is held for the re-read and the write of the one entry
only. `refresh` opens one transaction on the store's key `LockKey.derivedFacts(base, projectId)`, re-reads the file
under the lock, changes the one entry and writes the whole document through `AtomicStoreFile`.

A refresh made inside a scope transaction therefore takes `locks/derived-facts.lock` in its own transaction after the
caller's lock is held, which is the place of `LockLevel.DERIVED_FACTS` in the total order. The lock manager asserts the
order within one transaction only and does not see the caller's keys, so the order of a caller's transaction relative
to the store's is a convention of the callers. An acquisition of a scope lock while the derived-facts lock is held,
inside one transaction, is a violation the lock manager refuses.

Two refreshers of the same fact do not overwrite a newer result with an older one: the write re-reads the entry and,
when the stored entry carries the same digest with a later `derived_at`, keeps it and returns it
(link:../adr/core-store/056-the-derivation-runs-outside-the-lock.adoc[ADR 056]).

The invariants of the write, per crash window (R14 (8)):

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Crash after |What the store holds

|the decision read, before the derivation
|The file as it was. Nothing was written; the next call decides again.

|the derivation, before the lock
|The file as it was. The derived outcome is lost and derived again by the next call; no half entry exists.

|the lock, before its re-read
|The file as it was; the process's lock is released with the process.

|the re-read under the lock, before the write
|The file as it was. No half transition exists.

|the write, before it returns
|The new document, complete: `AtomicStoreFile` writes a temporary file, syncs it and renames it over the store, so a
reader sees the old or the new document and nothing in between.

|the write, before the reading returns
|The new document; the returned reading is rebuilt from the entry that was written.
|===

=== Stale and Failed Entries

A `Failed` outcome for a key whose entry carries a value rewrites the entry as `state: stale`; it keeps the value, the
source, the `input_digest` and the `derived_at` of the last good derivation and sets `failure` to the reason and the
instant of the attempt. The last good digest is kept because the kept value was derived from it; the next call derives
again because the state is not `current`, whatever the digest (the decision table retries `stale` and `failed` entries
without a back-off;
(link:../adr/core-store/057-a-stale-entry-keeps-its-last-good-digest.adoc[ADR 057]).

A `Failed` outcome for a key with no entry, or with only a `failed` one, writes `state: failed`, no value, the digest
that was attempted, the instant of the attempt and the failure. A later `Derived` outcome rewrites the entry as
`current` with no failure. A failed refresh of one key changes no other entry.

The failure reason is cut to the schema bound of 500 code points. The failure of a network derivation that the
credential is not permitted to perform carries the missing permission in the consumer's own text; the store carries it
unchanged. A failed entry names the placeholder derivation `unknown` and the first source class its kind admits,
because the schema requires a source on every entry and no derivation produced a value.

The stale and failed transitions hold per crash window (R14 (8)):

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Crash after |What the file holds

|the decision read, before the derivation
|The entry as it was. Nothing was written.

|the derivation, before the lock
|The entry as it was; the outcome is lost and derived again by the next call.

|the re-read under the lock, before the write
|The entry as it was. No half transition exists.

|the write, before it returns
|The new entry, and every other entry as it was: the document is replaced by atomic rename and the changed entry is
written with the rest.

|the write, before the reading returns
|The new entry; the reading is rebuilt from the entry that was written.
|===

=== The Readings

`reading(key, now)` and the reading a `refresh` returns are the four variants of `DerivedFactReading`:

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Reading |When

|`Value(fact)`
|The entry is `current` and, for a `network` kind, `now` is before `derived_at` plus `86400` seconds.

|`Indeterminate(reason)`
|The entry is `stale` or `failed`; the reason is the stored failure reason with the kind.

|`Expired()`
|The entry is `current`, its class is `network`, and its validity period has run out. A read never refreshes by itself;
the next call that needs the fact refreshes it.

|`Absent()`
|The store holds no entry for the key.
|===

`couldNotEvaluate()` answers the reason for every variant but `Value`. That is the failing-closed contract: a missing,
expired, stale or failed fact is never a pass, and the consumer maps it to `COULD_NOT_EVALUATE`, the
`guard_indeterminate` condition (link:../adr/core-store/058-the-reading-fails-closed.adoc[ADR 058]). A gate depends on
a fact through `DerivedFactStore.reading` and maps the reading with
`DerivedFactGuards.evaluate` in `pm-workflow` (see
link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-engine.adoc[Developer Guide § `workflow.engine`]).

=== The Operator-Only Projection

`modelFacing()` is the only collection a model-facing representation, the `pm://project/config` resource or a tool
result may be built from. It holds every entry whose kind is not `operatorOnly`, and `agent_identity` — qualified or
not — is excluded whether a consumer registered it or not: the exclusion is a property of the kind enumeration, not of
a registration (link:../adr/core-store/060-the-operator-only-exclusion-is-a-property-of-the-kind.adoc[ADR 060]).
`operatorFacts()` is the whole set for the operator's own clients; `reading`, `fact`, `refresh` and `snapshot` are the
server-side and operator-side reads.

The assembly (`plan-marshall-mcp`) owes the other half of this duty: every representation and tool result it builds,
and its `ModelFacingFieldAuditTest` registry, serves only `modelFacing()`; the operator's local API and the web app
serve `operatorFacts()`.

=== Refusals

An unreadable store file — content that does not parse — and a file of another `format_version` are a `StoreRefusal`
naming the file: the store is never silently rebuilt, and the operator deletes the cache file to force a re-derivation
(link:../adr/core-store/059-an-unreadable-store-is-a-refusal.adoc[ADR 059]). A file that cannot be read at the
filesystem level is the `UncheckedIOException` of the snapshot read.

=== Invariants

* *One shape per state*: a `DerivedFact` of `current` carries a value and no failure, `stale` its last value and a
  failure, `failed` no value and a failure; every other combination is refused at construction.
* *The key names the entry's kind*: `DerivedFacts` refuses an entry whose kind is not the kind of its key.
* *The schema is the contract*: the member lists of the codec, the enumerations, the patterns and the reason bound are
  pinned to the schema block by `DerivedFactsSchemaParityTest`; the block is test input and stays byte-stable (R10).
* *A kind is registered once*: a second registration is refused, and a kind never registered has no default schema.
* *A local fact is decided by its digest, a network fact by its digest and the fixed period*: the period is
  `NETWORK_VALIDITY`, a constant, and every read takes the instant it decides at.
* *One write, one entry, one lock*: a refresh reads and writes the whole document under `locks/derived-facts.lock`,
  changes one entry and loses no update of another key; it never overwrites a newer result of the same digest.
* *The reading fails closed*: only `Value` has no `couldNotEvaluate` reason; a missing, expired, stale or failed fact
  is never a pass.
* *No operator-only fact is model-facing*: `modelFacing()` excludes `agent_identity` through the kind enumeration,
  registered or not.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Behaviour

|The document declares another format version, or is unreadable

|A key outside the grammar, or a key whose kind part names no kind
|`StoreRefusal` naming the file.

|A key of another kind than its entry

=== Lock Keys and the Lock Order

`LockLevel` declares the eleven levels of the total lock order in acquisition order: workspace, plan, epic, epic
worktree, lesson store, local configuration, derived facts, build timings, queue, machine store, leaf.

`LockKey` is the canonical key of one lock: its level, the absolute and normalized lock file, and the id that orders it
within its level. There is one factory per lock (`LockKey.plan(base, projectId, planId)`, `LockKey.mergeQueue(base)`,
…), and the factories are the one list of the lock paths. A project, plan or epic id is checked against the identifier
pattern before it becomes part of a path. The lock files of a project lie below `projects/<project_id>/locks/`, those of
the machine stores below `locks/` (those of the machine scope below `machine/locks/`, the lock of an onboarding entry
below `onboardings/locks/`), outside the directories they protect. Only the two leaf keys name the guarded file
itself (`logs/audit.jsonl`, a plan's `mcp/mailbox.jsonl`). Keys are ordered by level, then by id, then by lock path; the
id of a machine store lock is its lock name without the extension, so `ab.lock` sorts before `ab-c.lock`.

`LockOrder` is the order assertion as a pure function of the held keys and the requested key; it opens no file. Its
`Decision` is `Reentry` (the key is held already), `Permitted`, or `Violation(held, requested)`: nothing after a leaf,
and every other key after every held key. The lock manager asks it on every acquisition and refuses a `Violation`
(link:core-service.adoc#_the_lock_manager_interface_and_the_lock_order[`core.service` § The Lock Manager Interface and
the Lock Order]).

The process locks of the runtime, its singleton lock `locks/runtime.lock` and the start lock
`locks/runtime-start.lock`, are held for a process, not a transaction. They have no `LockKey` and do not go through the
lock manager.

Adding a lock of an existing level is a factory in `LockKey` with its fixed path below `projects/<project_id>/locks/`
or `locks/`, and a case in `LockKeyTest`. A new level is a constant in `LockLevel` at its place in the order, then its
factory, and two new boundary cases in `LockOrderTest`; the order is stated in
link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7], which changes with it.

Tests: `LockOrderTest` (a refused acquisition at every level boundary, re-entry, nothing after a leaf, the order within
the plan, epic and machine store levels), `LockKeyTest` (the path of every factory and the identifier check).

[#_atomic_write_and_lock_free_read]
=== Atomic Write and Lock-Free Read

`AtomicStoreFile` is the one write path of a store replaced by rename. It is bound to the key of its sibling lock file;
a key whose lock file is the store itself is refused with `IllegalArgumentException`, because the rename replaces the
inode and a lock on the old file excludes nobody. `read` and `write` must be called inside a transaction that holds the
key; otherwise they throw `InternalFaultException` (reason `store_lock_not_held`) and nothing is written.

`write` writes the content to a sibling temporary file `<store>.tmp.<uuid>`, readable and writable by its owner only,
forces it to disk, renames it over the store (`ATOMIC_MOVE`), and then forces the directory of the store to disk, so
that the rename itself survives a crash. A temporary file of a failed write is removed. If the directory cannot be
forced, the store has already been replaced, and the `UncheckedIOException` says so. The journal of a scope transaction
is written by the package-private variant whose temporary file is named after the transaction id and is left in place
when the write fails, for the recovery.

`StoreSnapshot.read(store)` reads without any lock. A rename replaces the file in one step, so the read sees the content
before or after a write, never part of one. A store that does not exist is a snapshot without content, not a failure.
Given a recorded size and SHA-256 (`read(store, recordedSize, recordedSha256)`), the snapshot reports the
`StoreIntegrity` `VERIFIED` or `DIGEST_UNVERIFIED`; without them it is `UNCHECKED`. It never decides that a store was
edited, because it may have seen a transaction in flight: only a reader holding the store's lock decides.

Neither `AtomicStoreFile` nor `LeaseStore` creates the directory of a store; whoever creates the store's scope or
starts the runtime does.

Tests: `AtomicStoreFileTest`, `StoreSnapshotTest`.

[#_format_versions]
=== Format Versions and Refusals

A reader of a store document or record accepts exactly the current `format_version` of its file kind. What it does not
accept it throws as a `StoreRefusal`, an unchecked exception with:

* `outcome()`: one of the closed `StoreRefusal.Outcome` values `ABSENT`, `UNREADABLE`, `FORMAT_OUTDATED` and
  `FORMAT_NEWER`, each with its wire name in `code()` (`absent`, `unreadable`, `format_outdated`, `format_newer`);
* `file()`: the refused file;
* `found()`: the version the content declares, empty when the file is absent or unreadable;
* `current()`: the current version of the file kind.

The message names the file and both versions. A refusal is never repaired: no reader migrates or rewrites what it
refuses. The factories are `absent(file, current)`, `unreadable(file, current, detail, cause)` and
`ofVersion(file, found, current)`, which picks outdated or newer from the two versions.

Every versioned artifact is at `format_version` 1. A format change replaces version 1 in place: no reader carries a
migration, and no constant is raised for a change.

`StrictJson` is the reading every JSON store codec of `pm-core` shares, built on the streaming API of `jackson-core`;
the lesson codec is the one store codec of the package that reads YAML instead, with its own strict reader (see
<<_lesson_record_and_file_format>>).
`StrictJson.read(file, document, current, reader)` checks the version first (`requireCurrentVersion`: a JSON object
with exactly one integer `format_version`, equal to the current one), then lets the codec's reader read the object, and
refuses anything that follows it. Whatever the codec throws through the helpers, or the parser throws, becomes a
`StoreRefusal` naming the file.

A new codec follows the shape of `DigestSidecar`, the smallest one:

. Declare `public static final int FORMAT_VERSION` and write it first with
  `generator.writeNumberField(FIELD_FORMAT_VERSION, FORMAT_VERSION)`, using `StrictJson.factory()`.
. Read with `StrictJson.read(file, document, FORMAT_VERSION, Codec::readDocument)`. In `readDocument`, move from field to
  field with `nextField(parser, seen)`, which refuses a field that appears twice; skip `format_version`; read every value
  with its typed helper (`string`, `nullableString`, `integer`, `instant`, `nullableInstant`, `requireObject`,
  `requireArray`); and throw `undeclared(field)` in the `default` branch.
. After the loop, call `requireFields(seen, "<object>", …)` for the required fields, and turn an
  `IllegalArgumentException` of the record's constructor into `unreadable(message, cause)`.
. Write an instant with `writeInstant`, so it has the one shape `instant` reads: `Instant.toString()`, UTC with `Z`.
  `instant` refuses another spelling of the same moment as unreadable instead of normalising it, so a document written
  by one component and read by another round-trips byte-identical.

Tests: `StoreRefusalTest` (all four outcomes with file, found and current version), and the refusal cases of
`LeaseCodecTest`, `TransactionJournalTest` and `DigestSidecarTest`.

[#_jsonl_stores]
=== JSONL Stores

A `JsonlStoreFile` binds a JSONL store file to the `LockKey` that guards it. Every method that writes or reads under the
lock refuses a transaction that does not hold the key with `InternalFaultException` (reason `store_lock_not_held`),
before it touches the file.

* `append(transaction, records, auditSink)`: refuses an empty block or one that does not end with a newline
  (`IllegalArgumentException`), repairs a torn tail as `repairTail` does, then writes the whole block in one write on a
  channel opened with `StandardOpenOption.APPEND` and forces the file to disk. The first append creates the file,
  readable and writable by its owner only; the directory must exist.
* `repairTail(transaction, auditSink)`: if the last line has no newline, cuts the file back to the end of the last
  complete line, forces it to disk and hands `AuditEvent.jsonlTailRepaired` to the sink. A file that ends with a
  newline, is empty or does not exist is left alone, and nothing new is recorded. Before the cut the record is owed in
  the intent file `<store>.audit-intent` beside the store (`auditIntentFile()`), written by atomic rename and forced to
  disk; an intent that already owes the record of this very tail (a repair stopped between its intent and its cut) is
  completed and the record is not owed twice. The intent is deleted once the sink accepted every record it owes, and
  every later `repairTail`, so every `append`, hands what it still owes over again. An intent file that is unreadable or
  of another format version is a `StoreRefusal`, and nothing is cut.
* `read(transaction)` reads under the lock, `snapshot()` without it. Neither repairs. Both return a `Content`: the
  complete lines with their line numbers, counted from 1, and apart from them the byte offset of a torn tail, never a
  record.
* `Content.decode(formatVersion, decoder)` checks each line for the current `format_version`, so a record of another
  version is refused as outdated or newer and not as unreadable, and then hands it to the decoder. It returns one
  `Entry` per line: `Accepted(line, value)` or `Refused(line, refusal)`. A refused line does not stop the lines after
  it.

A JSONL store of a scope is appended to through its `ScopeTransaction` (<<_scope_transaction>>), not through `append`
directly; `append` is for the JSONL stores outside a scope, under their own key.

*Known limitation, leaf keys:* a leaf key names the store file itself (`logs/audit.jsonl`, a plan's
`mcp/mailbox.jsonl`), and on POSIX closing any channel on a file releases every OS lock the process holds on it. An
append or a repair under a leaf key therefore drops the OS lock, and only the in-process lock of the key remains. Such
a store is safe only while every writer runs in the one runtime that holds the in-process lock.

Tests: `JsonlStoreFileTest`.

[#_journal_and_sidecar]
=== Journal and Sidecar Documents

`TransactionJournal` is the record of the journal document, `format_version` 1:

[source,json]
----
{"format_version": 1, "transaction_id": "...", "scope_type": "plan", "scope_id": "...", "created_at": "...",
 "targets": [{"path": "...", "mode": "append", "staged_path": "...", "size_before": 120, "new_size": 160, "new_sha256": "..."},
             {"path": "...", "mode": "delete"}],
 "owed_events": [{"event": "cross_scope_reconciled", "category": "store", "outcome": "succeeded",
                  "details": {"reconciliation": "...", "store": "...", "transaction_id": "..."}}]}
----

* `transaction_id` is a `TransactionId`: a lower-case UUID of version 7, so ids sort by time. `TransactionId.ofSuffix`
  recognises it as the last name segment of a staged file, which is how the recovery tells a staged file from any other.
* `scope_type` is the wire name of a `JournalScopeType` (`plan`, `epic`, `workspace`, `lessons`); `scope_id` is the plan
  or epic id and `null` for the other two, enforced in both directions.
* `targets` is never empty. A `Target` has its `Mode` (`REPLACE`, `APPEND`, `DELETE`), its paths relative to the path
  base of the scope, `sizeBefore` and the `StoreDigest` of the store once applied. A create target of a scope
  transaction is journaled as a `REPLACE` (<<_create_target>>).
* `owed_events` lists the audit records the transaction hands over once its targets are applied, possibly none.

The reader refuses a target, as an unreadable journal, when:

* an `APPEND` target has no `size_before` (refused as well when the record is built); a `REPLACE` target may omit it;
* its `path` or `staged_path` is not a normalized relative path;
* the `staged_path` of a replace or append is not the name `TransactionId.stagedName(path, mode)` derives from the
  store, the mode and the transaction id, so a recovery never moves another file over a store;
* a `DELETE` target (`Target.delete(path)`) carries a member of a staged write: it is `{"path", "mode"}` alone.

`JournalRecovery` refuses in addition a journal whose store or staged path passes through a symbolic link below the path
base, as a journal no transaction of the scope wrote.

An owed record is the object `{"event", "category", "outcome", "details"}` that `OwedAuditEvents` reads and writes. A
detail value is a string, an integer or a list of strings, and the details are written in the sorted order of their
names; `ScopeTransaction.owe` refuses a record with another value type with `IllegalArgumentException`.

`DigestSidecar` is the record of the sidecar document `{"format_version": 1, "stores": {"<relative path>": {"size",
"sha256"}}}`. Its entries are written in the sorted order of their paths, so the same digests give the same bytes.
`DigestSidecar.load(file)` reads without a lock and returns an empty sidecar when it does not exist; `with(path, digest)`
returns a copy with one entry changed, `without(path)` one without the entry of a deleted store. `StoreDigest` is
`(size, sha256)` with `of(bytes)` (SHA-256 in lower-case hex) and `matches(bytes)`.

Neither type decides anything: the journal is written by `ScopeTransaction` and read by `JournalRecovery`; the sidecar
is written only through the journal.

Tests: `TransactionJournalTest`, `DigestSidecarTest`.

[#_scope_transaction]
=== Writing a Scope Transaction

A `JournalScope` says where a scope's journal and sidecar lie, which directories it owns, which key guards it, and the
path base its store paths are relative to. Its factories follow the per-project machine store `projects/<project_id>/`
below the base directory of the runtime:

[cols="1,2,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Factory |Path base |Journal |Sidecar |Lock key

|`JournalScope.plan(base, projectId, planId)` |`plans/<plan_id>/` |`mcp/transaction-journal.json` |`mcp/digests.json`
|`LockKey.plan`
|`JournalScope.epic(base, projectId, epicId)` |`epics/<epic_id>/` |`transaction-journal.json` |`digests.json`
|`LockKey.epic`
|`JournalScope.workspace(base, projectId)` |the project store root |`workspace/mcp/transaction-journal.json`
|`workspace/mcp/digests.json` |`LockKey.workspace`
|`JournalScope.lessons(base, projectId)` |`lessons/` |`.transaction-journal.json` |`.digests.json`
|`LockKey.lessonStore`
|===

The workspace owns only `workspace/` and `pending/` below its path base, never `plans/` or `epics/`; every other scope
owns its path base. `resolve(relativePath)` refuses a path that is absolute or leaves the path base.

[source,java]
----
var scope = JournalScope.plan(base, projectId, planId);
try (var transaction = lockManager.openTransaction()) {
    transaction.acquire(scope.lockKey());
    var write = ScopeTransaction.open(transaction, scope, auditSink, clock);
    write.replace("mcp/status.json", status);
    write.append("logs/work.jsonl", records);
    write.commit();
}
----

`open` refuses a transaction that does not hold the scope's key (`InternalFaultException`, `store_lock_not_held`).
`replace`, `append`, `delete` and `create` (<<_create_target>>) collect the targets; they refuse a second target for the
same path, the journal or the sidecar of the scope, a path outside the directories the scope owns or reached through a
symbolic link below the path base, and, for `append`, a block that is empty or does not end with a newline. `commit()`
runs the five steps:

. *Stage*: every replaced store to `<store>.tmp.<transaction id>`, every appended block to `<store>.append.<transaction
  id>`, each forced to disk. Before an append is staged, the tail of its store is repaired (`JsonlStoreFile.repairTail`),
  so the size before is the repaired size. A delete stages nothing.
. *Journal*: the journal, written by `AtomicStoreFile` with a temporary file named after the transaction id, and forced
  with its directory.
. *Apply*: every staged file renamed over its store (`ATOMIC_MOVE`), every staged block appended and its staged file
  deleted, every deleted store removed if it exists; then the directories of the stores are forced.
. *Digests*: the sidecar with the new digest of every replaced or appended store and without the entry of every deleted
  one; the other entries stay.
. *Close*: the owed records handed to the sink, then the journal deleted and its directory forced. If the sink refuses a
  record, the commit returns with its data complete and leaves the journal, which the next recovery closes after
  handing the records over.

A transaction is committed once. A commit without a target, a second commit, and a commit while the journal of an
earlier transaction is still open are refused with `IllegalStateException` before anything is written; the scope is
recovered first. A commit that fails throws (`UncheckedIOException` for a file that cannot be written) and leaves its
journal and staged files for the recovery; it is not tried again. `ScopeTransaction` creates no directory: the
directories of the stores exist before the commit.

`transactionId()` is generated when the transaction is opened; it is the id of the journal, the staged files and the
`transaction_rolled_forward` record. `owe(event)` adds an audit record to the journal, which the transaction hands over
at the close. The owed record carries an id of its own, issued by `recordId()`, never `transactionId()`
(<<_audit_log>>, One Transaction Id per Record).

Tests: `JournalScopeTest`, `ScopeTransactionTest`.

[#_recovery_of_a_stopped_transaction]
=== Recovery of a Stopped Transaction

`JournalRecovery.recover(transaction, scope, auditSink)` runs under the key of the scope (refused with
`InternalFaultException` otherwise) and answers with one of three outcomes:

* `NothingToRecover(deletedStagedFiles)`: there is no journal. Staged files that a transaction left before it journaled
  are deleted: every regular file in the scope's owned directories whose name ends in `.tmp.<transaction id>` or
  `.append.<transaction id>`.
* `RolledForward(journal, applied)`: every target was applied already or is applied now: a replace by renaming its
  staged file, an append by cutting its store back to `size_before` and appending the staged block, a delete by removing
  its store. A replace or append target is _applied_ when its store has the size and SHA-256 the journal records, a
  delete target when its store is absent; a delete is always appliable, so it never makes a journal violate its
  invariant. Then the sidecar takes the digests of the journal, one `transaction_rolled_forward` record and the records
  the journal owes are handed to the sink, and only then the journal and the staged files are deleted. If the sink
  refuses a record, the outcome is the same, but the journal stays; the next recovery finds every target applied,
  writes the same digests and hands the records over again. `applied` lists the targets this recovery applied.
* `InvariantViolation(journal, stores)`: a target is neither applied nor appliable. It is _appliable_ when applying its
  staged file yields the digest the journal records: the staged bytes of a replace have it, and the store of an append
  cut to its `size_before`, followed by the staged block, has it. A missing or changed staged file, and an append store
  that is shorter than, or differs from, what it was before the append, make it violating. Every target is checked
  before the first is applied, so nothing is applied, and the journal and every store stay as they are; `stores` names
  the violating stores in the order of the journal.

A journal or a sidecar of another format version, or one that is unreadable, propagates as `StoreRefusal`, and the
journal is left in place. A recovery of a consistent scope changes no byte and records nothing.

At runtime start, `JournalRollForwardStep` of `runtime.start` runs the recovery for every scope of a project; an
`InvariantViolation` or a `StoreRefusal` is a failure of that scope and stops the recovery of the project before the
cross-scope reconciliation
(link:../../../pm-runtime/doc/developer/runtime-start.adoc#_start_drain_and_recovery[`runtime.start` § The Start
Sequence, the Drain and the Recovery]). `ScopeIntegrity` runs it before every locked verification (<<_digests>>).

Tests: `JournalRecoveryTest`.

[#_digests]
=== Verifying and Re-Baselining Digests

`ScopeIntegrity` decides only under the scope's key, and only after the recovery of the scope has run:

* `verify(transaction, scope, relativePath, auditSink)` runs `JournalRecovery.recover` and compares one store with its
  sidecar entry. The answer is a `Verification`: `Verified(store, digest)`; `Mismatch(store, recorded, current)`, with
  `current` empty for a store whose entry outlives its file, the evidence of an edit outside a transaction;
  `Untracked(store)` when the store exists and the sidecar has no entry; `Absent(store)` when neither exists, which is
  what a transaction's delete leaves and is consistent; or the recovery's `InvariantViolation`, and then nothing was
  compared. A path that leaves the path base or passes through a symbolic link below it is refused with
  `IllegalArgumentException`.
* `verifyAll(transaction, scope, auditSink)` does the same for every entry of the sidecar, in the sorted order of the
  paths, or answers with the `InvariantViolation` alone.
* `snapshot(scope, relativePath)` takes no lock and decides nothing. It returns a `StoreSnapshot`: `DIGEST_UNVERIFIED`
  while the journal of the scope exists or when the content differs from the sidecar entry, `VERIFIED` when it matches,
  `UNCHECKED` when the sidecar has no entry.
* `rebaseline(transaction, scope, relativePath, auditSink)` accepts an edit: after the recovery it opens a
  `ScopeTransaction` that replaces the store by its own current bytes, so the new digest is recorded through the journal
  like any other, and then returns `verifyAll` of the scope. If the recovery answers `InvariantViolation`, that is the
  answer and nothing is written. A store that does not exist is refused with `IllegalArgumentException`, and nothing is
  journaled.

Tests: `ScopeIntegrityTest`.

[#_ordered_writes]
=== Ordered Writes and Reconcilers

An `OrderedWrite` is a non-empty list of `WriteStep(key, write)`, the authoritative store first. `run(lockManager)`
opens one lock transaction per step, acquires that step's key, runs its write and closes the transaction before the
next step, so no step holds the keys of two stores. What a step throws propagates once its transaction is closed, and
the steps after it do not run.

`OrderedWrite.reconcile(lockManager, reconcilers, auditSink)` runs the `CrossScopeReconciler`s in their order, passes
each the sink, and returns every `Reconciliation(name, store)` they applied. It records nothing itself: each derived
write owes its `cross_scope_reconciled` record on its own `ScopeTransaction` through
`OrderedWrite.oweReconciled(write, reconciliation)`, which gives the record its own `recordId()`, never the id of the
write. The record is thus in the journal before the derived store changes, and is delivered with the transaction or by
the next recovery of its scope. At runtime start, `CrossScopeReconciliationStep` of `runtime.start` runs the reconcilers
registered for a project after the journals of its scopes were rolled forward.

The substrate knows no concrete flow. A package that adds a write across scopes:

. Builds its `OrderedWrite`: the authoritative store first, then every store derived from it, each step a write of its
  own store under its own key (a `ScopeTransaction` for a scope's stores).
+
[source,java]
----
new OrderedWrite(List.of(
        new OrderedWrite.WriteStep(planScope.lockKey(), transaction -> writePlan(transaction)),
        new OrderedWrite.WriteStep(lessonScope.lockKey(), transaction -> claimLesson(transaction))))
    .run(lockManager);
----
. Implements a `CrossScopeReconciler` for it, `reconcile(lockManager, auditSink)`: read the authoritative store under
  its key; for each derived store, check under that store's key whether the derived write is in place and apply it if
  not, one `ScopeTransaction` per store, opened with the given sink, on which it calls `OrderedWrite.oweReconciled`
  before the commit; return a `Reconciliation` for every write applied, and nothing when everything is in place.
. Registers the reconciler for the reconciliation at runtime start, and proves the flow with a crash-point test
  (<<_testing_with_crash_points>>).

Tests: `OrderedWriteTest`.

[#_substrate_audit_records]
=== Audit Records

The substrate hands three records to the `AuditSink`, all of category `store` and outcome `succeeded`, built by the
factories of `AuditEvent`, which also hold the detail keys:

* `jsonl_tail_repaired` (`AuditEvent.jsonlTailRepaired`), from `JsonlStoreFile` and the staging of an append: the
  store, the number and SHA-256 of the bytes cut off, and the id of the repair, a `TransactionId` generated for it, as
  `transaction_id`;
* `transaction_rolled_forward` (`AuditEvent.transactionRolledForward`), from `JournalRecovery`: the type and id of the
  scope, the transaction id and the paths of the journal's targets; a scope without an id (workspace, lesson store) has
  no `scope_id` detail;
* `cross_scope_reconciled` (`AuditEvent.crossScopeReconciled`), owed by the scope transaction of a derived write: the
  reconciliation rule, the store it wrote and the record's own id.

Each record is delivered at least once. A durable intent holds it from before the mutation that makes its cause
disappear until `AuditSink.append` returned: the transaction journal for a roll-forward and for the records a
transaction owes, the intent file `<store>.audit-intent` for a tail repair.

The intent file is read, written and handed over by the package-private `AuditIntentFile`: the document
`{"format_version": 1, "owed_events": [<owed record>]}`, written by one atomic rename under the store's lock, which
exists only while a record is owed. Its hand-over deletes the file and forces its directory once every record was
handed over, rewrites it with the rest after a part of them, and leaves it as it is if none was. The enrolment store
keeps the records of its operator writes in the same document, `enrolments/<project_id>.json.audit-intent`
(<<_the_enrolment_store>>).

`OwedAuditEvents.deliver` hands the owed records over in their order and stops at the first one the sink refuses with
`UncheckedIOException`, the failure `AuditSink.append` declares. The intent then keeps what was not handed over, the
data outcome is still the one the mutation produced, and the warning `PM_CORE-100` (`AUDIT_RECORD_DEFERRED`) names the
record, its `transaction_id` and the intent. The next recovery of the scope, or the next repair of the store, hands the
records over again. A record handed over again carries the same `transaction_id`, by which a reader of the audit log
drops the duplicate (<<_audit_log>>). A process that stops between the mutation and the append leaves the intent in the
same way.

Tests: `AuditEventTest` (`core.service`), and the delivery tests of `JournalRecoveryTest`, `JsonlStoreFileTest`,
`ScopeTransactionTest` and `OrderedWriteTest`.

[#_testing_with_crash_points]
=== Testing with Crash Points

A crash is tested in-process. `ScopeTransaction` and `OrderedWrite` each take a package-private `StepHook`, called at
every boundary; production passes `StepHook.NONE`:

* `ScopeTransaction.StepHook.after(step, target)` is called after each `STAGE` and each `APPLY` of a target (with its
  index), after `JOURNAL` and after `DIGESTS`; only the close follows the last one.
* `OrderedWrite.StepHook.after(step)` is called after each step, once its transaction is closed, the authoritative step
  being 0.

A test stops the write by throwing from the hook at one boundary. Because nothing in the write path removes a staged
file or the journal, the files are then exactly what a killed process leaves. The test opens a new lock transaction, as
after a restart, runs `JournalRecovery` (and the reconcilers of a flow), and asserts the stores, the sidecar, the
absence of journal and staged files, and the audit records. `JournalRecoveryTest` runs this over every boundary of a
commit with two replaces and one append; `OrderedWriteTest` stops a flow of an authoritative and two derived stores,
each in a scope of its own, between its writes. Every such test has a matched control: a recovery or reconciliation of
a consistent state changes nothing and records nothing.

The fixtures of the `pm-core` tests in `de.planmarshall.core.store` serve them:

* `RecordingLockManager`: a `LockManager` that takes no lock of the operating system, records every key acquired and
  every transaction opened, and answers `holds` for the keys a transaction acquired until it is closed. It asserts no
  lock order; a test of the order uses `FileLockManager` of `runtime.lock`.
* `RecordingAuditSink`: keeps the records it is handed, in their order; `withoutTransactionIds` compares records whose
  id a test cannot know in advance.
* `FailingAuditSink`: refuses its first appends with `UncheckedIOException` (`refusing(n)`), or stops the caller there as
  a killed process (`stopping(n)`), and records the appends after them.

The hooks are package-private, so a crash-point test lives in the package `de.planmarshall.core.store`.

=== Pitfalls of the Substrate

* *Never write a scope store outside a `ScopeTransaction`.* An `AtomicStoreFile.write` or a `JsonlStoreFile.append` on a
  store of a scope changes it without its digest, and the next locked verification reports the change as an edit outside
  the runtime. The sidecar itself is written only by the digests step and the roll-forward.
* *Never decide a breach lock-free.* `snapshot` may see a transaction in flight; only `verify` and `verifyAll`, under the
  scope's key and after the recovery, decide.
* *An append journal entry always carries `size_before`.* Without it the recovery could not tell an applied append from a
  torn one, and would append the block a second time. Keep the refusal on both the build and the read path when the
  journal changes.
* *Never clean up a staged file or the journal in a `finally`.* A killed process runs no `finally`, so the cleanup would
  hide from the in-process crash tests exactly the state the recovery must handle.
* *Never leave a file named like a staged file in a scope's directories.* The recovery deletes every file there whose
  name ends in `.tmp.<transaction id>` or `.append.<transaction id>` when no journal names it.
* *Create the directories first.* Neither `ScopeTransaction`, `JsonlStoreFile`, `AtomicStoreFile` nor `LeaseStore`
  creates a directory.
* *An audit append after the mutation can be lost.* Once a store mutation is done, the condition that produced its
  record is gone: a recovery finds no journal, a repair no torn tail, a reconciler the derived store in place. Owe the
  record in a durable intent before the mutation, remove the intent only after `AuditSink.append` returned, and let the
  next recovery or repair replay it; never catch a refused append and drop the record.

[#_the_lease_store]
=== The Lease Store

A lease store document is `{"format_version": 1, "leases": [{"key", "owner", "acquired_at", "lease_expires_at"}]}`;
the key is any non-blank string, unique in the store. The nested `owner` object contains `project_id`, `scope_type`,
`scope_id`, `holder_instance` (`runtime_pid`, `runtime_started_at`) and the nullable `orphaned_at`.
`LeaseCodec` accepts exactly that shape and refuses every other with a `StoreRefusal` naming the store file and
carrying the version found and the current one (a lower version is `FORMAT_OUTDATED`, a higher one `FORMAT_NEWER`, a
missing or non-integer one `UNREADABLE`; <<_format_versions>>). An instant is written in exactly the shape of
`Instant.toString()`, so a lease round-trips byte-identical.

`LeaseRecord` is one lease, `LeaseOwner` names the scope that owns it, and `HolderInstance` is the runtime that serves
it: the process id and start instant a liveness check compares. `ScopeType` is the kind of scope a lease can cover
(`plan`, `epic`, `workspace`, `project`).

`LeaseStore` is built on an `AtomicStoreFile` whose key is a machine store lock (`IllegalArgumentException` otherwise),
and runs each write as one short transaction on that key: read, decide, write, release. A write that changes nothing
writes nothing.

* `claim(lease)`: granted when the key is free; otherwise nothing is written and the result names the holder.
* `release(key, expectedOwner)`: removes the lease only while its owner equals the expected one in every field;
  otherwise it writes nothing and returns the lease it found. `release(key)` removes the lease whoever owns it, and
  returns it, or nothing if the key was not held.
* `adopt(key, newHolder)`: sets the serving runtime and clears `orphaned_at`; the owning scope is unchanged.
* `orphan(key, orphanedAt)`: marks the lease; a lease that is orphaned already keeps its first mark, and nothing is
  written.
* `reviseAll(revision)`: reads, revises and writes every lease in one transaction; a lease for which the revision
  returns nothing is removed. A revision that returns a lease under another key is refused with
  `IllegalArgumentException`, and nothing is written. The revision runs under the store's lock, so it must be short and
  take no lock.
* `snapshot()`: the leases without a lock, before or after a write and never a part of one; nothing that a write
  depends on is decided on it.

A lease is a record, never an OS lock (link:../adr/core-store/013-leases-are-records-never-os-locks.adoc[ADR 013]):
holding a lease is not holding a lock, and a lease can be held across any lock acquisition. The model slots keep a slot
store of their own beside it (`ModelSlotStore`, <<_slots_metrics_budgets_ledger>>). The sweep over stale leases is
`runtime.lock`'s (link:../../../pm-runtime/doc/developer/runtime-lock.adoc[`runtime.lock` § The Stale Lease Sweep]).

Tests: `LeaseCodecTest`, `LeaseStoreTest`.

== The Enrolment Store

_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-EXEC-2[PM-EXEC-2], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-4[PM-SEC-4], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-MIG-1[PM-MIG-1], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-7[PM-IMPL-7]_

The enrolment store is split between `pm-core` and `pm-runtime`: this package holds the typed record, its codec, the
sidecar record, the store and the lifecycle gate, and holds no key; `pm-runtime` holds the integrity key, the HMAC, the
per-use checks, the check and the operator writes
(link:../../../pm-runtime/doc/developer/runtime-enrolment.adoc[`runtime.enrolment`]).

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Type |What it does

|`ProjectEnrolment`
|The enrolment of one project: every field of the closed schema typed, every name with a fixed set of values a closed
enum, every value validated in the compact constructors.

|`EnrolmentCodec`
|The strict reader of an enrolment file and the writer of its canonical bytes.

|`EnrolmentSidecar`
|The sidecar record `{format_version, size, sha256, hmac}` and its strict codec; it holds no key and computes no HMAC.

|`EnrolmentStore`
|The paths, the listing, the reads, the atomic pair write of the file and its sidecar with the recovery of a stopped
one under the project's lock, and the audit intent of the operator writes.

|`PairRecovery`
|What `EnrolmentStore.recoverPair` did with one stopped write of the pair: `Completed` or `Discarded`.

|`LifecycleGate`
|The compiled lifecycle gate and its decision per enrolment.
|===

=== Schema and Codec

`ProjectEnrolment` is the schema. Its nested records (`Wrapper`, `PinnedFile`, `HostTool`, `Build`, `Argv`,
`ProviderVerification`, `ProjectScript`, `Binding`, `ArgumentSchema`, `LanguageServer`) and enums (`WrapperName`,
`HostToolName`, `Interpreter`, `BuildSystem`, `Provider`, `WorkerKind`, `ContainerEngine`, `SchemaType`) carry the
patterns, closed name sets and bounds, so an instance that exists is one the schema admits. The compact constructors
enforce, among others: `project_root` is a normalized absolute path; an argument vector has at least one element and at
most one module-argument group, and a module-argument group at least one argument; a wrapper `pw` is run by `python3`
and every other wrapper by none; a project script is run by `python3` and has a budget of at least ten seconds; a
command budget is at least one second. What only the enrolment verb can decide (shell metacharacters in an argument
vector, a write path below the base, a refused environment name) is not checked here; `EnrolmentPolicy` of `pm-runtime`
checks part of it before an enrolment is signed.

The constants of `Provider` are named after the CLI each provider runs, `GIT`, `GH` and `GLAB`, like those of
`HostToolName`, because no member of `pm-core` may name a provider (PM-IMPL-1, guarded by `ProviderNeutralityArchTest`);
the wire names in the enrolment file are `git`, `github` and `gitlab` (`Provider.wireName()`).

The maps are copied into a stable order in the constructor (declaration order of an enum key, natural order of a string
key), and `EnrolmentCodec.write` relies on that: it writes every field, defaults included, in schema order, so the same
enrolment always gives the same bytes. Those are the bytes the HMAC is computed over; never compute it over bytes from
anywhere else.

`EnrolmentCodec.read(file, content)` and `EnrolmentSidecar.read(file, content)` read through `StrictJson` like every
codec of the package (<<_format_versions>>): the version first (`format_outdated` or `format_newer` naming
the file), every field at most once, no undeclared field at any level of nesting, every required field, one JSON type
per field. The optional fields `pilot`, `container_engine`, `released_at` and the `interpreter` of a host tool
read as their defaults when absent. A `StoreRefusal` from the sidecar names the sidecar file.

`EnrolmentSidecar` pairs a `StoreDigest` (size and SHA-256) with the HMAC, 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits; the
constructor refuses any other HMAC. `matches(content)` compares the size and SHA-256 only; the HMAC is the runtime's to
verify.

=== Paths, Listing and Reads

`EnrolmentStore(base)` names the paths below the base directory: `file(projectId)` is `enrolments/<project_id>.json`,
`sidecar(projectId)` is `enrolments/<project_id>.json.hmac`, and `lockKey(projectId)` is `LockKey.enrolment(base,
projectId)`, the machine-level lock `locks/enrolments/<project_id>.lock`, ordered by project id. `base()` returns the
base directory, absolute and normalized; the runtime compares the write paths of an enrolment with it and takes the
key-creation lock from it. A project id outside the identifier pattern is an `IllegalArgumentException` before it
becomes part of a path. The store does not create `enrolments/`.

Every read and write takes the transaction and refuses it as an internal fault (`InternalFaultException`) unless it
holds the project's own lock; the lock of another project does not count. Only `projectIds()` and
`auditOwingProjectIds()` run without a lock: they list, and a decision rests on `EnrolmentCheck.verify` of `pm-runtime`
in a transaction that holds the project's lock.

* `projectIds()` lists the enrolled projects: every file `<project_id>.json` with a valid id; sidecars, audit intents,
  staged and temporary files and anything else are not enrolments, and a missing directory lists none.
* `readFile` and `readSidecar` return the exact bytes, empty when the file does not exist; `decode(projectId, content)`
  reads them as the project's enrolment and refuses one whose `project_id` is not the name of its file as `unreadable`;
  `decodeSidecar` reads a sidecar record.
* `exists(transaction, projectId)` answers whether the file or its sidecar exists.
* `hasEnrolmentFile(transaction, projectId)` answers whether the enrolment file alone exists: exactly the fact
  `projectIds()` lists the project for; the sidecar, a staged name and the intent do not count. Where `exists` answers
  for the file or the sidecar, this one tells an enrolment from the orphan sidecar of an interrupted unenrol.
* `hasStagedWrite(transaction, projectId)` answers whether a stopped write of the pair left a staged name of the project,
  with the name matching of `recoverPair` and `remove`; a missing directory answers `false`, a directory that cannot be
  listed is an `UncheckedIOException`. It lets an unenrol cancel a first enrolment that was staged and stopped, with
  neither file nor sidecar in place.

=== The Pair Write and Its Recovery

* `write(transaction, enrolment, sidecar)` writes the canonical bytes and the sidecar as one pair. It refuses a sidecar
  whose size and SHA-256 are not those of the canonical bytes (`IllegalArgumentException`) before anything is written.
  It settles a stopped write of the pair first (`recoverPair`), then stages the file as
  `<project_id>.json.tmp.<transaction id>` and the sidecar as `<project_id>.json.hmac.tmp.<transaction id>` (one
  `TransactionId` for both, each written new, synced, mode `0600`), forces the directory, and only then renames the file
  and the sidecar into place, the file first, and forces the directory again. The staged sidecar is complete before the
  first rename, which is what makes a stop after it completable. A write that fails leaves what it staged for
  `recoverPair`.
* `recoverPair(transaction, projectId)` settles the stopped writes of the pair without a key and without signing
  anything: it only completes or discards a pair a write already produced. It groups the project's staged names by
  transaction id and processes them in ascending order. A staged file with a staged sidecar that reads strictly and
  matches it is moved into place (`PairRecovery.Completed`, logged `PM_CORE-1`); a staged file whose staged sidecar is
  missing, torn, unreadable or does not match it is deleted with the sidecar (`PairRecovery.Discarded`, logged
  `PM_CORE-101`), and the enrolment is as it was. A staged sidecar alone, after the file was already replaced, is moved
  into place when it reads strictly and matches the current file, and otherwise deleted, which leaves the file to the
  runtime's check. It returns one entry per stopped write, empty when nothing was staged or the directory does not
  exist; a staged file that cannot be listed, read, moved or deleted is an `UncheckedIOException`.
* `writeSidecar(transaction, projectId, sidecar)` replaces only the sidecar: the re-baseline. It settles nothing itself;
  its caller settles a stopped write of the pair first, under the same lock, as the runtime's re-baseline does with
  `recoverPair` before it reads and signs the file.
* `remove(transaction, projectId)` deletes the staged files of every stopped write of the pair first, so an unenrolled
  project is never rolled forward later, then the file and its sidecar, nothing else, forces the directory to disk, and
  reports whether the file or the sidecar existed. The file is deleted before the sidecar: its removal is the commit
  point of an unenrol, and a stop between the two deletions leaves a sidecar without its file, which `projectIds()` does
  not list, beside the record the unenrol owes.
* `removeOrphanSidecar(transaction, projectId)` deletes the sidecar of a project whose enrolment file is gone, forces
  the directory and reports whether a sidecar was deleted; while the file exists it touches nothing and returns
  `false`. It touches no staged name, no audit intent and nothing of another project; a sidecar that cannot be deleted
  is an `UncheckedIOException` naming the project. The runtime's background pass calls it after it handed the
  project's owed records over.

A staged name belongs to a project only when it is exactly `<project_id>.json.tmp.<id>` or
`<project_id>.json.hmac.tmp.<id>` for a transaction id: the project `ab` never claims or deletes the staged names of
`ab-c`, and the temporary `<store>.tmp.<uuid>` of `AtomicStoreFile`, which `writeSidecar` uses, is not a staged name of
a transaction. The transaction journal is not reused for the pair: it is bound to the scopes of the per-project machine
store, whose recovery deletes the staged files of its own directories, and in the flat `enrolments/` directory the
recovery of one project would delete the staged files of another project's write in flight.

The owed audit record of a write that `recoverPair` rolls forward stays the unconfirmed record (`failed`,
`not_confirmed`): the mutation completed, its completion was not recorded.

=== The Audit Intent

The audit intent of the operator writes is the file `enrolments/<project_id>.json.audit-intent`. It exists only while a
record is owed, is touched only under the project's lock, and is not an enrolment. Its document is the one of the intent
files of the JSONL stores, `{"format_version": 1, "owed_events": [<owed event>]}`, read and written by the
package-private `AuditIntentFile` that both share, each owed record an object of `OwedAuditEvents`
(<<_substrate_audit_records>>). An intent file that is unreadable or of another format version is refused as
a `StoreRefusal` naming it and is left in place.

* `auditIntent(projectId)` is the path of the intent file.
* `oweAudit(transaction, projectId, event)` writes the record durably to the intent before the mutation; a record whose
  `transaction_id` is already owed is not owed twice, and a detail value an intent cannot hold (anything but a string,
  an integer or a list of strings) is an `IllegalArgumentException` before anything is written.
* `confirmAudit(transaction, projectId, confirmed)` replaces the owed record of the same `transaction_id` by the
  confirmed one, in place and written durably, and keeps the others; when no record of that transaction is owed, the
  confirmed record is owed afresh.
* `owedAudit(transaction, projectId)` returns the records still owed, in order.
* `deliverOwedAudit(transaction, projectId, sink)` hands the owed records over in order and returns those handed over.
  It stops at the first record the sink refuses with an `UncheckedIOException`, which the substrate logs as
  `PM_CORE-100` and keeps owed, and never throws for it; any other failure of the sink propagates and leaves the intent.
  The intent file is removed, and its directory forced, once every record was handed over, and rewritten with the rest
  after a partial hand-over.
* `auditOwingProjectIds()` lists, without a lock, the projects whose intent file exists (valid ids only, a missing
  directory lists none): what the runtime's replay iterates.

=== The Lifecycle Gate

`LifecycleGate.RELEASE` is the gate of the release. Its position is the private constant `OPEN_IN_THIS_RELEASE`,
`false`; the constructor is package-private, so only the release constant and the tests of the open
position build a gate. `decide(enrolment)` returns `LIFECYCLE_CLOSED` (`lifecycle_closed`) for a released enrolment,
`ADMITTED` for an open gate or a pilot, and `LIFECYCLE_CLOSED` otherwise. It takes a verified enrolment: read it
through `EnrolmentCheck`, never with `EnrolmentStore.decode` alone, or an edited `pilot: true` would open the gate.

Besides the format refusals, `StoreRefusal` carries `ENROLMENT_TAMPERED` (`enrolment_tampered`), `ENROLMENT_INSECURE`
(`enrolment_insecure`) and `ENROLMENT_INVALID` (`enrolment_invalid`) with their factories. They are raised by the
runtime's check and operator writes, where the key and the per-use checks live.

=== Pitfalls of the Enrolment Store

* *Never rename the pair one file after the other without staging both.* A stop between two renames of files written
  one after the other leaves a pair the check refuses, and the operator must accept-edit. Stage the file and the sidecar
  completely, then rename. Guarded by `EnrolmentStoreTest` (a stop after each step is settled to the old or the new
  pair, never a half-new one).
* *The gate must not be openable from outside the release.* Keep the position a compiled constant, never read it from
  configuration, and feed `decide` only verified enrolments. Guarded by `LifecycleGateTest` (the release constant is
  closed; a non-pilot refused `lifecycle_closed`, a pilot admitted; the open position admits both).
* *Decide the integrity under the lock.* `projectIds()` is lock-free and only lists; everything a decision rests on goes
  through the runtime's check in a transaction that holds the project's lock.

== Path Confinement

_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-2[PM-SEC-2]_

`PathConfinement`, `WorkspaceRoots` and `ConfinementOutcome` confine a path to the workspace of a project: the enrolled
repository root and the live linked worktrees of it. `PathConfinement.confine(roots, rawPath)` answers a
`ConfinementOutcome`:

* `Confined(path)`: the canonical path, with every symbolic link resolved. A caller operates on that path, never on the
  text it passed in.
* `Rejected(reason)`: the path is refused, and `code()` is the closed outcome code `path_traversal_rejected` for every
  reason.

A refusal is a return value, not an exception: no checked exception type exists or is added. `WorkspaceRoots` holds the
enrolled root and the worktrees that count as live; `PathConfinement` resolves the path against them and decides whether
it lies inside the enrolled root or a live linked worktree. The runtime's adapter `WorkspaceConfinement` logs a refusal at
WARN with the code and the reason, never with the path
(link:../../../pm-runtime/doc/developer/runtime-workspace.adoc[`runtime.workspace`]).

The confinement is a library: a package that performs a file or repository operation for a project adopts the check
itself; a path that does not adopt it is not confined.

== Reading Helpers Shared with the Stores of Other Packages
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-2[PM-IMPL-2]_

A store of another package of `pm-core` is read with the same strictness as every store of this package. Its codec
uses `StrictJson` directly: the reading entry `read(file, document, current, reader)`, the field helpers, the
refusals `unreadable` and `undeclared`, and the instant reader and writer are public API of `core.store`. A `Malformed`
thrown by a helper is caught by `read` and becomes the `StoreRefusal` naming the file. `LockKey.projectStore(base,
projectId)` is public so such a store resolves `projects/<project_id>/<directory>/` with the one path grammar of the
locks.

== Engine Ports
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-4[PM-ARCH-4], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

The ports are interfaces in `core.spi`. No class of `pm-core` implements them: the runtime of `pm-runtime` implements
them later, and the tests of `pm-workflow` use recording doubles. `WorkflowEngine` of `pm-workflow` takes the four
together as `WorkflowEngine.Ports`.

[horizontal]
`JobStarter`:: Starts the job of a `job` state for a `StartRequest(scopeId, stateId, primitiveId)`. The result is
`Started(jobId)` with a `job_id` of the grammar `^[bcgp]-[0-9a-f]{16}$`, whose first character names the `JobKind`
(build, ci, git, project script), or `Refused(reason)` as a value, never an exception. Worker jobs are never started
here.
`QueueTaskCreator`:: Creates one queue task of a role for one entity of a `job llm` state; the result carries the
`queue_task_id` of the grammar `^qt-[0-9a-f]{16}$`. A role that cannot take the task is not refused here: the queue
closes such a task `lost`.
`QuestionRecordReader`:: Reads a question record (`^Q-[0-9]{3,6}$`) of a scope. The read is tri-state: `Open`,
`Resolved(outcome)`, or `Unreadable(reason)`; a record that cannot be read is never taken for an open one.
`WorkflowClock`:: The one source of every instant the engine writes (`updated_at`, `pushed_at`, `raised_at`, a job's
`started_at`), so persisted state never depends on the engine's memory and tests drive time deterministically.

`EnginePortsTest` guards the identifier grammars and the tri-state read.

== The Plan's History
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16]_

`PlanHistory` is the plan's append-only history `mcp/history.jsonl` (Store Schemas § The Plan's History):
`PlanHistoryLine` is one line (`format_version`, `seq`, `event`, `node`, `call_path`, `phase`, `outcome`,
`instance_version`, `at`), `PlanHistoryCodec` reads and writes it strictly, and the store appends the lines of one
transition as one append target of the plan's `ScopeTransaction`, so the digest sidecar covers the file and the state
change and its history are one journaled write. It never rewrites or removes a line; `nextSeq()` reads the sequence the
next line takes from the log itself, so a runtime that restarted continues the sequence.

The derived node states are not here: `de.planmarshall.workflow.plan.PlanProcessStates` reads the lines and the
instance version's composed files and derives every node's state (done, running, waiting for the operator, failed with
its outcome, planned, not taken, not in this plan with its prune reason).

== The Token Split and the Usage Aggregate
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14], link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3]_

`HarnessUsage` owns the split every view shows: `tokensIn()` sums input, cache read and cache creation, `tokensOut()`
sums output and reasoning. A `not_applicable` component counts `0`; an unmeasured one leaves the split unmeasured, so a
partial sum is never recorded. `totalTokens()` keeps its lower bound of `1`.

`UsageAggregate` is the one aggregate type every view calls, derived on read and never persisted. It carries the total,
the split, `rows`, `rowsUnmeasured`, `unmeasuredReasons`, `populationCount`, `excluded` and `totalIsFloor`. `of(rows)`
refuses a set that mixes populations; `byPopulation`, `byPhase`, `byRole`, `byModelAndHarness`, `byCauseClass` and
`bySpendClass` group the rows; `plus` sums two figures of one population and keeps a floor a floor.

* An empty population has no total; a population with a measured row has one, exact or a floor; a population whose
  every row is unmeasured has no total, never a fabricated `0`.
* `totalIsFloor` is true exactly while a row of the population is unmeasured; the constructor refuses any other pair.
* The split is empty when any row of the population left a component of it unmeasured.

`UsageAggregateTest` covers the sum, the not-applicable component, the unmeasured floor with its reasons, the empty
population, the refused task, the population refusal, the groupings and `plus`, and the paired equality of a harness that
reports reasoning apart and one whose output already contains it; `UsagePointersTest` covers the parsed figures and the
three-valued measurement of a turn.

read and the aggregate carries its coverage.

== The Intent Record

`intent.json` beside `request.md` is the plan's intent record (Store Schemas § Intent Record; Phase Workflows § The
Intent Record). `IntentRecord` is the model-supplied document and the named parameter schema `intent-record`: it
refuses a member out of its bounds, a criterion that is not in EARS form, a `fix` criterion without its current
behaviour and an `unchanged` criterion, a `current_behaviour` without a `fix` criterion or outside
`WHEN <condition> THEN THE <system> <behaviour>`, an unverified assumption without exactly one riskiest one, a repeated
identifier, and a source claim that lands in no item of the record.

`IntentStore` is the document around it: the accepted revision as `current`, every earlier one in `history[]`. Its
`accept(Revision)` is the data change of the accepting transaction — it refuses a revision that does not follow the
current one and an identifier an earlier revision removed, and it appends the current revision to the history without
changing it. `IntentCodec` reads and writes the document strictly, and `IntentStoreFile` runs the acceptance in one
transaction on the plan lock.

`IntentAcceptance` checks what needs the plan: an `operator` or `quorum` decision cites a part of an answered question
record of this plan through the matching channel, a `fact` decision names an evidence path that exists (or a `pm://`
URI), a capability link's `added`, `modified` or `removed` requirement agrees with the tree's fingerprints, and every
claim an epic seeds appears among the `source_claims`. `EpicIntent` derives those claims from the plan row's own
`done_when` and the epic's non-goals.

`ClarifiedRequestRenderer` renders `## Clarified Request` from the current record, each item with its identifier, and
lists the model's own calls under their own heading. `EarsForm` is the grammar: the structural words `WHERE`, `WHILE`,
`WHEN`, `IF`/`THEN`, `THE`, `SHALL` and `CONTINUE TO` are uppercase English tokens, the clause text between them is
written in the model language, and an uppercase structural word inside clause text is the documented exception.

== The Outline Record

`outline.json` is the plan's solution outline (Store Schemas § Outline Record). `OutlineRecord` is the model-supplied
document (`outline-record`): its deliverables carry their profiles, affected files with their intents, the change per
file, the verification commands, the `satisfies` entries, the added dependencies, interfaces and key links. It refuses
a dependency on a deliverable the outline does not hold, a dependency on itself, a dependency named twice, a repeated
deliverable number, a `write-conditional` file without its gate and a file of another intent that carries one.

`OutlineStore` holds the revision, its history and the server-owned state of every deliverable (`active`, `deferred` or
`removed`); a deliverable a re-planning no longer holds keeps its state. `OutlineCodec` is the strict reader and writer.
`OutlineRenderer` renders `outline.md` in the section form of the solution outline, deliverable by deliverable; the
rendered document is never read back.

`OutlineValidator` (in `core.spi.validation`) checks the record against the intent record: every `satisfies` entry is
an item of the record, every criterion is covered, no deliverable's `satisfies` is empty, and the guard of an
`unchanged` criterion verified by `test` does not depend, directly or through others, on the deliverable that changes
it.

== Capability Specs

`.marshall/capabilities/<capability>.json` is a git-tracked team file (Store Schemas § Capability Spec).
`CapabilitySpec` holds the capability, its purpose and its requirements with their scenarios; it refuses a repeated
requirement name, a text that is not in EARS form and a requirement without a scenario. `Requirement#fingerprint()` is
the SHA-256 of the RFC 8785 canonical JSON of the requirement without `plan_id` and `verified_by`, so a renamed test
changes no fingerprint.

`CapabilityIndex.read(projectRoot)` reads the tree's specs, refuses a file whose name is not its capability, and serves
both the planning roles' `facts.capability_index` (`facts()`) and the acceptance's capability checks
(`fingerprint(capability, requirement)`).
