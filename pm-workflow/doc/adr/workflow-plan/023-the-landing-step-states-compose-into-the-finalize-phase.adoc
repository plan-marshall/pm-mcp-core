= ADR 023: The Landing Step States Compose into the Finalize Phase
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-11-16

== Context

Workflow DSL § Plan Behaviour Axes, item 1 draws the landing axis differently from the other two. The method variant is
called at `plan.order-tasks` and the asking variant at the plan unit's `phases.ask`, so `InstancePruner` binds them like
any call site. A landing variant instead *holds the step states of the landing orders*: the instance composes the bound
variant's states into the composed file of `phase-6-finalize`, the states keep their `finalize.` identifiers, and the
phase's dispatch hub fires them like the phase's own steps. The variants not bound are pruned so the process in force
shows the choice.

The composition half of this was left open by PLAN-V2-43; only the call-site axes were bound. The unit files themselves
(the finalize phase and the landing variants) are resources of `plan-marshall-mcp`, so this repository can carry the
composition algorithm, not the units.

== Decision

* `LandingStepComposition.merge(phase, boundLanding, variants)` is a pure operation in `workflow.plan`: it composes the
  bound variant's step states into the phase's tree and lists the step states of the other variants as pruned states with
  the reason `plan_default` and the binding level as source fact.
* Only states whose id starts with `finalize.` take part: a landing variant's own entry and terminal states are scaffolding
  of the unit, not steps of the phase, and composing them would leave dead states in the phase.
* The merge refuses a state that two owners declare, a bound variant whose tree is not supplied, a tree that is no landing
  variant, and a bound variant that declares no step state — a step state has exactly one owner, and a silent override
  would hide a defect of the units.
* The phase's step list (`CompositionRequest.finalizeSteps`) is unchanged: the composition adds states at the phase's
  orders, and the instance's roster keeps naming them. A landing variant that needs its own roster entry is the
  assembly's; this class adds no step id.
* The composer keeps its call-site composition; the merge is to be called where the finalize phase is composed, which is
  the assembly's seam, and no caller of it exists in this repository yet. `holdsLandingSteps(unit)` names the unit the
  states belong to.

== Alternatives Considered

1. *A landing call site like the other axes.* _Not taken_: the specification says the variant holds the phase's step states;
   turning it into a sub-workflow call would move the landing work behind one state and give up the phase's roster.
2. *Deriving each variant's step ids from its states' `completes` clauses.* _Not taken_: the states declare no `completes`
   clause in this repository's fixtures, and the roster is the request's value; a derivation would invent a contract the
   units do not carry.
3. *Wiring the merge into `PlanInstanceComposer` unconditionally.* _Not taken_: the composer composes sub-units by call
   site; a landing merge needs the finalize phase and the variants as named inputs, and the assembly is the only caller
   that has them. The library delivers the algorithm and its tests; the wiring is named owed in the plan's table.

== Consequences

* A caller that composes `phase-6-finalize` gets the bound variant's step states in the phase's tree and the other
  variants' step states as a prune list, which it adds to the version's header. This repository's composer composes by
  call site and does not compose that phase, so the wiring is the assembly's — stated as owed in the plan's table.
* `LandingStepCompositionTest` covers the merge, the pruning, the identity of the phase and every refusal, including a
  state two variants declare.
* A variant unit that declares a state the phase declares is a blocker at composition time, not a silent override.
