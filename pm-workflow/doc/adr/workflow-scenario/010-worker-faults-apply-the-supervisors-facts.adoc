= ADR 010: The Worker Faults Apply the Supervisor's Reported Facts
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-11-16

== Context

The specification's deterministic scenario replay harness names the worker faults `kill_worker <role>`,
`stall_worker <role> <seconds>`, `end_turn <role>` and `kill_relay <role>`, and the runtime restart `restart_runtime`
(Workflow DSL § Deterministic Scenario Replay Harness, the simulated actor actions of regression scenario 12). The
harness refused them by name, with the work package that now ships them.

The supervisor that reports these facts lives in `pm-runtime` (`LossDetection`, `LossReason`, `WorkerEnding`), and
`pm-workflow` depends on `pm-core` alone: no scenario of this harness can drive it, and a harness that started a
process would stop being deterministic. What crosses the boundary is the fact the supervisor reports, and the task
protocol already carries it: `TaskProtocol.report(TaskCall, AttemptEnd, int, Instant)` ends an attempt with one of the
closed set of `AttemptEnd` values, and `TaskProtocol.fence(String, Instant)` fences a generation.

Model Work § Supervision & Recycling, Detection names the three facts a lost worker produces — the exit of the
worker's process, a missing acknowledgement, and silence past the silence limit — and `LossReason` maps them to the
attempt's end: `EXIT` → `exited_without_submit`, `ACK_MISSED` and `SILENCE` → `worker_lost`.

== Decision

Each fault word is one fact of the supervisor, applied by the harness at the instant of the manual clock.

[cols="1,2,1,2"]
|===
|Directive |Fact |Attempt end |The instant it is applied at

|`kill_worker <role>` (after an offer, before its acknowledgement)
|The offer was not acknowledged within the harness's acknowledgement deadline (`ack_missed`)
|`worker_lost`
|The offer's instant plus `WorkerParameters.ackDeadlineSeconds`

|`kill_worker <role>` (after the acknowledgement, before the submit)
|The acknowledged worker made no call and holds no bounded wait past the silence limit (`silence`)
|`worker_lost`
|The worker's last call plus `WorkerParameters.silenceLimitSeconds()`

|`end_turn <role>`
|The worker's process exited after its first call without a submit (`exit`)
|`exited_without_submit`
|The step's own instant: the supervisor observes an exit at once, never at a deadline

|`kill_relay <role>`
|The relay died under the wait its acknowledged worker held, so the silence counts again from the relay's death
|`worker_lost`
|The relay's death plus `WorkerParameters.silenceLimitSeconds()`

|`stall_worker <role> <seconds>`
|The worker makes no call for the given time; while the silence since its last call stays below the limit that governs
its open attempt nothing happens
|`worker_lost`, once that silence reaches the limit
|The limit's own instant, never the end of the given time
|===

The limit a stall is compared with is the limit of the attempt the worker holds: `ackDeadlineSeconds` while its offer is
unacknowledged, `silenceLimitSeconds()` (the bounded wait plus the silence grace) once it is acknowledged. Both are read
from `de.planmarshall.core.profile.WorkerParameters`; the harness re-implements neither the detector nor its timers. The
silence is measured from the worker's last call, not from the step, so two stalls whose times add up past the limit
lose the worker exactly as one does.

* **The order is the protocol's.** A fault reports the fact first and fences the generation after it. `report` ends
  the attempt, spends one re-offer where the end counts (`worker_lost` and `exited_without_submit` do, `restart` does
  not) and releases the lease; `fence` persists the fence and, since the lease is released already, only writes it.
  A fence applied first would end the attempt `restart` and spend none, which is the pause's fact, not a loss's. The
  supervisor of this repository fences through `WorkerEnding.exited` and names each loss's attempt end in
  `LossReason.attemptEnd()`, which has no caller here: the report that writes it with the loss reaches the protocol
  outside `pm-workflow`, and is reported as a lead rather than copied.
* **The replacement is the fencing's.** The replaced generation's late call is refused `generation_fenced`, which is
  the refusal the fence exists for, and the role's next `worker_wait` starts the successor generation, which takes the
  task from the released queue. A task whose re-offers are spent closes `lost` with `attempts_spent`, and the task
  state routes that to the unit's own outcome.
* **`restart_runtime` rebuilds what memory held.** The engine, the task protocol, the queue's object, its fence store,
  the consultation service and the status store are rebuilt over the same directory; the status is re-read from its
  bytes, and each protocol worker is rebuilt over its own generation with the task its lease still names. The harness's
  manual clock and its id counters are the script's, not the runtime's, and stay. A start-failure guard is not part of
  the rebuild: the harness has no process to fail to start (`pm-runtime` proves that guard, `SupervisorScenarioTest`).
* **The scenario's instants are the manual clock's.** A fault that ends an attempt advances the clock to at least the
  instant it applies the fact at, so a script's stored bytes and a task's `endedAt` both name the deadline rather than
  the moment the test happened to run.

== Alternatives Considered

1. *Drive the real supervisor from the scenario.* _Rejected_: `pm-workflow` does not depend on `pm-runtime`, and the
   supervisor starts and kills processes — the replay would stop being deterministic and would need a live model
   harness for a test-scope proof.
2. *Re-implement loss detection in the harness.* _Rejected_: the harness asserts on the protocol's records, never on a
   supervisor state; a second detector would prove the second detector.
3. *Model `kill_worker` as the exit fact, `exited_without_submit`.* _Rejected_: the specification's regression scenario
   12 demands that a lost worker be detected within its deadline and never before, and a killed worker's relay reports
   no exit the harness could date before a deadline. The exit fact is what `end_turn` applies, at the step's instant.
4. *Make `kill_relay` another immediate loss beside `kill_worker`.* _Rejected_: it would lose the one fact the row
   exists for — a held wait suppresses the silence (Model Work § Detection), so the loss falls a silence limit after
   the relay dies and not a silence limit after the worker's last call. The scenario holds the wait for a third of the
   limit first and asserts that the loss comes later than the unsuppressed deadline would have been.
5. *A `pause` directive for the protocol half of the pause at once.* _Rejected_: no such directive exists in the
   specification. The harness proves the protocol half (`pausePlan` frees the lease without spending a re-offer) in a
   test, and the supervisor half stays proven by `SupervisorScenarioTest`.

== Consequences

* The six scenarios of regression scenario 12 and the runtime-crash scenarios replay without a live model, and every
  property they name — one submit, detection at the deadline, the lease released, `generation_fenced` for a late call,
  no replacement of a live worker, an exhausted budget closing the task — is a record of the real queue.
* `assert_worker_state` reaches `offered`, `acknowledged`, `closed` and `fenced` (a released task whose last
  attempt's generation is fenced) through a directive. `queued` — a release the fence did not cause — is reached by the
  pause's protocol half only, since no directive releases a task without fencing it, and is asserted through the
  runner's own derivation.
* A fault names a role the script declared with `worker_wait` before it, and the harness refuses the script when it is
  read otherwise, as it does for every other protocol directive.
* The harness cannot produce a loss the supervisor would detect without a process: its facts are the protocol's.
