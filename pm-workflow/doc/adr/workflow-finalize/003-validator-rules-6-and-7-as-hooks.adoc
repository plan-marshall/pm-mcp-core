= ADR 003: Validator Rules 6 and 7 as Named Hooks Where a Descriptor Cannot Decide
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-13

== Context

Rule 6 of the validator requires a head-dependent step to declare its input scope, and a step whose `done` outcome is reachable through both a no-op and a work-performing branch to record `work_performed`.
Rule 7 forbids a step after the merge gate to declare an outcome that refuses on its inputs.
Rule 8 derives the finding-filing steps from the roster and refuses a finding whose source the filing step does not declare.
A descriptor holds neither the outcomes of a step nor the branches that reach them, and the refusal of rule 8 happens when a finding is written.
The operator decided that these parts are named hooks.

== Decision

The validator checks the input-scope part of rule 6 and derives the finding sources of rule 8 (`findingSources`).
The `work_performed` part of rule 6, rule 7 and the write-time refusal of rule 8 are named hooks: each `PipelineRule` constant carries the reason as a code constant, and the test of the validator enumerates the hooks and fails for a rule with neither a refusing case nor a hook.

== Alternatives Considered

1. **Adding the outcomes of a step to the descriptor**:
*Rejected*: the descriptor would restate the step's behaviour, which the step's own unit already defines, and the two could disagree.

2. **Leaving the undecidable parts unmentioned**:
*Rejected*: the exemption belongs at the check, where a test can enumerate it.

== Consequences

* The hooks are open for the packages that know the outcomes of a step and that write findings; the work package names them.
