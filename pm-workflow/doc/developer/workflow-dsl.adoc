== Purpose
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5], link:{docs}/requirements/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16]_

Package `de.planmarshall.workflow.dsl` is the line-oriented Workflow DSL: it reads the text of a unit into the
immutable tree of `workflow.ast` (link:workflow-ast.adoc[Workflow AST]) and prints a tree back to text. Every `.pmwf`
file opens with a frontmatter, which a reader of its own reads, and continues with the body, which the parser reads.
A refusal is a value with a rule, the resource, the line and the column; nothing in the package throws for a bad unit.

The grammar of the body is link:{spec}/workflow-dsl/01-grammar.adoc[Workflow DSL § Grammar]. This guide states the
format of the frontmatter, which the reader implements, and what the parser and the printer do with the frontmatter, the
entry bindings and the entry of a call. The decisions are recorded in `pm-workflow/doc/adr/workflow-dsl/`.

== Main Types
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

[horizontal]
`UnitFrontmatterReader`:: Reads the frontmatter of a unit, and nothing else: `read(resourceName, source)` returns the
sealed `UnitFrontmatterReader.Result`, either `Read` with the `UnitFrontmatter` and the line the body starts at, or
`Refused` with a `Diagnostic` of the rule `FRONTMATTER`. It stops at the closing `---`, so the body need not parse.
`WorkflowParser`:: `parse(resourceName, source, origin)` reads a whole unit and returns the sealed `ParseResult`:
`Parsed` with the `WorkflowDefinition`, or `Refused` with the first `Diagnostic`.
`WorkflowPrinter`:: Prints a `WorkflowDefinition` as canonical source, so that parsing the printed text yields the same
tree, source locations aside (the round trip).
`Diagnostic`:: A refusal: the `RefusalRule`, the resource, the line, the column and what is wrong.
`RefusalRule`:: The closed set of the reasons a unit is refused: `FRONTMATTER`, `STRUCTURE`, `RESERVED_KEYWORD`,
`MISSING_SCOPE`, `DUPLICATE_ID` and `INSTANCE_HEADER`.

[source]
----
.pmwf text ──► UnitFrontmatterReader ── Refused(FRONTMATTER) ─────────────────► ParseResult.Refused
                     │ Read(frontmatter, bodyLine)
                     ▼
               WorkflowParser (body from bodyLine) ── Refused(STRUCTURE, …) ──► ParseResult.Refused
                     │
                     ▼
               ParseResult.Parsed(WorkflowDefinition) ──► WorkflowPrinter ──► canonical text
----

== The Read Order
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

`WorkflowParser.parse` reads in two steps:

. *Frontmatter first.* The parser hands the whole text to `UnitFrontmatterReader.read`. A missing or refused frontmatter
  is returned as the parser's refusal, with the rule `FRONTMATTER`; the body is not read.
. *Then the body.* The lexer cuts the whole text into lines, and the parser keeps the lines from the reader's `bodyLine`,
  the line after the closing `---`. Line numbers are those of the file, so every refusal of the body names the line
  as an editor shows it. A body without a token is refused at `bodyLine`, column 1.

The lines of the frontmatter, which the lexer cuts with the rest of the text, are dropped before the parser reads a
line, so the lexer's comment rule (`#` to the end of the line) never applies to them: inside the frontmatter a comment
is refused.

[#unit-frontmatter]
== The Unit Frontmatter
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

The frontmatter is the first part of every `.pmwf` file: what the unit is for a person, its type, its interface (the
entries through which control arrives and the exits through which it leaves), and where it comes from. It is read
without the body, so a list of units, a catalogue of building blocks or a process view shows a unit's name,
description, type and interface from it alone, and a `call` is checked against the interface of its callee.

[source,yaml]
----
---
name: Commit and Push
description: Commits the changes of the plan's worktree and pushes its branch as the push mode says.
type: subworkflow
entry: commit
entry_description: The worktree holds the changes to commit, or is clean.
exit: done
exit_description: Commit and push are settled; the result record says what was committed, pushed, or landed.
modification_date: 2026-10-14
version: 1.0.0
author: plan-marshall
---
workflow git.commit_and_push version 1 format 1
scope subworkflow
…
entry commit -> git.inspect-worktree
----

=== Format

The frontmatter is a closed, line-oriented subset of YAML between two lines `---`, the first of which is the first line
of the file. `UnitFrontmatterReader` reads it with a parser of its own and never with a general YAML loader, so no tag,
anchor, alias or merge key is ever resolved
(link:../adr/workflow-dsl/005-frontmatter-read-by-its-own-line-parser.adoc[ADR 005]).

* *One field per line*: every line is `key: value` at column 1. There is no blank line, comment, indentation, nesting,
  flow collection, block scalar or document marker other than the two delimiters, and no line contains a control
  character, a format character (category Cf, a bidi override or a zero-width space among them), a line separator (Zl)
  or a paragraph separator (Zp). A frontmatter with one is refused with its category, its code point and its line, so a
  display value cannot reorder or hide what the operator sees
  (link:../adr/workflow-dsl/007-refused-characters-and-frontmatter-bounds.adoc[ADR 007]).
* *Bounds*: a unit file holds at most 1048576 bytes (`ProjectUnitLoader.MAX_UNIT_FILE_BYTES`), a frontmatter holds at
  most 200 lines including its two `---` delimiters (`UnitFrontmatterReader.MAX_FRONTMATTER_LINES`), and a refusal
  echoes at most 80 characters of the source, marked with `...` where it cuts
  (`UnitFrontmatterReader.MAX_ECHO_LENGTH`). The reader reads the frontmatter from the head of the source and never
  the body.
* *Line endings*: a line ends with a line feed (LF) or with a carriage return and a line feed (CRLF). The line ending is
  not part of the line, so a CRLF unit reads to the same frontmatter, with the same line and column numbers, as its LF
  twin. A carriage return anywhere else is a control character and refused.
* *Values*: a value is plain or double-quoted. A plain value has no leading or trailing blank, does not start with a
  YAML indicator character (`-?:,[]{}#&*!|>'"%@` and the backquote), and contains neither `": "` nor `" #"`. A
  double-quoted value is one line between `"` and `"` with the escapes `\"` and `\\` only. A value a plain scalar
  cannot carry is written double-quoted.
* *Closed keys*: the keys are `name`, `description`, `type`, `entry`, `entry_description`, `exit`, `exit_description`,
  `modification_date`, `version`, `author` and `signature`; any other key is refused. `name`, `description`, `type`,
  `modification_date`, `version` and `author` occur exactly once, `signature` at most once.
* *Repeated `entry` and `exit`*: a unit declares zero or more entries and zero or more exits, one name per line, the key
  repeated per name, kept in order and never collapsed. This is the one point where the frontmatter is not a YAML
  mapping.
* *Descriptions*: an `entry_description` stands directly below its `entry` line and an `exit_description` directly below
  its `exit` line, at most one each. A description below another key, below another description or as the first line is
  refused.
* *Order*: apart from the descriptions, the order of the lines is free. The printer writes the order of the field table
  below.

=== Fields

[cols="2,1,6"]
|===
|Key |Occurs |Value and check

|`name`
|1
|The display name of the unit, 3 to 100 characters.

|`description`
|1
|What the unit does and when it is used, 1 to 1000 characters.

|`type`
|1
|One of the wire names of `UnitType`: `workflow`, `recipe`, `subworkflow`, `system`, `task`, `instance`
(link:workflow-ast.adoc#unit-types[Workflow AST § Unit Types]).

|`entry`
|0..n
|The name of an entry: a DSL identifier `[a-z][a-z0-9_-]{0,31}` of the
link:{spec}/identifiers/01-general-rules-and-registry.adoc#id-dsl-identifier[Identifier Registry], unique among the
unit's entries. The header binds it with `entry <name> -> <state_id>`.

|`entry_description`
|0..1 per entry
|What a caller hands over or may assume when it enters through the entry on the line before, 1 to 500 characters.

|`exit`
|0..n
|The name of an exit, an outcome id of the same grammar, unique among the unit's exits, never `stepped_out` (the
implicit control exit of every sub-workflow).

|`exit_description`
|0..1 per exit
|What the caller can rely on when the unit leaves through the exit on the line before, 1 to 500 characters.

|`modification_date`
|1
|The date of the last change of the file, `YYYY-MM-DD`, a valid calendar date not after the day of the validation,
taken in UTC.

|`version`
|1
|The plan-marshall release the unit was created with or delivered by, in the
link:{spec}/identifiers/02-registry-stores-to-web.adoc#id-release-version[release version] grammar
(`[0-9]+.[0-9]+.[0-9]+` with an optional `-` suffix of `[A-Za-z0-9.-]`, at most 64 characters). It is not the unit's
revision, which the `version` of the `workflow` header counts.

|`author`
|1
|Who made the unit, 1 to 100 characters: `plan-marshall` for the units compiled into the binary and only for them. The
reader checks the length; the reserved name is checked per level by the loader, on the author's NFKC-normalised,
format-character-free, stripped and case-folded form
(link:workflow-unit.adoc#_the_author_of_a_unit[Workflow Units § The Author of a Unit]).

|`signature`
|0..1
|Reserved for a signature over the file's content. It is carried with the unit and not verified; a missing one is no
finding.
|===

=== Refusals

The reader stops at the first refusal and reports it with the rule `FRONTMATTER`, the line and the column:

[cols="3,2"]
|===
|Refused |Where

|No frontmatter: the first line is not `---`
|line 1, column 1

|The frontmatter is not closed by a line `---`
|the last line, column 1

|A blank line, an indented line, a merge key (`<<`), a line that is not `key: value`, an unknown key, a repeated single
key, a description that does not stand directly below its element, a second description of one element
|the line, column 1

|A tag (`!`), an anchor (`&`), an alias (`*`), a flow collection (`[`, `{`), a block scalar (`\|`, `>`), a comment (`#`),
or another YAML indicator at the start of a line or of a value
|the line, column 1 or the column of the value

|A comment (`" #"`) or a mapping (`": "`) inside a plain value, a trailing blank, an escape other than `\"` and `\\`,
content after the closing quote, an unclosed double-quoted value
|the line, the column of the offending character

|A control character, a format character (Cf), a line separator (Zl) or a paragraph separator (Zp)
|the line, its column

|A frontmatter of more than 200 lines, the two delimiters included
|the first line past the cap, column 1

|A field outside its bounds: a length, an unknown type, an entry or exit that is no identifier or is declared twice, the
exit `stepped_out`, an invalid or future date, a version outside the release version grammar
|the line, the column of the value

|A missing single key
|the closing line, column 1
|===

== The Body: Start State or Entry Bindings
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5], link:{docs}/requirements/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16]_

After its declarations, the header of the body carries either one `start <state_id>` line or one or more
`entry <name> -> <state_id>` lines (the EBNF `start_decl | entry_decl , { entry_decl }`):

* a body with a `start` line and an `entry` line, in either order, is refused as `STRUCTURE` at the second of the two;
* a body with neither is refused as `STRUCTURE` where the one or the other is expected;
* an entry name bound twice is refused as `DUPLICATE_ID` at the second binding.

The parser keeps what the body says and compares nothing with the frontmatter. A unit whose frontmatter declares entries
but whose body has a `start` line, a declared entry without a binding, a binding of a name the frontmatter does not
declare, and a binding to a state the unit does not have all parse, and the verifier refuses them
(link:workflow-verify.adoc#interface-rules[Workflow Verification § Interface Rules], rule 1). One owner per check keeps
every verifier fixture parseable.

A `call` clause may name the callee's entry: `call <unit> entry <name> (…)`. The entry is kept as `Optional<String>`
on `StateBehavior.SubWorkflowCall`; whether the callee declares it, and whether the call may omit it, is the verifier's
(rule 5).

The keyword `entry` is positional, not reserved: it is read only at the start of a header line and after the callee of a
`call`, so a state id such as `setup.entry` stays valid. The keyword `start` is reserved.

[#call-values]
== Call Values and the Empty List
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5], link:{docs}/requirements/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-25[PM-WF-25]_

A call argument and a `declare` line carry a `CallValue` (the EBNF `call_value`,
link:{spec}/workflow-dsl/01-grammar.adoc#_formal_ebnf_grammar_for_the_workflow_dsl[Workflow DSL § Grammar]): an
enumeration value, a non-negative integer, or a list of distinct enumeration values between `[` and `]`.
The list may be empty: `[]` is the list without an element, read as a `CallValue.EnumList` with no value, for example
`declare wait_after = []` of an asking variant that waits after no phase.
The empty list is the one change: every other list refusal of the parser stays (`STRUCTURE` for an unterminated list,
values without a comma between them, a trailing comma or a comma alone; a value listed twice), and what a list may hold where it is bound or declared
stays the verifier's and the declaration bounds' (link:../adr/workflow-dsl/006-empty-list-admitted-as-a-call-value.adoc[ADR 006]).
The printer writes the empty list back as `[]`, so it survives the round trip.

== The Printer and the Round Trip
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

`WorkflowPrinter` writes the frontmatter first, between its two `---` lines, in the order of the field table: `name`,
`description`, `type`, each `entry` followed by its `entry_description`, each `exit` followed by its `exit_description`,
`modification_date`, `version`, `author` and `signature`. A value is written plain when a plain scalar can carry it and
double-quoted otherwise (the rule is `UnitFrontmatterReader.isPlain`, the reader's own, so printer and reader cannot
drift apart), with `\` written as `\\` and `"` as `\"`. Then come the unit header and the declarations, the
`start` line or the entry bindings in source order, the migrations and the states; a `call` names the entry it names in
the tree.

Parsing the printed text yields the same tree, source locations aside: the frontmatter, the order of the entries and
exits, the entry bindings and the entry of every call survive the round trip. A frontmatter whose lines were in another
order is printed in the order of the table, and the descriptions stay with their elements.

== Adding a Refusal
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

. Decide who owns the check. The parser refuses only what the text alone decides: the grammar and the uniqueness of an
  identifier within the file. Everything that compares two parts of a unit, or a unit with another, is a rule of the
  verifier.
. Refuse it where it is found, with the line and the column of the offending token: in the frontmatter reader for the
  frontmatter, in the parser for the body. Reuse a `RefusalRule` when one names the reason; add a member, with its
  Javadoc, only for a new kind of reason.
. Add a malformed fixture below `src/test/resources/dsl/malformed/`. It opens with a valid frontmatter, unless the
  frontmatter itself is what is refused, and states its expectation in a comment
  `# expect: <RULE> line <n> column <n>` directly below the closing `---` (as the first line of a fixture without
  frontmatter). `WorkflowParserRefusalTest` refuses every fixture of the directory with exactly that rule, line and
  column.
. A refusal of the frontmatter is also a case of `UnitFrontmatterReaderTest`.

== Developer Pitfalls
_Requirements: link:{docs}/requirements/14-implementation.adoc#PM-IMPL-5[PM-IMPL-5]_

* *Never read the frontmatter with a general YAML loader.* A YAML loader resolves tags, anchors, aliases and merge keys,
  collapses the repeated `entry` and `exit` keys into one, and accepts what the closed format refuses. Read it through
  `UnitFrontmatterReader` only, also where only the name or the type of a unit is wanted.
* *Count lines from the file.* The body's line numbers are those of the file, the frontmatter's lines included. A
  fixture's expected line moves with every line its frontmatter gains.
* *Do not compare in the parser.* A check of the bindings against the declared entries in the parser would refuse the
  verifier's fixtures before the verifier sees them.

== Decisions

* link:../adr/workflow-dsl/001-parse-result-is-a-sealed-value.adoc[ADR 001]: the parse result is a sealed value.
* link:../adr/workflow-dsl/002-line-oriented-parser-and-round-trip-printer.adoc[ADR 002]: a line-oriented lexer and
  parser, and a production printer that round-trips.
* link:../adr/workflow-dsl/003-unit-without-scope-refused.adoc[ADR 003]: a unit without scope is refused.
* link:../adr/workflow-dsl/004-instance-header-json-kept-as-a-string.adoc[ADR 004]: the instance header JSON is kept as
  a string.
* link:../adr/workflow-dsl/005-frontmatter-read-by-its-own-line-parser.adoc[ADR 005]: the frontmatter is read by a line
  parser of its own, its refusals are values of the rule `FRONTMATTER`, and the frontmatter of a merged plan unit and
  of a composed instance is derived from a part.
* link:../adr/workflow-dsl/006-empty-list-admitted-as-a-call-value.adoc[ADR 006]: the grammar admits the empty list `[]`
  as a call value, and nothing else of the grammar changes.
