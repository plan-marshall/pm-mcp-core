== Purpose
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

Package `de.planmarshall.workflow.scenario` is the deterministic scenario replay harness `pm-scenario`. It lives in the
test scope of `pm-workflow` (`src/test/java`), not in a module of its own, and runs in process under JUnit: a scenario
script drives the real `WorkflowEngine` over a fixture unit written for the test, with the status store of the scope
and the queue of the replay in a temporary directory, without a live model and without starting any process. A
scripted fixture worker stands in for the model that would take the tasks of a task state: either the direct worker
that settles the queued task, or the protocol worker whose generation waits at the real
`TaskProtocol` and calls the links of its leased task entity.

The harness implements the part of
link:{dsl}/07-loop-back-diagrams-verification.adoc#_deterministic_scenario_replay_harness_pm_scenario_pm_test_4[Workflow DSL § Deterministic Scenario Replay Harness]
that the engine supports today; its place follows
link:{docs}/specification/module-structure.adoc#test-placement[Module Structure § Test Placement]. The engine it drives
is described in link:workflow-engine.adoc[Workflow Engine].

== Main Types
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

All types are package-private test code.

[horizontal]
`ScenarioScript`:: The parsed script: its name, the worker declarations, and the steps in order. `read(text)` reads it
from the text of a script and refuses every line the harness does not run; `REFUSED` maps each refused directive of
the specification to the work package that brings it.
`ScenarioDirective`:: The sealed step of a script, each with its line: `Start`, `AssertState`, `AssertOutcome`,
`CompleteJob`, `AnswerQuestion`, `AssertTaskOutcome`, `OperatorDo`, `WorkerWait`, `WorkerDo`, `KillWorker`, `EndTurn`,
`KillRelay`, `StallWorker`, `RestartRuntime`, `AssertWorkerState` and `AssertRefused`. `Worker` is the declaration of
the direct worker of one role; it is no step. Job ids, question ids, fates, task outcomes, the worker states and the
refusals are checked against the shapes and closed sets the engine and the task protocol state.
`ScenarioReplayRunner`:: Replays one script once against the real engine and keeps the bytes of the status store and
of the queue after every step (`snapshots()`); a deviation is a `ScenarioFailure` (an `AssertionError`) that names the
step's line.
`ScriptedFixtureWorker`:: The direct worker of one role: it settles the queue task of its role with the next task
outcome the script declares and yields the `EngineEvent.TasksSettled` of the task state.
`ScriptedProtocolWorker`:: The protocol worker of one role: its generation, and the task the real `TaskProtocol`
leased to it. It waits, acknowledges, submits and reports infeasible through the protocol; the harness never settles
its task (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/009-task-protocol-under-the-replay.adoc[ADR 009]). A worker the harness loses
is replaced: the role's next `worker_wait` starts the successor generation, and the lost generation's late call is
refused `generation_fenced`.
`ScenarioFixtures`:: The fixture plan units `PLAN_UNIT`, `OPERATOR_UNIT` and `CONSULT_UNIT`, their primitives, a
manual clock, deterministic doubles of the engine ports, the replay's queue with its lock manager, its task protocol
and its consultation service, and the assembly of the engine and of the journal scope in the replay's directory.

[source]
----
 text block ──read──► ScenarioScript ──run──► ScenarioReplayRunner ──start / advance(status, event)──► WorkflowEngine
                       (directives,              │        │                                              │ ports
                        worker declarations)     │        ├── resting task, direct role ──► ScriptedFixtureWorker
                                                 │        │                                   └► TasksSettled ────┘
                                                 │        ├── worker_wait / worker_do ──► TaskProtocol ──► QueueStore
                                                 │        │                              └► ConsultationService
                                                 ▼        │
                         ScopeTransaction ──► WorkflowStatusStore (mcp/status.json in @TempDir)
                                                 │
                                                 └──► Snapshot(line, status bytes, queue bytes) after every step
----

== Scenario Script
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

A script is text in the test, usually a Java text block, in the line syntax of the specification
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/005-scenario-script-as-text-in-the-test.adoc[ADR 005]). This script of
`ScenarioReplayRunnerTest` replays the fixture unit to its terminal with every supported directive:

[source,text]
----
scenario every-directive
worker reviewer submitted
start plan scenario-plan
assert_state execute.build                                 # prepare ran, the build job was started
complete_job b-0000000000000001 {"outcome":"succeeded"}
assert_state execute.review                                # the task of the reviewer is queued
assert_task_outcome reviewer submitted                     # the worker settles it first
assert_state execute.ask
answer_question Q-001 web {"branch_key":"approve"}
assert_state execute.confirm
answer_question Q-002 web {"branch_key":"assumed"}
assert_state completed
assert_outcome merged
----

* The first line that is not empty or a comment is `scenario <name>`, the name in `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]*`.
* One directive per line; words are separated by blanks; a `#` outside a quoted string starts a comment.
* `worker <role> <task outcome>...` declares the direct worker of a role and the task outcomes it delivers, one per
  task, in order; a role is declared at most once. `worker_wait <role>` declares the protocol worker of a role instead:
  the two forms are told apart by the directive, one role never uses both, and a script that does is refused when it is
  read (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/009-task-protocol-under-the-replay.adoc[ADR 009]).
* The JSON-shaped argument of `complete_job`, `answer_question`, `operator_do` and `worker_do` is read with its scalar
  members, and the directive uses one of them: `outcome` (the fate of the job), `branch_key` (the outcome the answer
  chooses), the free parameters of an operator link, the `question` of a consultation, the `answer` of a consultation
  task, and the `consultation_ids` a work task's submit lists. Other scalar members are accepted and not used.

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Directive |Step

|`start <plan\|epic> <scope id>`, `start workspace`
|Starts the scope at the unit's start state.

|`complete_job <job id> {"outcome":"<fate>"}`
|Delivers `JobSettled` of the job with the fate.

|`answer_question <question id> <channel> {"branch_key":"<outcome>"}`
|Resolves the question with the outcome and delivers `QuestionResolved`; the channel is recorded in the step only.
`answer_question` is the only way a script resolves a question: no decision ends by time, so the harness has no
directive that fires a deadline.

|`assert_state <state id>`
|Compares the state of the stored status.

|`assert_outcome <outcome>`
|Compares the outcome the top-level unit ended with.

|`assert_task_outcome <role> <task outcome>`
|Compares the outcome of the last task the worker of the role settled.

|`operator_do <link> <channel> [{"<name>":"<value>"}]`
|Delivers `OperatorAct` at the state the scope rests in: the operator follows the offered link on the channel `web` or
`cli`, with the free parameters of the link when it has any
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/008-operator-act-through-operator-do.adoc[ADR 008]). The channel is recorded in the step
only, as it is for `answer_question`; the engine refuses an act the resting state does not offer, at the step's line.

|`worker_wait <role>`
|Starts the generation of the role's protocol worker and calls the task protocol's `wait`: the role's task is offered
and leased to the generation. A wait the protocol answers with no offer, or with the end of the worker, fails at the
step's line.

|`worker_do [<role>] ack`
|Acknowledges the offer through the task protocol: the task is `executing` and the worker may submit. With no role the
call targets the worker of the script's most recent `worker_wait`, which is the form the specification's example
writes; a script that holds two protocol workers names the role.

|`worker_do [<role>] submit:<kind> [{"consultation_ids":"<id>,<id>"}]`
|Submits the work task: `<kind>` is the entity kind of the leased task, the shipped `ConsultationService.validateSubmit`
checks the consultation references the submit lists, and the task protocol closes the task once. The closed outcome
settles the task state of the engine.

|`worker_do [<role>] infeasible`
|Closes the work task with the outcome `infeasible` through the task protocol, and settles the task state.

|`worker_do [<role>] consult:<role> {"question":"<text>"}`
|Follows the `consult:<role>` link through the shipped `ConsultationService.open`: the task waits for the answer and a
task of the consulted role is queued.

|`worker_do [<role>] submit:answer {"answer":"<text>"}`
|Answers the consultation the worker holds through the shipped `ConsultationService.answer`: the consultation closes
and the answer is delivered to the asker's next `worker_wait`.

|`assert_worker_state <role> <state>`
|Compares the state of the role's protocol worker's task, read from its queue record: `offered`, `acknowledged`,
`closed`, `queued` (a release whose attempt ended other than the fence's `restart`) or `fenced` (the replay's fence
store holds the generation the task's last attempt belonged to).

|`assert_refused <reason>`
|Asserts that the last `worker_do` was refused with the wire reason, one of `TaskRefusal` (`job_ended`,
`generation_fenced`, `stale_entity`, `job_scope_mismatch`, `link_not_available`, `invalid_link_args`,
`skills_not_loaded`). A `worker_do` the script does not assert is expected accepted, and a refusal fails at its line.

|`kill_worker <role>`
|The role's protocol worker is lost: the attempt ends with the fact its open attempt's deadline reports
(`worker_lost`) at that deadline — the acknowledgement deadline of an unacknowledged offer, otherwise the silence
limit — and the generation is fenced there
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/010-worker-faults-apply-the-supervisors-facts.adoc[ADR 010]).

|`end_turn <role>`
|The role's worker ends its turn without a submit, which is the exit the supervisor observes after the worker's first
call: the attempt ends `exited_without_submit` at the step's instant and the generation is fenced.

|`kill_relay <role>`
|The relay of the role's acknowledged worker is killed under the wait it holds: a held wait suppresses the silence, so
the loss (`worker_lost`) falls one silence limit after the relay's death, and the generation is fenced there.

|`stall_worker <role> <seconds>`
|The role's worker makes no call for the given time, and the silence is measured from its last call, so stalls add up.
While that silence stays below the limit that governs its open attempt (`ackDeadlineSeconds` for an unacknowledged
offer, otherwise `silenceLimitSeconds()` of `de.planmarshall.core.profile.WorkerParameters`), nothing changes and the
worker is not replaced; once it reaches the limit — one stall or several — the attempt ends `worker_lost` at the limit,
never before, and the generation is fenced there.

|`restart_runtime`
|Rebuilds the engine, the task protocol and the stores' objects over the same directory of the replay: the status is
re-read from its bytes and each protocol worker is rebuilt over its own generation with the task its lease still
names. A lease, a fence and a queue record survive; nothing in memory does.
|===

Every other directive the specification names is refused by name when the script is read, with its line and the work
package that brings it: the link and entry-session directives, the unqualified role, the plan instance version and the
operator verbs (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/004-supported-directives-only-others-refused-by-name.adoc[ADR 004]). The
list and the packages are `ScenarioScript.REFUSED`, kept by hand, and `ScenarioScriptTest` runs every entry of it;
nothing compares it with the specification, so a change to the specification's directives changes `REFUSED` with it. A
word that is no directive at all is refused as unknown, and so is a missing or surplus argument, a malformed id, an
unknown fate, task outcome, worker state or refusal, a call, a fault or an assertion of a role the script never
declares with `worker_wait` before it, an `assert_refused` that does not directly follow a `worker_do` of the task
protocol, a second `scenario` line and a script without steps.
Every refusal is an `IllegalArgumentException` whose message starts with `line <n>:`.

== Runner
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

`new ScenarioReplayRunner(unit, directory)` assembles the engine over the unit; the engine verifies the unit first, so
a fixture unit that breaks a verifier rule never runs. `run(script)` replays the steps in order:

* `start` creates the journal scope in the directory (`ScenarioFixtures.scope`), calls `WorkflowEngine.start` and
  writes the first status;
* `complete_job` and `answer_question` call `advance(status, event, store, transaction)`; the new
  status is committed in one `ScopeTransaction` of the scope, opened at the clock's instant under a lock that holds
  every key, because the replay is the only writer;
* before every step that is not an `assert_state`, `assert_worker_state` or `assert_refused`, a task state the scope
  rests in and whose role has no protocol worker is settled by the direct worker of the queued task's role, so a script
  can assert the task state first and then what the task ended with;
* `worker_wait` and `worker_do` drive the real task protocol of the replay: the worker's generation waits, the task is
  leased to it, and its calls close the task; a call the protocol accepts that closes the task settles the task state of
  the engine with the fixture return evaluation, and a `worker_do` whose next step is no `assert_refused` must be
  accepted;
* a fault (`kill_worker`, `end_turn`, `kill_relay`, `stall_worker`) applies the fact the supervisor reports for it to
  the role's open attempt, at the instant that fact is detected, and fences the generation there: the attempt ends
  `worker_lost` or `exited_without_submit`, the task is queued again within its re-offer budget (or closed `lost` with
  `attempts_spent` when the budget is spent), and the role's next `worker_wait` starts the successor generation
  (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/010-worker-faults-apply-the-supervisors-facts.adoc[ADR 010]);
* `restart_runtime` rebuilds the engine, the task protocol and the stores' objects over the same directory, re-reads
  the status from its bytes and rebuilds each protocol worker over its generation and the lease the queue still names;
* the assertions read the stored status, the queue record of a protocol worker's task, the `Ended` step of the engine
  and the direct worker's record, and change nothing.

After every step the runner keeps the bytes of the status store (`mcp/status.json` of a plan) and of the queue files
of the replay with the step's line. The snapshots of the steps that ran are kept when a later step fails. When the
steps have run, every declared outcome of a direct worker that no task took fails at the line of its declaration. One
runner replays one script once, and one directory serves one replay: a second replay in the same directory would find
its queue task ids taken.

The stores live in the directory the test gives, a `@TempDir`; there is no git repository
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/003-stores-in-a-temporary-directory-without-git.adoc[ADR 003]).

=== Failure Modes

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Failure |Result

|An assertion expects another state, outcome or task outcome
|`ScenarioFailure` at the step's line with the expected and the actual value, for example
`line 12: assert_state expected 'completed' but the scope is in 'handed_back'`.

|`assert_outcome` before the scope ended
|`ScenarioFailure` naming the state the scope is in.

|An event the resting state does not wait for: another job, or a question resolution at a job state
|`ScenarioFailure` at the step's line with the engine's refusal; nothing is written and the step is not skipped.

|A step before `start`, or a second `start`
|`ScenarioFailure` at the step's line.

|A queued task whose role has no worker, a worker without an outcome left, or a task outcome the fixture evaluation
does not evaluate (`asked`)
|`ScenarioFailure` at the line of the step before which the task was settled, naming the role.

|A declared worker outcome that no task took
|`ScenarioFailure` at the line of the worker declaration, naming the role and the outcomes left.

|A `worker_wait` the protocol answers with no offer or with the end of the worker
|`ScenarioFailure` at the wait's line, naming the answer (`no_task`, `paused`, `fenced`, …).

|A `worker_do` the protocol refuses and no `assert_refused` follows
|`ScenarioFailure` at the call's line with the wire reason of the refusal.

|An `assert_refused` on an accepted call or on another reason
|`ScenarioFailure` at the assertion's line, with the expected reason and what the call did.

|A submit that omits a delivered consultation reference
|`ScenarioFailure` at the call's line with the reason of `ConsultationService.validateSubmit`, naming the missing id.

|An `assert_worker_state` on another state
|`ScenarioFailure` at the assertion's line, with the expected state and the state the queue record shows.

|A `kill_relay` or `end_turn` whose task the worker has not acknowledged
|`ScenarioFailure` at the fault's line, naming the role and the state of its task.

|A `kill_worker` or `stall_worker` on a task the protocol no longer leases to the worker's generation
|`ScenarioFailure` at the fault's line, naming the role and the refusal the protocol answered (`stale_entity`,
`job_scope_mismatch`).

|A `restart_runtime` of a scope that was not started or that already ended
|`ScenarioFailure` at the restart's line.

|The unit violates a verifier rule
|The engine's `UnverifiedUnitException` from the runner's constructor.
|===

== Fixtures and the Scripted Worker
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

`ScenarioFixtures` is written for the scenario tests and copied from no unit of the product; it does not reuse the
package-private `EngineFixtures` of the engine tests
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/006-own-fixtures-of-the-scenario-package.adoc[ADR 006]).

* `PLAN_UNIT`: a plan unit with an `auto` state (`execute.prepare`), a job state (`execute.build`), a task state of the
  role `reviewer` (`execute.review`, entity kind `change`), a question state of the class `review` (`execute.ask`), a
  question state of the class `clarification` (`execute.confirm`), and the terminals `completed` (outcome `merged`) and
  `handed_back`.
* `OPERATOR_UNIT`: a plan unit that starts in an operator wait (`execute.wait`) with the plain link `proceed` and the
  link `choose` with one free text parameter, both running a primitive of the fixtures, and the same two terminals.
  The fixture binder binds the link's parameter into the record of `scenario.note`.
* `CONSULT_UNIT`: a plan unit whose only state is a task state of the role `author` that declares `consult role
  reviewer`, with the same two terminals; it exists for the consultation scenario of ADR 009.
* The primitives `scenario.prepare`, `scenario.build` and `scenario.evaluate` each return one fixed outcome. The job
  state is entered without a job-starting outcome, so the engine starts its job through the job port.
* The entity kind `change` is registered with no entity unit and the return evaluation `scenario.evaluate`
  (`approved`, `rejected`).
* The ports: the EasyMock job port starts the build jobs `b-0000000000000001`, `b-0000000000000002`, … in order and
  its `jobRequests` capture holds each request; `QueueTasks` writes the real record of each queue task the engine
  creates into the replay's queue, creates the ids `qt-0000000000000001`, … from a counter and keeps each request in
  `requests()`; `ScriptedQuestionRecordReader` reports the resolution the runner set for a question and every other
  question open, and `ManualClock` starts at `T0` and moves by `STEP` (one second) per step.
* The replay's queue is `QueueStore.ofProject(directory, "scenario-project", locks)` below the directory the test
  gives; `Locks` is the queue's lock manager (no lock of the operating system), `TaskProtocol` is built over the queue
  with the queue's own fence store and the pause, job-state and skill-delivery fixtures, and `ConsultationService`
  runs over the same queue (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/009-task-protocol-under-the-replay.adoc[ADR 009]).

`ScriptedFixtureWorker` settles the queue task of its role directly with the next declared task outcome and the
fixture return evaluation (`ScenarioFixtures.evaluate`): `submitted` evaluates to `approved`, `infeasible` to
`rejected`, `lost` to `lost`. It follows no link, holds no lease and has no generation
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/001-worker-settles-tasks-directly.adoc[ADR 001]).

`ScriptedProtocolWorker` is the other form: the role's generation (from the fixtures' counter) and the task the
`TaskProtocol` leased to it. A `worker_wait` starts the generation and takes the offer, `worker_do` acknowledges,
submits, reports infeasible or consults another role, and the record of the task is what `assert_worker_state`,
`assert_refused` and `assert_task_outcome` read (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/009-task-protocol-under-the-replay.adoc[ADR 009]).

== Determinism
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

The script is the recorded scenario; there is no recording file
(link:../adr/workflow-scenario/002-replay-twice-and-compare-status-bytes.adoc[ADR 002]). Everything a replay writes is
fixed by the script and the fixtures: the clock and the transaction instant come from the manual clock, job, queue
task and generation ids from counters, and every primitive returns one fixed outcome. `ScenarioDeterminismTest` proves
it:

* the same script replayed twice from empty stores, in two temporary directories, yields byte-identical snapshots after
  every step — the bytes of the status store and of the queue files of the replay;
* the same script with a protocol worker (`worker_wait`, `worker_do`) yields byte-identical status and queue bytes,
  because the task's record carries the generation id, the attempt and the instants of the manual clock, never an
  instant or an id of the machine;
* every fault scenario of `WorkerFaultScenarios` — the eight of the fault matrix (including the stall whose two stalls
  add up), the budget exhaustion and the three runtime-crash scenarios — yields byte-identical status and queue bytes in
  two replays from empty stores, so a fault's deadline, its fence and the restart's rebuilt objects are all reproducible
  from the script;
* the same script against a unit with one changed transition (`answered -> handed_back` instead of
  `answered -> completed`) writes the same bytes as the unchanged unit up to the step that takes the changed
  transition, writes different bytes at that step, and fails at the script's next `assert_state`, which names its line.

A changed transition shows in the bytes at the step that takes it; the replay fails at the first assertion that
observes it, so a script asserts the state after every step whose route it pins. A consultation is the one exception:
`ConsultationService.freeId()` draws the consultation's queue task id from `QueueTaskId.random()`, so the consultation
scenario asserts its behaviour and not byte identity (the defect is reported, not fixed here — the harness takes no
production change).

== Writing a Scenario
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

. Write the script as a text block in the test: `scenario <name>`, a `worker` line for every role whose task the direct
  form settles or a `worker_wait` line for every role whose task a protocol worker takes, `start`, then alternate the
  actions and the assertions.
. Assert the state after every action; assert a task state before the next step settles it, and the task's outcome
  with `assert_task_outcome`.
. Use the job ids the job port hands out in order (`b-0000000000000001` for the first job state entered without a
  job-starting outcome), the queue task ids the fixture creator hands out (`qt-0000000000000001`, …) and any question
  id of the form `Q-NNN`.
. A fault follows the call whose attempt it ends: `kill_worker` after the offer or after the acknowledgement,
  `end_turn`, `kill_relay` and `stall_worker` after the acknowledgement. Assert the state before the fault (the worker
  still holds its task) and after it (`fenced`), assert the replaced generation's late call with `worker_do` +
  `assert_refused generation_fenced`, then `worker_wait` starts the successor and submits the task once
  (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/010-worker-faults-apply-the-supervisors-facts.adoc[ADR 010]).
. Replay it with `new ScenarioReplayRunner(ScenarioFixtures.planUnit(), directory).run(ScenarioScript.read(text))`, the
  directory a `@TempDir` of its own.
. A protocol worker's script waits before it acts, acknowledges before it submits, and asserts a refusal with
  `assert_refused` when the call is expected to fail; a call the protocol refuses without that assertion fails the
  step.
. A scenario that needs another unit writes its own unit as DSL text and parses it with `ScenarioFixtures.parse`; a
  new primitive or entity kind goes into `ScenarioFixtures.registry()` and `entityKinds()`. An operator wait is
  replayed through `ScenarioFixtures.operatorUnit()` and the `operator_do` directive; a consultation is replayed
  through `ScenarioFixtures.consultUnit()` and the `worker_do consult:<role>`, `worker_do submit:answer` and
  `worker_do submit:<kind>` sequence.

|===
|Test |Guards

|`ScenarioScriptTest` |Every supported directive and the worker declaration read with arguments and lines; every
refused directive refused by name and line with its package; malformed lines refused with their line; a protocol call
or assertion of a role the script never waits for refused when it is read; `assert_refused` refused without a
`worker_do` before it; the refusal set derived from `TaskRefusal`
|`ScenarioReplayRunnerTest` |A replay to the unit's terminal with every directive; each assertion failing at its line
with the expected and actual value; an unaccepted event failing, never skipped; the direct worker settling
`submitted`, `infeasible` and `lost`; a missing worker, an `asked` task and a surplus outcome failing with the role;
the bytes kept after every step; an operator act taking its link and its free parameters; a protocol worker waiting,
acknowledging, submitting, reporting infeasible and reading its closed outcome from the queue record, with the
role-less `worker_do` the specification writes; a refused call asserted and a wrong refusal or state failing at its
line; a wait answered with no offer failing; the consultation re-offering the asker's task at its next wait, with the
submit that omits its reference refused and the one that names it accepted; the eight fault scenarios of
`WorkerFaultScenarios` with their properties (one submit, the loss at its deadline and not before, the lease released at
the fence, `generation_fenced` for a late call, a live worker not replaced before its deadline), the budget exhaustion
closing the task `lost`, the three runtime-crash scenarios, the loss after a restart and the protocol half of a pause at
once, with the two refusals of a fault on an unacknowledged task and of a restart of an ended scope
|`ScenarioDeterminismTest` |Two replays with identical status and queue bytes after every step, for the direct worker,

|===

== Developer Pitfalls
_Requirements: link:{docs}/Requirements.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]_

* *Nothing in the stored bytes may come from the machine.* A wall-clock instant or a random id in the status or the
  queue breaks the byte comparison. Take every instant from the manual clock, every id from a counter of the fixtures,
  and every primitive outcome from the fixture; a new port double follows the same rule. `ConsultationService` is the
  one id source the harness cannot control (`freeId()` draws a random queue task id), which is why the consultation
  scenario asserts behaviour only.
* *A deviation fails, it is never skipped.* An event the resting state does not wait for, a task without a declared
  outcome, an outcome no task took, and a protocol call the script expected accepted are failures with the line. A
  runner that skipped them would let a script pass that no longer describes the run.
* *Prove a guard by making it fire.* The determinism test plants a changed transition and sees the replay fail; a new
  check of the runner gets the same kind of test, and failures are asserted by their message, not only by their type.
* *Drive the real producers.* The runner parses and verifies the unit and runs the real engine, status store, queue and
  task protocol; the directive values are checked against the sets `EngineEvent` and `TaskRefusal` state, not against
  copies.
* *Each replay owns its directory.* The stores of a replay live only in its `@TempDir`, and two replays never share one;
  nothing machine-wide is read or written.

== What Follows

The harness runs what the engine and the task protocol support today. These parts of the specification follow with
later packages:

* the link directives (`assert_links`, `assert_skill`, `assert_rejected`, `do`), the unqualified role and the plan
  instance version;
* the hermetic git repository, with the first package that has a git step;
* the sixteen regression scenarios of the specification, as the layers they need land.

The behavioural conformance harness of the specification (`PM-TEST-6`) and its comparison footprint are not a part of
this package: they live in `pm-conformance` of `plan-marshall-mcp` (link:../adr/workflow-scenario/007-conformance-harness-in-pm-conformance.adoc[ADR 007]).

== Decisions

* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/001-worker-settles-tasks-directly.adoc[ADR 001]: the scripted worker settles the queued
  task directly.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/002-replay-twice-and-compare-status-bytes.adoc[ADR 002]: the script is the recorded
  scenario; two replays are compared byte by byte after every step.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/003-stores-in-a-temporary-directory-without-git.adoc[ADR 003]: the stores live in a
  temporary directory, without git.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/004-supported-directives-only-others-refused-by-name.adoc[ADR 004]: only the
  supported directives run; every other one is refused by name.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/005-scenario-script-as-text-in-the-test.adoc[ADR 005]: the script is text in the test.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/006-own-fixtures-of-the-scenario-package.adoc[ADR 006]: the scenario package has its
  own fixtures.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/007-conformance-harness-in-pm-conformance.adoc[ADR 007]: the behavioural conformance
  harness and the comparison footprint live in `pm-conformance`, not here.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/008-operator-act-through-operator-do.adoc[ADR 008]: an operator act is given by the
  `operator_do` directive.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/009-task-protocol-under-the-replay.adoc[ADR 009]: the replay runs the real queue and
  task protocol; `worker_wait` and `worker_do` are the protocol worker's form, `worker` the direct one's, and a role
  uses one of them.
* link:../adr/workflow-scenario/010-worker-faults-apply-the-supervisors-facts.adoc[ADR 010]: a worker fault applies
  the fact the supervisor reports for it at the deadline the fixture worker values give that fact; `restart_runtime`
  rebuilds the engine, the protocol and the stores over the same directory.
