== What

The package link:runtime-lifecycle.adoc[`runtime-lifecycle`] is done and stays as it is: it built the periodic sweep (`PeriodicSweep` of `runtime.start`) with its two steps, the pending entry records and the fenced session generations, both removing technical residue by evidence and age. No done package built a deletion of work history: the retention purges of the specification (`job_logs_days`, `archived_plans_days`, and the other `retention` keys, run through `pm-operator sweep`) have no code, so this package replaces them in the specification and builds the cleanup in their place, without reopening the sweep. The removal of the pending entry records by age goes with link:start-from-clients.adoc[`start-from-clients`], not here.

* *Specification first*: the package writes the change One Cleanup into `plan-marshall-documentation` as part of its own work, once it and UC-SYS-3 are agreed: the requirement the change drafts, under the next free id of the requirements domain it names; the sections it names: Retention & Rotation (the work history leaves it, the technical residue stays), the `retention` schema of the project's local configuration and the machine's defaults (`archived_plans_days` and `job_logs_days` removed; `workspace_rows_days` no longer covers a plan's metrics rows; the other keys kept for the technical residue), the `cleanup` keys of `machine-config.json` and of `local-config.json`, the cleanup journal and the usage aggregate of the machine scope, the tombstones of the archives, `unenrolled_at` of the project store identity, the periodic sweep and the evidence-based removal sweeps, the operator verbs (`pm-operator sweep` keeps the technical residue, `pm-operator cleanup` deletes the work history), the unenrolment (its kept machine store becomes an item of the cleanup instead of a deletion outside the product), the report catalogue (the new kind), the Local API with the events, the audit record `cleanup_deleted`, and the recurring waivers counted over the `waiver_waived` audit records of the last 90 days instead of the retained plans.
* *Two classes*: the cleanup is the only place where work history is deleted, and only after the operator confirmed it in a cleanup job: archived plans (their archive entry with records, history, decisions, transcripts, and metrics rows), archived epics (their machine store `epics/<epic_id>/`; the archived ledger in the repository is the team's record and stays), the logs of ended work (job records and logs, worker and session logs of archived plans), read reports, and the machine stores of unenrolled projects. The technical residue (temporary files, caches, worker directories of ended jobs, stale locks, fenced generations, the candidates of the evidence-based sweeps) is removed automatically by the periodic sweep and never asked; the cleanup only shows what the sweep removed and when, so each run of the periodic sweep keeps its counts per step and its instant instead of only logging them. Unread reports and pending entries never expire and are never offered; an archived plan that still owes an item (an owed audit record or transaction) is never due.
* *The settings*: one duration per kind, `cleanup.plans_after_days`, `cleanup.epics_after_days`, `cleanup.logs_after_days`, `cleanup.reports_after_days` (read reports), `cleanup.unenrolled_after_days`, each `30` by default, at least `7`, or `never`, and `cleanup.remind_after_days` (`3`, at least `1`); the machine values in `machine-config.json` `cleanup` among the machine's settings (link:machine-onboarding-unit.adoc[`machine-onboarding-unit`]), a project's own value for plans, epics, and logs only in its `local-config.json` `cleanup` (link:project-onboarding-unit.adoc[`project-onboarding-unit`]), resolved by the project's value over the machine's; a value out of range refused with its reason. An item is due when its end (archived, read, unenrolled; an unenrolled project by `unenrolled_at`, which unenrolment now writes into its `store.json`) is older than the duration of its kind and project.
* *The journal*: `<PM_MCP_BASE>/state/cleanup.jsonl`, a journal of the machine scope under its own leaf lock, with the events `job`, `decided`, `deleted`, `delete_failed`, `kept`, and `released`, the current state folded from them; a job's id `cln-NNNN`, never reused; a job's states `open`, `postponed`, `deleting`, and `done`. A plan's usage goes into the usage aggregate keyed by its `plan_id`: an aggregate row carries the plan it came from, and a plan's contribution (all its rows) is appended as one journal record, written atomically, so it is either wholly there or not at all; a plan whose record is already there is not added again, so a deletion resumed after a stop never counts a plan's usage twice; the event `deleted` follows only after both the contribution and the removal are done. A cleanup job is a record of this journal, never a job of the job runtime.
* *The daily check* (`cleanup.check`, `cleanup.report`): run by the first pass of the periodic sweep after start and by its first pass of each UTC day; it collects the due items that are in no job not yet `done` and not kept; while a job is `open`, `postponed`, or `deleting` it creates none (the due items wait for the next job), so there is never more than one job not yet `done`; when there are any and no such job, it creates one job with them and writes one report of kind `system.cleanup_due` (its subject the counts, its `detail` the job), never one per item. The kind joins the closed catalogue of link:operator-reports.adoc[`operator-reports`] with its subject text in English and German; the quiet notice of reports applies, and nothing waits for the operator. At most one job is `open` at a time; items that become due meanwhile wait for the next check after it is decided.
* *The job* (`cleanup.job`): fixed when it is created; its summary (the counts per kind and the space it frees) and its details (the items grouped by project: each plan or epic with its title, outcome, end, and size, the logs counted per plan, each opening read-only as ended work, link:chat.adoc[`chat`]); every item checked, an unchecked one kept for good on delete. Opening the job's detail marks its report read; deciding the job closes it.
* *Delete* (`cleanup.confirm`, `cleanup.delete`): one confirmation naming the counts; the job goes `deleting`, then every checked item is deleted, irreversibly: a plan's archive entry with everything below it, leaving the tombstone `archive/<plan_id>/deleted.json`, an epic's machine store, leaving `epics/<epic_id>/deleted.json`, so a deleted id is never reused; the logs; the read reports, removed by a journaled rewrite of the report journal; an unenrolled project's machine store. A plan's branch, its pull request, and the remote are never touched, nor an epic's archived ledger in the repository. Each item ends with its event `deleted` or `delete_failed` (with its reason, the item offered again by the next check) and its audit record `cleanup_deleted` (link:audit-trail.adoc[`audit-trail`]); the job's decision is audited with who, when, channel, device, and the counts; a deletion stopped in the middle is resumed by the recovery from the journal, and the job goes `done` when every item has its event. A deleted plan or epic disappears from ended work; a report whose subject was deleted shows _deleted_ with the date in its detail.
* *The figures stay*: before a plan's metrics rows go, their usage is added to `<PM_MCP_BASE>/state/usage-aggregate.jsonl`, one row per day, project, model, and role; link:runtime-metrics.adoc[`runtime-metrics`] reads it at start beside the usage records, so the token and model figures of the system view are never recomputed downward.
* *Remind and ignore* (`cleanup.remind`, `cleanup.keep`): remind deletes nothing and sets the job `postponed`; after `cleanup.remind_after_days` it is `open` again with the same items and its own report. Ignore deletes nothing and keeps the items for good (event `kept`), never offered again. The kept items are shown in the cleanup view, from which the operator releases an item back to the cleanup (event `released`), due again at the next check.
* *The endpoints*: `GET /api/v1/system/cleanup` (the open job with summary and details, the settings in effect, the kept count, and the figures of the technical residue), `POST /api/v1/system/cleanup/{job_id}/delete` (with the unchecked items), `POST /api/v1/system/cleanup/{job_id}/remind`, `POST /api/v1/system/cleanup/{job_id}/ignore`, `GET` and `PUT /api/v1/system/cleanup/kept` (the kept list, an item released); the events `cleanup.job_opened`, `cleanup.decided`, `cleanup.progress`, and `cleanup.done` to every open client; accepted from `pm-operator` and from a paired web device and audited with their channel; the job and its items addressed through link:entity-addressing.adoc[`entity-addressing`]; the same acts as the operator verb `pm-operator cleanup`. A decision on a job that is already decided is answered with its decision, never applied twice.

== Done When

The requirement and the specification of the change are merged in `plan-marshall-documentation`; nothing of the work history is deleted without a decided job, shown by a test that passes every duration with no job decided; with a fixed clock the check at start and the daily one each write one report of kind `system.cleanup_due` for the items newly due and none while nothing is, never one per item, and never offer an unread report, a pending entry, or a kept item; a project's own duration wins over the machine's and `never` keeps its kind; delete removes exactly the checked items of each kind, keeps the unchecked ones for good, leaves the branch, the pull request, and the archived ledger in the repository untouched, leaves a tombstone so the id is never reused, writes one `cleanup_deleted` audit record per item, and is resumed by the recovery after a stop in the middle; an item that fails is recorded `delete_failed` and offered again; a duration below 7 days or a reminder below 1 is refused, and a project's own value is accepted for plans, epics, and logs only; an archived plan that owes an item is never due; the check runs on the first sweep pass after start and the first of each UTC day only; an item that became due after the job was created is not deleted with it; remind brings the same items back as a new job after its days, ignore keeps them, and a kept item released is offered again; a decided job answers a second decision with its decision; the token figures of the system view are the same before and after a deletion and after a restart, read from the usage aggregate; the journal folds to the same jobs and kept list after a restart; a report whose subject was deleted shows it deleted; each run of the periodic sweep keeps its counts, shown in the cleanup view; every endpoint answers both channels, audited with its channel.
