== What

The packages link:https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/603d4f7/doc/plans/runtime-lifecycle.adoc[`runtime-lifecycle`] and link:https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/603d4f7/doc/plans/service-unit.adoc[`service-unit`] are done and stay as they are: they built the drain on a signal, the recovery that resumes work after it, the version mismatch, and the unit file. This package adds the lifecycle as acts of the operator in both clients on top of them; it is a package of its own because those two are closed, and because its acts need the operator endpoints, the consent store, and the jobs, workers, and tasks they stop, none of which existed when they were cut.

* *Implements* link:../specification/runtime-lifecycle/01-startup-recovery-drain.adoc#runtime-lifecycle-operations[Runtime Lifecycle § Drain, Stop & Upgrade] (the operator's stop, restart, update check, and upgrade, with the lifecycle and version states), the consent kind `check_updates` of link:../specification/store-schemas/12-machine-stores.adoc#consent[Store Schemas § Consent Before Acting], and the version state and the upgrade's result of link:../specification/store-schemas/12-machine-stores.adoc#machine-state-records[§ Machine State Records] (`PM-ARCH-7`, `PM-DIST-1`).
* *The lifecycle state*: `GET /api/v1/status` carries the version and the lifecycle state: `running`; `draining` (who asked it: `operator`, `signal`, `service_manager`; whether it can be withdrawn; what follows, `exit` or `restart`; when the drain timeout ends); `stopping`; `upgrading` with its step; beside it the version state (the newest version known and when it was checked, a failed check with its reason, whether the runtime can upgrade this installation, the release notes at `GET /api/v1/system/release-notes`) and an upgrade's last result.
* *Stop*: `POST /api/v1/operator/runtime/stop` with `when` (`drain`, the default, or `now`) and `then` (`exit`, the default, or `restart`). `drain` is the drain of link:https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-core/blob/603d4f7/doc/plans/runtime-lifecycle.adoc[`runtime-lifecycle`] with its participants; `now` is that drain with its wait cut to zero: what still runs is stopped and settled as after the drain timeout (tasks offered again after the next start, jobs recorded `killed`, worker generations fenced), so the recovery resumes it; a stop with `now` during a drain cuts that drain's wait. The answer names what runs, so the client can ask its confirmation with it.
* *Withdrawing a drain*: `DELETE /api/v1/operator/runtime/stop` withdraws a drain the operator asked while its wait runs, the runtime admitting jobs and offering tasks again; once it terminates what runs, the withdrawal is refused `not_withdrawable`; a drain a signal started cannot be withdrawn. `pm-operator drain` stays a signal for a runtime in any state.
* *Restart*: with `then: restart` the runtime finishes its drain, starts its successor detached through the on-demand start procedure of `pm-api`, and exits, releasing the singleton lock the successor waits for; under the service unit it exits with `75` (`EX_TEMPFAIL`) instead, on which the unit restarts the service. A stopped runtime is started only from the terminal client (on demand, or `pm-operator runtime start`).
* *Update check*: `POST /api/v1/operator/runtime/upgrade/check`, and, while `check_updates` is remembered, the runtime itself at most once a day (the update check interval), asks the organisation's registry of `PM-DIST-1` for the newest release of the machine's platform under that consent; nothing about the machine is sent beyond the request. The installed version is a local read without consent. The registry credential and the form of a release are an open question of the documentation repository (`registry-credential`); the package blocks on it for the check and the fetch.
* *Upgrade*: `POST /api/v1/operator/runtime/upgrade` with the version and `when`: fetch, verify the build attestation and the archive against its digest (`release_unattested` otherwise), unpack into a new versioned installation directory, rewrite an installed service unit, record the upgrade (`from`, `to`, step `switching`), switch both `PATH` links, then stop with `then: restart`. `DELETE /api/v1/operator/runtime/upgrade` withdraws it until the links are switched, then `not_withdrawable`. A failure before the switch changes nothing and is the result with its reason; a successor that does not reach its authenticated round trip within the runtime start timeout is terminated, the links and the unit switched back, and the previous version started; the starting runtime settles the record (`runtime.upgraded`, or `runtime.upgrade_failed` with the reason). The previous directory stays as the way back until the next upgrade. An installation not laid out in versioned directories with the two links, or reached through `PM_MCP_HOME`, is refused `upgrade_not_possible` with its reason. Pre-1.0: no store migration beyond what the specification defines.
* *The acts*: privileged operator acts under `/api/v1/operator/`, accepted from `pm-operator` and from a paired device, audited with their channel, and announced on the runtime-wide stream (the events of the link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/runtime-model/06-event-catalogue.adoc[Event Catalogue]), so every open client sees the runtime draining, stopping, or upgrading.

== Done When

With a killed-runtime fixture and fixture jobs and workers: a stop now ends every job and fences every worker at once and the next start resumes each plan from its last committed state; a drain lets the running task and job finish and starts nothing new; a withdrawn drain leaves everything running; a drain turned into a stop now behaves like a stop now; a restart comes back on the same installation without a terminal; a drain a signal started and a drain past its wait are refused `not_withdrawable`; the update check runs only with `check_updates` given, never asks again while it is remembered, and runs by itself at most once a day; an upgrade from a staged release switches to the new directory and resumes the running work; a failure before and one after the switch each leave the previous version running with the reason; an installation without versioned directories is refused `upgrade_not_possible`; every act is audited with its channel, and every open event stream sees the runtime stopping.
