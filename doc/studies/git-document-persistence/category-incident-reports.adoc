Incident response reports are written in Confluence today. A form system that produces AsciiDoc and takes care of
review, approval and deadlines is the alternative examined here.

== Handling and Report Are Two Things

* *Handling the incident* (alerting, coordination, escalation in real time) does not belong in Git. Chat, ticket
  system and on-call service do that.
* *The report on the incident* (course, cause, impact, lessons, measures, notification duties) is a document with
  structure, review and approval. It fits the Git approach very well.

The live phase therefore stays in Confluence, the chat or the ticket system; only the report moves into the form
system, ideally with an import of the timeline from the ticket or the chat.

== What a Form System Gains over Confluence

. *Mandatory fields and deadlines are enforced.* Security incidents carry hard notification deadlines: GDPR Art. 33
  (notification within 72 hours; documentation of every personal data breach, para. 5) and, where it applies, NIS2
  (early warning within 24 hours, notification within 72 hours, final report within one month) _(unverified;
  whether NIS2 applies depends on sector and size)_. A form with a schema shows the deadlines and checks the
  mandatory fields; a Confluence page cannot.
. *Review and approval* by pull request, immutable after approval, timestamped: evidence for ISO 27001
  A.5.24–5.28 (among them 5.27, learning from incidents, and 5.28, collection of evidence).
. *Machine-readable*: evaluations across all incidents (categories, time to detection and resolution, recurring
  causes); in Confluence that is free text.
. *Links*: an incident creates risks in the ISMS register, measures as issues and, where it applies, a review of
  the threat model of the affected application.
. *Data protection and confidentiality* as designed in link:data-protection.adoc[Data Protection]: a repository per year
  with a retention period (`isms-incidents-2026`), personal details encrypted through the keyring, read access for a
  small circle.

Where Confluence is better: joint live editing and commenting, familiarity for non-technical participants, and it
runs already. That is why the live phase stays there.

== AsciiDoc Is the Output, Not the Truth

When the form produces AsciiDoc and someone edits that AsciiDoc by hand, the form can no longer load it cleanly.
Hence:

----
incidents/<id>/
  incident.yaml        ← structured fields (schema): times, category, severity, notifications, measures
  narrative/*.adoc     ← free-text sections (course, root-cause analysis, lessons) as AsciiDoc fragments
  report.adoc          ← generated: a template joins the YAML and the fragments
----

The structured data stay in YAML; free text may be AsciiDoc with tables and images. `report.adoc` is generated
reproducibly and committed as readable evidence; a CI check makes sure it matches the YAML. PDF for authorities or
customers is generated from it as well.

== Existing Alternatives

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Tool |Kind |Assessment

|incident.io, FireHydrant, Rootly, PagerDuty
|Commercial SaaS, focused on operational incidents (SRE)
|Strong in the live phase with chat integration, produce postmortems; the data lie with the vendor

|Jira Service Management with Confluence templates
|Commercial, Atlassian
|Close to the current setup, but free text without a schema

|Netflix Dispatch
|Open source incident management with Slack
|Live phase; whether it is still actively maintained is not verified

|DFIR-IRIS _(unverified)_
|Open source handling of security incidents (forensics), report templates
|Closest for security incidents; a database and operation of its own

|TheHive _(unverified)_
|Platform for security incidents in a SOC
|For SOC teams; commercial licence since v5 to this study's knowledge

|Postmortems as Markdown in Git
|Established practice (template of the Google SRE book)
|Shows that the approach carries, but without form, schema and deadlines
|===

No tool known to this study structures reports, reviews them, stores them without additional operation and links
them to the ISMS, the threat models and the notification duties. The gap is the same as for SAMM and threat models.
