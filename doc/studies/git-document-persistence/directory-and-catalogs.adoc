= Directory and Catalogs
:nofooter:
:toc: macro
:toclevels: 2

Part of the link:README.adoc[study on Git as the persistence of versioned documents]. Statements marked
_(unverified)_ come from general knowledge and were not checked against a current source.

toc::[]

The master data every category refers to: a small directory of persons with their name, team and role, and the
catalogs of roles, teams and services. A user who logs in for the first time fills in a form and is entered in the
directory. Documents refer to roles, teams and services by stable ids; the directory resolves them.

== Scope of the First Iteration

* *Roles are names only.* They describe who is responsible or the contact for something; no right is derived from
  them. Approvals and access are not tied to a role of the directory.
* *Hence no staging.* An assignment a user makes is valid at once; there is no pending state and no confirmation by a
  team lead or an administrator. The history of the directory shows who changed what.
* *Roles and teams come from a catalog.* A user picks them; free text is not allowed. A missing entry is added to the
  catalog through a review.

A later iteration may derive rights from roles; it then needs a confirmation of assignments (see _Later Iterations_).

== Layout

----
directory (repository)
  catalog/roles.yaml            ← catalog of roles, not personal, plain text
  catalog/teams.yaml            ← catalog of teams, not personal, plain text
  catalog/services/<id>.yaml    ← one file per application or service, not personal, plain text
  persons/<pseudonym>.enc       ← one entry per person, encrypted with the data key of the person
  keys/keyring.enc              ← the data keys and the `sub` per pseudonym, encrypted with the KEK
----

The keyring and its erasure by key rotation are described in link:data-protection.adoc[Data Protection].

The directory gets a repository of its own. The catalogs are widely readable; the entries of persons are encrypted
anyway. One file per person and per service keeps concurrent saves free of conflicts; the small catalogs of roles
and teams change rarely.

== Catalogs of Roles and Teams

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/roles.yaml
- id: role:security-champion
  name: Security Champion
  description: Contact for security in the team
- id: role:service-owner
  name: Service Owner
  description: Responsible for an application or service
----

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/teams.yaml
- id: team:payments
  name: Payments
  parent: team:platform          # optional, for hierarchies
----

* *Stable ids instead of names.* Documents refer to `role:security-champion` and `team:payments`; renaming an entry
  in the catalog breaks no reference.
* *Retire instead of delete.* An entry gets `retired: true`; old documents keep referring to a retired team.
* *Changes by review.* A new or renamed entry is a pull request or a reviewed save, like every other document.

== Catalog of Applications and Services

The service catalog is the link most categories hang on:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
|Category |Relation to the service

|Threat models
|A threat model belongs to one service; its repositories are the basis to detect an outdated model

|SAMM
|The scope of an assessment is a team or a service

|Incident response reports
|Which services were affected, with their protection needs for the assessment of notification duties

|ISMS
|The inventory of assets (Annex A 5.9): applications are assets, risks and measures hang on them
|===

An entry is larger than one of the small catalogs, hence one file per service:

[source,yaml]
----
# catalog/services/payment-api.yaml
id: service:payment-api
name: Payment API
description: Payment processing for the web shop
system: system:webshop                 # optional: the product or system above
lifecycle: active                      # planned | active | deprecated | retired
owner:
  team: team:payments
  role: role:service-owner             # responsibility by role, not by person
repositories:
  - https://git.example/payments/payment-api
dependsOn:
  - service:customer-db
  - external:psp-provider              # later: the supplier register of the ISMS
protection-needs:                      # confidentiality, integrity, availability
  confidentiality: high
  integrity: high
  availability: high
data-classes: [personal, payment]
----

* *Responsibility by team and role*: the directory resolves them to persons, and the catalog itself holds no personal
  data.
* *The protection needs* (confidentiality, integrity, availability, as in BSI IT-Grundschutz) are what the ISMS
  counts. They decide, for example, how often the threat model is reviewed, which SAMM targets apply and how an
  incident is classified.
* *`repositories`* links catalog and code: threat model in the application's repository, detection of outdated
  models, later SBOMs.
* *`dependsOn`* yields a dependency graph: in an incident the affected services follow from it, and the protection
  needs inherit by the maximum principle.
* *Maintenance*: the owning team keeps the entry through a form; repositories can be proposed from the Git server; a
  service without a repository is allowed (a bought SaaS application).

=== Alignment with Backstage and Import

Many development organisations already keep such a catalog. The Backstage software catalog holds its entries as
`catalog-info.yaml` in the repository of each service, with the kinds Component, System, API, Resource, Group and
User _(unverified)_; the principle "the description lives in the service's repository" fits this approach exactly.
Operations often keep a CMDB (i-doit, iTop, ServiceNow).

Therefore:

* *The schema leans on Backstage*: the same terms and, where possible, the same fields (`system`, `lifecycle`,
  `owner`, `dependsOn`), so that entries map one to one.
* *Import*: where Backstage exists, components and systems are imported and kept in sync; groups map onto the team
  catalog. The own catalog adds only what is missing: protection needs, data classes, the links to security and the
  ISMS.
* *Where nothing exists*, the own catalog is the source of truth.
* Whether the additional fields stand in `catalog-info.yaml` itself (as annotations) or in the central catalog is
  configurable per organisation.

== The Directory of Persons

[source,yaml]
----
# persons/<pseudonym>.enc, decrypted
name: Erika Example
memberships:                     # role per team, not global
  - team: team:payments
    role: role:security-champion
  - team: team:payments
    role: role:developer
since: 2026-11-12
----

*Memberships as pairs of team and role* instead of separate fields: a person often belongs to several teams with a
different role in each, and assignments almost always ask for "role X in team Y".

=== First Login

. The login through Token Sheriff yields `sub` and usually the name. The application finds no pseudonym for that
  `sub` in the keyring, creates one and shows the form.
. The form is prefilled with the name from the token. Team and role are picked from the catalogs; a missing entry can
  be requested for the catalog.
. Saving is a signed commit (level 1, see link:identity-and-signing.adoc[Identity and Signing]); the entry is valid at
  once.
. Later the user changes the own entry in the same form.

The directory is a link:schema-driven-documents.adoc[schema-driven document] itself: form and workflow come from
`git-forms`; it needs no mechanism of its own.

=== Staying Current

Self-entered data age: teams change and nobody updates. Hence:

* Where the identity provider delivers groups or departments (through Token Sheriff, possibly by SCIM _(unverified)_),
  they are taken over and prefill the form.
* A periodic confirmation, yearly for example or on the first login after a reorganisation: "Is your team still
  right?"

== Data Protection

* *The whole entry is personal*, not only the name: pseudonym, team and role together often identify a person ("CISO
  in team X"). The whole entry is therefore encrypted with the data key of the person; the catalogs are not personal.
* *Data minimisation*: name, team and role only. Phone or e-mail only where they are really needed; the e-mail comes
  from the token anyway.
* *Information duty* under GDPR Art. 13 in the form itself: what the directory is for and how long it is kept. The
  legal basis (legitimate interest or employee data protection) is agreed with the data protection officer; the
  German rule on employee data protection (§ 26 BDSG) is disputed since a judgment of the CJEU of 2023
  _(unverified)_.
* *Leaving*: the entry is erased through the rotation of the key-encryption key. Documents keep the pseudonym and
  show "former member"; because responsibilities in documents run through roles (`owner: role:…`), they stay
  understandable.

== What It Means for the Architecture

* The directory closes a gap: documents refer to roles instead of persons, and the directory resolves `role:`,
  `team:` and `service:` to persons and entries, whether or not the identity provider delivers groups.
* All categories need it: owners in the ISMS, assessors in SAMM, teams and services for threat models, participants
  and affected services in incident reports. It is a shared part, not one category among others.
* Persons, roles, teams and services are free of any domain, and the directory builds on `git-store`, `git-crypto`
  and `git-forms`; it is therefore the module `git-directory` of the core repository (see
  link:extraction-from-pm-mcp-core.adoc[Extraction from `pm-mcp-core`]): the catalogs with stable ids, retirement,
  review and periodic confirmation; the directory of persons with encrypted entries and the first-login form; the
  resolution of references; the import from Backstage.
* Which catalogs exist is configuration; further catalogs (suppliers, locations) cost little.

== Later Iterations

* *Rights from roles.* When approvals are tied to roles, a self-chosen role must not grant them: assignments of such
  roles need a confirmation by a team lead or an administrator (a pending state and signature level 2), or come from
  the identity provider. A workflow then counts confirmed assignments only.
* *Confirmed protection needs.* Changes of the protection needs of a service, on which review intervals and
  notification duties depend, may need a confirmation by the information security officer.
* *Two-way synchronisation with Backstage*, writing the additional fields back as annotations.
