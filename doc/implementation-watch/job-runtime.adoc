== Outcome Derivation

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-1]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-1: Job outcomes are derived at the boundary

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#outcome-derivation[Job Runtime § Outcome Derivation],

link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-EXEC-4[PM-EXEC-4]
Hazard:: Two vocabularies (`indeterminate` in the wrapper, `unknown` at the boundary) named one state, and a wrapper
could claim a status the boundary never derived.
Guard:: The runtime derives job outcomes itself; a `project_script` output `status` is validated against the
observed exit and parse evidence and never adopted blindly.
Source:: plan-marshall truthful-signals PLAN-TRUTH-155 D5.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-23]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-23: A crash at job end loses at most a usage row, a freshness mark, or a timing sample

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#outcome-derivation[Job Runtime § Outcome Derivation] (Write order at job end),
link:../specification/store-schemas/10-job-and-lesson-records.adoc#job-record[Store Schemas § Job Record] (Write order at job end)
Hazard:: Settling a job in an arbitrary order lets a crash leave a released build slot with an unsettled record, or a
ledger row that stamps a build whose job record never recorded its fate.
Guard:: Crash injection after each step of the fixed order (job record fate with a worker's token revocation and
fence, for a worker the queue task record with its attempt end and lease release, the metrics row of the attempt
that ended with the job, change-ledger row, timing observation, slot release, and last a worker's job-private
directory removal): after restart every store is consistent, and the only losses are a task's missing metrics row
(`record-metrics` reports it as the divergence `row_absent_from_metrics`, never as `0`; the job record carries no
usage), a missing ledger row (the next freshness check re-runs the build), or a missing timing sample; a task lease
left on the ended generation is released by the recovery and the task queued again once, and the recovery removes
the job-private directory of a settled job; no other step is re-derived and no marker file exists.
Source:: design review.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-5]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-5: Bounded waits are wall-clock and say what happened

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#_bounded_long_poll_progress_reset_waiting_primitives_pm_wf_8_pm_impl_4[Job Runtime § Bounded Long-Poll & Progress-Reset Waiting Primitives],

link:../specification/timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#waits[Timeouts & Budgets § Waits & Long-Polls],
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-IMPL-4[PM-IMPL-4]
Hazard:: A wait budget passed as an iteration count expired in seconds and reported a bot "absent" while it was
running; a wait at its ceiling could not tell waiting from re-polling finished work; wait cost was invisible.

not absent; the only calls a model holds open are a worker's `pm_wait` and a running `pm_build`: a `pm_wait` returns

time deferred by a provider rate limit is attributed

Fixture:: 600 s budget burned in seconds ("CodeRabbit absent" while running); a script cost rollup of 2,051 calls, 14,423,580 ms, dominated by the three wait notations
build_server 27.4%, ci 27.3%, ci_complete_precondition 23.1% (77.8% together). Wait attribution by reason:
deliberate (an operator ruling to wait out a CodeRabbit quota window) vs poll miss (8 `ci_complete_precondition`
polls averaging 417 s), never one "waste" figure.
Source:: plan-marshall review-apparatus PLAN-PR-048 D2; plan-marshall lesson 2026-09-23-05-003; post-run-quality PLAN-PRQ-11 D0
and D2.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-19]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-19: One shared provider poll honours `304` and `Retry-After`

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#_bounded_long_poll_progress_reset_waiting_primitives_pm_wf_8_pm_impl_4[Job Runtime § Bounded Long-Poll & Progress-Reset Waiting Primitives],

Hazard:: Every wait polls the provider on its own and ignores rate-limit signals; the credential's rate budget is

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#_bounded_long_poll_progress_reset_waiting_primitives_pm_wf_8_pm_impl_4[Job Runtime § Bounded Long-Poll & Progress-Reset Waiting Primitives] (Wait Attribution),

== Linux Filesystem Confinement (Landlock)

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-20]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-20: A job cannot read the PM-MCP base directory

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#_linux_filesystem_confinement_landlock[Job Runtime § Linux Filesystem Confinement] (Read set),
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: Landlock rules only grant access, so a read rule on `/` also grants `<PM_MCP_BASE>`; a job that reads
`run/runtime.token` can call every endpoint of the local API, the operator endpoints included, and a job that reads a
fallback credential file, an enrolment, or another project's machine store learns what the operator never gave it.
Guard:: On Linux with Landlock, a job fixture tries to read `<PM_MCP_BASE>/run/runtime.token`, a fallback credential
file under `<PM_MCP_BASE>/credentials/`, and an entry of the per-project machine store, and each read fails with a
permission error; positive control: the same job reads a binary under `<PM_MCP_HOME>`, a sibling of `<PM_MCP_BASE>`
in its parent directory, and a sibling of each directory on the path from `/` (read set built by sibling
enumeration); control: an entry created on that path after job start is not readable by the job, which is the
accepted limitation, and an entry created elsewhere stays readable; the one exception: a worker (kind `llm`)
reads its own job-private directory `<PM_MCP_BASE>/run/jobs/<job_id>/` and fails to read that of another job and
every other entry of `<PM_MCP_BASE>`; the jobs that run build tools read and write their project's daemon store and
no other project's.
Source:: design review.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-21]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-21: A job writes only its own project root

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#_linux_filesystem_confinement_landlock[Job Runtime § Linux Filesystem Confinement] (Write set),
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: A write set built from every enrolled project root, or one that still covers `<PM_MCP_BASE>`, lets a job of
one project rewrite another project's sources or the runtime's own stores and enrolments.
Guard:: On Linux with Landlock, a job fixture of project A writes into the enrolled root of project B and into
`<PM_MCP_BASE>` (an enrolment file and a machine-store file), and each write fails with a permission error; positive
control: the same job writes into its own project root, one of its worktrees, `.marshall/local/temp/`, a declared
toolchain cache, and `/tmp`, and each write succeeds; for a worker (kind `llm`) additionally the harness sub-paths
its client profile declares (`headless.writable_paths`: login or credential file, caches, session directory)
succeed, while its own job-private directory `<PM_MCP_BASE>/run/jobs/<job_id>/` (which it can only read, apart from
its `data/` subdirectory where its harness declares a per-worker data home), a settings, command, or skill file of
its harness, the home of another harness, the data directory of another worker, and the rest of `<PM_MCP_BASE>`
fail; a `build` job writes its project's daemon store and fails to write that of another project.
Source:: design review.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-38]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-38: A worker reads only its listed read set

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#worker-read-set[Job Runtime § Worker Read Set],
link:../specification/client-profiles/01-profile-schema.adoc#headless-readable-paths[Client Profiles § Readable Paths],
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: Two ways round. A read set that still holds a rule on `/`, the home directory, or a grant that contains a
credential location (an installation prefix that resolves to the home directory, a toolchain cache such as `~/.gradle`
that holds `gradle.properties`) lets a worker's shell read an SSH key or a forge login and hand it to its model,
without any network of its own. A read set that misses a file the harness needs makes the harness fail, or run on
without it; a harness that skips its unreadable settings runs without the deny rules of its bundle.
Guard:: On Linux with Landlock and on macOS, a worker fixture reads decoy files placed at `~/.ssh/id_ed25519`,
`~/.aws/credentials`, `~/.config/gh/hosts.yml`, and `~/.netrc`, a file of another enrolled project without a grant,
and a new file directly in the home directory, and each read fails with a permission error; a fixture whose harness
executable lies in `~/bin` gets `~/bin` and not the home directory; a declared cache `~/.gradle` is absent from a
worker's sets and present in a `build` job's. Positive control: the same worker reads its project root and worktree,
the system locations, its harness's installation, every bundle-managed file of its harness, its declared readable
paths, and another project's root while its plan holds a read grant. Profile validation refuses a readable or
writable path that is the home directory or holds a credential location. Matched control: a `build` job of the same
project still reads a file directly in the home directory.
Source:: design review; doc/articles/abliterated-models-backdoor.adoc (plan-marshall-documentation).

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-40]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-40: A name lookup is a route out

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#worker-name-resolution[Job Runtime § Name Resolution],
link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#macos-confinement[Job Runtime § macOS Confinement],
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: Three ways out that admit no tunnel. A proxy that resolves the requested host before it matches the
allowlist sends a refused name to the name server itself, so `<data>.attacker.example` leaves through the refusal.
On macOS the lookup goes to `mDNSResponder` over Mach IPC, which the network rules of the sandbox profile do not
govern. On Linux the network namespace has no name server, but the systemd-resolved, `nscd`, and system bus sockets
are pathname sockets that Landlock governs only from ABI 9, and the C library finds them by itself. A fourth hazard
the other way round: an admitted name whose record points to `127.0.0.1` leads a tunnel to the runtime's listeners.
Guard:: A proxy test sends `CONNECT` for a refused name to a proxy whose resolver is a recording fixture and asserts
that the fixture saw no query, and for an admitted name that resolves to `127.0.0.1`, `169.254.169.254`, and
`10.0.0.1` asserts `address_not_public` with no connection opened. A worker fixture on macOS calls `getaddrinfo` for
an arbitrary name and fails; on Linux from Landlock ABI 9 the same lookup fails, below ABI 9 it succeeds through the
resolver socket and the test asserts exactly that, so a kernel upgrade turns the assertion over by itself. Positive
control: the worker's harness reaches its endpoint through the proxy in both platforms.
Source:: doc/articles/openshell-rogue-agents-openshell.adoc (plan-marshall-documentation); OpenShell routes every
query to a resolver of its supervisor and checks a DNS round trip before an agent starts.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-41]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-41: Below Landlock ABI 3 a job truncates what it cannot write

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#abi-floor[Job Runtime § Linux Filesystem Confinement] (ABI Floor),
link:../specification/store-schemas/10-job-and-lesson-records.adoc#job-record[Store Schemas § Job Record],
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: `LANDLOCK_ACCESS_FS_TRUNCATE` arrives with ABI 3; before it, `truncate(2)` and `open(O_TRUNC)` are always
allowed. A launcher that records `confined` on ABI 2 claims a write set that a job can bypass by emptying a file:
the settings file with a worker's deny rules, a file of another project, a toolchain configuration. A launcher that
handles the right on ABI 3 without granting it with the write set breaks every build that rewrites a file.
Guard:: Linux test of `pm-exec` per applied ABI: a confined job truncates a file of its write set (positive control)
and a file outside it; on ABI 2 the second truncation succeeds and the launch report says `partial(abi=2)`, from
ABI 3 it fails with `EACCES` and the report says `confined`. A Maven and a Gradle build under ABI 3 pass.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-42]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-42: A job signals the runtime

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#landlock-scopes[Job Runtime § Linux Filesystem Confinement] (Scopes),

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-43]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-43: The system call filter breaks what it should protect

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#seccomp-filter[Job Runtime § Linux Filesystem Confinement] (System call filter),
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: A seccomp filter fails quietly in both directions. Installed before the launcher creates a worker's user and
network namespace, it refuses the launcher's own setup and every worker fails to start. Without an architecture check
a call through the x32 ABI or the 32-bit entry point slips past the syscall numbers of the filter. `clone3` carries
its flags in memory the filter cannot read, so a filter that allows it allows `CLONE_NEWUSER`; one that refuses it
with `EPERM` instead of `ENOSYS` makes the C library fail rather than fall back. `io_uring` refused with `EPERM`
fails a runtime that would fall back on `ENOSYS`.
Guard:: Linux test of `pm-exec`: a confined job's `unshare(CLONE_NEWUSER)`, `clone` with `CLONE_NEWNET`, `mount`,
`bpf`, and `socket(AF_PACKET, …)` fail with `EPERM`, `clone3` and `io_uring_setup` with `ENOSYS`, and a call through
the x32 ABI ends the process; positive control: a worker starts in its namespaces, `pthread_create` and `fork` work,
and a Maven build, a Gradle build, and an `npm ci` pass under the filter. The launch report and the job record carry
`seccomp: applied`.
Source:: doc/articles/openshell-rogue-agents-openshell.adoc (plan-marshall-documentation); OpenShell installs its
filter as the last step and lists the same families.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-44]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-44: A worker runs on after its runtime died

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#runtime-loss[Job Runtime § Process Termination] (Runtime Loss),
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG` is the obvious tool and the wrong one: it fires when the thread that forked the launcher
ends, and the runtime starts processes from threads that end long before the runtime, so every worker would be killed
at random. A launcher that does not watch its parent at all leaves a worker running with its login copy until the next
start, which may never come.
Guard:: Test of `pm-exec`: a worker fixture started from a thread that ends right after the start keeps running; the
same fixture whose parent process is killed ends its tree, a `setsid` descendant included, within two seconds on
Linux and on macOS; the recovery of the next start settles the job `killed` without a live process. A `build` job
of a killed parent keeps running.
Source:: doc/articles/openshell-rogue-agents-openshell.adoc (plan-marshall-documentation); OpenShell freezes an agent
whose supervisor disconnected and ends it after a deadline.

[#PM-WATCH-JOB-45]
=== PM-WATCH-JOB-45: A job record claims a fence that does not hold

Anchor:: link:../specification/job-runtime/02-confinement-and-environment.adoc#confinement-self-check[Job Runtime § Confinement Self-Check],
link:../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-SEC-5[PM-SEC-5]
Hazard:: `confined`, `seatbelt`, and `egress: enforced` say what the launcher applied. A distribution kernel, a
security module, a sysctl, or a macOS update can leave the mechanism applied and not refusing, and every record goes
on claiming it. A check that runs only its refusals passes on a confinement that refuses everything, the harness
included.
Guard:: A self-check test whose fixture launcher reports a mechanism applied but lets the write outside the write set
through: the runtime records `confinement: unavailable` for the following jobs, the runtime status shows the failed
check, and a `confinement_check_failed` record names it; a fixture whose positive control fails refuses every
worker start; a fixture whose direct `connect(2)` succeeds sets `egress: unenforced` and holds workers for the
operator's acceptance. Positive control: on a confining platform every check passes and the records state
`confined` or `seatbelt` and `egress: enforced`.
Source:: doc/articles/openshell-rogue-agents-openshell.adoc (plan-marshall-documentation); OpenShell confirms its
boundary with active probes before the agent starts.
