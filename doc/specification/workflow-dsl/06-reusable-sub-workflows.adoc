== Reusable Compound Sub-Workflows & Call-Stack Execution (`PM-WF-16`)
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16: Reusable Compound Sub-Workflows & Config-Driven Git Commit/Push]_

Workflows are composed not only of atomic `do` / `auto` / `job` primitives, but also of reusable, config-aware *compound sub-workflows* (`scope subworkflow`). A compound sub-workflow encapsulates a multi-state sub-process (deterministic `auto` checks, configuration lookups, task states for the judgments it needs, and recovery branches) and handles its internal cases locally with its own states, so that Phase 5 (`execute`), Phase 6 (`finalize`), the workspace setup, and the epic invoke the exact same state machine rather than duplicating states or collapsing recovery into opaque Java code. Its exit contract towards the caller is small and control-only (xref:01-grammar.adoc#sub-workflow-exits[Sub-Workflow Exit Contract]). The shipped sub-workflows are the six phases the plan unit calls and the `land` lane's `land`, the variants of the plan behaviour axes (xref:09-plan-instance.adoc#plan-axes[Plan Behaviour Axes]), `git.commit_and_push` (below), the short landings `git.land-removal`, `git.land-unit` and `git.land-setup` (<<short-landings>>), the remediation policy units (xref:08-remediation-units.adoc#remediation-units[Remediation Policy Units]), the re-planning unit `replan`, the task protocol unit `worker-task` (xref:02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#worker-task-unit[Task Protocol Unit]), the decision unit `decision` and the chat unit `chat`, which every scope calls (xref:02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#question-states[Question States & Decisions]; link:../state-catalogue.adoc#chat[State Catalogue, Chatting with the Planning Role]), the aspects of the machine's onboarding (xref:01-grammar.adoc#machine-scope[Machine Scope]), and the compiled-only source units of a start, whose call sites (`start.source-<kind>`, `start-epic.source-issue`) are documented in the link:../state-catalogue/09-bootstrap-and-lessons.adoc[State Catalogue, Bootstrap & Source Units]. A sub-workflow of any scope may contain operator waits (xref:02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#operator-wait[Waiting for the Operator]).

=== Call-Stack Execution & Persistence Semantics
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-45[PM-WATCH-DSL-45]_

The call of a sub-workflow (the frame pushed onto the persisted `call_stack` with the bound arguments and `pushed_at`), the resting states inside a sub-workflow that are persisted with the call stack, the return of a terminal that pops exactly one frame and continues at the calling state's transition in the same transaction (an outcome routed twice by the result guard that holds over the frame's result record), and the step-out that unwinds exactly one frame which the caller routes once (a caller that is itself a sub-workflow to a state of its own that continues the unwinding) are documented in link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-engine.adoc#_call_stack_and_exit_contracts[Workflow Engine Developer Guide § Call Stack and Exit Contracts]. The phase a call carries is the phase sub-workflow on the stack, never a member of the frame (link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-engine.adoc#_the_phase_of_a_scope[§ The Phase of a Scope]). The compiled invariants that cross a phase boundary are decided over the call-resolved graph of the composed version: a `call` edge enters the callee's named entry and a terminal of the callee returns to the caller's transition of its outcome, so the guarantee the merged plan unit stated holds over the calls, and `INV-GEN-4` is not weakened (link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-verify.adoc#_compiled_invariants[Workflow Verification Developer Guide § Compiled Invariants]; link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/adr/workflow-verify/033-invariants-over-the-call-resolved-graph.adoc[ADR workflow-verify 033]). In addition:

1. *Sub-Workflow Invocation (`call`)*: A call enters the callee through the entry it names, or the callee's one entry, records that entry in the frame (`call_stack[].entry`), and binds its arguments with their provenance (link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-engine.adoc#_entering_a_callee_through_its_entry[Workflow Engine Developer Guide § Entering a Callee Through Its Entry], link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/developer/workflow-engine.adoc#_the_input_bind[§ The Input Bind at a Call]). `callerWorkflowId` is the id of the caller's definition unit: `plan` for a phase call of the plan unit, the phase's id for a call made within a phase (xref:02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#workflow-definition-units[Workflow Definition Units]). `paramBindings` are the effective values the plan instance binds at the call site, whose provenance (template file, composition rule, or waiver) the instance header records (xref:09-plan-instance.adoc#plan-instance[Plan Instance]). Because the phase is derived from the phase sub-workflow on the stack (xref:01-grammar.adoc#state-phase[Phase of a State]), phase-scoped guards (such as `in_worktree`), phase metric accounting (`metrics.json`), and terminal-title updates are the same whichever caller invoked the sub-workflow.
2. *Resting States Inside a Sub-Workflow*: The scope's view and an operator wait's representation for the clients resolve against the active sub-workflow state at the top of `call_stack`, and the tasks a resting state inside a sub-workflow created carry their creating frame.
3. *Sub-Workflow Return (`terminal <outcome_id> <outcome_class>`)*: The result record of `done` is validated against the unit's `result` schema.
4. *Step-Out (`stepped_out`)*: A terminal of class `stepped_out` returns `{by: server, cause}`, whose `cause` is the reason the entering outcome carries (xref:05-primitives.adoc#outcomes-and-reasons[Outcomes and Reasons]), for example `oracle_failed` of `exhausted` and `push_failed` of `retry_spent`, and the entering outcome itself where it carries none (`attempts_exhausted`, `rebase_failed`, `push_state_unknown`, `credential_shaped`, `infeasible`, `lost`); in a sub-workflow a workspace, machine, or entry unit calls, the operator's implicit `step_out (reason)` of an operator wait (xref:02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#implicit-job-links[Implicit Links]) returns like a terminal with the outcome `stepped_out {by: operator, cause: step_out, reason}`. A step-out consumes no budget and writes no waiver; the gap record of a fallback that a caller's routing of a step-out leads to names `cause: stepped_out` with its `step_out_cause` (and the reason of the operator's `step_out`). The engine's unwind of an undeclared `lost` (xref:02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#model-job-states[Task States] item 4) is no step-out a caller routes: its gap names `cause: exhausted` with the task's `queue_task_id` and `task_reason`.
5. *Guided Step-In*: A `call` makes the sub-workflow's first resting state current, and the view shows the call-path breadcrumb (the caller states of every frame). Each task state of a sub-workflow asks for exactly one piece of model judgment, naming its skill section (`skill` clause, Rule 6); it asks only for inputs the server cannot derive and never bundles unrelated inputs. This is a design rule for every sub-workflow, including the remediation units, whose decision task asks only for the remedy and leaves the triage of individual findings to a separate task.

=== Canonical Reusable Sub-Workflow: `git.commit_and_push` (`git-commit-and-push.pmwf`)

The `git.commit_and_push` sub-workflow models the complete git commit-and-push sub-process grounded in `workflow-integration-git`, `phase-5-execute` (Step 10a), and `phase-6-finalize` (Step 3 item 5f, `standards/push.md`, and post-PR re-push). The unit and its states stay in `pm-workflow`; the git operations its primitives invoke are contracts of the provider module `pm-provider-git` (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/module-structure.adoc#module-pm-provider-git[Module Structure, `pm-provider-git`]).

==== Bound Parameter Contract

The sub-workflow frontmatter declares its inputs with `input` and `input_type` and the header its result record with `result` (see the DSL definition below); every `call git.commit_and_push (…)` site must bind each required input exactly once with a declared value, and may omit an input that carries a default, a fallback or the optional form (Rule 6 in xref:04-ast-node-model.adoc#static-verification-rules[Static AST Verification Rules (Build Time & Every Unit Load)]).

[cols="1,2,3"]
|===
|Parameter |Allowed Values |Semantics

|`message_mode`
|`auto_deliverable` \| `step_or_fallback` \| `interactive` \| `none_assert_clean`
|Controls commit message sourcing: `auto_deliverable` formats `feat(<module>): complete deliverable <N> - <title>` from the active deliverable (*a `PM-MCP` choice*: `plan-marshall`'s `phase-5-execute` Step 10a derives the commit message from the task title instead); `step_or_fallback` uses the fixed message of the caller (Stage 3), or the call's bound `message`; `interactive` creates a task of the role `commit-message` in `git.compose-message` with diff analysis facts; `none_assert_clean` expects a clean working tree (used by `finalize.push-barrier`) and routes any unexpected dirty files through `git.compose-message` before pushing.

|`push_mode`
|`never` \| `if_push_remote` \| `always` \| `post_pr_conditional`
|Controls push execution after commit (or on a clean tree): `never` stops after local commit; `if_push_remote` pushes when the project has a push remote (derived fact `push_remote`, link:../store-schemas/09-derived-facts-and-build-timings.adoc#derived-facts[Store Schemas Specification, Derived Facts Store]; a `stale` or `failed` fact is `indeterminate`, never "no remote", and raises `precondition_unmet` in `git.evaluate-push-policy`); `always` pushes unconditionally; `post_pr_conditional` pushes if and only if the order of the persisted `settle_trigger` is greater than `20` (after `create-pr`) and a push remote exists. `settle_trigger {step_id, order}` is written by the transition entering `finalize.settle-commit`: the step whose firing is open, or order `0` when the caller is not a step (`execute.exit-barrier`, `land.exit-barrier`, `finalize.adopt-pr`), so such a caller never pushes. No configuration key suppresses a push (`plan-marshall`'s local-only setting `commit_and_push: false` has no counterpart).

|`push_target`
|`branch` (default) \| `base`
|Where the push goes: `branch` pushes the scope's branch (Stage 6); `base` lands the change on the base branch as one commit, pushed onto `origin/<base_branch>` with the explicit lease of the observed base commit (Stage 6, *Push onto the base*). Every landing onto the base branch of the landing variant `landing.main` binds `base` (<<_call_site_reuse_matrix_across_workflows>>).
|===

*Exit contract*: `done` with the result record `commit-result {commit?, pushed, clean, landed}`, where `commit` is the SHA of the commit this call made (absent when none), `pushed` means that the push target holds the call's result after the call (the remote branch holds `HEAD`; with `push_target: base` the remote base branch is observed at the landed commit, and `commit` names that commit) (so a clean tree whose `HEAD` the remote already holds, the branch state `synced`, returns `{pushed: true, clean: true}` without a push), `clean` that the working tree was clean on entry, and `landed` that the plan's branch already landed (its pull request merged and the remote branch deleted, `remote_absent_landed`; then `pushed` is `false`); plus `stepped_out {by: server, cause, reason}`, returned by the server after an exhausted attempt cap of one of the unit's loops (`cause: attempts_exhausted`), a task of the unit other than `commit-message` that closed `infeasible` or `lost` (`cause: infeasible` or `lost`), an exhausted `.gitignore` oracle retry (`cause: oracle_failed`), a commit refused or failed for a reason that raises no condition (`cause: failed`), a rebase that could not start (`cause: rebase_failed`), a push failure other than a rejection whose server-rule retries are spent (`cause: push_failed`), or a remote branch state the unit cannot establish (`cause: push_state_unknown`). Callers branch on the result with result guards (`guard result.pushed`, `guard result.landed`); no caller routes a push-mode-specific outcome.

---
name: Commit and Push
description: Commits the changes of the plan's worktree and pushes its branch, or lands them on the base branch, as the push mode and the push target say.
type: subworkflow
icon: git.commit
entry: commit
entry_description: The worktree holds the changes to commit, or is clean.
exit: done
exit_description: Commit and push are settled; the result record says what was committed, pushed, or landed.
input: message_mode
input_type: enum[auto_deliverable|step_or_fallback|interactive|none_assert_clean]
input: push_mode
input_type: enum[never|if_push_remote|always|post_pr_conditional]
input: push_target
input_type: enum[branch|base]
input_default: branch
input: message
input_type: commit_message[1..2000]?
input: files
input_type: list<path>[1..500]?
reads_config: local-config.commit_trailer
reads_config: local-config.commit_identity
reads_fact: push_remote
reads_fact: agent_identity
modification_date: 2026-10-14
version: 1.0.0
author: plan-marshall
---
workflow git.commit_and_push version 1 format 1
scope subworkflow
result schema[commit-result]
entry commit -> git.inspect-worktree

state git.inspect-worktree
  auto git.inspect-worktree
    dirty                   -> git.detect-artifacts
    clean_skip_push         -> git.done
    clean_needs_push_check  -> git.evaluate-push-policy

state git.detect-artifacts
  auto git.detect-artifacts
    clean_or_safe_deleted   -> git.resolve-message
    uncertain_or_prohibited -> git.artifact-review
    oracle_failed           -> git.artifact-escalate

state git.artifact-escalate
  auto git.retry-oracle                         # bounded re-run of the detection
    clean_or_safe_deleted   -> git.resolve-message
    uncertain_or_prohibited -> git.artifact-review
    exhausted               -> git.stepped-out       # cause oracle_failed

state git.artifact-review
  skill pm/git/artifact-cleanup
  facts git.artifact-candidates
  job llm artifact-review role artifact-review  # submit: confirm_cleanup (action, paths?)
    resolved                -> git.resolve-message
    prohibited_remaining    -> git.artifact-review
    attempts_exhausted      -> git.stepped-out
    infeasible              -> git.stepped-out
    lost                    -> git.stepped-out

state git.resolve-message
  auto git.resolve-message
    ready                   -> git.stage-and-commit
    needs_interactive       -> git.compose-message

state git.compose-message
  skill pm/git/commit-message
  facts git.diff-summary
  job llm commit-message role commit-message    # submit: commit (message)
    valid                   -> git.stage-and-commit
    infeasible              -> git.template-message
    lost                    -> git.template-message

state git.template-message
  auto git.template-message                     # the caller step's template message from git.diff-summary
    ready                   -> git.stage-and-commit

state git.stage-and-commit
  auto git.stage-and-commit
    committed               -> git.evaluate-push-policy
    nothing_to_commit       -> git.evaluate-push-policy
    hook_failed             -> git.commit-fix       # attempt cap left
    attempts_exhausted      -> git.stepped-out
    failed                  -> git.stepped-out      # cause failed

state git.commit-fix
  skill pm/git/commit-recovery
  facts git.hook-failure
  job llm commit-fix role implement             # fixes the hook's findings; submit: fixed (message?)
    fixed                   -> git.stage-and-commit # re-runs the footprint check and the commit
    infeasible              -> git.stepped-out
    lost                    -> git.stepped-out

state git.evaluate-push-policy
  auto git.evaluate-push-policy
    skip_push               -> git.done
    push_required           -> git.verify-freshness

state git.verify-freshness
  auto git.pre-commit-verify-freshness
    fresh_or_exempt         -> git.check-branch-parity
    stale_or_undecidable    -> git.freshness-blocked

state git.freshness-blocked
  facts git.freshness-status
  auto build.enqueue-verification               # server: a verification build, capped per call
    started                 -> git.freshness-build-running
    attempts_exhausted      -> git.stepped-out

state git.freshness-build-running
  job build.verify-worktree
    succeeded               -> git.verify-freshness
    failed                  -> git.freshness-blocked
    failed_unparsed         -> git.freshness-blocked
    refused                 -> git.freshness-blocked
    cancelled               -> git.freshness-blocked
    timed_out               -> git.freshness-blocked
    killed                  -> git.freshness-blocked
    indeterminate           -> git.freshness-blocked

state git.check-branch-parity
  auto git.push-barrier-decision
    first_push              -> git.execute-push
    refire                  -> git.execute-push
    rewritten_by_rebase     -> git.push-with-lease
    synced                  -> git.done        # pushed: true
    landed                  -> git.done        # pushed: false, landed: true
    push_state_unknown      -> git.stepped-out
    credential_shaped       -> git.stepped-out # an open credential-shaped hit without a waiver

state git.execute-push
  auto git.push
    pushed                  -> git.done
    up_to_date              -> git.done
    rejected_non_ff         -> git.sync-required
    rejected_lease          -> git.sync-required
    push_failed             -> git.push-retry-wait   # server-rule retry budget left
    retry_spent             -> git.stepped-out       # cause push_failed

state git.push-with-lease
  auto git.push-with-lease
    pushed                  -> git.done
    up_to_date              -> git.done
    rejected_non_ff         -> git.sync-required
    rejected_lease          -> git.sync-required
    push_failed             -> git.push-retry-wait   # server-rule retry budget left
    retry_spent             -> git.stepped-out       # cause push_failed

state git.push-retry-wait
  composite_job git.push-retry-wait             # waits the server retry backoff (TIME)
    retry                   -> git.check-branch-parity
    cancelled               -> git.check-branch-parity

state git.sync-required
  facts git.branch-divergence
  auto git.rebase-onto-upstream                 # server: fetch and rebase, capped per call over push rejections
    rebased                 -> git.verify-freshness
    conflict                -> git.resolve-conflicts
    rebase_failed           -> git.stepped-out
    attempts_exhausted      -> git.stepped-out

state git.resolve-conflicts
  skill pm/git/sync-recovery
  facts git.rebase-conflict
  job llm conflict role implement               # resolves the conflicted files; submit: resolved (summary)
    resolved                -> git.continue-rebase
    infeasible              -> git.stepped-out  # the server aborts the rebase first
    lost                    -> git.stepped-out

state git.continue-rebase
  auto git.rebase-continue                      # continues the rebase with the resolved files
    rebased                 -> git.verify-freshness
    conflict                -> git.resolve-conflicts    # attempt cap left
    attempts_exhausted      -> git.stepped-out          # the server aborts the rebase first

state git.done terminal done success

state git.stepped-out terminal stepped_out stepped_out
----

NOTE: The sub-workflow has no operator escalation, no override state, and no link state: the server moves it, and a model acts in it only through the tasks of its four task states. The `.gitignore` oracle is retried by the server: `git.artifact-escalate` re-runs the detection a compiled number of times and then steps out (`cause: oracle_failed`); nothing is ever staged past an unclassified path. A stale or undecidable freshness verdict is never pushed: `git.freshness-blocked` runs a verification build by itself, capped per call. A repository hook failure is never bypassed: `git.commit-fix` is a task of the role `implement` (the plan's writer role), whose worker fixes what the hook reported in the checkout and submits `fixed`, optionally with a recomposed message, and `git.stage-and-commit` re-runs the footprint check and the commit; `git.stage-and-commit` returns `attempts_exhausted` once the failed commit attempts on an identical staged change set (the staged tree id and the message) reach their cap (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#commit-fix-change-set-cap[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, `git.commit-fix` Change-Set Cap]); the counter resets when the staged tree or the message changes (a fix, a hook that rewrote files, a recomposed message), and the cap of `git.commit-fix` tasks per call (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#commit-attempt-caps[Commit Sub-Process Attempt Caps]) bounds the loop however the change set moves. Hooks run only in the CLI variant, so only that variant reaches `git.commit-fix`. `attempts_exhausted` enters `git.stepped-out`, and so does a rebase that could not start (the fetch or the rebase start failed: `rebase_failed`, server step-out with `cause: rebase_failed`); there is no escalation state for it. Every other loop of the unit is capped as well, each by its own compiled attempt cap per call (link:../timeouts.adoc[Timeouts & Budgets Specification]): the artifact review while prohibited paths remain, the verification build while the freshness verdict stays stale, the rebase of `git.sync-required` (the loop `git.sync-required` -> push -> rejection), and the conflict resolution while `git.continue-rebase` meets conflicts; the primitive returns `attempts_exhausted` once the cap is reached, which enters `git.stepped-out`. A rebase conflict is model work: `git.resolve-conflicts` is a task of the role `implement`, whose worker resolves the conflicted files in the checkout and submits `resolved`, and the server continues the rebase in `git.continue-rebase`. A task of `artifact-review` or `implement` that closes `infeasible` or `lost` (its role unqualified or without a startable worker, or its re-offers spent) steps out of the unit (`cause: infeasible` or `lost`), the unit's conservative fallback; a task of `commit-message` that closes `infeasible` or `lost` takes the template message of the caller's step (`git.template-message`) instead. Before any step-out leaves an unfinished rebase, the server aborts it and restores the pre-rebase branch. The only forced push is `git.push-with-lease`, entered automatically when the parity decision finds that a server rebase rewrote the plan's own, already pushed branch (Stage 6); every other force push is refused.

Every task state (`git.artifact-review` of the role `artifact-review`, `git.compose-message` of the role `commit-message`, `git.commit-fix` and `git.resolve-conflicts` of the role `implement`) asks for one judgment through its task, naming its skill section (`pm/git/artifact-cleanup`, `pm/git/commit-message`, `pm/git/commit-recovery`, `pm/git/sync-recovery`). The unit is the same in a plan, an epic, and the workspace setup, so it routes every `infeasible` and `lost` explicitly (Rule 2). The states are catalogued in link:../state-catalogue/05-git-commit-and-push.adoc#state-catalogue-git[State Catalogue, Sub-Workflow `git.commit_and_push`]. `stepped_out` never ends the plan, the epic, or the workspace run by itself: the caller routes it (Rule 6, <<_call_site_reuse_matrix_across_workflows>>).

==== Six-Stage Execution Contract of `git.commit_and_push`
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-6[PM-WATCH-DSL-6], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-7[PM-WATCH-DSL-7], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-10[PM-WATCH-DSL-10], link:../../implementation-watch/store-schemas.adoc#PM-WATCH-STORE-9[PM-WATCH-STORE-9], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-73[PM-WATCH-PHASE-73], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-74[PM-WATCH-PHASE-74], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-24[PM-WATCH-DSL-24], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-25[PM-WATCH-DSL-25], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-33[PM-WATCH-DSL-33], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-41[PM-WATCH-DSL-41], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-42[PM-WATCH-DSL-42], link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-46[PM-WATCH-DSL-46]_

* *Stage 1 — Working-Tree Inspection (`git.inspect-worktree`)*:
** Resolves the target checkout path from the locked scope context: `worktree_path` of a plan while it is set, the main checkout (the enrolled `project_root`) for a plan whose `worktree_path` is `null` and for `workspace`, and the shared epic worktree (link:../filesystem-layout/01-repository-workspace.adoc#shared-epic-worktree[Filesystem Layout Specification]) when `epic`. Git-tracked epic state is never committed on the primary checkout (`PM-ARCH-5`).
** Executes `git -C <tree> status --porcelain` (ignoring `.marshall/local/**`).
** If uncommitted changes exist (`staged`, `unstaged`, or `untracked`), returns `dirty -> git.detect-artifacts`.
** If the working tree is clean: returns `clean_skip_push -> git.done` (result `{pushed: false, clean: true}`) when `push_mode == never`; otherwise returns `clean_needs_push_check -> git.evaluate-push-policy` so clean-tree push barriers (`finalize.push-barrier` or post-rebase pushes) proceed directly to the freshness and parity checks without creating an empty commit.
* *Stage 2 — `.gitignore`-Aware Artifact Detection & Cleanup (`git.detect-artifacts`)*:
** Scans the working tree against the artifact patterns, a compiled table of `pm-core` (a classpath resource, never a file of the project or of `<PM_MCP_BASE>`: `*.temp`, `*.backup*`, `*.orig`, `*.swp`, `.DS_Store`, `Thumbs.db`, coverage/build scratch files) while querying `git -C <tree> check-ignore`.
** Fails closed (`oracle_failed -> git.artifact-escalate`) if the `.gitignore` oracle cannot be evaluated: `git.retry-oracle` re-runs the detection immediately inside the primitive, within the compiled server-rule retry budget (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#server-rule-retry[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Server-Rule Retry Budget]; no backoff) and, when the oracle still fails, ends the call with the server step-out `stepped_out {by: server, cause: oracle_failed}`. There is no override: a path the oracle could not classify is never staged.
** Unconditionally excludes `.marshall/**` and any nested git worktrees or submodules (`git submodule status` / `.git` file markers) from deletion.
** Automatically deletes `safe` untracked artifact files and records `artifacts_deleted` in the audit log.
** Transitions to `git.artifact-review` only for paths in the change set of this commit (porcelain entries: staged, unstaged, untracked) that match artifact patterns and are `uncertain`, or for prohibited secret files (`.env*`, `*.pem`, `*.key`, `credentials.json`) in that change set. Unchanged tracked files are never candidates; unchanged tracked files that match artifact patterns are recorded only as a count (`tracked_pattern_matches`) in the audit record, never rendered, since no link acts on them.
* *Stage 3 — In-Memory Commit Message Resolution, Validation & Trailer Injection (`git.resolve-message` / `git.compose-message`)*:
** When `message_mode` is `auto_deliverable` or `step_or_fallback`, resolves the deterministic commit message in memory (`git.resolve-message`, link:../../../pm-providers/pm-provider-git/doc/developer/provider-git-primitive.adoc#_the_message_primitives[Commit Primitives Developer Guide § The Message Primitives]). Under `step_or_fallback` it is the fixed message of the caller, one per call site of the matrix (<<_call_site_reuse_matrix_across_workflows>>):
*** `finalize.settle-commit`: `chore(finalize): apply <step_id> changes`, the `step_id` of the persisted `settle_trigger`; for a trigger of order `0` (no step) `chore(finalize): settle changes`.
*** `finalize.land-main`: the plan's pull-request title (`status.metadata.pr_title`) as the subject and the server sections as the body, as a squash merge would carry them (link:../phase-workflows/06-finalize-re-entry-and-merge.adoc#landing-variants[Phase Workflows Specification, Landing Variants]).
*** `epic.commit-ledger`: `chore(orchestrator): update <epic_id> ledger`; while the epic archives `chore(orchestrator): archive <epic_id> ledger`.
*** `decomposition.commit`: `chore(orchestrator): record <epic_id> decomposition`.
*** `git.land-removal`: `chore(land): remove documents moved into a source directory`.
*** `git.land-unit`: `chore(land): place <unit_id>` or `chore(land): remove <unit_id>`.
*** `git.land-setup`: `chore(land): apply project setup changes`.
*** `finalize.hand-back`, settling uncommitted work: `chore(finalize): settle changes at hand-back`.
*** The ADR renumbering of the landing (`finalize.merge-sync`, `finalize.land-main`, `finalize.land-files`; link:../phase-workflows/06-finalize-re-entry-and-merge.adoc#adr-number-at-landing[Phase Workflows Specification, ADR Number at Landing]): `chore(finalize): renumber ADRs against <base_branch>`.
** No step supplies a message of its own. A call site may bind `message` from a slot of its unit (`message = slot.<name>`), which then replaces the fixed message; no call site of the matrix binds it. The value is validated against the slot's type where it is filled: a `commit_message` slot refuses a text that is no Conventional Commit or carries a `Co-Authored-By:` line (the rule `input.commit-message`) with `invalid_link_args`, and the submission or the act that carried it is not accepted (xref:04-ast-node-model.adoc#static-verification-rules[Rule 6]). The only trailer in the commit is the one the server appends here.
** When `message_mode == interactive` (or `none_assert_clean` when unexpected dirty files were found), transitions to `git.compose-message`, whose task carries `facts` from `git.diff-summary` (changed file count, module scope, priority-ordered suggested type `fix > feat > perf > refactor > docs > style > test > chore > ci`) and whose submit carries `message: text[1..2000]` directly in `pm_do` `args`.
** Validates the message as a Conventional Commit and appends exactly one trailer `Co-Authored-By: {name} <{email}>`, resolved per half from the operator's `commit_trailer` (link:../store-schemas/08-project-configuration-local.adoc#local-config[Store Schemas Specification, Operator Local Configuration]), then `machine-config.json` `project_defaults.commit_trailer`, then the compiled default `plan-marshall <noreply@cuioss.de>`; no team file is consulted, a `Co-Authored-By:` line in a model-supplied `message` is rejected (`invalid_link_args`, `detail` names the line), and the commit's audit record carries `trailer_source: {name, email}`, never rendered in `facts` (link:../../../pm-providers/pm-provider-git/doc/developer/provider-git-primitive.adoc#_the_commit_message_and_the_trailer[Commit Primitives Developer Guide § The Commit Message and the Trailer]). A `commit_message` body carries no control character, a tab included (the rule `input.commit-control`): a line break is the only control character the type admits (link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/adr/workflow-input/006-path-and-commit-body-rules-stated.adoc[ADR workflow-input/006]).
** The trailer names the system that produced the commit; it is independent of the commit's author and committer (Stage 4), which are never taken from the message.
* *Stage 4 — Staging, `stdin`-Piped Commit & Change-Ledger Stamping (`git.stage-and-commit`)*:
** *Footprint check first*: Before anything is staged, the primitive runs the footprint check for every caller (plan, epic, and workspace scope; <<_call_site_reuse_matrix_across_workflows>>), so no caller reaches a commit around it: (1) no dirty path of the target checkout lies outside the staging set derived below, and for a plan none outside its worktree; (2) no enrolled wrapper's `pinned_files` (link:../job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#_verify_not_resolve_execution_pipeline[Job Runtime Specification, Verify-Not-Resolve]) changed in the target checkout; (3) for a plan scope, no main-checkout path outside `.marshall/local/` is absent from `entry_baseline` or changed since it (`mcp/status.json`, link:../store-schemas.adoc[Store Schemas Specification]); for a `local_changes` plan this is the `entry_baseline` re-recorded by `land.worktree-init` after the capture restored the main checkout's captured paths, so the captured changes and their restoration never count as a violation (link:../phase-workflows/01-planning-phases-1-2.adoc#_plan_source_contracts[Phase Workflows Specification, Plan Source Contracts]). A violation raises the `footprint_violation` condition on `git.stage-and-commit` (xref:04-ast-node-model.adoc#condition-overlays[Condition Overlays (Engine-Owned)]) naming the offending files, and no commit is made; the condition's bounded self-remedy (in a plan, a task of the plan's writer role that reverts the offending changes; otherwise a re-check after the cause is removed) runs the check again (link:../../../pm-providers/pm-provider-git/doc/developer/provider-git-primitive.adoc#_the_staging_set_and_the_footprint_check[Commit Primitives Developer Guide § The Staging Set and the Footprint Check]).
** Derives the staging set from the scope, never from whatever the checkout holds: a plan's dirty paths inside its worktree, an epic's `.marshall/epics/**` and, while it archives (its archive flag, set by `epic.archive`), `.marshall/archived-epics/**`, and the workspace setup's `.marshall/config/**`, `.gitignore` and `.marshall/project-architecture/**`; a dirty path outside that set is never staged. A plan's paths beyond the declared footprint are unplanned changes, staged and recorded with their provenance (the task or the loop-back) for the PR body and the landing record.
** A bound `files` narrows the COMMIT set to a subset of that staging set, never the footprint check, which keeps judging the whole set: a dirty path outside `files` but inside the scope's set is left unstaged and is no violation, and a dirty path outside the scope's set stays a violation. Absent `files` means every path of the staging set. A list of no entry is outside the input's type (`list<path>[1..500]`, the rule `input.count`) and is refused where the slot is filled: it never means every change. A bound path outside the staging set is refused at the bind (`input_refused`, the rule `path.staging`, or `path.confinement` for a path outside the checkout), a check only the bind can make. The syntactic rules of a path are those of a literal staging: a segment may not begin or end with a space or end with a dot (`path.edge-space`, `path.trailing-dot`), because a filesystem that folds either would reach another name (`.git ` reads as `.git` on NTFS), while the characters `*`, `?` and `[` are admitted, because the staging names the path literally (link:../../../pm-workflow/doc/adr/workflow-input/006-path-and-commit-body-rules-stated.adoc[ADR workflow-input/006]).
** Stages the scoped files literally and invokes `git -C <tree> commit -F -`. Every path, bound or derived, is data and never a pathspec: the CLI variant runs `git -C <tree> --literal-pathspecs add --pathspec-from-file=- --pathspec-file-nul` with the paths NUL-separated on `stdin`, the native variant stages each path as an exact path. No character of a path is read as pathspec magic or as a glob, so a dirty file named `:(glob)**`, `-rf` or `app/[slug]/page.tsx` is staged as that file, and the staging refuses no path for the characters `*`, `?` or `[` (link:../../implementation-watch/workflow-dsl.adoc#PM-WATCH-DSL-41[PM-WATCH-DSL-41]). The commit streams the validated UTF-8 commit message + trailer directly to child `stdin` in memory (`PM-EXEC-5`) with zero `.plan/temp/` files.
** *Author and committer*: The commit's author is the operator's `commit_identity{name, email}` (link:../store-schemas/08-project-configuration-local.adoc#local-config[Store Schemas Specification, Operator Local Configuration]), captured in project setup; the committer is the agent identity resolved from the provider credential (the derived fact `agent_identity`: for a GitHub App its `<app-slug>[bot]` noreply address, for a token the token's user), else the compiled identity `plan-marshall-mcp <noreply@cuioss.de>`; the operator's own git `user.*` configuration is never read at commit time (link:../../../pm-providers/pm-provider-git/doc/developer/provider-git-primitive.adoc#_git_stage_and_commit[Commit Primitives Developer Guide § `git.stage-and-commit`]). Both variants produce identical metadata: the CLI variant passes the author as `-c user.name=… -c user.email=…` and the committer through `GIT_COMMITTER_NAME` and `GIT_COMMITTER_EMAIL`. Author and committer are recorded in the commit's audit record and never rendered in `facts` (link:../hypermedia-format/01-representation-and-link-forms.adoc#model-facing-minimality[Hypermedia Format Specification, Model-Facing Minimality]).
** Any other failure of the commit — a message the rules refuse (`message_refused`) or a commit the git contract could not make (`commit_failed`) — is the outcome `failed` with its reason, which raises no condition and enters `git.stepped-out` (`cause: failed`); the reason `footprint_violation` of the same outcome raises the condition above (xref:05-primitives.adoc#outcomes-and-reasons[Outcomes and Reasons]).
** Captures `commit_sha` (`git -C <tree> rev-parse HEAD`) and git-sourced `changed_paths` (`git -C <tree> diff-tree --no-commit-id --name-only -r <commit_sha>`), appending a `kind = "change"` entry to `change-ledger.jsonl`.
** When invoked from a Phase 6 `mutates_source: true` step: re-stamps `head_at_completion = commit_sha` on `head_dependent: true` steps in `mcp/status.json`, and when the commit follows the latest successful build (any such step, not only steps at `order >= 10`, `pre-push-quality-gate.order`; for example `architecture-refresh` at `9` on re-entry; link:../phase-workflows/05-finalize-review-metrics-descriptor.adoc#_finalize_commit_instrumentation_freshness_reconcile_post_pr_re_push_pm_wf_16[Phase Workflows Specification, Finalize Commit Instrumentation] item 3), writes a `freshness_reconcile` record (`commit_sha`, `step_id`, `prior_build_worktree_sha` from the latest passing build row in `change-ledger.jsonl`) so the downstream push gate recognizes the finalize-internal commit without requiring a redundant full build when no source-affecting inputs changed.
** If `git commit` exits non-zero due to a repository pre-commit hook, returns `hook_failed -> git.commit-fix`.
* *Stage 5 — Config-Driven Push Policy & Freshness Precondition (`git.evaluate-push-policy` -> `git.verify-freshness`)*:
** Evaluates `push_mode` together with the derived fact `push_remote`; a `stale` or `failed` fact is never read as "no remote": the state raises the `precondition_unmet` condition (xref:04-ast-node-model.adoc#condition-overlays[Condition Overlays]) naming the fact; the server re-derives and re-evaluates it within the condition's self-remedy budget, after which the phase's fallback is taken. Otherwise: `push_mode == never` skips push (`skip_push -> git.done`); `push_mode == if_push_remote` requires push if and only if a push remote exists; `push_mode == post_pr_conditional` requires push if and only if a push remote exists and the persisted `settle_trigger` has `order > 20` (after `create-pr`), automating post-PR re-push for late source-mutating steps (`adr-propose`, in-branch fixes); `push_mode == always` requires push unconditionally.
** Before any push, `git.pre-commit-verify-freshness` verifies `change-ledger.jsonl` freshness (`fresh` corroborated on both notation and scope dimensions against the current `worktree_sha`, computed by the git contract operation `git.worktree-sha`, one node for both variants that returns the digest and its `worktree_sha_version`, `exempt` when `build-decision == not_necessary`, a `baseline_red` row of the same `worktree_sha` and hash version (link:../store-schemas/04-epic-and-change-ledger.adoc#_change_ledger_record_change_ledger_jsonl[Store Schemas Specification, Change Ledger Record], Baseline-Red Rows), or reconciled via `freshness_reconcile`). If `stale` or `undecidable`, transitions to `git.freshness-blocked`, which runs a verification build of the server; a stale tree is never pushed, and there is no override.
** When no reconciliation record names the live `HEAD`, `git.freshness-status` reports `stale_reason: reconciliation_record_absent` together with the causes that absence admits (`admitted_causes` ⊆ `source_drift_unbuilt`, `no_successful_build`, `record_owed_not_emitted`, `commit_outside_reconcile_membership`). The refusal reports what it observed and never asserts a single inferred cause; the fail-closed halt is unchanged.
** `git.freshness-blocked` records in its facts, beside `stale_reason`, the evidence it would accept, which the scope's view shows: the required build notation(s) and the scope (`whole_tree` or a module list); the current `worktree_sha` is recorded in the audit record, not rendered.
* *Stage 6 — Branch Sync Parity Probe & Push Execution (`git.check-branch-parity` -> `git.execute-push`)*:
** `git.push-barrier-decision` first runs, for a plan's call, the credential-shaped check of the added-line scan over the commits `base_ref..HEAD` and their messages (link:../phase-workflows/05-finalize-review-metrics-descriptor.adoc#added-line-scan[Phase Workflows Specification, Added-Line Scan], item 4): an open hit without a waiver refuses every push and ends the call with the server step-out `cause: credential_shaped`. It then reads the persisted fact `branch_pushed_at` in `mcp/status.json`. When it is unset, the branch has never been pushed by this plan and the decision is `first_push` (`git -C <tree> push --set-upstream origin <branch>`) without consulting the probe. When it is set (re-entry), the decision follows the push-barrier mapping: it runs `git.branch-sync-state` and returns `refire` only for `ahead`, and `rewritten_by_rebase` (a *PM-MCP* addition) when the plan's recorded last push (`branch_pushed_at`) is no ancestor of `HEAD` because a server rebase (`sync-baseline` or the merge-path sync) rewrote the branch; `synced` returns `synced`, which ends the call with `done` (`pushed: true`, the remote branch holds `HEAD`); `remote_absent_landed` returns `landed`, which ends the call with `done` (`pushed: false`, `landed: true`); `remote_absent_unverified` and any unrecognised state return `push_state_unknown`, a server step-out (`cause: push_state_unknown`) that the caller routes to its fallback. A never-pushed branch and a squash-merged-and-deleted branch are indistinguishable locally, so `remote_absent_unverified` is resolved with provider evidence before it is classified: `ci.pr-find` by the head branch finding a merged pull request turns it into `remote_absent_landed` (`landed`, re-pushing would resurrect a landed branch); finding no pull request at all turns it into `never_pushed`, which pushes (`refire`); any other observation (an open or closed-unmerged pull request, or a provider that cannot be observed) keeps `remote_absent_unverified`, which never pushes and steps out. *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: `plan-marshall` never pushes on `remote_absent_unverified`, consults no provider evidence, and reports every skipped state as pushed.
** `git.branch-sync-state` classifies the plan's recorded `branch_name` (`mcp/status.json`), never the ambient `HEAD` of the checkout, and fetches the remote ref before classifying. A classification made without a fetch in the same transaction is labelled unverified (as `remote_absent_unverified` already is) and is never used to claim that the branch is unpushed. *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: its `branch-sync-state` is a read-only comparison against the local remote-tracking ref and never fetches.
** *Push onto the base* (`push_target: base`): the parity probe and `branch_pushed_at` do not apply. The server fetches `origin/<base_branch>` and records its commit as the lease; a base that is no ancestor of `HEAD` goes to `git.sync-required` first, which rebases onto it. It then builds one commit with the tree of `HEAD`, the observed base commit as its only parent, and the call's resolved message and trailer (Stage 3) and author and committer (Stage 4), and pushes it onto `refs/heads/<base_branch>` naming the observed base commit as the explicit lease, never as a force push. A `rejected_lease{remote_sha}` (the base moved meanwhile) returns to `git.sync-required` within the same budgets as a rejected branch push; a push the provider refuses (a protected base branch, a missing permission) is a `push_failed` as below. The call ends `done` with `pushed: true` only once the remote base branch is observed at the landed commit.
** `git.execute-push` (and `git.push-with-lease`) sets `branch_pushed_at` (timestamp and pushed `HEAD`) in `mcp/status.json` on the first successful push and refreshes it on every later successful push, so it always names the last pushed commit. A push answered `up_to_date` pushed nothing and ends the call with `done` (`pushed: true`).
** If `git push` is rejected as non-fast-forward (`rejected_non_ff{remote_sha}`), transitions to `git.sync-required`, where the server rebases onto the fetched upstream; a conflict enters `git.resolve-conflicts` (a task of the role `implement`, never an operator decision).
** Any other push failure (`push_failed`: the transport, the authentication, or the remote refused without a rejection of the ref update) is a bounded server rule: while the server-rule retry budget is left (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#server-rule-retry[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Server-Rule Retry Budget]), `git.push-retry-wait` waits the server retry backoff (`TIME`) and returns to `git.check-branch-parity`, which re-observes the remote before it pushes again; the spent budget (`retry_spent`) enters `git.stepped-out` with `cause: push_failed`.
** *Explicit push lease*: The only forced push is `git.push-with-lease`, a server rule rather than a decision: it is entered automatically when the parity decision returns `rewritten_by_rebase`, pushes only the plan's own branch, and always names its lease explicitly as the expected remote SHA, in both variants: the `remote_sha` observed at the last rejection, else the SHA of the last recorded push (`branch_pushed_at`). The local remote-tracking ref is never the lease, because a fetch between observation and push moves it and turns the lease into a plain force. A `rejected_lease{remote_sha}` (the remote moved meanwhile) returns to `git.sync-required`; any other force push is refused. Before every retry of a rejected push, the remote branch is re-read (`ls-remote`) and the retry uses the re-read SHA. *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: `plan-marshall` pushes `--force-with-lease` without an expected value, taking the lease from the tracking ref.

==== Call-Site Reuse Matrix Across Workflows

Every call site routes the exit contract `done` / `stepped_out` (Rule 6); a site whose continuation depends on the result branches with a result guard.

A call site that replaces its fixed commit message (Stage 3) binds `message = slot.<name>` beside `message_mode` and `push_mode`; a call site that commits only part of its staging set binds `files = slot.files`. Both slots are declared and filled in the caller's unit. The rule applies to every row below. No call site in the table binds `message` or `files`.

The epic ledger (`epic.commit-ledger`, `decomposition.commit`) and the short landings, the project setup's changes among them (`git.land-setup`), reach the base branch through the project's landing variant (xref:09-plan-instance.adoc#plan-axes[Plan Behaviour Axes]): with `landing.pull-request` through a branch and its pull request, with `landing.main` by a commit pushed onto the base branch (`push_target: base`), and with `landing.files` they stay uncommitted in the working tree.

[#short-landings]
=== Short Landings (`git.land-removal`, `git.land-unit`, `git.land-setup`)
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16: Reusable Compound Sub-Workflows & Config-Driven Git Commit/Push]_

_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-22[PM-WF-22: Designing a Process]_

A *short landing* lands one small change of a project that no plan makes: `git.land-removal` the removal from git of documents the operator chose to move into a start's source directory (link:../state-catalogue/09-bootstrap-and-lessons.adoc#documents-source[State Catalogue, Documents as a Source]), `git.land-unit` a workflow unit placed or removed at project level (xref:04-ast-node-model.adoc#process-designer[Designing a Process]), and `git.land-setup` the team configuration a project's setup changed (link:../state-catalogue/06-setup-epic-other.adoc#state-setup.land-changes[State Catalogue, `setup.land-changes`]). All three are compiled-only sub-workflows (type `system`, called) with the input-free entry `land` and the exit `done` with the result record of `git.commit_and_push` (beside the implicit `stepped_out`), and each lands exactly one commit, which stages only the paths of its change, through `git.commit_and_push` and the project's landing variant: with `landing.pull-request` on a branch of its own through a pull request, merged like the epic's ledger; with `landing.main` committed and pushed onto the base branch (`push_target: base`); with `landing.files` nothing is committed and the change stays in the working tree. A short landing runs on after the act that started it (the source stored, the unit placed, the setup's `land`); until it has landed it shows in the project's running work, and a landing that failed waits for the operator as the hold `landing_failed` (link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#holds[State Catalogue, Holds and Their Acts]). `git.land-removal` never removes anything but the moved documents, `git.land-unit` never anything but the placed or removed unit, and `git.land-setup` never anything outside its staging set.
