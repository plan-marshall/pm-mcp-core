[#plan-pause]
== Pausing and Starting a Plan
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-23[PM-WF-23: Operator Acts on Plans and Epics]_

A plan that exists always runs until the operator pauses it: starting a plan, by the operator or by its epic, creates and runs it in one act, and an epic never creates a plan to wait. Beside its terminal states a plan's *activity* is one of `running`, `pausing` (running until the point its pause names), and `paused`; it is derived on read from the plan's persisted `pause` record, never stored separately (link:../store-schemas/02-workflow-state-store.adoc#_workflow_state_store_mcpstatus_json[Store Schemas Specification, Workflow State Store]), and shown by both clients, never as a kind of `needs_operator`. An operator wait is expressed by `needs_operator`, a stalled plan by its hold `stalled` (<<stalled-plans>>), and an overdue wait by the hold `overdue` (<<overdue-waits>>), not by the activity (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/runtime-model/01-processes-and-transport.adoc#needs-operator[What Needs the Operator]).

* *Pause*: the action `pause` of the plan (`POST /api/v1/plans/{plan_id}/actions/pause`) with the point where the plan holds, `point`, an operator act of either client:
** *after the current task* (`task`, the default): what runs finishes and its result is kept; the engine starts no new task and no new job and takes no transition beyond the state the plan reaches, and the plan is `paused` once nothing of it runs.
** *after the current phase* (`phase`): the phase sub-workflow on the plan's call stack runs to its return; the plan holds before the plan unit's next call, whatever it calls next, a loop-back to an earlier phase or a re-entry included.
** *now* (`now`): the plan's running tasks, its chat's reply excepted, are withdrawn as on a recycle (their workers ended and fenced, their attempts ended uncounted, the tasks kept for the start, `PM-WORK-2`), its running jobs, the job of its chat's worker excepted, are cancelled by the engine, and a server retry waiting for its backoff stops its clock; the plan is `paused` at once. A plan inside the rebase-to-merge critical section of the merge path holds when it leaves that section, so a pause never leaves a merge half done (link:../phase-workflows/06-finalize-re-entry-and-merge.adoc[Phase Workflows Specification, Finalize Re-Entry & Merge]).
* *What a pause keeps*: the plan's operator waits stay open and can be answered, its holds keep their acts, and its leases stay with it except the slots: a paused plan holds no build or model slot and is offered no task, except its chat, whose worker holds its own slot and whose replies are offered, since a reply changes nothing (link:../state-catalogue.adoc#chat-context[State Catalogue, What a Chat Knows]). A plan of an epic keeps its place in the epic's `parallelization_scope`; to stop new starts of an epic the operator switches its automatic mode off (link:../model-work/08-epic-coordination.adoc#epic-informed[Model Work Specification, The Epic Is Told]). A paused plan is never `stalled` (<<stalled-plans>>).
* *Start*: the action `start` (`POST /api/v1/plans/{plan_id}/actions/start`) resumes a paused plan from its current state: a withdrawn task is offered again, a job state whose job the pause cancelled enters its job again, and a retry backoff runs on from its persisted instant. On a pausing plan it withdraws the pause. Start and pause on a plan whose activity does not offer them are refused `act_not_available`, on an ended plan `plan_ended`.
* *A pause survives a restart*: the pause and its point are part of the plan's state; after a restart of the runtime a paused plan stays paused, and a pausing plan holds at its point (link:01-startup-recovery-drain.adoc#per-project-recovery[Per-Project Recovery]).
* Every pause and start is audited with its channel and announced (`activity.changed`, link:https://github.com/plan-marshall/pm-mcp-clients/blob/main/doc/specification/runtime-model/06-event-catalogue.adoc[Event Catalogue]); the plan's epic is told by its inbox (link:../model-work/08-epic-coordination.adoc#epic-informed[Model Work Specification, The Epic Is Told]). Cancelling and abandoning a plan are specified in link:../state-catalogue/04-plan-finalize-terminal.adoc#plan-end[State Catalogue Specification, Cancelling and Abandoning].

[#liveness-classes]
== Liveness Classes at Run Time
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7: Runtime & Relay Lifecycle, Crash Recovery & Job Drain]_

The verifier proves that every non-terminal position of a scope is engine-progressing, a bounded wait, an operator position, or a resident rest (link:../workflow-dsl/04-ast-node-model.adoc#liveness-guarantee[Workflow DSL Specification, Liveness Guarantee]). At run time the classes tell a scope that waits as designed from one that is stuck:

* *Engine-progressing and bounded waits* move by themselves; a scope that awaits nothing and does not move is `stalled` (<<stalled-plans>>), and a scope whose stall-watched wait passes its bound is `overdue` (<<overdue-waits>>).
* *Operator positions* are shown as waiting for the operator, in `needs_operator`, never as `stalled` or `overdue`.
* *Resident rest* is legitimate idling without a bound: an epic resting in its watch, the workspace, and the machine are never `stalled` and never `overdue`.

Both holds are derived on read, never stored as workflow state, and neither changes the scope's route.

[#stalled-plans]
== Stalled Plans
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/runtime-lifecycle.adoc#PM-WATCH-RUN-2[PM-WATCH-RUN-2], link:../../implementation-watch/runtime-lifecycle.adoc#PM-WATCH-RUN-8[PM-WATCH-RUN-8]_

The runtime continues every plan and epic itself, so a scope that does not move although nothing holds it is a fault of the supervision or of the environment, made visible to the operator, never to a model:

* *Definition*: A non-terminal plan or epic is `stalled` when it is neither paused nor pausing, is in no operator wait and under no hold of another kind, has no pending condition, awaits no event (no running job, no task queued or leased within its budget, no open CI, merge-queue, or review-bot wait, no prerequisite wait), and its last transition or task progress is older than the stalled threshold (link:../timeouts/02-runtime-leases-operator-web.adoc#stalled-threshold[Timeouts & Budgets Specification]). A role the supervisor cannot serve stalls nothing: its tasks close `lost` with `role_unavailable` and their states take the conservative fallback, and the start-failure guard reports the harness's error text to the operator (link:../model-work/03-supervision-and-recycling.adoc#start-failure-guard[Model Work Specification, Start-Failure Guard]). A scope under an integrity hold is held by that hold, not `stalled`. Stalled is derived from persisted state, the supervisor's records, and the audit log on read, never stored as workflow state.
* *A hold*: a stalled scope is shown as the hold `stalled` with its acts (link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#holds[State Catalogue Specification, Holds and Their Acts]); the hold clears by itself with the scope's next transition or task progress, and its event says so.
* *Cause*: Every stalled scope names its cause; the closed cause set is `no_progress` (with the idle span).
* *Visibility*: both clients, `pm-operator status`, and the epic's resource show `stalled` with its cause and count it in `needs_operator`; the runtime announces it on the event streams (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/runtime-model/01-processes-and-transport.adoc#event-streams[Events per Scope]). A scope in an operator wait is shown as waiting for the operator, never as `stalled`.
* *Record*: The first time a scope is found stalled in an episode (an episode ends with the scope's next transition or task progress), the runtime appends one `plan_stalled` audit record with the scope and the cause; metrics count the gap as idle time.
* *Minimality*: `stalled`, its cause, and `plan_stalled` never appear in a model-facing representation (link:../hypermedia-format/01-representation-and-link-forms.adoc#model-facing-minimality[Hypermedia Format Specification, Model-Facing Minimality]).

[#overdue-waits]
== Overdue Waits
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7: Runtime & Relay Lifecycle, Crash Recovery & Job Drain]_

_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/runtime-lifecycle.adoc#PM-WATCH-LIFE-2[PM-WATCH-LIFE-2]_

A wait that has no deadline of its own, the CI wait, is *stall-watched*: it continues until it ends, and two clocks show the operator, never a model, when it has waited far longer than expected: a stall clock that restarts on every observed change of what it awaits, and the wait's own duration, which no change restarts, so a wait whose observed state keeps changing without end is shown too:

* *Definition*: A non-terminal plan or epic is `overdue` when it rests in a stall-watched wait (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#ci-wait[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, CI Wait]) and either no change of what it awaits has been observed for the overdue factor times the expected duration recorded with the wait (link:../timeouts/02-runtime-leases-operator-web.adoc#overdue-factor[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Overdue Factor]), or the wait has lasted the long-wait factor times that expected duration, whatever changed (link:../timeouts/02-runtime-leases-operator-web.adoc#long-wait-factor[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Long-Wait Factor]); time a provider deferred the next observation counts (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#retry-after-cap[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, `Retry-After` Cap]). A paused or pausing plan, a scope in an operator wait, and a scope under an integrity hold are never `overdue`. Overdue is derived on read from the wait's record and its observations, never stored as workflow state.
* *A hold*: an overdue scope is shown as the hold `overdue` with what it awaits and its acts (link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#holds[State Catalogue Specification, Holds and Their Acts]); the hold clears by itself with the scope's next transition, a hold of cause `no_change` also with the next observed change, and its event says so; a hold of cause `long_running` stands until the wait ends or an act ends it. The route is unchanged: the wait continues, nothing is decided by time, and no act is taken by itself.
* *Cause*: Every overdue scope names its cause; the closed cause set is `no_change` (with the span since the last observed change and the expected duration) and `long_running` (with the wait's duration and the expected duration).
* *Visibility*: as for `stalled`: both clients, `pm-operator status`, and the epic's resource show `overdue` with its cause and count it in `needs_operator`; the runtime announces it on the event streams with the events of every hold (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/runtime-model/01-processes-and-transport.adoc#event-streams[Events per Scope]).
* *Record*: The first time a scope is found overdue in an episode (an episode ends when the hold clears), the runtime appends one `plan_overdue` audit record with the scope, the wait, and what it awaits (link:../store-schemas/12-machine-stores.adoc#audit-log[Store Schemas Specification, Security & Transition Audit Log]).
* *Minimality*: `overdue`, its cause, the expected duration, and `plan_overdue` never appear in a model-facing representation (link:../hypermedia-format/01-representation-and-link-forms.adoc#model-facing-minimality[Hypermedia Format Specification, Model-Facing Minimality]).

[#clock-gap]
== Clock Gap
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7: Runtime & Relay Lifecycle, Crash Recovery & Job Drain]_

_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/runtime-lifecycle.adoc#PM-WATCH-LIFE-4[PM-WATCH-LIFE-4]_

The stall clocks count only time the machine was awake. The runtime reads two monotonic clocks, one that counts while the machine sleeps and one that does not (on Linux `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` and `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, on macOS `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` and `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`); when they diverge by more than the clock gap threshold over one observation interval (link:../timeouts/02-runtime-leases-operator-web.adoc#clock-gap-threshold[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Clock Gap Threshold]), the machine slept for the divergence. The runtime keeps the sleep spans it observed and excludes them from the clocks of `stalled`, `overdue`, and the heartbeats (link:01-startup-recovery-drain.adoc#runtime-self-watch[Runtime Self-Watch]), so no scope becomes stalled or overdue through a sleep and a hold already open stays open, and attributes each span as a pause in the wait slices of the metrics (link:../job-runtime/01-pipeline-and-subprocesses.adoc#_bounded_long_poll_progress_reset_waiting_primitives_pm_wf_8_pm_impl_4[Job Runtime Specification, Wait Attribution]). The wall clock is never the signal: an adjustment of it, by the operator or by time synchronisation, changes no stall clock. Deadlines and budgets keep their own rules.

[#pause-stalled-verification]
== Verification

* *Pause tests*: A pause with each point holds where its point names (`phase` also when the phase ends in a loop-back), a pause with `now` withdraws a running task and offers it again on start without spending its re-offer budget, a paused plan holds no slot and is never reported `stalled`, and an answer to an operator wait of a paused plan is recorded and taken on start.
* *Stalled tests*: An aged fixture (a non-terminal plan without pending condition or awaited event whose last transition is older than the stalled threshold) is reported as the hold `stalled` with cause `no_progress` in both clients and on the event stream, produces exactly one `plan_stalled` record per episode, and changes no model-facing representation; a plan in an operator wait is shown as waiting for the operator, and a paused plan by its activity, never as `stalled`.
* *Overdue tests*: A CI wait fixture whose check runs show no change for the overdue factor times the expected duration is reported as the hold `overdue` with cause `no_change` in both clients and on the event stream, produces exactly one `plan_overdue` record per episode, keeps its route, and clears with the next observed change; a provider that keeps deferring with `Retry-After` reaches `overdue` the same way; a CI wait whose check runs keep changing past the long-wait factor times the expected duration is reported `overdue` with cause `long_running` and stays held until the wait ends; matched control: a CI wait whose check runs change within the span and that ends within the long-wait span is never `overdue`.
* *Clock-gap tests*: On test clocks whose sleep-counting clock runs ahead of the other by more than the clock gap threshold, an idle plan and a CI wait become neither `stalled` nor `overdue` through the gap, a CI wait already `overdue` stays so, the span is attributed as a pause, and a deadline due within the gap is processed as its own rule says; matched controls: the same idle span awake reports `stalled`, and a wall-clock jump alone changes no stall clock.
