=== Lifecycle Topology (Phases 1–4)

The planning lifecycle is a server-enforced state machine spanning `phase-1-init`, `phase-2-refine`, `phase-3-outline`, and `phase-4-plan`. Sub-states (open decisions, the Q-Gate remediation budgets, and the refine relaunch counter) are persisted in the plan store (question records in `questions.jsonl`, the per-plan policy counters and the current state in `mcp/status.json`) so that a runtime restart resumes at the exact sub-state; neither a client nor a worker holds plan state. The process a plan runs is its own composed plan instance (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#plan-instance-composition[Plan Instance Composition]); every blocking condition of a planning phase ends in a bounded self-remedy and, when that is spent, in the `returned` fallback (<<plan-outcomes>>). A state that needs the operator (a decision, a waiver request, an option asked where the plan first needs it, a phase transition the plan's asking variant waits at) is an operator wait (link:../workflow-dsl/02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#operator-wait[Workflow DSL Specification, Waiting for the Operator]): it halts this plan only, has no time limit, and ends only by the operator's act.

Every state of Phases 1–4 is catalogued in the State Catalogue (link:../state-catalogue/02-plan-phases-1-3.adoc#state-catalogue-init[`init`], link:../state-catalogue/02-plan-phases-1-3.adoc#state-catalogue-refine[`refine`], link:../state-catalogue/02-plan-phases-1-3.adoc#state-catalogue-outline[`outline`], link:../state-catalogue/03-plan-phases-4-5-land.adoc#state-catalogue-plan[`plan`]; generic per-phase states in link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#state-catalogue-generic[Generic Per-Phase States]; condition overlays in link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#state-catalogue-conditions[Conditions]).

A plan started from the `local_changes` source does not traverse Phases 2–4: it runs in the `land` lane (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#land-lane[Land Lane (`local_changes`)]), whose plan unit calls `init`, then `execute`, within which the `land` sub-workflow runs its verification states, then `finalize`. A plan started from the `pr` source does not traverse Phases 2–5: it runs in the `adopt` lane (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#adopt-lane[Adopt Lane (`pr`)]), whose plan unit calls `init` and then `finalize`, entered at `finalize.adopt-pr`, which adopts the pull request's head branch (<<_plan_source_contracts>>).

A plan in the `light` planning lane does not traverse the Phase 2 states (`refine.baseline-reconcile`, `refine.analyze`, the confidence loop, `refine.qgate`): after the server-side light gate (`init.light-gate`) its plan unit calls `outline`, which runs one collapsed light envelope (`outline.light`) that folds refine without the loop, the Simple outline, and deliverable derivation together, mirroring `plan-marshall`'s light lane. The deep lane keeps the full refine loop -> outline -> plan pipeline. How the light envelope satisfies the Phase 2 and Phase 3 artifact gates is specified in xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#planning-lanes[Planning Lanes & One-Way Ratchet (`light` -> `deep`)].

[#plan-unit-phases]
=== The Plan Unit as a Workflow of Phases

The plan unit is a workflow of phases (link:../store-schemas/03-findings-and-review-records.adoc#phase-sub-workflows[Store Schemas Specification, Phases as Sub-Workflows]): each of the six phases `init`, `refine`, `outline`, `plan`, `execute`, and `finalize` is a sub-workflow the plan unit calls, and the outcome the phase ends with decides the plan unit's next call.

* *Entries and Exits*: Each phase declares its interface in its frontmatter (link:../workflow-dsl/01-grammar.adoc#interface-rules[Workflow DSL Specification, Interface Rules]): an entry for every point at which it is entered (every phase at `start`; `outline` at `light`, the light lane's change-type detection, and again at `rework` and `rework_light`, the authoring entry of each lane after a loop-back; `execute` at `land` for the `land` lane and at `reentry` after finalize; `finalize` at `adopt` (`finalize.adopt-pr`) for the `adopt` lane, at `hand_back`, and at `post_run`) and an exit for every outcome the plan unit continues on differently (for example `refine` ends `ready` or `returned`; `execute` ends `exit`, `loop_back_outline`, or `hand_back`; `finalize` ends `merged`, `no_change`, `handed_back`, `returned`, `reenter_execute`, or `loop_back_outline`). The complete list of entries and exits is the plan unit's catalogue (link:../state-catalogue/18-plan-unit-axes-short-landings.adoc#state-catalogue-plan-unit[State Catalogue Specification, Plan Unit]). No transition crosses from one phase into another: a loop-back is the plan unit calling an earlier phase again at its entry, and a re-entry from finalize into execute is the finalize phase ending with that outcome. The plan unit has neither entries nor exits: it starts at its starting point and ends in its stop points, the plan's terminal states.
* *Lanes Are Choices of Phases*: A lane is the plan unit's choice of the phases it calls, never a phase of its own: `deep` calls all six, `light` calls no `refine` (its refine work runs in the light envelope of `outline`), `land` calls `init`, `execute`, and `finalize`, and `adopt` calls `init` and `finalize`. The ratchet from `light` to `deep` is the plan unit calling `refine` after `outline` ended with the escalation.
* *The Plan's Phase*: A plan's phase (`mcp/status.json` `phase`) is the phase sub-workflow on its `call_stack`; every sub-workflow called within a phase (a remediation unit, `git.commit_and_push`, the re-planning unit, a chat) has that phase, and the records it writes (findings, tasks, re-planning records) carry it. A phase sub-workflow is the first level of the call stack (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-16[PM-WF-16]).
* *Between Phases*: Where the plan's asking variant waits at a phase transition (link:../workflow-dsl/09-plan-instance.adoc#plan-axes[Workflow DSL Specification, Plan Behaviour Axes]), the plan unit enters an operator wait before its next call, showing the ended phase's result; otherwise it calls the next phase at once. A pause the operator set after the current phase holds the plan at this point (link:../runtime-lifecycle/02-plan-pause-and-stalled-plans.adoc#plan-pause[Runtime Lifecycle Specification, Pausing and Starting a Plan]).
* *Composition*: The plan instance composes the plan unit with the phase sub-workflows it calls, each resolved by level (project, machine, compiled default; link:../workflow-dsl/04-ast-node-model.adoc#process-designer[Workflow DSL Specification, Designing a Process]); a phase the plan does not call is a pruned call with its prune reason, so the diagram shows the plan's process phase by phase (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#plan-instance-composition[Plan Instance Composition]).

[#phase-roles]
=== Model Work per State (Role Tasks)

The server drives every phase. A state that needs no model is server logic (`auto`, `do`, `job`); a state that needs one is a `job llm <entity_kind> role <role> [each <primitive>]` state, which creates one task per entity for the named role, done by a worker of that role through the task protocol (link:../model-work/02-task-protocol.adoc[Model Work Specification, Task Protocol]); a state that needs the operator is an operator wait, whose links are operator endpoints of the local API served to the operator's two clients (link:../workflow-dsl/02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#clients-only[Workflow DSL Specification, All Interaction in the Operator's Clients]). The plan representation is a view: it offers no model-facing link. The default units name only roles of the shipped default role set (link:../model-work/01-roles-and-configuration.adoc[Model Work Specification, Roles & Configuration]); `implement` is the only writer role among them, so a plan has at most one writer worker, and every other role they name is `read_only` or `zero_tool`.

* *Composition*: A unit that names a role the effective role set does not define is refused when the plan instance is composed (`role_undefined`), never when the state is reached, and so is a role whose class differs from the class the state's entity kind requires (`class_mismatch`); a `skill` clause naming a skill that does not resolve likewise (`skill_unresolved`).
* *Closed Outcomes*: A task ends only `submitted`, `asked` (a question record, xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Questions, Waivers & Operator Influence]), `infeasible` (with its evidence), or `lost`. A task that ends without its required submission is offered again within the state's compiled self-remedy budget; then the task closes `lost` and the state takes the fallback its row names (last column), else the fallback of its position. No task ends a plan, and no plan state offers a model a `hand_back` or `step_out` link: a plan ends early only through a server rule, a closed decision a task submits where its state offers one (the `return` of a refuted hypothesis, the `hand_back` of a re-planning), or an operator act that ends it (<<plan-outcomes>>).
* *Unqualified Roles*: While a role has no valid qualification record, its tasks are not served; the state takes the fallback of the last column, the task is recorded with the reason `role_unqualified`, and no decision reaches the operator (link:../model-work/01-roles-and-configuration.adoc[Model Work Specification, Roles & Configuration]). In the evaluation `replan`, `triage-build`, and `triage-ci` did not qualify (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/evaluation.adoc#limits[Evaluation Reference, Limits]); while they are unqualified, a re-planning takes its trigger's conservative fallback (an infeasible deliverable or a spent remediation loop hands the plan back, every other trigger continues the plan) and a build or CI failure gets its one `retry` before the fallback.
* *Evidence in Facts*: The facts of every decision task of a phase (`refine.hypothesis-refuted`, `replan.review`, the triage, and every `decide`) carry the evidence the decision rests on, by the rule of link:../model-work/06-envelopes-questions-deadlines.adoc[Model Work Specification, Envelope Tasks, Questions & Decisions]: the claims and their artifacts, the re-planning trigger with its evidence (the infeasible deliverables with their server-computed dependents, the base commits, the conflicts, the caps, the spent budgets), the findings, and the structured failures with their budgets.

[#plan-outcomes]
=== Plan Outcomes, Fallback Routes & Remediation Policies
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-90[PM-WATCH-PHASE-90], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-75[PM-WATCH-PHASE-75], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-91[PM-WATCH-PHASE-91]_

The process enforces itself: its gates are process conformance, not security, and no setting and no answer of the operator passes a gate its evidence does not pass. Every blocking condition has a bounded self-remedy and, when that is spent, a defined fallback to one of the fallback outcomes `handed_back` and `returned`. A plan waits for the operator only in an operator wait (a decision, a waiver request, an option asked where the plan first needs it, a phase transition its asking variant waits at), and it halts otherwise only in a pause the operator set (link:../runtime-lifecycle/02-plan-pause-and-stalled-plans.adoc#plan-pause[Runtime Lifecycle Specification, Pausing and Starting a Plan]) or in an integrity breach. The operator influences a running plan only by operator acts: waivers, pausing and starting it, cancelling or abandoning it, a revision accepted in a chat, and the plan's own settings (xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Questions, Waivers & Operator Influence]).

[cols="1,1,4"]
|===
|Outcome |Terminal state |Meaning

|`merged`
|`completed`
|The work landed on the base branch (`merge_outcome: merged`): the plan's pull request merged (`landing.pull-request`), or its commits were pushed onto the base branch (`landing.main`; xref:06-finalize-re-entry-and-merge.adoc#landing-variants[Landing Variants]).

|`handed_back`
|`handed_back`
|The work is delivered as an open pull request (a draft after a fallback, ready for review when the plan's asking variant leaves the merge to the operator and the pre-merge barrier passed, `merge_outcome: awaiting_human_merge`), without a push remote or pull request as a branch, or with `landing.files` as uncommitted changes in the main checkout's working tree (`merge_outcome: no_pr`); the gaps are documented in an idempotent pull-request comment and the label `pm-mcp:handed-back` where a pull request exists, and in the landing record.

|`returned`
|`returned`
|Only the planning result exists; nothing was implemented, no branch and no pull request are left behind, and the planning artifacts are archived.

|`no_change`
|`completed`
|A recipe run whose harvest yielded no targets: nothing to change, no commit and no pull request (`merge_outcome: no_pr`, fact `empty_harvest`); not a fallback.

|`cancelled`
|`plan.aborted`
|The operator cancelled the running, pausing, waiting, or held plan, with a reason.

|`abandoned`
|`plan.aborted`
|The operator abandoned the paused plan, with a reason.

|`released`
|`plan.aborted`
|The operator released the plan's project; set on every plan of the project that has not ended, whatever its state.

|`aborted`
|`plan.aborted`
|The operator resolved an integrity pause of the plan by its `cancel` act (the integrity abort).
|===

An empty harvest reports `no_change`, never `merged`; `no_change` keeps "nothing merged" distinct from a merge in the landing and the metrics. Both outcomes end in the terminal `completed`, declared `terminal merged success`; that declared outcome id is nominal, and the plan's `outcome` field is authoritative.

The landing record carries the outcome beside the `merge_outcome`. The first four outcomes are reached only through `finalize.archive-plan` after the post-run band, so metrics, the landing, and the archive run for every plan that runs to its end; the last four are reached only through `plan.aborted` by an act of the operator (below) and are archived the same way, with their reason (link:../state-catalogue/04-plan-finalize-terminal.adoc#plan-end[State Catalogue, Cancelling and Abandoning Archive]).

* *Begun Steps*: The transaction that enters `plan.return`, `finalize.hand-back`, or `plan.aborted` records every begun, unsettled step `failed`.
* *Gap Record*: A fallback records its gap (gate, policy, evidence summary, and `cause`: `exhausted` with the spent budget, `stepped_out` with the server's step-out cause, `decided` with the decision task's reason and evidence, `infeasible` with the task's evidence, or the condition that ran out of self-remedy; a gap of a task's fallback names the task and, for a `lost` task, its `task_reason`, for example `role_unqualified`; link:../store-schemas/14-plan-instance-waivers-questions.adoc[Store Schemas Specification, Gap Record]); the landing record and the hand-back comment render it, and the hand-back comment names every waiver request the operator declined.
* *Server Step-Out*: Inside a sub-process of a plan (a remediation unit, `git.commit_and_push`) only the server leaves a frame early: when the frame's attempts or bounded retries are spent it returns `stepped_out` with its cause, its caller routes it once, and a phase-level caller routes it to the same fallback as `exhausted`. A step-out consumes no budget and writes no waiver.
* *Operator Ends*: The operator ends a plan in either client, with a reason, through `plan.aborted` in its next transaction: *cancel* (a running, pausing, waiting, or held plan; outcome `cancelled`) and *abandon* (a paused plan; outcome `abandoned`), choosing in the confirmation whether an open pull request of the plan is closed with a comment naming the reason or left open; its branch stays (link:../state-catalogue/04-plan-finalize-terminal.adoc#plan-end[State Catalogue, Cancelling and Abandoning Archive]). `pm-operator abort plan <plan_id> --reason` is the shell form of both. `plan.aborted` is entered only by cancel, abandon, the `cancel` act of an integrity pause, whose resolution is the integrity abort (outcome `aborted`), the release of the project (outcome `released`, link:../credentials-and-enrolment/02-enrolment-and-project-lifecycle.adoc#project-release[Credentials & Enrolment Specification, Releasing Ends Work as the Operator Ends It]), and `delete-plan`; open decisions and waiver requests close `cancelled`, the plan's open tasks are withdrawn and their workers ended, and the plan is archived like a completed plan.
* *Integrity Pause*: A tampered plan or epic store, or a tampered enrolment (every running plan of the project), raises the condition overlay `integrity_breach` (`kind: store_tampered | enrolment_tampered`, the store, recorded and current digest; link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#state-catalogue-conditions[State Catalogue, Conditions]). No task of the scope is offered, and every `pm_do` is rejected with `integrity_paused`. The pause is the hold `integrity_pause` of the scope (link:../state-catalogue/01-conventions-generic-conditions.adoc#holds[State Catalogue, Holds and Their Acts]), which shows the tampered file with its recorded and current size and digest and offers the operator's acts `accept-edit` and `cancel` (the integrity abort, outcome `aborted`); it is unbounded and has no fallback, and the plan's operator waits stay open while it lasts. `accept-edit` re-baselines, and the engine re-verifies before clearing the condition.
* *Other Conditions*: Every other engine-raised condition runs its self-remedy, bounded by a compiled per-condition-instance budget (default `3`): the server retries where a retry can clear it, and where clearing it needs judgment or work, it creates a task of the role whose work raised it (for example a `fix` of a footprint violation by the `implement` role); the operator may remove the cause at any time: a condition is shown in both clients as a hold of kind `condition` with its cause and the acts that resolve it (a missing credential keeps its bounded server `retry`, and its hold offers the credential's entry). A spent budget takes the fallback of the current position with the condition as the gap; where the fallback itself lacks what is missing (no push credential), `handed_back` degrades to the local branch with the gap stated in the landing record.

==== Remediation Policy Calls

The self-remedy of a failure class is an ordinary sub-workflow unit per class (link:../workflow-dsl/08-remediation-units.adoc#remediation-units[Workflow DSL Specification, Remediation Policy Units]): the server gathers the evidence as structured failures (`{failure_id, class, kind, source, file?, line?, message, fingerprint, excerpt_uri?}`; logs and excerpts stay behind untrusted resources `pm://plan/{plan_id}/evidence/{evidence_id}`), and the unit's `decide` chooses from the closed actions `retry` (reason `enum[flaky|infra|timeout]`), `fix` (in-branch only after the pull request exists; before it, at the security-audit and self-review call sites, only in the `loop_back` form), and `request_waiver` (rationale; offered for a failing gate of the waivable list wherever the call binds it, its waiver request an operator wait, xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Waivers]), each validated by the server and bounded by its budget. The decision is a task of the policy's role whose facts carry the evidence and the remaining budgets, and whose submit names one action of the set its representation offers; where the bound budgets and actions leave exactly one action, the server takes it without a task, and where they leave none, the unit ends `exhausted`. Which of these applies is decided at runtime from the bound remedies alone, never by the unit (link:../workflow-dsl/08-remediation-units.adoc#remediation-invariants[Workflow DSL Specification, `INV-REM-7`]). A unit ends with a reachable subset of the domain outcomes `resolved`, `waived`, `exhausted`, `fix_requested`, plus the control outcome `stepped_out`. Every caller routes `resolved` to its continuation, `waived` to the continuation completing the begun step `waived`, `exhausted` and `stepped_out` to the fallback of its position (in Phase 5 outside the `land` lane and in finalize before the merge, `exhausted` of every policy first enters the phase's re-planning call state with the trigger `ceiling`, xref:08-dynamic-re-planning.adoc#replan-existing-mechanisms[Dynamic Re-Planning]), and `fix_requested` (only where the fix runs in the caller) to its authoring or allocation state, which calls the unit again after the next verification.

* *Budgets*: The defaults are call arguments of the project-owned phase units (team-adaptable), bounded by the compiled hard caps (link:../timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#remediation-budgets[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, Remediation Budgets]). The plan instance carries the effective ceiling per policy with its source (the unit file's call argument, or the `raise` waiver of `fix_budget` or `retry_budget` that lifted it). The consumed budget is a per-plan, per-class counter of the plan status, persisted and never reset: re-entering a unit, a new `HEAD`, or a re-composed instance continues the same count.
* *Server Checks in `decide`*: `retry` is refused with `retry_not_applicable` when the same failure fingerprints already failed after a retry with the same reason; `fix` is charged in the transaction that submits it, a `no_change` result included (link:../workflow-dsl/08-remediation-units.adoc#remediation-units[Workflow DSL Specification, Remediation Policy Units, Budget accounting]), and a `fix` choice on an unchanged worktree or artifact is refused with `fix_unchanged`; `request_waiver` is offered only where the call binds it and the failing gate is waivable (non-waivable failures carry `waivable: false` in the evidence); the unit then rests in the waiver request's operator wait until the operator's `waive` (`waived`) or `decline`, after which the unit takes its next transition with the remaining actions.
* *The Fix Is a Task*: The work a `fix` needs is a task of the plan's writer role `implement` (remediation tasks of Phase 5, an in-branch fix of the unit, `git.resolve-conflicts`) or of the phase's authoring role (a Q-Gate revision); the `decide` task never edits. The task of the last `fix` a budget allows runs on the escalation assignment of its role where one is set and qualified (link:../model-work/01-roles-and-configuration.adoc#escalation-assignment[Model Work Specification, Escalation Assignment]), and its facts carry the earlier attempts of the same policy call: each round's diff and the findings it left (`facts.prior_attempts`), framed as the task's own from now on.
* *Unqualified `decide` Role*: The server takes the unit's `retry` once where its bound budgets offer one, and then, or at once, ends the unit `exhausted`; the task is recorded with the reason `role_unqualified` (<<phase-roles>>).
* *Land Lane*: For a `land` plan the composition binds every policy call with `actions: [retry]`, so `decide` is server logic (`auto`): it retries within the retry budget and then ends the unit `exhausted` (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#land-lane[Land Lane (`local_changes`)]).

=== Phase 1 (`init`): Plan Sources, Phantom-ID Refusal & Classifiers

==== Plan Source Contracts
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-76[PM-WATCH-PHASE-76], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-88[PM-WATCH-PHASE-88], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-92[PM-WATCH-PHASE-92]_

A plan is created by its start, never by a tool call (link:../state-catalogue/09-bootstrap-and-lessons.adoc#start-from-clients[State Catalogue, Starting from the Clients]). The operator opens a start of kind `plan` for a project in either client (`POST /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/entries`); the start is a pending entry addressed by its `entry_id`, whose states are operator waits served to both clients (link:../workflow-dsl/02-units-jobs-decisions.adoc#operator-wait[Workflow DSL Specification, Waiting for the Operator]). It never expires: it stays among the project's open entries until it is started, abandoned, or deleted with the release of its project. The server opens the start of a plan its epic launches (source `epic_row`, xref:06-finalize-re-entry-and-merge.adoc#epic-plan-start[Epic Plan Start]). The setup gate of `start.open` refuses a start while the machine's onboarding or the project's onboarding is not complete, or while `.marshall/config/` is dirty (`config_dirty`), a team file or workflow unit fails validation (`config_invalid`), or one is at another format (`format_outdated`, `format_newer`), each with its reason and file; nothing is created. `start.choose` offers a plan from sources or a recipe:

* *From Sources*: `start.sources` holds the entry's list of sources. Each source sub-workflow (`source-<kind>`, exit contract `done {source}` or `stepped_out`) gathers and verifies its source and adds it to the list, and the operator removes one; a description is stored by the main workflow itself (`start.store-description`), since it needs nothing gathered. A new way to start is one more source unit, never a tool parameter or CLI flag. The facts of `start.open` list only local candidates (unclaimed lessons, local changes); an issue or a pull request is named by its number or URL, never listed (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/later-improvements.adoc#_l_6_issue_and_pull_request_candidates_in_the_plan_bootstrap[Later Improvements, L-6]). Once a source is stored and every external one screened, `start.sources` offers a chat about the entry with the role `refine`, whose one change is a description (link:../state-catalogue.adoc#chat[State Catalogue, Chatting with the Planning Role]).
* *A Recipe*: Starting a recipe creates a plan whose process is the recipe's own workflow, a unit of type `recipe` resolved by level (link:../domain-extensions/05-recipes-and-extension-points.adoc#recipe-workflow[Domain Extensions Specification, A Recipe Is a Workflow]); a recipe is no source, and no state of the plan workflow routes into a recipe. The start validates the recipe's parameters against its schema and, where the recipe takes them, gathers sources as above; the plan's id is derived as `recipe-{recipe_key}-{yyyy-mm-dd-hh-mm-ss}` (UTC; link:../domain-extensions/05-recipes-and-extension-points.adoc#recipe-plan-id[Domain Extensions Specification, Recipe Plan ID]), so repeated runs of one recipe never collide in branches and worktrees, and it is not changeable at `start.ready`; the title is.

*The Source Directory*: The server stores every source in the entry's source directory `pending/<entry_id>/sources/` (link:../filesystem-layout/03-project-store.adoc#source-directory[Filesystem Layout Specification, The Source Directory]), with one manifest `sources.json` naming each source: its id, kind, reference (for an issue or a pull request always its URL), where it came from, trust, digest, size, when it was added, and for an external source its screening verdict. A source is never changed once stored; a source added later is a new entry of the manifest. External text (the title and body of an issue or a pull request) is fetched by a bounded job, cleaned by the deterministic level-1 validator, and stored before the level-2 screening of `screener` tasks reads the stored copy (link:../model-work/07-untrusted-text-screening.adoc#screen-stored[Model Work Specification, Screened Once Stored]); a `suspicious` verdict is an operator wait of the source showing the flagged spans, with `accept` (the text is used, still marked untrusted, and the acceptance audited) or the removal of the source (link:../model-work/07-untrusted-text-screening.adoc#two-level-guard[Model Work Specification, Two-Level Guard for External Text]). What is on this machine (a description, documents, a lesson, the local changes) is trusted.

*Creation*: `start.ready` shows the sources, the title, and the `plan_id` proposed from them (<<_plan_id_derivation_phantom_id_refusal_guard>>); every other option of the plan is asked where the plan first needs it (<<_deterministic_init_classifiers_workflow_settings>>). The `create` link of `start.ready` runs `start.create-scope`, and the engine creates the plan in its scope transaction (outcome `created`, terminal `start.committed`): the plan directory (`<PM_MCP_BASE>/projects/<project_id>/plans/<plan_id>/`, link:../filesystem-layout/03-project-store.adoc#machine-store[Filesystem Layout Specification, Per-Project Machine Store]) with the source directory moved into it (`plans/<plan_id>/sources/`), `request.md`, `references.json`, `mcp/status.json` with `origin` naming the plan's sources by their manifest ids, `entry_baseline`, and the plan instance version 1; the plan enters `init.classify` and runs at once. `request.md` keeps its sections: its original input is composed by the server from the descriptions and a reference to each other source, and the plan's models read the sources in the source directory. The writes outside the plan scope (the epic queue row, the lesson claim, the move of the entry's chat transcript to `plans/<plan_id>/chat/`, the deletion of the pending entry) follow in a fixed order after it, and the startup recovery completes them from the existing plan (link:../state-catalogue/09-bootstrap-and-lessons.adoc#state-catalogue-bootstrap[State Catalogue, Bootstrap]). Before its first write, `start.create-scope` re-checks what creation requires (the identifier is free, the setup gate is open, the lifecycle gate is open or the project is enrolled as a pilot, the lesson is unclaimed, the epic row is launchable and not `running`); a failing check creates nothing and returns the entry to `start.ready` with the reason in `facts`.

==== Plan ID Derivation & Phantom-ID Refusal Guard

The server derives or validates `plan_id` against a strict lexical grammar before creating any directory on disk:

1. *Canonical Grammar*: A `plan_id` matches the link:../identifiers/01-general-rules-and-registry.adoc#id-plan-id[`plan_id` grammar] of the Identifier Registry, the single definition of the plan ID grammar; every schema that restates it (`plan_id` tool properties, `scope_id`, queue rows) matches it exactly. A `plan_slug` (link:../identifiers/01-general-rules-and-registry.adoc#id-plan-slug[registry]) becomes the `plan_id` verbatim; without a slug the server derives one from the plan's sources. Recipe plans are `recipe-{recipe_key}-{yyyy-mm-dd-hh-mm-ss}` (link:../domain-extensions/05-recipes-and-extension-points.adoc#recipe-plan-id[Recipe Plan ID]), which the length bound admits because a `recipe_key` has at most 37 characters (link:../identifiers/01-general-rules-and-registry.adoc#id-recipe-plan-id[registry]). Epic IDs use the same grammar (mirroring `plan-marshall`, which validates the epic slug with its plan-id validator).
2. *Phantom-ID Refusal*: The validator explicitly rejects any candidate `plan_id` or `plan_slug` matching either the link:../identifiers/02-registry-stores-to-web.adoc#id-lesson-id[`lesson_id` grammar] or a UUID / hex-digest pattern (`^[0-9a-fA-F]{8}-[0-9a-fA-F]{4}-[0-9a-fA-F]{4}-[0-9a-fA-F]{4}-[0-9a-fA-F]{12}$`, a bare hex string of 12 or more characters `^[0-9a-fA-F]{12,}$`, or a token beginning case-insensitively with `execution-context` or `agent-`). Rejecting these shapes prevents a lesson ID or a worker or job UUID from becoming a `plan_id` and a phantom plan directory; a `set_slug` naming such a slug is refused with `invalid_link_args`. A supplied or derived `plan_id` beginning with the reserved prefix `baseline-` is refused as well (link:../identifiers/01-general-rules-and-registry.adoc#id-plan-id[Identifier Registry, `plan_id`]), so no plan worktree can collide with a baseline scratch worktree `baseline-<plan_id>-<sha12>`.
3. *No Reuse While Live, Archived, or Staged*: A candidate `plan_id` that already names a live plan (`plans/<plan_id>/` of the project's machine store), an archived plan (`archive/<plan_id>/`, link:../filesystem-layout/03-project-store.adoc#archives[Filesystem Layout Specification, Plan & Epic Archives]), or a staged row of any of the project's epics (other than the row the plan is created from) is never used for a new plan; the same check runs when an epic stages a row, so no two staged rows and no staged row and plan share an identifier. The collision is resolved in the start's `start.ready`, before anything is created: its `facts` show the proposed `plan_id` and whether it is free, and `set_slug (plan_slug)` re-derives it (not for a recipe's plan, whose id is derived). The creation transaction re-checks the identifier, and a taken one returns the entry to `start.ready`. Replacing an existing plan is operator deletion (`delete-plan`) followed by a new start. Because the archive path carries no date, this rule is what keeps every archived plan addressable by its `plan_id` alone; an archived plan the cleanup deleted leaves a tombstone under its id (link:../timeouts/03-retention-host-config.adoc#cleanup[Timeouts & Budgets Specification, One Cleanup]), so its id is never used again. After `delete-plan` the identifier is free again, while the plan's branch, pull request, and capture ref `refs/pm-mcp/captures/<plan_id>` may still exist on the remote or in the repository; a new plan with the same identifier then collides with them at its first push, pull-request creation, or capture, which ends in that step's own failure route, never in a silent reuse.

==== Deterministic Init Classifiers & Workflow Settings
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-19[PM-WF-19: Uniform Decision Records & Corpus Export]_

_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-1[PM-WATCH-PHASE-1]_

During `init.classify`, the engine executes pure, model-free classifiers in a fixed order; the domain detection and a projected posture each append their decision record with the scores they compared (link:../model-work/11-decision-records.adoc#decision-points[Model Work Specification, Decision Points]). A classifier that needs an answer raises a *server-raised decision* (xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Questions, Waivers & Operator Influence]) with server-owned options, which the adjudication decides or delivers to the operator as an operator wait; `init.classify` is re-entrant: every init answer records the answer and returns to `init.classify`, which raises the next unanswered decision in the fixed order sibling collision, domain choice, never re-asks an answered question, never rewrites a write-once output, and exits only when no init decision is open. The stop answer of `init.sibling-collision` is an ordinary option whose effect is the `returned` route (`plan.return`, <<plan-outcomes>>). The lesson premise is no init question: it is checked in the start before the plan exists (source `lesson`, <<_plan_source_contracts>>). *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: its `rename` answer (delete the plan and restart init) does not exist; the stop answer of `init.sibling-collision` followed by a new start covers it.

1. *Request-Aspect Classification*: The server computes the request-aspect classification (`status.metadata.request_aspect`) over the request's original input without a model call (link:../domain-extensions/05-recipes-and-extension-points.adoc[Domain Extensions Specification, Recipes & Extension Points]); it routes nothing: a recipe is chosen by the operator at the start, never by a classifier.
2. *Initial Planning-Lane Router*: Computes the initial `planning_lane` (`light` or `deep`) from the request with zero discovery (`deep` for every plan when the team's `workflow.json` `plan.phase-1-init.minimum_lane` is `deep`, xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#planning-lanes[Planning Lanes & One-Way Ratchet (`light` -> `deep`)]), and the pre-route `scope_estimate` (`none`, `surgical`, `single_module`, `multi_module`, `broad`). `scope_estimate` is derived from expected-modification paths only; read-only references never raise it. The pre-route value is persisted immutably as `scope_estimate_pre_route`; every later update of `scope_estimate` (Phase 2 or Phase 3) records its own provenance under `scope_estimate_provenance[]` instead of overwriting the pre-route evidence.
3. *Sibling Collision Detector*: Scans live plans for overlapping target modules or affected file paths; if an overlap is found, it raises the `init.sibling-collision` decision (`proceed`, or stop, which ends the plan `returned`).
4. *Workflow Settings Resolution*: Reads the project's effective configuration (link:../store-schemas/07-project-configuration.adoc#project-configuration-store[Store Schemas Specification, Project Configuration]): the team's `minimum_lane` (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#planning-lanes[Planning Lanes & One-Way Ratchet (`light` -> `deep`)]), `compatibility`, and `simplicity`, and the plan's behaviour axes, landing, method, and asking, each from the nearest level that sets it: the plan's own value, the project's, the machine's default (link:../workflow-dsl/09-plan-instance.adoc#plan-axes[Workflow DSL Specification, Plan Behaviour Axes]); the instance binds the chosen variants and prunes the others with reason `plan_default`. A landing variant that does not fit the project (`landing.pull-request` without a provider, `landing.main` on a protected base branch) was refused at the start with its reason.
5. *Posture Where the Lane Is Chosen*: The execution posture (`minimal | standard | full`) is the plan's own value, else the project's `plan_defaults.posture`, else the machine's; the key is optional at both levels and unset by default. Where none of them sets it, `init.classify` asks it, once the initial lane is routed, in the operator wait `init.options` that asks every open option of the plan together, with the server projection as its proposal (link:../state-catalogue/02-plan-phases-1-3.adoc#state-init.options[State Catalogue Specification, `init.options`]; link:../state-catalogue/09-bootstrap-and-lessons.adoc#options-when-needed[State Catalogue, Asked When Needed]); the answer is the plan's own value (`overrides.json` `posture`, link:../store-schemas/14-plan-instance-waivers-questions.adoc#plan-overrides-store[Store Schemas Specification, Plan Overrides]) and composes the next instance version (composition event `override`, link:../workflow-dsl/09-plan-instance.adoc#plan-overrides[Workflow DSL Specification, Plan Overrides]). Its effect, the steps pruned with reason `posture`, is shown with its provenance in the version that binds it, the posture preview (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#plan-instance-composition[Plan Instance Composition]); floor immunity keeps every mandatory step whatever the value. *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: its five auto-continue flags (`init_`, `plan_`, `execute_`, `finalize_`, `loop_back_without_asking`) are generalised by the asking axis, its `lane_selection` prompt switch does not exist, and the posture is asked only where nothing sets it. The refine confidence threshold (`95`), the lane prune thresholds, and the hard caps of the remediation budgets are compiled constants.
6. *Base Stamp*: The plan's base branch is the project's (its team setting, detected in the project's onboarding); where the project sets none, `init.classify` asks it in the same operator wait `init.options` before this stamp, the first state that needs it and before the plan's branch is created, with the repository's default branch as its proposal; the answer is the plan's own value (`overrides.json` `base_branch`). Stamps `main_sha` in `mcp/status.json` from `git rev-parse origin/<base_branch>`. It is written once here and re-stamped only by the `sync-baseline` and `branch-cleanup` rebases. Before Phase 5 a drift of `main_sha` is informational only (rendered in `facts`, never refusing); from Phase 5 on the exit-barrier freshness gate and the merge-path rebase act on the base.

At `phase-4-plan` entry the lane predicate is re-evaluated with the settled inputs (declared deliverables and their expected modifications) and the result is logged as `lane_reevaluation` in `audit.jsonl`; the re-evaluation never re-routes the plan (see xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#_lane_ratchet_rules[Lane Ratchet Rules]).

=== Phase 2 (`refine`): Quality Aggregation, Domain Widening & Bounded Relaunch

The Phase 2 states run for `deep`-lane plans only (including a plan ratcheted from `light` to `deep`). A `light`-lane plan never enters `refine.analyze` or its confidence loop; its refine work is folded into the collapsed light envelope (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#planning-lanes[Planning Lanes & One-Way Ratchet (`light` -> `deep`)]).

==== Phase Entry & Baseline Reconciliation
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-2[PM-WATCH-PHASE-2], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-86[PM-WATCH-PHASE-86]_

At `phase-2-refine` entry, the engine caches loop-invariant inputs (`request.md`, `references.json`, and the architecture module inventory with its overview, link:../domain-extensions/03-module-discovery.adoc#_server_owned_module_discovery_enrolled_discovery_jobs[Domain Extensions Specification, Server-Owned Module Discovery]) so they are read once per phase rather than re-read inside the confidence loop. When a base branch is configured, `refine.baseline-reconcile` runs a non-mutating merge-conflict probe against upstream and classifies drift into three outcomes:

* `no_overlap`: Upstream commits touch disjoint files; advances directly to `refine.analyze`.
* `overlap_no_content_conflict`: Overlapping files merge cleanly; records `auto_reconcilable: true` without mutating the tree (deferring the actual rebase to Phase 5/6) and advances to `refine.analyze`.
* `overlap_with_content_conflict`: Content conflicts exist; emits blocking Q-Gate findings so the conflict is absorbed during request refinement.

* *Probe Variants*: The probe is one operation of the git contract with both variants. The native variant merges in memory with JGit's `ResolveMerger` and takes the conflicted paths from its merge result; nothing is written to the object database or the worktree. The CLI variant runs `git merge-tree --write-tree --name-only -z` with `LC_ALL=C` and reads only the conflicted-file section; informational and message lines are never paths, and the locale pin applies to this probe only. In both variants the conflict count is the number of distinct conflicted paths.
* *Overlap Population*: Overlap is computed between the upstream changed paths and the plan's declared ∪ derived footprint known at that point, never only the in-flight diff (which is empty before execution), so a first Phase 5 entry never reports `conflict_count: 0` merely because nothing was written yet.
* *Drift Routing*: Content overlap (`overlap_with_content_conflict`) is the only drift routed to refinement. A `main_sha` ancestry drift is informational before Phase 5 and is cleared only by a sync or rebase transition; a refine relaunch is never offered as its remedy, and no operator override exists.

==== Five Quality Dimensions & Fixed-Weight Confidence Aggregation
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-6[PM-WATCH-PHASE-6], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-68[PM-WATCH-PHASE-68]_

During `refine.analyze`, a `job llm` state of role `refine` (<<phase-roles>>), the refine task executes source-premise verification (checking cited files, flags, and APIs against the codebase) and proposed-fix verification (constructing a semantic probe to verify whether a proposed fix resolves the stated symptom), maps affected modules, estimates scope (`none`, `surgical`, `single_module`, `multi_module`, `broad`), authors a conventional-commit `pr_title` ($\le 72$ characters, distinct from the human plan `title`), and submits raw scores (`0..100`) for the five quality dimensions plus architectural module mapping.

* *Premise Verdicts*: Source-premise verification records each premise with the verdict record of epic Spec Re-Grounding (link:../model-work/08-epic-coordination.adoc#_server_derived_landing_facts_spec_re_grounding_cross_plan_locks[Model Work Specification, Verdict Record]): label `observed` or `hypothesis`, verdict `corroborated | contradicted | partially_exercised | unverifiable`, and `scope_change` (`none | rescoped`); no second vocabulary exists. A command flag mandated by the request is a premise and must name a module of the command's actual reactor that the flag affects.
* *Open Hypotheses of Epic Plans*: For a plan its epic started (source `epic_row`), every `hypothesis`-labelled claim of its spec section is surfaced in `facts.open_hypotheses`. `submit:refine` must carry a verdict per claim (`confirmed` or `refuted`, citing the named artifact), and any `refuted` claim routes to `refine.awaiting_answers` for re-scoping instead of `refine.ready`; that pass counts as the one refine relaunch. A claim refuted again in the second pass routes to `refine.hypothesis-refuted`: the server appends a `hypothesis_refuted` event (claim, evidence, plan) to the epic inbox and creates a task of role `refine`, whose facts carry the claim, both refutations with their cited artifacts, and the spec section; its submit names one decision of the closed set its representation offers: `continue_narrowed` (`narrowed_scope: text[1..2000]`) enters the `auto` state `refine.apply-narrowing`, whose primitive writes the intent amendment (`source: model`, listed in the pull-request body and the landing record) and continues to `refine.ready`; `return` (`reason: text[1..2000]`) ends the plan `returned` (gap `cause: decided`), so the epic re-stages it with a corrected premise; while `refine` is unqualified the state takes `return`. There is never a third refine pass.

The server-side `ConfidenceAggregator` computes the overall confidence deterministically using fixed weights (zero LLM arithmetic):

[cols="2,1,4"]
|===
|Dimension |Fixed Weight |Verification Criteria

|`correctness`
|20%
|Technical validity of referenced modules, APIs, flags, source premises, and proposed fixes against the live architecture.

|`completeness`
|20%
|Presence of clear scope boundaries, acceptance criteria, test expectations, and cross-module dependencies.

|`consistency`
|20%
|Absence of internal contradictions or conflicting constraints across request sections and architecture rules.

|`non_duplication`
|10%
|Absence of redundant or overlapping requirement statements.

|`ambiguity`
|20%
|Single unambiguous interpretation of terminology, target scope, and measurable outcomes (`100` = unambiguous).

|`module_mapping`
|10%
|Confidence in mapping the request to concrete project modules and dependency directions.
|===

$$\text{confidence} = \lfloor 0.20 \cdot S_{\text{corr}} + 0.20 \cdot S_{\text{comp}} + 0.20 \cdot S_{\text{cons}} + 0.10 \cdot S_{\text{nondup}} + 0.20 \cdot S_{\text{ambig}} + 0.10 \cdot S_{\text{mod}} \rfloor$$

* The intent record (<<intent-record>>) and all six dimensions are required parameters of `submit:refine`; a submission omitting one is rejected with `invalid_link_args`, so no absent score is ever folded into the aggregate as `0`. *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: `plan-marshall` scores the dimensions discretely (`100` on pass and `0` on an issue; completeness `100`/`50`/`0`; non-duplication `100`/`80`); `PM-MCP` accepts each dimension as `int[0..100]`.
* *Evidence Class*: `submit:refine` carries `evidence_class` (`self_assessment`, `operator_answered`, or `downstream_reproduced`), persisted beside the score and shown in `facts`. The server checks it against its records: `operator_answered` is refused (`invalid_link_args`) unless a `refine.awaiting_answers` round of this plan has at least one answer the operator gave (channel `web` or `cli`; a `quorum` answer does not count), and `downstream_reproduced` requires an `evidence_ref` naming the reproducing artifact (a build job, test run, or re-grounding verdict). No heuristic fires on an all-100 score by itself: only `self_assessment` with every dimension at `100` on a plan no epic started adds the advisory fact `self_assessed_full_confidence` (never blocking, never a link change), while an all-100 score on an emitted plan's pre-verified specification is expected and adds nothing.
* *Server Checks on Every Submission*: For every deep plan, `submit:refine` is refused with `invalid_link_args` when its module mapping names a module that the architecture module inventory does not know, or maps no module for an expected modification (module-mapping existence, blocking). The consistency of `scope_estimate` with the lane is checked where a `light` plan records an estimate (a ratchet trigger, xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#_lane_ratchet_rules[Lane Ratchet Rules]); a deep plan is never ratcheted down. *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: its refine Q-Gate also judges scope realism and confidence justification on every plan; both are dropped, because the fixed-weight `ConfidenceAggregator` and the evidence class cover them.
* The aggregated score is persisted to `status.metadata.confidence` and compared against `confidence_threshold`, the compiled constant `95` (not configurable: lowering it would only admit less-clarified requests); every comparison appends a `refine.confidence` decision record with the score against the threshold (link:../model-work/11-decision-records.adoc#decision-points[Model Work Specification, Decision Points]). *Deliberate change vs. `plan-marshall`*: its `confidence_threshold` is a project setting (default `95`).
* The server also validates that `compatibility` (`breaking | deprecation | smart_and_ask`, compiled default `breaking`) and `simplicity` (`lean | pragmatic | defensive`, compiled default `lean`) are resolved from the effective configuration and persisted in the plan.

==== Widen-Only Domain Re-Merge
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-67[PM-WATCH-PHASE-67]_

Once concrete `affected_files` are identified in `refine.analyze` (for a `light`-lane plan: on acceptance of the light envelope's submission, before the single narrowing pass of Phase 3), the server runs `domain-detect` over those file paths (`file_globs` and `always_on` inclusion legs) and computes the set union with the existing `metadata.domains` of `mcp/status.json`:

$$\text{domains}_{\text{new}} = \text{domains}_{\text{cached}} \cup \text{detect}(\text{affected\_files})$$

* *Monotonic Widening Invariant*: Phase 2 may **only widen** `metadata.domains`, never narrow it, ensuring convergence across refine iterations. If `affected_files` produce zero narrative or glob matches, `domain-detect` returns all offerable domains under `reason = "over_provisioned_resolve"`; that temporary over-provisioning is narrowed exactly once in Phase 3 after deliverables are declared.
* *Match Provenance*: `domain-detect` returns, per domain, its `source` (`always_on`, `glob`, or `narrative`) and `matches: N`, rendered in `facts`. A result resting only on `always_on` legs with zero matches is reported `floor_only`, never as a confident single-domain answer.

[#intent-record]
==== The Intent Record
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-106[PM-WATCH-PHASE-106], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-107[PM-WATCH-PHASE-107]_

_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-10[PM-WF-10: Phase 1–4 Planning Invariants, Lane Ratchet & Artifact Gates]_

The refine task does not submit the clarified request as prose. It submits the plan's *intent record*, `intent:schema[intent-record]` (link:../store-schemas/06-task-and-references.adoc#intent-record-store[Store Schemas Specification, Intent Record]), and the server renders `## Clarified Request` of `request.md` from it. The record is what later phases cite: the outline names the criteria each deliverable satisfies (xref:02-planning-lanes-phases-3-4.adoc#outline-satisfies[Phase 3, `Satisfies` Coverage]), the self-review judges against the criteria, and the pull-request body lists them. Wherever this specification names the clarified request as text (the change-type heuristic, the issue write-back), it means that rendered section.

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Member |Content

|`why` |The problem, who has it, and why now (`20` to `1000` characters).
|`goal` |One outcome sentence that a check can decide (`10` to `300` characters).
|`criteria[]` |One to `30` acceptance criteria `AC-<n>`, each a statement in EARS form (below), its `kind` (`new`, `fix`, `unchanged`), and how it is verified (`test`, `demonstration`, `inspection`).
|`current_behaviour` |For a record with a `fix` criterion: the behaviour the fix replaces, in the form `WHEN <condition> THEN THE <system> <behaviour>`; `null` otherwise.
|`non_goals[]` |One to `20` items `NG-<n>`: what the plan deliberately does not do.
|`constraints[]` |Items `CN-<n>` that each rule something out, with their `source` (`code`, `operator`, `external`) and, for `code`, the path or `pm://` URI that shows it.
|`decisions[]` |Items `DC-<n>`: the decision, the options weighed, the architecture decision record it follows or contradicts (`adr`, optional), and the `decider`: `operator` or `quorum` (with the `question_id` of the answered part), `model` (the role's own call), or `fact` (found by exploring, with its evidence path or `pm://` URI).
|`assumptions[]` |Items `AN-<n>`, each `verified` or `unverified`; at most one marked `riskiest`, with a cheap test that would refute it.
|`open_questions[]` |Questions left for `exploration` or an `external` party; a question only the operator can answer is asked, never left here.
|`deferred[]` |Items `DF-<n>` split off from this plan, each with its reason.
|`source_claims[]` |Every load-bearing claim of the plan's sources (one without which a later step would decide differently), each with where it landed: the id of an item of this record, or `dropped` with a reason.
|===

*Server checks on every submission* (a violation is refused with `invalid_link_args`, `rejected.detail` naming the member):

. *EARS form*: every criterion matches the EARS grammar: optional preconditions `WHERE <feature>`, `WHILE <state>`, `WHEN <trigger>` (in that order, each at most once), optionally `IF <condition> THEN`, then `THE <system> SHALL <response>`; a `kind: unchanged` criterion's response begins with `CONTINUE TO`. The keywords are these uppercase English tokens whatever the project's model language (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/module-structure.adoc#i18n[Module Structure, Languages]); the rest is written in the model language.
. *A fix carries its triple*: a record with a `fix` criterion has `current_behaviour` and at least one `unchanged` criterion.
. *Deciders are true*: a `DC` item of decider `operator` or `quorum` names a part of an answered question record of this plan whose answer channel matches (`web` or `cli` for `operator`, `quorum` for `quorum`); a `fact` names its evidence, and a path must exist in the tree. A model's call is never recorded as the operator's, and a decision that `contradicts` an architecture decision record has decider `operator`.
. *Assumptions*: a record with an `unverified` assumption marks exactly one `riskiest`, which carries its test.
. *Identifiers*: unique within the record; an identifier that a revision of the record removed is never reused for another item (checked against the plan's previous records).
. *Preservation*: every `source_claims` entry lands in an existing item or is `dropped` with a reason. For a plan its epic started (source `epic_row`), the server seeds the required claims: the row's `done_when` and each of the epic's non-goals (link:../model-work/08-epic-coordination.adoc#epic-intent[Model Work Specification, Epic Intent]) must appear among the `source_claims` by their exact text; the task's facts carry them as `facts.required_claims`.

*Revisions*: every accepted submission writes the record to `intent.json` and appends the previous one to its history in the same transaction; an answer of a relaunch round, a Q-Gate remediation pass, and a re-planning that changes intent each submit a new revision. An answer given after refine that changes intent (the outline review's `revise`, a task question) stays an amendment (xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Questions, Waivers & Operator Influence]) and does not rewrite the record until a re-planning submits one. An amendment that changes a criterion bound to a capability is folded into a new revision before the capability specs are written (<<capability-specs>>), so a capability never takes the text the amendment replaced.

*Rendering*: `## Clarified Request` shows the record's members as sections in the model language, each item with its identifier; the model's own calls are listed under their own heading, never mixed with the operator's decisions. A plan whose confidence stayed below the threshold (`low_confidence_manual_review`) also shows the six dimension scores of its last pass there.

*Refine role skill*: Besides the record's rules, the role skill of `refine` states, as guidance (class `guidance`):

* *Anchored scores*: for each of the six dimensions an example sentence for the levels `0`, `25`, `50`, `75`, and `100`; the role scores the record it submits, never the conversation that led to it, and between two levels it takes the lower.
* *Framing*: a request that names a solution without the need it serves cannot fill the record's `why`; the role asks for the need (who has the problem, what they do today, why now) before it scores, and never writes the solution's description into `why`.
* *Proportion*: the record is as large as the change, on either lane. A fix is its triple (the current behaviour, the `fix` criterion, the `unchanged` criteria it must not break) and its test; a criterion is written only for behaviour the change adds, fixes, or must keep, never for behaviour it does not touch; one defect or one requested behaviour is one criterion, never split into several. The bound of `30` criteria is a ceiling, not a target. Each plan's planning weight is published (xref:05-finalize-review-metrics-descriptor.adoc#planning-weight[Planning Weight]), so a record out of proportion to its change shows in the figures.
* *Risks*: a risk is not written as prose. What must keep working is an `unchanged` criterion with its verification; what might be false is an assumption with its test; a residual risk the plan accepts is a non-goal. A constraint states what must never happen whichever way the criteria are met, and the role writes one for every loss the operator named as unacceptable.
* *Trade-offs nobody named*: a goal that improves one measure (speed, throughput, size, cost) names the measures that must not get worse, each an `unchanged` criterion or a constraint with its bound, because one improved number can hide a worse one; a rewrite, a port, or a replacement states whether what existing callers observe stays the same, as `unchanged` criteria or a non-goal that says it does not; and a change that adds or changes a queue, a cache, a buffer, a retry, or a limit states what happens when the limit is reached (what is dropped, delayed, or refused) as a criterion, or asks the operator when nothing in the sources says it.
* *Specification sources*: a source written in another framework's format (a Spec Kit `spec.md`, an OpenSpec proposal and its spec deltas, read through the `documents` source) is mapped, not copied: its functional requirements, success criteria, and scenarios become criteria in EARS form, its out-of-scope items non-goals, its clarifications decisions with the decider they name, and every requirement it states is a source claim.
* *Searching what is known*: the planning roles (`refine`, `outline`, `replan`, `decompose`) may search the project's knowledge, its lessons, architecture decision records, capability requirements, and decision records, through the resource `pm://project/knowledge` (link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/specification/mcp-tools/03-resource-uris.adoc#knowledge-resource[MCP Tools Specification, Knowledge Search]); their role skills say when: before a decision of the record that an earlier plan may have taken.
* *Settled decisions*: the task's facts carry the project's architecture decision records as an index (`facts.adr_index`: identifier, title, status, the modules it names). A decision of the record that follows or departs from an accepted record names it (`adr`: its identifier and `follows` or `contradicts`); the server refuses a `contradicts` decision whose decider is not `operator`, so a settled decision is reopened only by the operator.
* *A fix is tested*: a `fix` criterion is verified by `test` where a module the plan affects has a test framework in its domain (its build reports test counts, link:../job-runtime/03-parsers-and-gates.adoc[Job Runtime Specification, Parsers]); another verification states in the criterion why no test can hold the fix (a build file, a workflow file, documentation), because a fix delivered without a test is correct once and unguarded the next time. The outline's deliverable that satisfies the criterion writes the test that reproduces the defect, and the test fails on an assertion, not on loading, before the fix.

[#capability-specs]
==== Capability Specs
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-118[PM-WATCH-PHASE-118]_
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-10[PM-WF-10: Phase 1–4 Planning Invariants, Lane Ratchet & Artifact Gates]_

A plan states a change; the project's *capability specs* state what the system does after it. They are git-tracked team files, one per capability, `.marshall/capabilities/<capability>.json` (link:../store-schemas/06-task-and-references.adoc#capability-spec[Store Schemas Specification, Capability Spec]): a capability is a durable behaviour of the system named for what it does (`http-header-parsing`), never for a piece of work, and holds its requirements, each a name, a statement in EARS form, and its scenarios. Nobody back-fills them: a capability spec grows only from the plans that change it, so what it states was true when the plan that wrote it merged.

* *A criterion changes a capability*: a criterion of the intent record may name the requirement it changes (`capability`: the capability, the requirement's name, `added`, `modified`, or `removed`, and, for `modified` and `removed`, the `base` fingerprint of the requirement it changes: the SHA-256 of its canonical JSON at the plan's base). A criterion without one stays the plan's own. The server checks on every submission that an `added` requirement does not exist, and that a `modified` or `removed` one exists with that fingerprint in the tree the plan started from.
* *Facts*: the refine and outline tasks receive the index of the capability specs (`facts.capability_index`: each capability with its purpose and its requirement names); the repository holds the rest.
* *Merged with the code*: the floor-immune finalize step `architecture-refresh`, which already commits the project's derived inventory, first writes the plan's changes into the capability specs of the plan's worktree in one transaction, so they land in the plan's own pull request (xref:04-finalize-step-bands.adoc#_normative_step_catalogue_across_ordering_bands[Phase Workflows Specification, Step Catalogue]). Before writing, it compares each `base` with the requirement in the worktree as it then is (after the baseline sync): a requirement another merged plan changed since raises the re-planning trigger `capability_conflict` (xref:08-dynamic-re-planning.adoc#replan-triggers[Dynamic Re-Planning, Triggers]), whose revision states the change again against the current requirement; a change is never written over one it did not see.
* *Amended after refine*: an amendment names the items of the record it changes (`amendments[].changes`, xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Intent Amendments]). Before `architecture-refresh` writes anything, it looks for an amendment that no later revision of the record has folded (a revision accepted after the amendment's `at`) and that names a criterion with a `capability`, or whose `changes` is unknown while the record holds such a criterion; finding one, it raises the re-planning trigger `intent_amended` (xref:08-dynamic-re-planning.adoc#replan-triggers[Dynamic Re-Planning, Triggers]), whose revision states the criterion again as the amendment changed it. What implementation taught the plan reaches the capability spec, never the criterion it replaced.
* *Verified by*: each requirement written records `verified_by`, the test files that the self-review named as evidence when it judged the requirement's criterion `met` (files under a test source root of their module, as module discovery reports it); a requirement whose criterion is verified by `demonstration` or `inspection` records none. A later plan that deletes such a test without changing that requirement gets a blocking finding before the merge (xref:05-finalize-review-metrics-descriptor.adoc#capability-test-guard[Capability Test Guard]); a rename that git detects is followed: `architecture-refresh` writes the new path.

[#builder-check]
==== Builder Check
_Implementation watch: link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-109[PM-WATCH-PHASE-109], link:../../implementation-watch/phase-workflows.adoc#PM-WATCH-PHASE-111[PM-WATCH-PHASE-111]_
_See Requirement link:../../../../plan-marshall-documentation/doc/Requirements.adoc#PM-WF-10[PM-WF-10: Phase 1–4 Planning Invariants, Lane Ratchet & Artifact Gates]_

Refine scores its own record. Before a deep-lane plan leaves refine, one task tests the record from the position of someone who must build from it and cannot ask: `refine.builder-check`, a `job llm` state of the role `qgate` (<<phase-roles>>). Its facts carry the intent record and nothing of the conversation that produced it (no original input, no clarifications); its worker reads the repository like every `read_only` worker. It submits once, through `submit:builder-check` (`questions:schema[builder-questions]`), every question it would still need answered to build the plan, each sorted:

* `answered`: the record answers it; the entry names the item (`AC-<n>`, `DC-<n>`, …).
* `minor`: the builder's own call, with the decision it would take. The server adds it to the record as a decision of decider `model`, marked as from the builder check, in a new revision.
* `operator`: only the operator can answer it, as a part of a `decision-envelope`. Two criteria that cannot both hold (restated as condition and response, one demands what the other excludes) are always such a question, naming both criteria; so is a decision (`DC-<n>`) or a criterion that permits what a constraint (`CN-<n>`) rules out, restated the same way, naming both items: a default that the operator accepted as recommended does not settle it, because the constraint is the operator's word and the default is the model's. A goal the criteria do not reach is such a question too: an implementation that meets every criterion as written while the goal sentence stays false, restated as that implementation and what of the goal it leaves undone, naming the goal and the criteria it passes, because which criterion is missing decides what the plan delivers, and that is the operator's call.

The role skill of the builder check (class `guidance`) names three questions a builder of a performance, rewrite, or capacity change asks when the record is silent on them, sorted like every other: which measures must not get worse while the goal's measure improves, whether existing callers observe the same behaviour afterwards, and what happens when a limit the change adds or changes is reached. A silence the record cannot answer is an `operator` question, never the builder's `minor` call, because what the system may lose is the operator's decision.

*Routing*: the state runs once per plan, entered where `refine.analyze` would leave for `refine.qgate` or `refine.ready`. Its return evaluation: `operator` questions while the refine relaunch is unused go to `refine.awaiting_answers` as the one batch (at most four parts, the rest listed) and back to `refine.analyze`, whose next exit skips the builder check; `operator` questions after the relaunch was spent are kept as `facts.builder_open_questions`, shown in the outline review and the pull-request body, and the plan continues; otherwise the plan continues to `refine.qgate` or `refine.ready`. Every pass appends a `refine.builder-check` decision record with the counts per sort. While `qgate` is unqualified, or when the task ends `infeasible` or `lost`, the check is recorded `not_run` with its reason and the plan continues: the builder check adds evidence and never holds a plan.

==== Single-Batch Clarification & Relaunch Ceiling

If $\text{confidence} < \text{confidence\_threshold}$:

1. The refine task submits its best-judgment intent record (<<intent-record>>) alongside a single batched `refine_prompt` (`decision:schema[decision-envelope]?`, xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#phase-question-envelope[Question Envelope Schema]) whose parts are at most 4 clarification questions prioritized by `Correctness > Consistency > Completeness > Ambiguity > Non-Duplication`. A question asks for a decision, never for a fact: what the repository or a source can answer the role looks up and records as a `fact` decision of the record with its evidence (the refine role skill states this, class `guidance`). Parts whose answer depends on another part's answer declare it (`depends_on`), so the operator answers the independent ones first and a dependent part only when its premise holds.
2. The engine records the batch as one decision of question records (class `clarification`, xref:07-questions-waivers-operator-influence.adoc#questions-waivers-operator-influence[Questions, Waivers & Operator Influence]) and transitions to `refine.awaiting_answers`, where the adjudication decides it or delivers it to the operator as an operator wait without a time limit.
3. When every question of the batch is answered, by the operator or by a quorum, the engine writes the `## Clarifications` section, records whether every part was answered with its recommended option and nothing else (no own words, no note: `all_recommended: true` on the decision, shown in `facts` of the next pass and in the pull-request body, so an approval without a single change of course is visible), increments `refine_relaunch_count` in `mcp/status.json`, and creates a new `refine.analyze` task **at most once** (`refine_relaunch_count <= 1`).
4. If the second pass still yields $\text{confidence} < \text{confidence\_threshold}$, the engine does **not** loop again (a hypothesis refuted again goes to `refine.hypothesis-refuted` instead, see Open Hypotheses of Epic Plans); it flags `low_confidence_manual_review: true` in `facts` and advances to `refine.qgate` for a lesson-derived plan, otherwise to `refine.ready`.
5. A remediation pass of `refine.analyze` requested by the Q-Gate policy (`fix_requested` of `remediation.qgate-findings`, xref:03-q-gate-and-execute.adoc#_q_gate_validation_loop[Q-Gate Validation Loop]) is never counted by `refine_relaunch_count` and never opens a new `refine.awaiting_answers` round; its questions and a below-threshold score are recorded as `low_confidence_manual_review`.
