= ADR 004: Deadlines from an Injected Clock and Timer
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-26

== Context

A worker generation has three deadlines: the exit the watcher observed, the missing acknowledgement of an offer, and
silence past the bounded wait plus the host's silence grace. The values come from the harness's `WorkerParameters`, so
the detector has no timing literal of its own. A test that waits for a real deadline would sleep for the bounded wait
plus the grace and would fail intermittently when the machine is slow; the scenarios must assert the state at
deadline−1, at the deadline and after it without a sleep.

The question was how the detector reads time and how it is woken at a deadline.

== Decision

`LossDetection` reads every deadline from an injected `InstantSource` and schedules the next one through an injected
`WorkerTimers`. It arms one cancellable timer at the earliest deadline still open and re-arms it whenever a call opens,
closes or clears a deadline. `due(Instant)` is the pure predicate: it answers a signal only when a deadline lies at or
before the instant asked about, so no timer can end a worker earlier than the specification says.

The production `WorkerTimers.system(clock)` measures its delays from the same injected clock, in nanoseconds, so a
deadline carried by an instant with sub-millisecond precision is not shortened; a test supplies `VirtualClock`, which
implements both `InstantSource` and `WorkerTimers` and fires a due timer when the scenario moves the clock.

== Alternatives Considered

1. *Poll the deadlines with `Thread.sleep`.* _Not taken_: it sleeps for the whole bound, is slow and flaky, and
   contradicts the package's sleep-free scenario contract.
2. *Use a `ScheduledExecutorService` directly.* _Not taken_: the wall clock would be the time source and a scenario
   could not move a deadline.
3. *Fire a deadline from the timer without re-checking the clock.* _Not taken_: a spurious wake-up would fire a signal
   before its deadline; the timer must ask `due` at the instant the clock actually reached.

== Consequences

* Every deadline is testable by moving the clock; `SupervisorScenarioTest` asserts the state at deadline−1, at the
  deadline and after it, and no scenario sleeps.
* A new deadline is added by reading it from the worker parameters and arming it in the detector's `reschedule`, not by
  adding a `System.currentTimeMillis()` call.
* The timer fires at the deadline and `due` re-checks it, so a timer that runs late still reports the deadline it
  crossed rather than the delayed instant.
* A timer that wakes before its deadline re-arms at that deadline instead of losing the signal, and the check and the
  effect run under the detector's monitor, so an acknowledgement or an exit that lands at the same instant wins.
