= ADR 003: The Head Is Keyed by the Enrolment Integrity Key, Which the Audit Path Only Reads
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-14

== Context

The specification protects the head `logs/audit.head` and the checkpoint record of a rotation with an HMAC-SHA-256
under the integrity key of the enrolment store. That key (`EnrolmentKey`) is created on the first enrolment write,
under the credentials lock (`EnrolmentKey.creationLock`), and never replaced. The head is written inside the writer's
append, under the audit lock, the leaf of the total lock order
(link:../../../../pm-core/doc/adr/core-store/011-one-total-lock-order-asserted-at-acquisition.adoc[`core-store` ADR 011]):
no lock may be taken after it. Records reach the audit log before any enrolment exists, so the key can be absent, and
a failing keyring can make it unreadable.

== Decision

* `AuditHead` signs the head over `AuditCodec.signedContent` (the canonical JSON of the other fields), and
  `AuditRotation` signs a checkpoint over the UTF-8 bytes of `{"record_count":<record_count>,"head_hash":"<head_hash>"}`,
  both with HMAC-SHA-256 under the enrolment integrity key.
* The audit path only reads the key, through `EnrolmentKey.read()`, which takes no `LockKey`; it never calls
  `readOrCreate()` and never takes the credentials lock. A key once read is kept, since it is never replaced.
* *Key absent* (no enrolment yet): no head is written and no rotation starts, since a checkpoint needs the HMAC; the
  record stands and nothing is logged. The first append after the key exists writes the head over the whole log. The
  verification answers `NotVerified`: nothing is anchored yet. A pending rotation is not completed (see below).
* *Key unreadable* (`SecretStoreException`): no head and no rotation starts, and a pending rotation is not completed
  (see below). Otherwise the record stands and `PM_RUNTIME-123` (`AUDIT_HEAD_NOT_WRITTEN`) names the reason code, never
  the key. The verification answers `NotVerified` naming the code.
* *A pending rotation is completed only over a checkpoint that verifies.* A log whose last line is a checkpoint is a
  rotation interrupted after its checkpoint; the next append completes it (index entry, rename) without a second
  checkpoint. Its last line merely having the event `checkpoint` proves nothing: anyone who can write the log can cut
  its tail and append a line with that event, and completing it would enter the cut in the index, rename it, and let the
  next head be counted over the new log, so `verify()` would answer `Intact`. Before completing it, the rotation
  therefore verifies the checkpoint under the key exactly as the verification does (`AuditRotation.checkpointVerifies`):
  it counts the records before it, names the hash of the line before it and chains to that line, and its HMAC verifies.
  A checkpoint that does not verify is not completed, also when the last index entry already names it: no index entry,
  no rename, and the append is refused, since no record may follow a checkpoint, with `PM_RUNTIME-125`
  (`AUDIT_ROTATION_PENDING`) naming the reason `checkpoint_unverifiable`. The same holds while the key is absent or
  unreadable, since the checkpoint cannot be verified then. The log stays as it is, so the verification reports the
  checkpoint as `link_broken` at its line; a genuine pending rotation is completed by the next append with the key.
* *The record count of a new head* is the previous head's count plus one when the previous head verifies and anchors
  exactly the line the appended record chains to. Otherwise the lines are counted, but a recount never hides a cut: it
  is accepted only without a head file (the first head after the key appears), after a rotation since that head (the
  last index entry chains to the index line it anchored, and the rotated file it names is closed by a checkpoint that
  verifies under the key and still holds the line the head anchored at its position), or when the log still holds the
  line it anchored at its position (the gap a stop between an append and its head leaves). An index entry and a rename
  are plain file operations, so a rotation since the head is accepted only on the evidence of its verifying checkpoint;
  otherwise a cut closed by a forged checkpoint, entered and renamed by hand, would be re-signed by the next head. A log that no longer holds that line, cut short or
  with its last line replaced, gets no new head: the previous head stays, so the verification reports `truncated` or
  `head_mismatch`, and `PM_RUNTIME-123` names the reason `anchored_line_missing`.
* *A stop between a rotation's rename and the record of the same append* leaves no current log and the head of the
  last append before the rotation, anchoring the index line before the new entry and the line before the checkpoint
  of the rotated file. The head is not rewritten for it: the next append heals the state, its record chaining to the
  checkpoint line and its head counted over the new log as a rotation since the head, on the evidence of the verifying
  checkpoint. Until then the verification checks the same evidence and reports the state intact only when every piece
  verifies under the key (link:004-verification-never-repairs-and-survives-rotation.adoc[ADR 004]).
* *A damaged head is never replaced.* The head is replaced atomically, so the only legitimate state without a
  verifying head is an absent head file. A head file that exists but does not parse or does not verify under the key
  gets no new head and no recount: it stays in place, the verification reports `head_mismatch`, and `PM_RUNTIME-123`
  names the reason `head_unverifiable`. Otherwise truncating the log and damaging the head would let the next append
  re-sign the cut.
* *No checkpoint over a cut log.* A checkpoint signs the record count of the log, and the recount after a rotation
  accepts the new log, so a rotation over a cut log would turn the cut into a consistent rotated file. Before it signs
  a checkpoint, the rotation runs `AuditHead.closeCheck`: the log is closed only without a head file, or when the head
  verifies, anchors the current last index line, and the log still holds the line it anchors at its position. A
  refused rotation is logged as `PM_RUNTIME-124` (`AUDIT_ROTATION_FAILED`) with the reason `head_unverifiable` or
  `anchored_line_missing`, and the record goes to the current log, where no head is re-signed over the cut. A pending
  rotation is completed without the check, but only over a checkpoint that verifies under the key (above): a forged
  checkpoint, which anyone can append after a cut, is never completed.
* The verification classifies a head that does not match the log as `head_mismatch`: more records than the head
  anchors, a last line other than the one it names, an HMAC that does not verify, an `index_head_hash` that is not the
  last index line, or no head for a trail with records. An empty log whose head anchors records is `reset`, and so is
  a log whose first line starts at genesis while the index continues the chain. A missing log whose head anchors 0
  records is intact.

== Alternatives Considered

* A step before the audit lock (`prepare()`) that creates the key on the audit path: rejected; the key stays created
  by the first enrolment write alone, and the audit path never reaches the credentials lock at all.

== Consequences

* The credentials lock is never taken after the audit lock, and the one total lock order holds.
* Records written before the first enrolment are chained but not anchored until the first head after the key appears;
  that head counts the whole current log.
* A crash between the creation of the key and the first head leaves a non-empty log without a head, reported as
  `head_mismatch` at the next start. The window is accepted: it is reported, never repaired, and closes with the next
  append.
* A cut of the log stays reported after later appends: no head is re-signed over it and no checkpoint closes it, so a
  cut trail keeps its last verifying head, and its current log grows unrotated, until an operator deals with it. The
  same holds for a damaged head.
* While a forged checkpoint closes the log, or the key is absent or unreadable over a pending rotation, every append is
  refused with `PM_RUNTIME-125` (`checkpoint_unverifiable`): the audit trail takes no record until an operator deals
  with the forged line, or the key can be read again. Refusing is preferred to appending behind a checkpoint or
  completing one that does not verify.
* The refusal holds while the checkpoint is the last complete line. `AuditLog.append` cuts a torn tail before it calls
  the anchor, so a torn tail behind a checkpoint, which no writer of the trail leaves, is cut and its
  `jsonl_tail_repaired` record follows the checkpoint; the log is then no longer pending and the append is not refused.
  Integrity is not affected: the record is chained, a checkpoint that does not verify gets no index entry and no
  rename, no head is written over a log that no longer holds the anchored line, and the verification still reports a
  cut.
* Two gaps stay open: a deleted head file reads as absent, so the next append counts the whole log, and an index entry
  carries no HMAC of its own; a rotation since the head is accepted only through the verifying checkpoint of the
  rotated file the entry names.
* Residual risk, as the specification states it: the anchoring is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. A process that
  holds the integrity key (on macOS, anything that runs the `pm-mcpd` binary; on hosts without a keyring, any process
  of the user) can forge a consistent head.
