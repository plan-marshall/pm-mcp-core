= ADR 009: The Wait for the Launch Report Has a Bound of Its Own, Apart from the Budget
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-18

== Context

A start of `SubprocessJobRunner` is synchronous: it answers only once the launch report, the first line of the job log,
is read and the post-spawn record is written (ADR 005). It is also the engine port `JobStarter` (ADR 004), so a caller
of the port waits for it. The wait ran until the report arrived, an output line arrived, the process exited or the
budget of the job passed, and it did not look at a cancel. A launcher that hangs before it writes its report, for
example while it applies the confinement, therefore held the start for the whole budget, up to 1800 seconds or more,
and a cancel during the wait was noticed only after it.

The specification fixes no bound for the launch report: it says the launcher writes it right before `execve` and that a
launcher that fails before `execve` writes none and exits `64`, `126` or `127`. It names no consequence for a launcher
that is alive and silent.

== Decision

* The wait for the launch report is bounded by `REPORT_WAIT`, 10 seconds from the spawn, and never beyond the budget of
  the job when that is shorter. The launcher does only `setpgid`, the confinement and the write of one line before
  `execve`, so the bound is generous for a healthy launcher and short against a hung one. It is a compiled constant,
  stated in the Timeouts Specification (Launch report wait), not a configuration key.
* The wait ends at once when the job is cancelled: it reads the cause of the running job, as the thread that waits for
  the job's end does.
* A wait that ends without a report, whether by the bound, by the cancel or by the exit of the launcher, hands the job
  to the thread that waits for its end exactly as a launcher that wrote no report is handled today: the record keeps the
  write-ahead state without a PID, the start answers `Started(jobId)`, and the job is settled by its exit, the cause of
  a cancel or its budget. The runner does not terminate the job on the bound. A launcher that is alive and silent past
  the bound is not known to be broken, and a termination on a guess would kill a healthy job on a loaded host; the
  specification gives the bound no such consequence. A crash of the runtime in that state is covered by the recovery
  rule for a record without a PID (rule (c)).
* The interrupt handling of the wait is unchanged: an interrupt ends the wait after the current read and is restored.

== Alternatives Considered

1. *Terminate and settle the job (`settleUnsupervised`) when the bound passes without a report.*
_Rejected_: the specification names no consequence for a silent live launcher, and the termination of a job the
runner cannot identify by a report rests on the PID of the process handle alone; further reasons not recorded.
2. *Keep the budget as the bound.*
_Rejected_: the start, and with it the engine port, would be held for the whole budget by a launcher that never writes
its report.

== Consequences

* A hung launcher no longer blocks the start for the budget, and a cancel during the wait takes effect at once.
* A launcher that writes its report after the bound is not recorded as a post-spawn record; its report is still
  stripped from the job's output when the job has ended.
* The answer of the start for a job whose launcher wrote no report is unchanged, `Started(jobId)` with a record that
  has no PID; only the time the start takes to give it is bounded.
