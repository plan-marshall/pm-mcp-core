= ADR 004: One Runner Class Implements `JobRunner` and `JobStarter`
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-17

== Context

`pm-core` declares two interfaces through which a job is started: the engine port `JobStarter` of `core.spi`, which
starts the job of a `job` state of the workflow for a state and a primitive and answers a `job_id` of the grammar
`^[bcgp]-[0-9a-f]{16}$`, and the service `JobRunner<R, C>` of `core.service`, which starts a job of any kind or a probe,
cancels a job by its cause and reads its record (ADR core-service/001 of `pm-core`). Both start the same jobs: verified,
recorded, launched through `pm-exec`, terminated and settled the same way. A workflow primitive does not yet carry the
job spec its job would need: which build verb, which tool, which arguments.

== Decision

* `SubprocessJobRunner` implements `JobRunner<JobRequest, ProbeCandidate>` and `JobStarter` in one class; both ports
  reach the same verification, records, launch, termination and settlement.
* `JobStarter` is not changed. `JobStarter.start(StartRequest)` maps the state and its primitive to a job through the
  caller's `PrimitiveJobs`; a primitive for which it answers no job spec, and a worker, is refused
  `primitive_without_job` as a value. The id it answers keeps the port's grammar.
* The runner starts no worker: `start` refuses the kind `llm`, whose launch belongs to the supervisor.
* The runner verifies a job in a lock transaction it opens and closes itself before the launch, so the enrolment lock
  of the verification is never held while a job is launched or awaited.
* The admission of a job and the stop of the admission for the drain are decided under one lock (`stopAdmitting`), so
  a job admitted before the stop is among the running jobs the drain waits for; a job asked for afterwards is recorded
  `queued`, never spawned, and settled `killed` without a signal when the drain closes.
* A job leaves the running jobs only after its settlement has run, on every path the runner owns: a launch that
  cannot be composed or a launcher that cannot be started settles it as the launcher's exit `127`, unless a cancel was
  answered `true` before the settlement closed the job's cancel door (the first thing it does, one atomic point that
  decides both the answer and the cause it reads), whose cause then decides it as on every other path (`cancelled`,
  row 2; `timed_out`, row 3, for the budget; `killed`, row 4, for the drain); a cancel after the door closed is answered `false`, so the answer `true` and the fate by the cause are the
  same fact. The door is the one arbiter of every job, not of the unlaunched job alone: the runner closes it when it
  observes the end of the process (the waiting thread when it sees the exit, and the settlement from the observed end
  after the process group was terminated) and reads the cause then, so a cancel answered `true` before the door closed
  decides the fate by its cause (row 2, 3 or 4) whatever the exit status, also when the process had exited by itself
  before the waiting thread was scheduled, and a cancel after it is answered `false`; a post-spawn record
  that cannot be written, or a waiting thread that cannot be started, terminates its process group, awaits the end of
  the process and settles it from that end before the failure is passed on to the caller of the start; and a failure
  of the waiting thread itself (an exception or error of the output parser, of the settled record or of the
  termination protocol) is followed by a fallback settlement. After the end of the process was observed, the fallback
  settles the job from the same observation without the parser's finding and never calls the parser again; before it,
  the fallback terminates the group again by the PID of the process handle, destroys the process through its handle
  when the protocol fails or its signal is refused, awaits its end and settles the job from what it observed then. The
  failure is passed on to the thread's handler and logged (`PM_RUNTIME-208`). The runner keeps the invariant with
  `try`/`finally` and a flag that records whether the settlement has started, not by catching a generic exception.
* What is guaranteed is that the settlement ran, not that it succeeded: the job is settled, or its settlement stopped
  at a failed step (a fate that cannot be written, `PM_RUNTIME-207`), or a fault of the lock manager inside the
  settlement was passed on. In the last two cases the record stays `running` and the recovery of the next runtime
  start settles it. A failure of the fallback settlement itself replaces the failure it follows.
* No row of the outcome derivation names the termination of a job whose post-spawn record failed, and the runner
  invents none: it records no termination cause, and the evidence decides. A process ended by the runner's signal is
  `killed` by row 4, which the specification names for a process ended by any other signal and which is the closest
  row; a process that had exited before the signal is decided by its exit, and a cancel answered `true` before the
  settlement closed the job's cancel door, which it does once the end of the process is observed, by its cause; a
  cancel after the door closed is answered `false`. The settled record carries the PID and the process start of the launch report, because the process ran.

== Alternatives Considered

Alternatives not recorded beyond those of ADR core-service/001 of `pm-core`.

== Consequences

* The engine can start the job of a state as soon as the caller's `PrimitiveJobs` answers a job spec for its primitive;
  until then the port refuses it with `primitive_without_job`, and no job is recorded.
* A job started through either port is the same job: one record, one log, one cancel.
* A later supervisor launches workers itself and records them in the same job record store.
* `recordOf(jobId)` and `awaitSettled(jobId, timeout)` answer the settled record of a job that left the running jobs,
  from a bounded window of the most recent settled records (ADR 012); the durable record of an older one is read from
  its store. A record whose fate could not be written, or whose settlement met a fault of the lock manager, stays
  `running` until the next runtime start.
* A job whose output parser fails is settled without the parser's finding: an exit `0` is then `indeterminate` by
  row 8, a non-zero exit decided by row 10.
