= ADR 008: Process-Group Signals Through FFM Behind a Replaceable Primitive
:nofooter:

== Status

Accepted

== Date

2026-10-17

== Context

A job is the leader of its own process group, whose id is the job PID, and the termination protocol signals the group
(`kill(-pgid, sig)`), probes it (`kill(-pgid, 0)`) and, in the window before the group exists, the PID itself (PM-EXEC-4,
PM-TECH-4). Java's `Process` and `ProcessHandle` signal one process, never a group, and they report no `errno`, so
`ESRCH` (no such process or group) cannot be told apart from a refused signal. The recovery after a runtime start must
never signal a job it cannot identify, and its tests have to prove that no signal was sent. The module has bound C
library functions through FFM before (`MacSecurity` of `runtime.credentials`), and their downcall descriptors are
registered for native image in the reachability metadata of the assembly in the repository `plan-marshall-mcp`.

== Decision

* `ProcessGroupSignals` is the lowest signalling primitive of the job runtime: `signalGroup(pgid, signal)` and
  `signalProcess(pid, signal)` answer what the call answered as a value, `Delivered`, `NoSuchProcess` for `ESRCH` or
  `Refused(errno)`, for the signals `PROBE` (0), `SIGKILL` and `SIGTERM`.
* `ProcessGroupSignals.system()` binds `kill(2)` of the C library through FFM (`Linker`, `captureCallState("errno")`),
  no JNI library. It refuses a PID of 1 or less and one above the range of `pid_t` with `IllegalArgumentException`,
  because such an id would address every process, the caller's own group or `init`.
* Everything that signals a job goes through the interface: `TerminationProtocol`, and the recovery step of
  `runtime.start`. A test replaces it with a recording fake and asserts at the lowest signalling call, also that none was
  made.
* `TerminationProtocol` reads `ESRCH` as a fact: `ESRCH` from the group and then from the PID means the process has
  already ended, so nothing further is signalled and the report ends `GROUP_NOT_REACHED`; `ESRCH` from the group alone is
  the window before `setpgid`, retried against the group.
* The native-image metadata of the `kill` downcall (two `int` parameters, an `int` result, `errno` captured) is not
  registered in `pm-runtime`. It is registered in the reachability metadata of the assembly in `plan-marshall-mcp`, where
  the descriptors of `MacSecurity` are, and verified by the native build of `pm-mcpd` against the deployed `SNAPSHOT`.
  An earlier sentence of the plan's outline that the metadata travels with `pm-runtime` was wrong (Q-Gate finding
  1a85eb of PLAN-20). The registration is a follow-up in `plan-marshall-mcp`.

== Alternatives Considered

1. *Signal through `Process.destroy()` and `ProcessHandle.destroyForcibly()`.*
_Rejected_: they signal one process, not its group, and report no `errno`, so neither a group with descendants nor the
window before the group exists can be handled; further reasons not recorded.
2. *Register the downcall descriptor in `META-INF/native-image/de.planmarshall/pm-runtime/`.*
_Rejected_: the FFM descriptors of the module are registered in the assembly (finding 1a85eb).

== Consequences

* The recovery tests assert zero `kill()` calls for the rules that must send none.
* Until the follow-up in `plan-marshall-mcp` registers the descriptor, a native `pm-mcpd` cannot signal a job; the JVM
  runtime and every test of this repository can.
* A real `ProcessGroupSignals.system()` is exercised against real process groups by `TerminationProtocolTest` and
  `JobRecoveryKilledRuntimeIT`, on Linux.
