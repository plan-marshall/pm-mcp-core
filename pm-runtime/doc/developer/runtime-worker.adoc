== Purpose

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14], link:{req}/01-architecture.adoc#PM-ARCH-8[PM-ARCH-8]

Package `de.planmarshall.runtime.worker` is the supervisor of the worker processes. One supervisor serves one project
and one role. It starts the workers a request asks for as far as the role's form and `count`, the start-failure guard,
the verification-record gate and the machine's model slots admit them, keeps each started generation in a registry, and
drives that generation through its deadlines, its end path and its recycling. It keeps no launch composition and no
process of its own: it composes through `runtime.launch` and starts through `runtime.job`, and it reaches the task
protocol through the module-boundary adapter `WorkerProtocol`.

The package serves the three requirements above. Its start path also enforces
link:{req}/13-client-profiles.adoc#PM-CLIENT-5[PM-CLIENT-5] and link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-8[PM-SEC-8] through
the verification-record gate, its off-track detection serves link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-7[PM-WORK-7], and its
scenario tests serve link:{req}/12-testing.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]. The model slots are
link:{req}/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14]'s.

The decisions of the package are recorded in `pm-runtime/doc/adr/runtime-worker/`:

== Core Types

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/01-architecture.adoc#PM-ARCH-8[PM-ARCH-8]

[horizontal]
link:{src}/WorkerSupervisor.java[`WorkerSupervisor`]:: The supervisor of one project and role. It owns the registry of
started generations, serializes starts, bounds the registry by the role's form and `count`, and exposes
`stopAdmitting()`, `quiet()` and `awaitQuiet(Instant)` for the drain. A start answers `StartOutcome.Started` or
`StartOutcome.Declined`; a declined start names a `WorkerStartRefusal` and leaves the caller's task queued.
link:{src}/SupervisorEnvironment.java[`SupervisorEnvironment`]:: The collaborators and installation values the supervisor
is built with: the project and its enrolment, the runtime base and home, the relay socket, the effective client profile,
the landed launch and job contracts, the state writer, the job tokens, the model slots, the protocol adapter, the audit
sink, the clock, the timers, the lock manager and the two seams `StartGuard` and `EligibilityGate`.
link:{src}/WorkerGeneration.java[`WorkerGeneration`]:: One worker generation: the immutable facts it was started with
(generation id, role, harness, model, form, working directory, plan, configuration digest, spawn instant, job id, model
slot) and the state the supervision advances (lifecycle, leased task, served tasks, idle wake-ups, context tokens,
worktree mark). It is an immutable snapshot; `@With` methods produce the successor, and equality is by generation id
alone.
link:{src}/WorkerLifecycle.java[`WorkerLifecycle`]:: The closed lifecycle state of a generation and its mapping onto the
job record's `WorkerState.State`. The table allows `STARTING` to `WAITING`, `ENDING`, `LOST` or `START_FAILED`;
`WAITING` to `OFFERED`, `ENDING` or `LOST`; `OFFERED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `WAITING`, `ENDING` or `LOST`;
`ACKNOWLEDGED` to `EXECUTING`, `CONSULTING`, `WAITING`, `ENDING` or `LOST`; `EXECUTING` to `CONSULTING`, `WAITING`,
`ENDING` or `LOST`; `CONSULTING` to `EXECUTING`, `HOLDING`, `WAITING`, `ENDING` or `LOST`; `HOLDING` to `EXECUTING`,
`ENDING` or `LOST`; and `ENDING` to `ENDED` or `LOST`. Every other transition, and every transition out of a terminal
state, is refused. The protocol's `SUBMITTED` is folded into `WAITING`, because a submitted task ends the attempt and
the worker waits again.
link:{src}/WorkerStartRequest.java[`WorkerStartRequest`]:: What one start needs beyond the environment: the resolved
effective role, the working-directory kind and path, the plan of a plan-worktree start, the contract envelope of a fresh
worker's task, the prompt facts and the optional session to resume.
link:{src}/WorkerStartRefusal.java[`WorkerStartRefusal`]:: The closed set of reasons the supervisor declines a start
itself: `ROLE_GUARD_HELD`, `HARNESS_INELIGIBLE`, `NO_MODEL_SLOT` and `LAUNCH_REFUSED`. The set is disjoint from the
launch's own refusals, which the runtime records on the refused job.

== The Start Path and the Generations

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14],
link:{req}/13-client-profiles.adoc#PM-CLIENT-5[PM-CLIENT-5], link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-8[PM-SEC-8],
link:{req}/01-architecture.adoc#PM-ARCH-8[PM-ARCH-8]

A start is one ordered decision, and each step can decline it. The order is the code's, not the caller's:

. `StartGuard.holds(role, harness)` — a held guard declines every request of the call with `ROLE_GUARD_HELD`.
. `EligibilityGate.admits(role, harness)` — a harness whose verification record does not establish the role's claims
  declines every request with `HARNESS_INELIGIBLE`.
. The role's form and `count` — a `fresh` role admits one worker per queued task, a `warm` role one worker per working
  directory, never more than `count` workers of that form at once. Requests beyond the role's room are not attempted and
  have no outcome.
. `ModelSlots.claim` — an admission that grants no lease (queued or deferred, including the second writer worker of a
  plan) declines the start with `NO_MODEL_SLOT`; the task stays queued.
. `HarnessVerifier.verify` and `LaunchComposer.compose` — a refusal before the spawn is recorded through
  `WorkerStarter.refuseWorker` as the refused job, the claimed slot is released, and the start is declined with
  `LAUNCH_REFUSED`.
. `WorkerStarter.startWorker` — a started job registers the generation; a runner refusal or a runner exception releases
  the slot and declines the start.

The registration fixes the generation's facts, its `STARTING` lifecycle, its job id and the slot it holds for its whole
life, uses the budget key `worker:<role>` with the role's floor budget, and logs `WORKER_GENERATION_STARTED` exactly
once. `WorkerSupervisor.replaced(WorkerGeneration)` publishes a successor snapshot so the next decision sees the new
lifecycle and terminal state; `generation(id)` and `generations()` read the registry in start order.

Starts of one supervisor are serialized on the supervisor's own monitor, so two concurrent callers never start the
role's workers beyond its count. A successful start is the only path that keeps the claimed slot; every refusal path
releases it. The terminal worker state is written at the end path, not here (<<_ending_fencing_and_drain>>).

The start path is ADR 001's (one supervisor per project and role) and ADR 002's (the landed launch contracts and the
two seams). The job record's worker block is ADR 003's.

== Detection

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/13-client-profiles.adoc#PM-CLIENT-2[PM-CLIENT-2],
link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-7[PM-WORK-7]

link:{src}/LossDetection.java[`LossDetection`] owns the three deadlines of one generation and keeps no timing literal:

* *Exit* — the per-worker watcher observed the process exit. An exit after the first call is
  `LossReason.EXIT`; an exit before the first call is a start failure, not a loss, and `exit(Instant)` answers empty so
  the start-failure guard counts it (<<_the_start_failure_guard_and_the_start_spacing>>).
* *Acknowledgement missed* — an offer (or a fresh worker's first call) was not acknowledged within the host's
  acknowledgement deadline, where the acknowledgement is not marked informational and the role has a connection.
* *Silence* — the worker made no call and holds no open bounded wait for longer than the host's silence limit. A held
  wait is an open call and suppresses the signal; the answer re-arms it.

The detector is armed from the spawn, so a worker that never makes its first call counts as silent from there. Every
value comes from the harness's `WorkerParameters` and the injected clock. `due(Instant)` answers a signal only when a
deadline lies at or before the instant it is asked about; it never ends a worker early. The detector arms and re-arms
one `WorkerTimers.Cancellable` at the earliest open deadline and hands the fired signal to its consumer.

A `zero_tool` worker has no MCP connection: it receives no offer, is never silent and produces neither an
acknowledgement nor a silence signal. Its exit and its worker budget alone decide its task. A live worker that is alive
but makes no progress is not a detection case: that is the off-track signal of PM-WORK-7, which the task protocol owns
and which ends the worker through `EndCause.OFF_TRACK` (see `agent_started` below).

link:{src}/LossSignal.java[`LossSignal`] carries the reason, the generation, the instant the loss was observed, the
deadline that was crossed (equal to the observation instant for an exit), whether the first call was made, and the
leased task. link:{src}/LossReason.java[`LossReason`] maps the reason to the job record's `WorkerState.EndReason` and to
the attempt end the queue task carries: `EXIT` → `EXITED_WITHOUT_SUBMIT`, `ACK_MISSED` and `SILENCE` → `WORKER_LOST`.
Every deadline rides an injected clock and injected timers (ADR 004).

== Ending, Fencing and Drain

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-2[PM-WORK-2],
link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-7[PM-WORK-7]

link:{src}/WorkerEnding.java[`WorkerEnding`] is the one end path of every generation, whatever the cause:

. The end is requested, so the transport answers the worker's next wait with `end`.
. When the worker does not exit within the termination grace (`TerminationProtocol.GRACE_PERIOD`, five seconds), the
  force reaches the process only through `WorkerStarter.cancel`, which is the job runtime's termination protocol. The
  supervisor starts no process and sends no signal itself.
. After the exit the generation is fenced through `WorkerProtocol.fence`, its job token revoked through
  `JobTokenStore.revoke`, its model slot released, and its terminal `WorkerState` written through the state writer.
  The end is logged once as `WORKER_GENERATION_ENDED`, and a successor is started when work remains.

A detected loss takes the same path but forces at once: `end(generation, LossSignal)` does not wait for the grace. A
drain, a pause at once and an off-track restart also reach the forced end through the drain participant or the
off-track detector.

link:{src}/EndCause.java[`EndCause`] is the closed set of causes and the fact each writes:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1"]
|===
|Cause |Wire name |Job record end reason |Spends a re-offer |Terminal state

|`TASK_DONE` |`task_done` |`TASK_DONE` |no |`ENDED`
|`RECYCLE` |`recycle` |`IDLE_RECYCLE` |no |`ENDED`
|`DRAIN` |`drain` |`DRAIN` |no |`ENDED`
|`PAUSE_AT_ONCE` |`pause_at_once` |`WITHDRAWN` |no |`ENDED`
|`LOST` |`lost` |`EXITED` (refined by the `LossSignal`) |yes |`LOST`
|`OFF_TRACK` |`off_track` |`WITHDRAWN` |no |`ENDED`
|`START_FAILURE` |`start_failure` |`START_FAILURE` |no |`ENDED`
|===

A pause at once reaches the runner with `JobRunner.CancelCause.PLAN_PAUSED_NOW`; every other end reaches it with
`SUPERVISOR_END`. The runner derives the job's fate and records the ended worker's usage as unmeasured.

link:{src}/WorkerDrainParticipant.java[`WorkerDrainParticipant`] makes the supervisor the runtime's drain participant
`workers`. It stops admitting starts, ends every idle warm generation with `DRAIN`, waits until no generation runs (at
most the drain deadline), then forces the remaining workers. Their tasks stay queued and are offered again after the
next start, without counting a re-offer. The participant closes the supervisor's timers after the drain.

The end path is ADR 003's (the terminal worker state in the job record) and ADR 004's (the grace is a timer on the
injected clock).

== Recycling

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14]

link:{src}/WorkerRecycling.java[`WorkerRecycling`] decides the four recycle triggers and the idle yield. A recycle never
interrupts a task: every decision hands the generation to `WorkerEnding` through `EndCause.RECYCLE`, so the one end path
fences, revokes, releases the slot, writes the terminal state and starts a successor when work remains. Every recycle
logs `WORKER_GENERATION_RECYCLED` exactly once with its link:{src}/RecycleCause.java[`RecycleCause`].

* *Idle* (`IDLE`) — a warm generation that ended its bounded waits without a task `min(host.worker.idle_wakeups,
  role.recycling.idle_wakeups)` times in a row. The role can only lower the host's bound. A generation holding a lease
  (a consulting worker or one holding for an answer) is not idle: its `wait_again` counts no wake-up.
* *Token budget* (`TOKEN_BUDGET`) — the context of the generation's latest turn reached the role's token budget. Only
  the latest turn's context is read, never a run total; an unmeasured context is ignored. A generation that has closed
  no task yet is recycled only while it holds its task for an answer, because a worker's starting context can exceed a
  small budget.
* *Tasks per generation* (`TASKS_PER_GENERATION`) — the generation closed the role's `recycling.tasks_per_generation`.
* *Configuration changed* (`CONFIGURATION_CHANGED`) — the role's current configuration digest, carried by the wait,
  differs from the digest the generation was started with. This trigger also clears the role's start-failure guard on
  its harness.
* *Idle yield* (`IDLE_YIELD`) — another role's queued task waits for a model slot. The longest-idle warm generation that
  is `WAITING`, holds no lease and serves another role is recycled, so idle warm workers never starve queued work.

A `fresh` generation is never recycled: it ends after its one task. The idle yield and the lease-aware idle rule are
ADR 008's; the deadlines and the timers are ADR 004's.

== The Start-Failure Guard and the Start Spacing

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-6[PM-WORK-6]

link:{src}/StartFailureGuard.java[`StartFailureGuard`] counts the consecutive starts of one role on one harness that
failed before the worker's first call, or that the runtime refused before the spawn. A `zero_tool` worker makes no call,
so it is never counted; the supervisor consults the guard for a role that has a connection only.

* *Count* — `startFailed(role, harness, logTail)` counts one failure per role and harness. The
  link:{src}/StartFailureReport.java[`StartFailureReport`] bounds the worker's own error text to the job record's
  `MAX_HARNESS_ERROR` tail and redacts a minted job token, a minted worker secret, the user information of a URI and an
  e-mail address. The report goes to the audit log as `workerStartFailed` and to the assembly's clients; it is never
  rendered to a model.
* *Hold* — at `StartFailureGuard.HOLD_THRESHOLD` (three) consecutive failures the guard holds. It logs
  `WORKER_START_GUARD_HELD` and closes the role's queued and new tasks through `WorkerProtocol.closeUnavailable` as
  `lost` with the reason `role_unavailable`, with their conservative fallback (PM-WORK-6).
* *Probe* — after `PROBE_BACKOFF` (1,800 seconds) the next start of the role on that harness is admitted as one probe
  instead of a hold. A probe that failed restarts the backoff; a probe that never produced a started worker — the caller
  declined it, or the start threw before the worker started — restarts it too (`probeDeclined`), so a consumed probe
  never holds the guard forever; a probe's first call clears the guard.
* *Clear* — `firstCall(role, harness)` clears the count on the first call of any worker of the role on the harness.
  `clear` does the same when the role's configuration changes or the harness is enrolled again.

The count lives in a link:{src}/StartFailureState.java[`StartFailureState`] per role and harness. It is rebuilt from the
supervisor's generation registry when the guard is constructed, so a guard built beside a supervisor that already
started workers resumes the count of the generations that exited before their first call instead of counting from zero.

link:{src}/HarnessStartSpacing.java[`HarnessStartSpacing`] is the machine-wide spacing of one harness. It is shared by
every supervisor of the runtime and keys on the harness's client id alone, so it spans all projects. The next start waits
until the previous worker of the harness made its first call or ended, at most `SPACING_BOUND` (30 seconds). Past the
bound the next start proceeds, so the spacing only ever delays a start and never refuses one. A collision that still
makes a worker exit before its first call is a start failure and counts for the guard. The production instance is the
compiled bound; a test lowers the bound instead of sleeping.

The guard and the spacing are part of the supervisor's start ownership (ADR 001).

== `agent_started`

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-7[PM-WORK-7], link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3]

link:{src}/AgentStartedDetector.java[`AgentStartedDetector`] detects that a worker started a second agent. On every pass
it refreshes the generation's recorded process tree through `ProcessTreeTracker`, reads each member's executable path and
digest, and compares them with every enrolled harness executable and with the installed operator binary at
`bin/pm-operator` under the runtime home.

The comparison is by path *and* digest, never by name: a binary that carries an enrolled executable's name with other
content is not the enrolled executable, and a process that runs a program the supervisor never enrolled is not reported.
The tree is the one `ProcessTreeTracker` records for the worker's process, so a member that leaves the process group or
starts a new session is still examined — that detachment is what a started agent does.

link:{src}/ProcessExecutables.java[`ProcessExecutables`] answers a decision, never an exception: `Found` carries the
absolute path and the `sha256:` digest, `Absent` means the process is gone or the operating system answered no
executable, and `NotRegular` means the path is absent or is not a regular file (a binary deleted after it was started,
for example). link:{src}/SystemProcessExecutables.java[`SystemProcessExecutables`] is the operating system's own answer:
`/proc/<pid>/exe` on Linux and the `libproc` `proc_pidpath` downcall on macOS, each digested through
`HarnessVerifier.digestExecutable`. Nothing is read from `/proc/<pid>/cmdline` or from `ps` output, and no path is
resolved through `PATH`: a command line is the process's own claim and a `PATH` lookup answers the name the process was
asked for, while this reading must answer the file it runs. The `proc_pidpath` descriptor is registered in the module's
reachability metadata next to `kill`; a platform that is neither Linux nor macOS answers `Absent` for every PID.

At the first match the detector logs `AGENT_STARTED_DETECTED`, reports the fact through
`WorkerProtocol.agentStarted(generationId, queueTaskId, attempt, executable, at)` with the process's start instant, and
ends the worker through `EndCause.OFF_TRACK`. A generation is reported at most once, so one started agent never ends a
worker twice. The protocol stays the owner of the signal entry, the activity entry, the `security` audit record and the
re-offer; the supervisor reports the fact and never writes those records itself. The signal's threshold is `1` and its
action `restart`, fixed in the detector — it reads no role artifact, which is how those two values hold whatever the
artifact names for its other off-track signals.

The detection is ADR 005's (read the executable path and digest, never trust a command line); the protocol ownership is
ADR 009's.

== The Verification-Record Gate

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/13-client-profiles.adoc#PM-CLIENT-5[PM-CLIENT-5], link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-8[PM-SEC-8]

link:{src}/ProfileWorkerEligibility.java[`ProfileWorkerEligibility`] is the pure predicate the supervisor consults before
every start. It decides whether a machine or project profile's claims may be believed on the platform the runtime runs
on. The supervisor's `EligibilityGate` reads only `admits(role, harness)`; a report names the refusal from `verdict()`.

No value of a profile is believed because the profile says so:

* A *shipped* profile is trusted — the release compiled its document.
* A profile that makes *no claim* (`worker.eligible: false` and no `zero_tool` class) passes untouched.
* A claimed profile makes exactly two claims. `worker.eligible: true` is honoured only when the record carries `faults`
  and `egress` as `passed`. A non-null `zero_tool` class is honoured only when `classes` and `isolation` passed. Beyond
  the claim's own stages, every stage the record carries must be `passed`: a record that measured a failure anywhere
  does not establish the profile's claims.
* The gate refuses a claimed profile with `RECORD_ABSENT`, `OTHER_PLATFORM`, `CLAIM_STAGE_ABSENT`, `STAGE_FAILED` or
  `STAGE_NOT_RUN`. The stages the claim rests on are examined first, so the refusal names the stage that decided the
  claim where one exists. link:{src}/EligibilityRefusal.java[`EligibilityRefusal`] carries the closed reason, the stage
  (when a stage decided it) and the profile's client id; link:{src}/EligibilityVerdict.java[`EligibilityVerdict`] has
  exactly two members, `Eligible` and `Refused`, so a caller cannot read a third state into it.

link:{src}/RuntimePlatform.java[`RuntimePlatform`] is the operating system and architecture in the record's spelling
(`linux`, `darwin`; `x86_64`, `aarch64`), normalised from `os.name` and `os.arch`. It compares the record's `platform`
with the machine the runtime runs on; the record's `platform.confinement` is not part of the value, because confinement
is decided per job by the launcher. A record written on another operating system or architecture refuses, leaving the
profile usable for detection and rendering but its harness ineligible for workers.

The egress-acceptance half of the specification is deliberately not here: whether the operator accepts unenforced egress
is a machine-level decision the egress owner holds, and this gate only requires the record's `egress` stage to be
`passed`. The gate is ADR 006's; the controls its claims rest on are ADR 007's.

== The Three Verification Controls

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/13-client-profiles.adoc#PM-CLIENT-5[PM-CLIENT-5], link:{req}/12-testing.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]

The record's stages attest three controls built in this package. The stage runner that uses them is the verification's,
not the gate's; the gate only reads the stages the controls produced.

link:{src}/ProbeWorkspace.java[`ProbeWorkspace`]:: The fresh probe workspace of one verification run: a temporary
directory below the JVM's temporary directory with mode `0700`, outside `<PM_MCP_BASE>` (which no job may read or write),
and the only project root in the probe worker's write set. It carries the run's canary — an unpredictable token generated
afresh for every workspace and written to `canary.txt`; a worker that names it has left its own configuration. Beside the
workspace the control creates an outside read path (`pm-mcp-probe-outside-*` with `outside.txt`) that the worker may read
but must never write. `observe(output)` returns `Observation(canaryReturned, outsideWrites)`; `stayedInside()` is true
only when the output named no canary and the run created, changed or removed nothing outside. The workspace and its
outside path are removed together on close, on a clean run and on a detected violation alike.
link:{src}/DecoyServers.java[`DecoyServers`]:: The loopback-only decoy MCP server and decoy model endpoint of one run.
Each binds `127.0.0.1` on an ephemeral port — `/mcp` and `/models` — and records the requests a worker made of it as
`METHOD path`; `touched()` is true when a worker reached it. The servers never leave loopback, so a decoy cannot be
reached from the network. A worker that lists or calls a decoy tool, or that sends a request to the decoy endpoint, has
left its own configuration.
link:{src}/VerifyingBinaryCheck.java[`VerifyingBinaryCheck`]:: The verifying-binary control of the load-time check. It
runs the installed binary with the fixed argument vector `--version`, never with an argument taken from the record, and
waits at most ten seconds. The output is read as the version and the commit, and only an exact match of both is accepted.
A binary that answers nothing or answers with fewer than two fields is `Unreadable`; a different build is `Mismatch`.

The controls are ADR 007's (built in `runtime.worker`, no separate watch item). The workspace's isolation and the decoy
hits are what the verification's `isolation` and `classes` stages record.

== Ports and the Assembly Adapter

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/01-architecture.adoc#PM-ARCH-8[PM-ARCH-8], link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3],
link:{req}/02-workflow.adoc#PM-WF-14[PM-WF-14]

Every collaborator of the supervisor is the landed type of its package, and no port stands between the supervisor and
`runtime.launch`, `runtime.job` or `pm-core`. `SupervisorEnvironment` carries the `HarnessVerifier`, the `LaunchComposer`,
the `SubprocessJobRunner` adapter, the `JobTokenStore`, the `SkillSource`, the effective `LoadedProfile` and the
`AuditSink`. Three adapter seams exist, and each exists for a stated reason:

* `SupervisorEnvironment.WorkerStarter` — the start-path adapter over `SubprocessJobRunner`. `startWorker` forwards the
  landed start, `refuseWorker` the landed refusal, and `cancel` the landed termination call, so the supervisor calls
  exactly the landed paths and a test can record the composed `WorkerStart` and `WorkerStart.Refusal` without a spawn.
* `SupervisorEnvironment.WorkerStateWriter` — the terminal-state writer over `JobRecordStore`. It parses the generation
  id as a job id, requires the job to be of kind `LLM`, reads the current worker block through `WorkerStateCodec`, and
  writes `withState`, `withStateAt`, `withEndReason` and `withStartFailure` back through the one `writeWorker` point in
  one lock transaction. The supervisor and the transport never each parse the block.
* `WorkerProtocol` — the module-boundary contract to the task protocol. `pm-runtime` may not reference `pm-workflow`, so
  the assembly `pm-mcp-server` adapts `de.planmarshall.workflow.task.TaskProtocol` to this interface. The protocol stays
  the owner of the fences, the re-offers and the `agent_started` security record: the supervisor reports the fact and
  never writes those records itself.

`StartGuard` and `EligibilityGate` are the two further seams of the start path. Their production implementations live
beside the supervisor (`StartFailureGuard`, `ProfileWorkerEligibility`); their `open()` defaults hold for no role and
admit every role, which is the supervisor's behaviour before they are wired.

`ModelSlots` is the adapter over the landed `runtime.launch.ModelSlotScheduler`. `claim` maps `Granted` to a lease and
both `Queued` and `Deferred` to no lease, so a queued request keeps its FIFO place and a plan's second writer worker
declines the start instead of waiting. `release`, `nextWaitingRole` and `releasePlan` carry the rest of the scheduler's
surface into the supervisor; the scheduler's admission transaction is the single decision.

`WorkerCalls` is the supervisor's inbound API, consumed by the assembly's transport. It hands over a heartbeat, an opened
wait with the role's current configuration digest, a completed turn with its context size, a closed task, a plan paused
at once and a harness enrolled anew. A wait answer is one of the protocol's three: `Offered`, `WaitAgain` or `End`. Every
call names the generation it concerns; the supervisor resolves it from its registry and ignores a call of a generation it
does not know.

The landed-contract decision and its two seams are ADR 002's; the protocol adapter is ADR 009's; the one writer block is
ADR 003's.

== The Failure Modes

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/16-model-work.adoc#PM-WORK-3[PM-WORK-3], link:{req}/12-testing.adoc#PM-TEST-4[PM-TEST-4]

[cols="1,2,2"]
|===
|Failure |What observes it |What the supervisor does

|The launch is refused before its spawn
|`HarnessVerifier` or `LaunchComposer`
|Records the refused job through `WorkerStarter.refuseWorker`, releases the claimed slot and declines with
`LAUNCH_REFUSED`. The start-failure guard counts the failure; the task stays queued.

|The worker exits before its first call
|`LossDetection.exit` answers empty and sets `startFailure`
|The supervisor records the start failure (`LossDetection.startFailure()`); the assembly counts it through the guard and
the task takes its start-failure path. A `zero_tool` worker is never counted.

|The worker exits after its first call
|`LossDetection.exit`
|`LOST` with `LossReason.EXIT`; the task is offered again within its re-offer budget.

|An offer is not acknowledged
|`LossDetection.due` at the acknowledgement deadline
|`LOST` with `LossReason.ACK_MISSED`; the attempt ends `WORKER_LOST`.

|The worker is silent with no open wait
|`LossDetection.due` at the silence deadline
|`LOST` with `LossReason.SILENCE`; the attempt ends `WORKER_LOST`. An open bounded wait suppresses the signal.

|The worker is alive but makes no progress
|The task protocol's off-track signals (PM-WORK-7)
|The protocol reports it; the supervisor ends the worker through `EndCause.OFF_TRACK` on the action the role artifact
chose. `agent_started` is the one fixed `restart` signal.

|A second agent starts in the worker's tree
|`AgentStartedDetector`
|Reports `WorkerProtocol.agentStarted` and ends the worker through `EndCause.OFF_TRACK`; the protocol owns the audit
record and the re-offer.

|No model slot is free, or the plan's writer holds it
|`ModelSlots.claim` returns no lease
|Declines with `NO_MODEL_SLOT`; the task stays queued and the FIFO place is kept.

|The role's guard holds
|`StartGuard.holds`
|Declines with `ROLE_GUARD_HELD`; the guard has already closed the queued tasks as `lost` with `role_unavailable`.

|The harness is ineligible
|`EligibilityGate.admits`
|Declines with `HARNESS_INELIGIBLE`; the task stays queued and the refusal names the reason, stage and client id.

|A warm worker reached a recycle trigger
|`WorkerRecycling`
|Ends through `EndCause.RECYCLE`; a successor starts when work remains. A fresh worker is never recycled.

|The runtime drains
|`WorkerDrainParticipant`
|Ends idle warm workers, waits up to the drain deadline, then forces the rest. The tasks stay queued and are offered
again without counting a re-offer.

|The operator pauses the plan at once
|`WorkerCalls.planPaused`
|Forces the plan's workers with `EndCause.PAUSE_AT_ONCE`; the end reason is `WITHDRAWN` and the task is queued again
without a re-offer.

|A start of one harness collides with the previous one
|`HarnessStartSpacing`
|Delays the next start by at most 30 seconds, then proceeds. It never refuses; a real crash before the first call counts
as a start failure.
|===

The failure-mode coverage is the fault matrix of PM-TEST-4 and the scenarios of `SupervisorScenarioTest`.
