== Purpose

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6]

Package `de.planmarshall.runtime.audit` of `pm-runtime` makes the hash chain of `logs/audit.jsonl` checkable for
tampering. The chain itself, its formats and the production audit sink are in `core.store` of `pm-core`
(link:../../../pm-core/doc/developer/core-store.adoc#_audit_log[Scope Stores § The Audit Log]); a chain alone shows a
removed, reordered or edited record, but not a log that was cut short, replaced or started over. This package adds
what anchors the chain beyond itself:

* the head `logs/audit.head`, which counts the records and names the last line under an HMAC keyed by the enrolment
  integrity key;
* the rotation of the log by size and age, with a `checkpoint` record that closes a log and the hash-chained audit
  index `logs/audit-index.jsonl` that lists the rotated files;
* the verification: incremental at every runtime start, and full for `pm-operator audit verify`.

The package was work package `audit-trail`; it is its own package because it fills two seams, `AuditAnchor` of
`core.store` and `AuditChainVerification` of `runtime.start`, and `runtime.start` changes only at its seam
(link:../adr/runtime-audit/001-audit-trail-in-its-own-package.adoc[ADR 001]).

== Core Types

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6]

[horizontal]
link:{src}/AuditHead.java[`AuditHead`]:: Implements `AuditAnchor`, the seam `AuditLog` calls under the audit lock in
every append. `beforeAppend` lets `AuditRotation` rotate the log when a limit is reached; `emptyLogPredecessor()`
returns the `checkpoint_hash` of the last index entry; `afterAppend` writes the head. It reads the enrolment integrity
key and never creates it (<<_the_key>>). `read(headFile)`, `sign` and `verifies` are the head operations the
verification shares.
link:{src}/AuditIndex.java[`AuditIndex`]:: The index `logs/audit-index.jsonl`: one `AuditIndexEntry` per rotated file,
appended only at a rotation, under the audit lock, through the JSONL append path; each entry chains to the SHA-256 of
the line before it, the first to 64 `0`. `snapshot()` reads it without a lock and reports the entries up to the first
line that does not parse or does not chain, the hash of its last line and that first broken line. An entry is never
removed.
link:{src}/AuditRotation.java[`AuditRotation`]:: Rotates the log when its size reaches `maxBytes` or its first record
`maxAge`, whichever comes first. `Limits` are supplied by the caller; `Limits.DEFAULTS` are 64 MiB and 30 days
(link:../adr/runtime-audit/002-rotation-limits-supplied-by-the-caller.adoc[ADR 002]).
link:{src}/AuditVerification.java[`AuditVerification`]:: Implements `AuditChainVerification` of `runtime.start` with the
incremental `verify()`, and offers `full()`, the verification `pm-operator audit verify` requests. It reads without the
lock, never writes and never throws.

== The Head

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6]

After every append `AuditHead` writes `logs/audit.head`: the file (`audit.jsonl`), the record count, the event id of the
last record, the SHA-256 of the last line (`head_hash`), the SHA-256 of the last line of the index (`index_head_hash`,
64 `0` before the first rotation) and the HMAC-SHA-256 under the enrolment integrity key over the canonical JSON of the
other fields (`AuditCodec.signedContent`). The head is replaced atomically through the store file of `core.store`,
forced to disk, and readable and writable by its owner only.

The head is replaced atomically, so the one head that anchors nothing is an absent head file. A head file that exists
but does not parse or does not verify under the key is never replaced: no head is written over it, it stays in place,
so the verification reports `head_mismatch`, and `PM_RUNTIME-123` names the reason `head_unverifiable`. Damaging the
head is therefore no way to have a cut log recounted.

The record count is the previous head's count plus one when the previous head anchors exactly the line the appended
record chains to and verifies under the key. Otherwise the complete lines of the log are counted, but only where the
count cannot hide a cut (link:../adr/runtime-audit/003-head-keyed-by-the-enrolment-integrity-key.adoc[ADR 003]):

* no head file exists: the first head after the key appears covers the whole current log;
* the log was rotated since the previous head: the last index entry chains to the index line that head anchored, and
  the rotated file it names is closed by a checkpoint that verifies under the key and still holds the line the head
  anchored at its position, as the rotation signs a checkpoint only over such a log (<<_the_rotation>>). An index
  entry and a rename are plain file operations; without the checkpoint as evidence, a cut closed by a forged
  checkpoint, entered and renamed by hand, would be recounted and re-signed by the next head;
* the log still holds the line the previous head anchored, at its position: a stop between an append and its head
  left records without a head, and the recount covers them.

A log that no longer holds the anchored line, cut short or with its last line replaced, gets no new head. The previous
head stays, so the verification reports `truncated` (fewer records than it anchors) or `head_mismatch`, and
`PM_RUNTIME-123` names the reason `anchored_line_missing`. Nothing re-signs a cut as the new baseline.

Two gaps stay open: a deleted head file reads as absent, so the next append counts the whole log; and an index entry
carries no HMAC of its own.

[#_the_key]
=== The Key

The audit path only reads the key, through `EnrolmentKey.read()`, which takes no `LockKey`: the audit lock stays the
leaf of the lock order, and the credentials lock that guards the creation of the key is never taken after it. A key
once read is kept, because it is never replaced
(link:../adr/runtime-audit/003-head-keyed-by-the-enrolment-integrity-key.adoc[ADR 003]).

[cols="2,3"]
|===
|Key |Behaviour of an append

|Present
|The rotation runs when due, the head is written.

|Absent (no enrolment yet)
|No rotation starts (a checkpoint needs the HMAC) and no head; the record stands and nothing is logged. The first append
after the key exists writes the head over the whole log. A pending rotation is not completed and refuses the record
(`PM_RUNTIME-125`, `checkpoint_unverifiable`).

|Unreadable (`SecretStoreException`)
|No rotation starts and no head; the record stands, and `PM_RUNTIME-123` (`AUDIT_HEAD_NOT_WRITTEN`) names the reason
code, never the key. A pending rotation is not completed and refuses the record (`PM_RUNTIME-125`,
`checkpoint_unverifiable`) until the key can be read again.
|===

A head that cannot be read or written is logged as `PM_RUNTIME-123` with the reason `head_unwritable`, one that is
withheld over a damaged head with `head_unverifiable`, and one withheld over a cut log with `anchored_line_missing`.
Nothing of the head
throws into the append: a gap of the head is what the next verification reports. The one failure `AuditHead` throws
into the append is a rotation that stays pending after its checkpoint (<<_the_rotation>>).

== The Rotation

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6]

Before an append, with the key present, `AuditRotation.rotateIfDue` reads the first and the last complete line of the
log. A log that does not exist, holds no complete line, or whose first line does not parse is not rotated. When the
size of the log reaches `maxBytes`, or the `timestamp` of its first record lies `maxAge` or more in the past, it closes
the log, under the audit lock and inside the writer's append, in this order:

. the `checkpoint` record (`category: security`, `scope_type: machine`, `details{record_count, head_hash, hmac}`: the
  records before it, the hash of the line before it, and the HMAC-SHA-256 under the key over the UTF-8 bytes of
  `{"record_count":<record_count>,"head_hash":"<head_hash>"}`) is appended through `AuditLog.appendCheckpoint`;
. an index entry names the file `audit-<first_event_id>.jsonl`, its first record, the checkpoint as its last record,
  its record count with the checkpoint, and the hash of the checkpoint line as `checkpoint_hash`;
. the log is renamed to that file name by an atomic move, and the directory is forced to disk.

A rotation logs `PM_RUNTIME-25` (`AUDIT_LOG_ROTATED`) with the rotated file and its record count. The new log is
empty, so the writer chains the next record by its predecessor rule to `emptyLogPredecessor()`, the checkpoint line.

*No checkpoint over a cut log.* A checkpoint signs the record count and the last line of the log, and the recount after
the rotation accepts the new log, so a checkpoint over a log cut below the line the head anchors would turn the cut into
a consistent rotated file that `verify()` and `full()` report intact. Before it signs a checkpoint, `rotateIfDue`
therefore runs the `CloseCheck` the caller passes; `AuditHead.closeCheck` lets the log be closed only when no head file
exists, or when the head verifies, anchors the current last index line, and the log still holds the line it anchors
at its position. Otherwise the rotation is refused and logged as `PM_RUNTIME-124` (`AUDIT_ROTATION_FAILED`) with the
reason `head_unverifiable` (the head file does not parse or does not verify) or `anchored_line_missing`. The record is
appended to the current log and gets no new head, so the cut stays reported as `truncated` or `head_mismatch`, and every
later due append is refused again. A head that anchors the index line before the last entry, left by a stop between a
rotation and the head of the next append, is refused as well: the record goes to the current log, its head is counted
after the rotation, and the next due append rotates. Completing a pending rotation runs no `CloseCheck`, but it
verifies the checkpoint under the key (below).

*A stop during a rotation.* Because the index entry is written before the rename, a stop after the rename still finds
the entry, and the next record links to the checkpoint. A stop between the rename and the record of the same append
leaves no current log (or an empty one the audit lock creates, or a torn first line), an index whose last entry names
the rotated file, and the head of the last append before the rotation, which anchors the index line before that entry
and the line before the checkpoint of the rotated file. The verification reports that state intact only when every
piece of it verifies (<<_the_verification>>), and the next append heals it: its record chains to the checkpoint line,
and its head is counted over the new log as a rotation since the head (<<_the_head>>) and anchors the last index line.
A log whose last line is a checkpoint is a _pending rotation_,
one a stop or a failure interrupted after its checkpoint: the next append with the key completes it without a second
checkpoint, writing the index entry unless the last entry already names that line, and then renaming the log. A due
log that the last index entry already names as its rotated file is never entered a second time.

*Only a checkpoint that verifies is completed.* A last line that merely has the event `checkpoint` proves nothing:
anyone who can write the log can cut its tail and append one. Completing it would enter the cut in the index and rename
it, the next head would be counted over the new log, and `verify()`, which reads no rotated file, would answer
`Intact`. Before completing a pending rotation, `AuditRotation` therefore verifies the checkpoint as the verification
does (`AuditRotation.checkpointVerifies`): it counts the records before it, its `head_hash` is the hash of the line
before it and its `prev_hash` chains to that line, and its HMAC verifies under the key. A checkpoint that does not
verify, also one the last index entry already names, is not completed: no index entry, no rename, and the append is
refused, because no record may follow a checkpoint. `AuditHead` logs `PM_RUNTIME-125` (`AUDIT_ROTATION_PENDING`) with
the reason `checkpoint_unverifiable` and throws into the append. While the key is absent or unreadable the checkpoint
cannot be verified, so a pending rotation is refused the same way (`AuditRotation.refusePending`) and completed by the
next append once the key can be read. The log stays as it is, and both verifications report the forged checkpoint as
`link_broken` at its line (<<_the_verification>>).

*A torn tail behind a checkpoint.* The refusal holds while the checkpoint is the last complete line of the log.
`AuditLog.append` cuts a torn tail before it calls `beforeAppend`, so a torn tail behind a checkpoint is cut first, and
its `jsonl_tail_repaired` record follows the checkpoint; the log is then no longer pending, and the append is not
refused. No writer of the trail leaves such a tail, since none writes after a checkpoint: it is a foreign write. The
integrity of the trail is not affected: the repair record is chained, a checkpoint that does not verify still gets no
index entry and no rename, no head is written over a log that no longer holds the anchored line, and the verification
still reports a cut.

*A torn tail of the index.* The index entry is appended through the JSONL append path, which first cuts a torn tail of
the index. Its `jsonl_tail_repaired` record cannot go into the log being closed, so the rotation refuses the hand-over:
the record stays owed in the intent file of the index (logged `PM_CORE-100` by the substrate). After the rename the
rotation hands the owed records into the new log through `AuditLog.appendOwedRepair`, which forms each record by the
mapping of `AuditLogSink` (an unknown category or outcome is refused there as by the sink) and chains it by the
predecessor rule to the checkpoint line.

*The write paths of the package.* `runtime.audit` writes into the log through exactly two members of `AuditLog`, and
only inside a rotation: `appendCheckpoint` closes the log, and `appendOwedRepair` hands an owed `jsonl_tail_repaired`
record of the index into the new log. Both require the audit lock, chain by the predecessor rule and never call the
anchor, so a rotation cannot recurse. Every other record reaches the log through `AuditLog.append`.

*A rotation that fails before its checkpoint* (the log or the index cannot be read, the checkpoint cannot be written, an
intent file is unreadable) is logged as `PM_RUNTIME-124` (`AUDIT_ROTATION_FAILED`); the record is appended to the
current log, and the next append retries the rotation.

*A rotation that fails after its checkpoint* (the index entry cannot be written, the rename fails) stays pending. No
record may follow the checkpoint, so `AuditHead` logs `PM_RUNTIME-125` (`AUDIT_ROTATION_PENDING`) and throws into the
append: the record is not appended, and the producer keeps what it owes, as for any refusal of the audit sink. The next
append with the key completes the rotation, its checkpoint verifying. Until then the verification reports the pending
rotation intact (<<_the_verification>>).

Reading the limits from `machine-config.json` (`audit_rotation.max_bytes`, `audit_rotation.max_days`) belongs to the
assembly; this package takes them as `Limits`.

== The Verification

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6], link:{req}/01-architecture.adoc#PM-ARCH-7[PM-ARCH-7]

`verify()` is step 4.1 of the start: its cost depends on the current log only, not on the number of projects, plans or
jobs. It checks, in this order:

. the key: absent is `NotVerified` (nothing is anchored yet), unreadable is `NotVerified` naming the reason code;
. no head, no log line and no index entry: `NotVerified` (no audit chain yet);
. the chain of the index;
. the head: present, parsed, its HMAC verified under the key, and its `index_head_hash` the hash of the last index line;
. the current log: its first record chains to the `checkpoint_hash` of the last index entry (to genesis when the index
  is empty), every other one to the line before it, the count equals the head's `record_count`, and the last line and
  its event id are the ones the head names.

A current log whose last line is a checkpoint is a pending rotation (<<_the_rotation>>). It is checked as the rotated
file it is about to become: against the last index entry when that entry names its checkpoint line, otherwise its chain
and its checkpoint; and the head anchors the line before the checkpoint and the index line before that entry. Its
checkpoint is verified as the rotation verifies it before completing it: it counts the records before it, names and
chains to the line before it, and its HMAC verifies under the key. A genuine pending rotation is therefore reported
intact, not as `head_mismatch` or `reset`, and a checkpoint appended to a cut log is reported as `link_broken` at its
line, by `verify()` as by `full()`.

A rotation stopped between its rename and the first record of the new log (<<_the_rotation>>) leaves a current log
without a complete line and a head that anchors the index line before the last entry. That state is intact exactly
when every piece of it verifies, and `verify()` reads the rotated file for it:

* the head verifies under the key (checked first, as for every trail);
* the last index entry chains to the index line the head anchors;
* the rotated file that entry names exists and matches it: its first record chains to the checkpoint of the entry
  before it (genesis for the first), every other one to the line before it, its record count, its first event id, its
  checkpoint event id and the hash of its checkpoint line are the ones the entry names;
* its checkpoint verifies as above, its HMAC under the key included;
* the head anchors the line before that checkpoint: its record count, its `head_hash` and its event id.

Every partial match is a break: a rotated file that is gone is `missing`, one that does not match its entry or whose
checkpoint does not verify is reported as `full()` reports a rotated file (`link_broken`, `truncated`), and a head that
anchors another line is `head_mismatch`. Without the key the trail is `NotVerified`, never intact. Once a record of the
new log is written without its head, the state is the gap of any append without its head, reported as
`head_mismatch` until the next append writes the head. A head file and a log rolled back together to an earlier state
stay as undetectable here as anywhere else in the trail: the head carries no sequence the verification could compare.

`full()` checks, after the head and before the current log, every rotated file the index lists, in index order: its
first record chains to the checkpoint of the file before it (genesis for the first), its count, its first event id and
its checkpoint are the ones its entry names, and the checkpoint counts the records before it, names the hash of the
line before it and its HMAC verifies. A rotated file that is gone (purged) is accounted for by its entry and skipped.

A break is an `Outcome.Broken` with its reason:

[cols="2,4"]
|===
|Reason |When

|`link_broken`
|A line does not parse, or does not chain to the line before it (a removed, reordered or edited record); also a broken
index chain, with the index line and a diagnostic naming `audit-index.jsonl`, and in `full()` a line after the
checkpoint of a rotated file or a checkpoint that is not the one its entry names. The only reason with a first broken
line, counted from 1 in the file the diagnostic names.

|`missing`
|The current log is missing while the head anchors records, or, after a rotation stopped after its rename, the rotated
file the head anchors. A missing log whose head anchors 0 records is `Intact`.

|`reset`
|The log is empty while its head anchors records, or its first line starts at genesis while the index continues the
chain.

|`truncated`
|The log, or a rotated file, holds fewer records than its head, or its entry, counts.

|`head_mismatch`
|No head for a trail that has records or index entries, a head that does not parse, does not verify under the key or
does not anchor the last index line (the index line before it while a rotation is pending or stopped after its
rename), more records than the head anchors, or a last line that is not the one the head names (after a stop after
the rename: not the line before the checkpoint of the rotated file).
|===

The diagnostic names the file and the position, never a key or a hash secret. `StartupSequence` records the reason and
the line, not the diagnostic (link:runtime-start.adoc#_the_audit_chain_seam[`runtime.start` § The Audit Chain
Seam]).

*No lock, no repair.* The verification reads without the audit lock and never writes. A rotation or an append between
its reads would show a state that never existed, so it reads the head, the index and the log, then the head and the
index again, and repeats the reads (at most three times) until the second read matches the first; a `Broken` result is
checked once more against a fresh consistent read and is reported only when that read shows it too. A rotation in the
middle of a verification is therefore never reported as a break. A trail that cannot be read is `NotVerified`, never an
exception (link:../adr/runtime-audit/004-verification-never-repairs-and-survives-rotation.adoc[ADR 004]).

== Wiring in the Assembly

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6]

The package ships no default wiring. The assembly `pm-mcp-server` of `plan-marshall-mcp` builds the trail from the
runtime's base directory, the enrolment integrity key (`EnrolmentKey`), its clock and the running user, and replaces
with it `AuditChainVerification.NONE` and the audit sink that refuses every record:

[source,java]
----
Files.createDirectories(base.resolve("logs"));            // the lock manager never creates it
var head = new AuditHead(base, integrityKey, AuditRotation.Limits.DEFAULTS, clock, user);
var log = new AuditLog(base, head, clock, user);
AuditSink auditSink = new AuditLogSink(lockManager, log);
AuditChainVerification auditChain = new AuditVerification(base, integrityKey);
----

* `logs/` must exist before the first append: the audit lock is a leaf key whose lock file is the log itself, and
  `FileLockManager` creates that file but never its directory.
* `FileLockManager` takes the audit sink in its constructor, and `AuditLogSink` takes the lock manager: the assembly
  breaks the cycle, for example with a sink that forwards to the `AuditLogSink` once it is built.
* The limits come from `audit_rotation` of `machine-config.json` once the assembly reads it; until then
  `Limits.DEFAULTS`.
* Exactly one `AuditLog` per base: its appends serialize on the in-process lock of the audit key of the one lock
  manager, and the runtime lock excludes a second runtime.

== Pitfalls

_Requirements:_ link:{req}/08-security.adoc#PM-SEC-6[PM-SEC-6]

1. **Never create the key on the audit path**: `readOrCreate()` takes the credentials lock, which must never follow the
   audit lock. Only the first enrolment write creates the key.
2. **Write into the log only through `appendCheckpoint` and `appendOwedRepair`**: any other path, `AuditLog.append`
   included, would chain by another rule or call the anchor again inside a rotation.
3. **Index entry before the rename**: the entry carries the link from the new log to the checkpoint; renamed first, a
   stop would leave the next record chaining to genesis, reported as `reset`.
4. **Never repair in the verification**: a break is reported and recorded, and the trail stays as it was found.
5. **Re-read before reporting**: a verification without the lock that reports its first read sees rotations as breaks.
6. **Never let a record follow a checkpoint**: a rotation that stays pending after its checkpoint refuses the append,
   and the next append with the key completes it. The one record that can follow a checkpoint is the repair record of
   a foreign torn tail behind it, cut before the anchor is called (<<_the_rotation>>).
7. **Never trust a checkpoint by its event name**: a pending checkpoint is completed, and a rotation since the head
   recounted, only on a checkpoint that verifies under the key; without the key nothing is completed.
8. **Never re-sign a cut**: a head over a log that no longer holds the line the previous head anchored would accept the
   cut as the new baseline, and so would a checkpoint over such a log, or a recount that replaces a damaged head.
