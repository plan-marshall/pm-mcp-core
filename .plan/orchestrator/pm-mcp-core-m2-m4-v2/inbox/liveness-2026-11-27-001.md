# Liveness guarantee: a workflow never gets stuck unnoticed (code and plan follow-ups)

This message comes from a design session of `plan-marshall-documentation` about one question: what happens when a workflow gets stuck, and how it is prevented. The operator decided the design, and the documents are merged:
- core #634 (`0dad305e`): the engine specifications and the watch;
- clients #600: the design of the clients;
- docs #205: requirements, roadmap, use cases, the timeline resource and the rejection reason.

The code was not touched. Little of it is built; §1 lists what is built and now has to grow. The epic decides how to cut and order the work. Line numbers are those of `origin/main` at `0dad305e` and may have moved.

## The decisions (the target text is in the merged specifications)

- **Liveness guarantee** (`workflow-dsl/04-ast-node-model.adoc#liveness-guarantee`). Every non-terminal position of a scope is in exactly one of four classes: engine-progressing, bounded wait, operator, or resident rest.
  - From every engine-progressing or bounded-wait position, every path reaches a terminal, an operator or resident-rest position, or a smaller measure within a bound.
  - An obligation table names the rule that discharges each part and the watch item where the code lags: PM-WATCH-DSL-1, DSL-4, DSL-5 (existing), DSL-49, PHASE-133 (new).
  - No new verifier rule.
- **Bounds of awaiting primitives** (`timeouts/01-job-budgets-and-waits.adoc#awaiting-bounds`). Every primitive whose `awaitedEvent()` is not `NONE` has a row with its bound source, from a closed list: `job budget`, `counter <name>`, `deadline <key>`, `holder lease`, `operator`, `resident`, `stall-watched`.
  - A registry test pins that every registered awaiting primitive has a row and that no row is unbounded (PM-WATCH-DSL-49).
- **Overdue waits** (`runtime-lifecycle/02-plan-pause-and-stalled-plans.adoc#overdue-waits`). The CI wait and the analysis provider's wait are stall-watched: the wait continues and nothing is decided by time. The scope is shown as the hold `overdue` in two cases:
  - cause `no_change`: no observed change for the overdue factor `3` × the expected duration (learned figure, else the operator budget seed, else `3600`). The hold clears on the next observed change.
  - cause `long_running`: the wait has lasted the long-wait factor `10` × the expected duration, whatever changed. The hold stays until the wait ends.

  Further rules:
  - Acts: `observe_now` and `cancel_run`, beside the scope's cancel.
  - One `plan_overdue` audit record per episode.
  - A `Retry-After` deferral is honoured up to a cap of `3600` and counts toward the stall clock (`timeouts/01#retry-after-cap`).
  - The values are fixed `pm-runtime` properties (`timeouts/02#overdue-factor`, `#long-wait-factor`).
  - Watch: PM-WATCH-LIFE-2.
- **Hold record** (`state-catalogue/01#holds`). Gains:
  - `awaited` {kind, id of the job/task/wait/lease, since, bound or expected duration, last observed change};
  - the position {`state`, `call_path`, `instance_version`};
  - the correlation ids that apply.

  `plan_stalled` details gain the correlation ids. `plan_overdue` details are `{state, cause, wait_reason, awaited, overdue_since}` (`store-schemas/12`).
- **Prerequisite cycles** (`phase-workflows/08-dynamic-re-planning.adoc#replan-actions` item 4). A `wait_for_prerequisite` whose prerequisite waits, directly or transitively, on the submitting plan is answered `rejected`, reason `prerequisite_cycle`, with the plans of the cycle in `detail`.
  - A `pull_request:<number>` that is the recorded pull request of a running plan counts as that plan.
  - The reason is added to the closed list in docs `mcp-tools/04-errors-and-testing.adoc#rejection-reasons`.
  - Watch: PM-WATCH-PHASE-133.
- **Runtime self-watch** (`runtime-lifecycle/01-startup-recovery-drain.adoc#runtime-self-watch`). The drive loop and the periodic sweep each stamp their own in-memory heartbeat and are judged only by their own stamps.
  - A watchdog thread writes one thread dump to the runtime log per episode when a heartbeat is later than `60` s (`timeouts/02#heartbeat-threshold`). It stops and restarts nothing.
  - `pm-operator status` reports "not responding", naming the late heartbeat with the age of its last stamp, also while the API answers.
  - Watch: PM-WATCH-LIFE-3.
- **Clock gap** (`runtime-lifecycle/02#clock-gap`). Sleep is detected from two monotonic clocks, one that counts while the machine sleeps and one that does not: Linux `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` / `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, macOS `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` / `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`.
  - A divergence above `30` s per observation interval is a sleep.
  - The span is excluded from the stall clocks of `stalled`, `overdue` and the heartbeats, so an open hold stays open, and it is attributed as a pause in the wait slices.
  - The wall clock is never the signal.
  - Watch: PM-WATCH-LIFE-4.
- **Model slot of an ended job** (`store-schemas/13` Ownership vs Liveness, `store-schemas/01` Worker end, `runtime-lifecycle/01` recovery step 4). A slot whose job record already carries an end fate is positive evidence for its release.
  - Worker-end write order: job record, then queue task record, then the model slot.
  - Watch: PM-WATCH-STORE-63.
- **Metric** `work.waits{kind}`: a count kept by the wait registrations, with the kind from the wait reasons of Wait Attribution (`runtime-lifecycle/03-runtime-metrics.adoc`).
- **Plan resource and timeline.** These are specified in docs `runtime-model/01-processes-and-transport.adoc` (`#local-api` plan row, `#timeline-resource`), and the engine supplies the data.
  - The plan resource carries every counter with its cap: retry counters, policy counters, watch restarts, task re-offers, outline loop-backs, the re-planning budget.
  - It also carries a running job's elapsed time against `budget_seconds`, and an open CI wait's elapsed time against its expected duration.
  - `GET /api/v1/plans/{plan_id}/timeline` merges history entries with their durations, step firings, job records, task attempts, wait slices, and conditions and holds. It is derived on read, has no store and never reads the audit log; each entry carries `node_id` and the correlation ids.

## 1. Code already built that has to grow

- `pm-core/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/core/spi/AwaitedEvent.java:17` and `PrimitiveRegistry.java:94` already refuse a primitive without an awaited event (`MISSING_AWAITED_EVENT`). The bound-table registry test (DSL-49) can sit beside that check. It needs the primitive's bound source as data, for example a `bound()` declaration or a table keyed by primitive id that the test reads; decide the form. Four primitives implement `Primitive<P, R>` today, all in `pm-provider-git`.
- `pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/start/StalledScopeDetector.java` (`THRESHOLD_KEY` :51, `Cause.NO_PROGRESS` :119, `awaitsExternalEvent` :94, :206). This is the natural home of `overdue` (an open stall-watched wait is exactly what `awaitsExternalEvent` exempts today) and of the clock-gap exclusion. Its properties resource `start/stalled-scope.properties` gains the overdue factor, the long-wait factor, the heartbeat threshold and the clock-gap threshold. The timeouts rows state the values literally and can switch to includes once the file carries them.
- `pm-core/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/core/service/AuditEvent.java:235, 371`: `PLAN_STALLED` and its builder. `plan_overdue` with its details is new, and `plan_stalled` gains the correlation ids. Check `audit-record.schema.json` for an event enumeration.
- `pm-runtime/src/main/java/de/planmarshall/runtime/job/SettlementStep.java:31-33, 116-117` says "A worker's model slot is no lease-store slot and is released through `ModelSlotScheduler.release`". The specification (`store-schemas/13`) treats the model slot as a lease record with `lease_expires_at: null`.
  - Verify first which is true.
  - If model slots are only in memory, a restart frees them and the new evidence clause has nothing to release; then correct the specification rather than build a store.
  - If they are persisted, add the end-fate evidence to the orphan release.
- No code exists yet for the hold record's `awaited`, position or correlation ids, the timeline resource, the counters with caps on the plan resource, the CI and analysis stall clocks, the prerequisite cycle check, the heartbeats and watchdog, or `work.waits` (grep of `pm-*/src/main`).
