## Objective

Implement the work package `epic-decomposition` of `doc/plans/epic-decomposition.adoc`: the decomposition of an epic, and the intent it is cut from. Serves: roadmap Milestone 12 (the epic workflow and its decomposition; the epic's intent); Use Cases, UC-STA-6; the decomposition in both clients of `pm-mcp-clients`. Where: pm-workflow (the compiled-only sub-workflow `decomposition`, called by `epic.decompose`; the return evaluation of `epic.recut`); pm-core (the draft `epics/<epic_id>/decomposition.json` apart from the ledger until agreed; the epic's intent in the ledger). The package is the decomposition half of what PLAN-75 (#85) merged on 2026-10-17: pm-mcp-core #111 (72aec5c, PLAN-78) split it, the review of an epic's findings and watches is the package `epic-review` (PLAN-92, cut from this spec by cleanup on 2026-10-21), and the spec of PLAN-52 stays superseded. The package file is the brief; this spec adds what the orchestrator observed and the constraints of the epic.

## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/epic-decomposition.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated by cleanup at 99971f3 from the package file).

1. Implements Model Work § The Decomposition Is Reviewed, State Catalogue § Decomposition: `decomposition`, and Store Schemas § Decomposition Draft (`PM-WF-15`, `PM-WF-20`).
2. The draft of the role `decompose` (`decomposition.draft`) held apart from the ledger, kept across leaving the review and a restart and served by the epic's resource while open; a task that falls back holds the decomposition (`decomposition.held`); the server's checks over it (disjoint surfaces of plans that may run together, no dependency cycle, a workstream per plan); a failing check shown on the draft with its row and cause, never discarding it; the operator wait `decomposition.review` with keep, edit, drop, add, chat (`decomposition.chat`, the sub-workflow `chat` with the draft as its scope), and draft again with an instruction; _agree_ offered only while every check passes; `decomposition.commit` writing rows, sections, and workstreams (with the epic's sources on the first run) in one ledger write under the epic lock, committed through `git.commit_and_push` and landed by `epic.landing`.
3. Edit and add: `decomposition.instruct` (the operator's words for the plan), `decomposition.rewrite` (the role drafts or rewrites the plan), `decomposition.read-back` (old against new: accept to `decomposition.check`, instruct again to `decomposition.instruct`, discard back to `decomposition.review`); every model change through that read-back or the chat's.
4. How many plans run at once is no part of the decomposition: it is the project's `epic.parallelization_scope` (machine default 1), never asked at the start; the operator raises it through _Next plan_ (`epic.raise-scope`, `epic-resource-and-release`) or the project's settings.
5. A later run (decompose again, stale rows) over staged rows only, each change against the row it replaces; no plan of a new epic starts before its first decomposition is committed. A recut (`epic.recut`, for a `stale` row or a landing analysis) that only re-orders staged rows or amends a staged row's declared surface or prerequisites is applied without the review once the checks pass (outcome `applied`, committed by `epic.commit-ledger`, reported `epic.recut_applied`); one that adds, drops, or re-scopes a plan goes to `epic.decompose` and waits in `decomposition.review` (outcome `review`), whatever the epic's automatic mode (`epic-auto`).
6. Where `epic.decompose` and the commit of a decomposition have already been implemented without the review, this package restructures them.
7. The intent of an epic: the epic states where it ends before it is cut into plans — its goal, its non-goals and the claims its plans must carry — as `epic-intent`, the named parameter schema of the `decompose` entity's `submit intent`, persisted into `epic.json`'s `intent` and into the draft (`decomposition.json`'s `intent`) in the same ledger write that commits the decomposition; the server seeds each plan's required claims from the row's `done_when` and the epic's non-goals, a plan revision never changes the epic's intent, and the completion check of `epic.through` reads the intent's `done_when` checks (Model Work § Epic Intent; Store Schemas § Epic Header; Phase Workflows § The Intent Record).

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): A decomposition reaches the ledger only through the operator's agreement with every check passing, the first one and every later run that adds, drops, or re-scopes a plan; only a recut after a landing that re-orders staged rows or amends a staged row's surface or prerequisites is applied without it, once the checks pass (`epic-auto`); each operator act of the decomposition and each branch of `decomposition.read-back` is tested; no state of the start or the decomposition asks `parallelization_scope`; a later run changes only staged rows and reports each change. The epic's intent round-trips through `submit intent` into `epic.json` and the draft, and the intent and the committed decomposition are written in one ledger write; an epic-started plan's required claims are seeded from the row's `done_when` and the epic's non-goals, a plan revision leaves the epic's intent unchanged, and `epic.through` reads its `done_when` checks.
