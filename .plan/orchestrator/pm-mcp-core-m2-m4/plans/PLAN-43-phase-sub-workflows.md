## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/phase-sub-workflows.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated by cleanup at 99971f3 from the package file).

1. Implements Store Schemas § Phases as Sub-Workflows and the Phase of a Record, State Catalogue § Plan Unit `phases`, and Store Schemas § The Plan's History, with § The Start of the Current Phase (`PM-WF-16`, `PM-IMPL-5`).
2. Phases as sub-workflows: the plan unit (`plan.pmwf`, unit id `plan`, its call states `phases.*`) becomes a workflow of the six phases `init`, `refine`, `outline`, `plan`, `execute`, and `finalize`, each the sub-workflow `phase-1-init.pmwf` … `phase-6-finalize.pmwf` the Phase Workflows specify, with the entries and exits of its frontmatter (`start` for every phase, `rework` of `outline`, `land` and `reentry` of `execute`, `adopt`, `hand_back`, and `post_run` of `finalize`), called by the plan unit at the entry its call state names; the frontmatter, `entry <name> -> <state>`, `call <unit> entry <name>`, and the interface rules are implemented (Workflow DSL Developer Guide § The Unit Frontmatter, Workflow Verification Developer Guide § Interface Rules and Unit Types); a loop-back a call of a phase again, a re-entry from finalize into execute an outcome of finalize. The `phase` clause of a state (`StateNode.phase`, `phase inherit`) and every mapping of a state to its phase go, and with the merge of the phase files `PlanUnitMerger` and `partVersions`; the call stack holds at most five frames, the phase level adding one frame to every call path: the code's limit of four goes up to five in every place that states it, the status store (`WorkflowStatus.MAX_CALL_DEPTH`), the engine's `CallStack.MAX_DEPTH` and its push, and the static verifier's depth rule (`WorkflowValidator`, `VerificationRule`), so a fifth frame is admitted and a sixth refused, statically and at run time alike; the plan instance composes the phases, one instance file per composed unit, and prunes a phase as a call, with its reason.
3. Lanes as a choice of phases: a lane is never a phase. The land lane calls no refine, outline, or plan phase, and its `land.*` states (today `land.pmwf`, run in phase `execute`) become a sub-workflow called within the phase `execute`; the adopt lane calls `init` and then `finalize`.
4. The phase of a sub-workflow: a plan's phase is the phase sub-workflow on its call stack, and every sub-workflow called within a phase (a remediation unit, `land.*`, a chat) has that phase; no unit and no state declares a phase of its own, the entity units included.
5. The plan's history: an append-only history per plan of states entered and left, sub-workflows called and returned, with their outcomes, the instance version in force, and the time; archived with the plan; the runtime derives each node's state from it: done, running, planned, waiting for the operator, failed (with its outcome), not taken, and, for a pruned phase, not in this plan with its reason.
6. Where `workflow-engine`, `plan-instance`, or `project-units-and-invariants` have already implemented the flat plan unit, this package restructures them.
7. The plan's intent record: the record `intent.json` beside `request.md` in the plan store, its schema `intent-record` (the named parameter schema the model submits), with `criteria` (`AC-<n>`, an `unchanged` one verified by a test where the plan guards behaviour), `constraints` (`CN-<n>`, each ruling something out with its `source`), non-goals (`NG-<n>`), decisions (`DC-<n>`), assumptions and source claims; `submit:refine`, the light lane's `submit:outline` and a re-planning that changes intent carry the record, a risk is written only as an `unchanged` criterion with its verification, a tested assumption or a non-goal, never as prose, and `request.md` § Clarified Request is rendered from the record; for a plan its epic started, the server seeds the required claims from the epic row's `done_when` and the epic's non-goals (`epic-intent`); every earlier revision moves to `history[]` in the accepting transaction and is never changed. The record's new link and identifier forms and the watch items `PM-WATCH-PHASE-106` and `-107` are implemented with it (Store Schemas § Intent Record; Phase Workflows § The Intent Record; Filesystem Layout § The Source Directory; Store Schemas § Epic Header; PM-WATCH-PHASE-106; PM-WATCH-PHASE-107).
8. The outline record and the specification parts of the planning phases: the outline task submits the outline record `outline:schema[outline-record]`, the server keeps `outline.json` and renders `outline.md` from it, and validation is no state of its own; the builder check asks the operator about two criteria that cannot both hold, and about a decision or criterion that permits what a constraint rules out; review results and escalation are carried by the phases; the capability specification (`.marshall/capabilities/<capability>.json`) and its measurement are read by the planning roles; the scope-held Q-Gate row, the opt-in mutation verification step (`default:mutation`) and the knowledge search are the parts of the same specification that the phases own (Phase Workflows § Phase 3 Validation; § Builder Check; § Q-Gate & Execute; Store Schemas § Project Configuration; § Capability Spec; Model Work; § Completion, Usage & Spend; § Decision Records).
9. The `review-simplify` restraint ladder: the finalize step `finalize.simplify` runs a task of the read-only role `review-simplify` (vocabulary `skill://pm/finalize/simplify`, no write tools, never the plan's writer slot) that submits `submit:simplify`, a list of proposals (`file`, `change`, `rationale`) recorded as proposal records and never prose, and edits nothing; its facts carry the clone candidates the server computes before the task in-process and without a model (token-based clone detection of the added lines against the rest of the tree, identifiers and literals normalized, a run of at least `50` tokens, at most `20`, the longest first), which are evidence for the restraint ladder's "is it already in the codebase?" and never a finding by themselves; `finalize.simplify-apply` applies the accepted subset in the plan worktree, records every other proposal `declined`, and routes the applied change through `finalize.settle-commit`; the restraint ladder and the never-simplify list (behaviour-pinning tests, trust-boundary validation, security checks, data-loss error handling, operator-kept code) are vocabulary content (Phase Workflows § Finalize Step Ordering Bands).
10. Constraints and non-goals in the planning phases: the pre-submission self-review returns one result per constraint `CN-<n>` (`respected` with its evidence, `violated` as a finding, or `not_verified`) and per non-goal `NG-<n>` (`respected`, `crossed` as a finding, or `not_verified`) beside the criteria, and a submission that omits one is refused `invalid_link_args`; the builder check always asks the operator about a decision or criterion that permits what a constraint rules out, restated as condition and response and naming both items, and a default the operator accepted as recommended does not settle it; `OutlineValidator` refuses a deliverable that satisfies an `unchanged` criterion verified by `test` downstream of the deliverable that satisfies the `new` or `fix` criterion it guards — directly or through others — naming both deliverables and the criterion, while a deliverable that satisfies both kinds is not refused.
