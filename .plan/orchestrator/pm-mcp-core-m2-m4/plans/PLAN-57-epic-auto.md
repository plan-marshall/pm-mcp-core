## Objective

Implement the work package `epic-auto` of `doc/plans/epic-auto.adoc`: an epic runs by itself. Serves: roadmap Milestone 12 (the operator's acts on epics, automatic start on and off); Model Work § An Epic Runs by Itself; the epic's automatic mode and its reactions on a finished plan (UC-CTL-2), the mode in force shown at the start of an epic (UC-STA-2); the automatic toggle and the overlap wait in both clients of `pm-mcp-clients`. Where: pm-workflow (the epic workflow: starting by itself, the overlap admission, the reactions on a finished plan, the completion review once the epic is through); pm-core (the epic header's `auto_start` and `overlap_max_files`, the queue row's wait reason `overlap`, the project's operator values `epic.auto_start` and `epic.overlap_max_files` in `local-config.json` with their machine defaults); pm-runtime (the toggle and the overlap limit as operator endpoints and verbs: `PUT …/auto` and `PUT …/settings` of the epic). The package file is the brief; this spec adds what the orchestrator observed and the constraints of the epic.

**Parked:** waits on packages that are not done: `plan-and-epic-control`, `epic-decomposition`. Re-ground this spec before emitting it.

## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/epic-auto.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated at 710da61, after the specification moved into this repository).

1. Implements Model Work § An Epic Runs by Itself, with the header fields of Store Schemas § Epic Header, the wait reason and `overlap_found` of § Queue Row, and the project's operator values of the `epic` object of § Operator Local Configuration (`PM-WF-15`, `PM-WF-23`, `PM-WF-21`).
2. The setting: `epic.auto_start` (`true` or `false`), a project's operator value in `local-config.json`, never in the team file, with the machine default `true`, so the specified behaviour stays the default; each epic carries its own value in its header, taken from the project at the start and never asked there (UC-STA-2 shows it on the ready step as the value in force). `parallelization_scope` stays the upper limit of plans of the epic running at once (machine default 1, raised by the operator, `plan-and-epic-control`).
3. The toggle: _Automatic: on/off_ among the epic's actions in every state that has not ended (UC-CTL-2, the actions by state of `plan-and-epic-control`), an operator endpoint of both clients, a ledger write of the header, audited and told in the epic's events; switched on, the epic runs `epic.next-work` at once.
4. On: whenever a place frees or a row becomes launchable, `epic.next-work` starts every launchable row up to the limit by itself. Off: the epic starts nothing by itself; the operator starts the next plan with _Next plan_ (`plan-and-epic-control`); every reaction below still runs.
5. Too many common changes: a launchable row is admitted beside the running plans only while its declared surface shares at most `epic.overlap_max_files` files with each running plan's declared surface and realized changes (a project's operator value in `local-config.json` with a compiled upper bound of 20, machine default 0: any common file; held in the epic header like `auto_start`, taken from the project at the start, and changeable from the epic's actions through `PUT …/settings`, an audited header write like the toggle; `epic.pmwf` stays compiled-only). Over it, the row waits with the reason `overlap` naming that plan, beside `dependency`, `lock`, and `scope`, derived on read like them, until that plan has ended; it is never started in parallel, in either mode, also not by _Next plan_.
6. Overlap found while running: at the re-planning checkpoint after each deliverable's build, a running plan whose realized changes come to overlap another running plan's declared surface or realized changes beyond the limit is recorded on both queue rows (`overlap_found`: the other plan, the paths, the time) and the epic is told; neither plan is stopped (runtime drift never blocks); the one that lands second meets the base-advanced trigger of re-planning (PM-WF-17). The report of it to the operator is `operator-reports`'s.
7. Reacting on a finished plan: every end of a plan of the epic reaches its inbox (the landing, and the operator's acts of `plan-and-epic-control`); the epic workflow then: ** for a landed plan: records the landing and runs the landing task's downstream impact judgment; a change of staged rows it proposes goes through `epic.recut`: one that only re-orders rows or amends a staged row's surface or prerequisites is applied once the server's checks pass; one that adds, drops, or re-scopes a plan waits for the operator in `decomposition.review` (`epic-decomposition`), in both modes; ** for a plan that ended without landing (cancelled, abandoned, handed back, aborted): holds the rows that depend on it with that reason, and `coordinate` raises a decision for the operator (a release ends the epic with its plans, `plan-and-epic-control`); ** then `epic.next-work`: with automatic on it starts the next launchable rows up to the limit; with it off it records what is launchable now for the epic's view and _Next plan_ (reported as `epic.launchable`, `operator-reports`).
8. Through: the epic is through when every row is shipped or ended; with automatic on it then runs `epic.review-completion` and reports the result (`epic.through`), with it off it reports that it is through and the completion review runs at the operator's _archive_; archiving stays the operator's act (`plan-and-epic-control`).
9. A restart: the epic's mode and its waiting rows are read back from the ledger; an epic with automatic on runs `epic.next-work` once recovered, starting nothing twice.

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): A new epic takes `auto_start` and `overlap_max_files` from the project, the project's values from the machine defaults `true` and 0, a value over the compiled upper bound is refused, and its start asks for neither; with automatic on, a freed place or a newly launchable row starts every launchable row up to `parallelization_scope` and no more, also after a restart and without starting a row twice; with it off nothing starts by itself while every reaction still runs and what is launchable is recorded; the toggle and a change of the overlap limit are audited header writes told in the epic's events and refused on an ended epic; a row whose surface overlaps a running plan's beyond `epic.overlap_max_files` waits with the reason `overlap` naming that plan in both modes and through _Next plan_, and is admitted once that plan has ended; an overlap found between running plans is recorded and told and stops neither; a landing's re-order or amendment of staged rows is applied after the checks pass, an added, dropped, or re-scoped plan waits in `decomposition.review`; a plan ended without landing holds its dependants with the reason; an epic through with automatic on runs its completion review and is not archived by itself.

(2) While a durable in-progress record exists, every other writer of the state it switches is refused under the same lock, and an authorization is verified against durable state, never asserted by the caller. (3) The record that drives recovery is deleted last, after every side effect (audit event, clean-up after a failure) is durable. (4) A decision with side effects (which store, which key) is decided once and persisted, never re-derived per call from a probe that can flap. (9) A test that asserts a file location derives the expected path from the production layout independently, never from the object under test.
