## Objective

Implement the work package `decision-engine` of `doc/plans/decision-engine.adoc`: decisions as the operator meets them. Serves: roadmap Milestone 7 (the operator wait and decisions with their structure); Use Cases, UC-PLN-2; decisions in both clients of `pm-mcp-clients`. Where: pm-workflow (the decision's states, the interpretation and read-back); pm-core (the decision record, its revisions, the support per option); pm-runtime (the open decisions across scopes, the channel `cli`). The package file is the brief; this spec adds what the orchestrator observed and the constraints of the epic.

**Parked:** waits on packages that are not done: `operator-wait-and-machine-scope`, `start-from-clients`. Re-ground this spec before emitting it.

## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/decision-engine.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated at 710da61, after the specification moved into this repository).

1. Implements the engine side of UC-PLN-2 (`PM-WF-12`, `PM-WF-18`, `PM-WF-20`): Model Work § The Decision Structure, § Delivery to the Operator, § Answers in the Operator's Own Words, § Answers & Amendments; Store Schemas § Answers from the Terminal Client, § Question Record; State Catalogue § Unit `decision`. The client side is `decision-contract` of `pm-mcp-clients`.
2. The decision as a structure with its id (`<scope_id>/<question_id>`) and revisions; what it blocks and how reversible it is; the support per option derived by the runtime from the quorum's votes.
3. The interpreting role `interpret` (`read_only`, its corpus topic `own_words`), the read-back (`decision.read-back`: _confirm_, _edit_ with new words interpreted again, _choose_ an option instead) and the clarification; the confirmation `decision.confirm` before `decision.record` for a decision of more than one part or one not `reversible`, showing the whole answer and what follows from it, with _confirm_ and _back_.
4. None fits and later: _none fits_ (with a note; offered only where the decision names a role that can ask anew or a quorum is declared that can vote again) goes to `decision.withdraw`: a worker's question closes with the answer `none_fits`, which the continuing task carries in `facts.answers` with its `ask` link offered again within the question bound, and a quorum's decision is voted again on a new revision; _later_ answers nothing, the decision stays open and announced (`decision.announce`), the client leaves it, and its subject (the plan, the epic, the setup, or the entry) waits.
5. A waiver request is announced like a decision and answered by `waive` or `decline` (the outcome `declined`, the remediation unit going on; the wait is `operator-wait-and-machine-scope`'s), both operator overrides: no preselection, no interpretation, no chat.
6. A project's setup asks: a decision of a project's workspace scope (a question of its setup) is delivered, announced with its project, and answered like a plan's, through the same states and record; it holds the setup only, never a plan; Chat over it is offered as for a plan's, disabled with its reason where the setup's workflow raised the question and no planning role exists (the routing is `chat`'s).
7. A start's entry asks: a decision of a start's entry is announced with its entry, held in the entry's `questions.jsonl` and `decisions.jsonl`, answered through the same states and record, and holds only the entry; the stores and their move into the new scope at `start.create-scope` are `start-from-clients`'s, the routing of its chat to `refine` or `decompose` `chat`'s.
8. The open decisions across scopes, the workspace scope of a project included, with since when each waits; the channel `cli`, the second channel of content answers beside `web` (and `quorum` for a decision a quorum took); the answer endpoint (pm-runtime), one scope-qualified endpoint for both clients, whose validation and recording of the answer are one transaction of the decision's scope, so the first valid answer of either channel wins: an answer to an older revision refused with `conflict` and the current revision, a second answer or an answer to a closed record with `already_answered`, an answer the part does not allow (an unknown option, a value failing the part's `value_schema`, own words, a note, a delegation, or _none fits_ where not allowed) with `invalid_answer`, and an answer to a part whose dependency is unanswered with `dependency_unanswered`.
9. Chat before answering: `decision.answer` and the decision record leave room for the choice Chat that `chat` adds on top of this package (the record saying that an answer followed a chat, the transcript kept with the decision, a decision closed as withdrawn with its reason); the chat itself, its routing, and its rules are that package's.

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): Every scenario of `decision-contract` replays against the runtime; an answer in own words reaches the decision only after its read-back, _edit_ interpreting the new words again and _choose_ taking an option instead; a decision of several parts or not `reversible` is recorded only after `decision.confirm`, and _back_ returns to the answer; _none fits_ closes a worker's question with `none_fits` and offers its `ask` link again, and has a quorum vote again on a new revision, _later_ leaves the decision open and changes nothing; the first valid answer of either channel wins, also when two answers arrive at once, and a test per refusal shows `conflict` with the current revision, `already_answered`, `invalid_answer`, and `dependency_unanswered`, none of them changing the record; a decision of a project's setup is announced, answered, and recorded like a plan's, holds no plan, and offers its chat disabled where the setup's workflow raised it; a decision of a start's entry is announced with the entry and holds only it.

- **Checklist for `pm-workflow` code (found by PLAN-69, 2026-10-18).** (1) A refusal added for a workflow-DSL construct has exactly one owner, the parser or the verifier; every verifier refusal fixture is a tree the parser accepts, and fixture-count success criteria are written after that split. (2) A new pre-parsing layer in front of the lexer accepts at least what the lexer accepts (line endings, whitespace); add a CRLF twin fixture for every new line-oriented reader. (3) Where a printer and a reader must agree on a lexical rule, the printer calls the reader's predicate. (4) A completeness test over an enum derives the expected set from the enum at run time. (5) An exception to an invariant for one caller is scoped to that caller, with a refusing fixture from every other caller that reaches the check. (6) When a concept is generalised (start state to starting states), sweep every diagnostic message that names the old one.
- **Checklist for durable multi-step protocols in `pm-runtime` and `core.store` (found by PLAN-74, 2026-10-18; four review rounds each found another unguarded transition of the credential-store move).** (1) A resume decides from the pair (record phase, state actually recorded), never from the phase alone, and a guard added to one phase branch gets its symmetric guard on every other branch; enumerate (phase x recorded state x writer) with a test per cell before the first review. (2) While a durable in-progress record exists, every other writer of the state it switches is refused under the same lock, and an authorization is verified against durable state, never asserted by the caller. (3) The record that drives recovery is deleted last, after every side effect (audit event, clean-up after a failure) is durable. (4) A decision with side effects (which store, which key) is decided once and persisted, never re-derived per call from a probe that can flap. (5) A copy-then-switch move has a test with a pre-populated target (an overlapping key and a target-only key) and a failure injected after the copy; the pre-existing-target hazard is checked for every kind of object the protocol copies (entries, integrity key, sidecars) in the same task. (6) A store's temp-file namespace is disjoint from its legal entry-name namespace. (7) A fix of a state-machine transition lists the specification section, the ADR, the Javadoc and the test that state it among its affected files and updates them in the same commit; ADR and test are checked against the specification text, not against the code they document. (8) The invariants of a multi-write protocol are stated per crash window, not per phase. (9) A test that asserts a file location derives the expected path from the production layout independently, never from the object under test. (10) A native binding is loaded once; only what can change at runtime (a lock state) is probed per operation.
- **Facts about `pm-runtime` (found by PLAN-20, 2026-10-18).** (1) FFM downcall descriptors (`MacSecurity`, `Posix`, the `kill(2)` descriptor of `KillBinding` and `ProcessGroupSignals`) are registered in `pm-runtime/src/main/resources/META-INF/native-image/de.planmarshall/pm-runtime-credentials/reachability-metadata.json`, not in the `plan-marshall-mcp` assembly; verify a location claim with a path query, not with a Javadoc sentence. (2) `ProjectRecovery` is public and wired by the `pm-mcp-server` assembly in `plan-marshall-mcp`: a change of its factory methods needs a source-compatible overload or a named follow-up in `plan-marshall-mcp` after the SNAPSHOT deploy; the outline says which. (3) Signal numbers differ between Linux and macOS: a table of signal names for a job killed by a signal is chosen per OS, never taken from the Linux table. (4) In `pm-runtime`, suppress a Sonar rule inline at the declaration with a stated reason beside it where the design requires the flagged shape (seen: S2142, S3077, S6539, S1075), rather than reshaping the design or excluding the rule for the file.
- **Renumber the ADRs against `origin/main` before finalize and after every rebase (found by PLAN-14, 2026-10-18).** An ADR added to a module ADR directory (for example `pm-core/doc/adr/core-store/`) can take a number another plan took in the same directory; different slugs mean different file names, so git reports no conflict and the duplicate-number gate of the branch-cleanup step scans only `doc/adr`. List the touched ADR directories with `git ls-tree origin/main` and renumber the plan's ADRs to the next free numbers; PLAN-14 renumbered twice.
- **Log messages: the holder of your module, next free identifier** (operator decision 2026-10-13; pm-mcp-core #64, ADR `doc/adr/005-one-log-message-holder-per-module.adoc`). A new INFO, WARN or ERROR record goes into the holder of the module whose production code logs it, at the next free identifier of its level (INFO 1-99, WARN 100-199, ERROR 200-299, the same ranges in every holder); for this plan: `WorkflowLogMessages` (`de.planmarshall.workflow.log`, prefix `PM_WORKFLOW`); `CoreLogMessages` (`de.planmarshall.core.log`, prefix `PM_CORE`). A record two modules log goes to the lower one (`pm-core`); a module logs only through its own holder and `CoreLogMessages`. There are no workstream blocks and nothing is renumbered. A provider's first record creates its holder `de.planmarshall.provider.<x>.log.<X>LogMessages` (prefix `PM_PROVIDER_<X>`) with a `package-info.java`, and adds the prefix to `KNOWN_PREFIXES` in `CoreLogMessagesTest`. After a rebase, run the holder's test, which refuses an identifier used twice or outside its level's range, and move to the next free one. The same identifiers go into `doc/LogMessages.adoc` of this repository, under the holder's section.

- **Format versions stay 1 (operator decision 2026-10-13).** Nothing is released yet, so every versioned artifact (`format_version` of a store document, record, sidecar, journal, codec) stays at version 1. A format change replaces version 1 in place: no version bump, no reader for an older version, no migration.
