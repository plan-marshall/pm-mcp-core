## Objective

Implement the work package `process-and-overview` of `doc/plans/process-and-overview.adoc`: the plan's process, running work, and ended work as resources. Serves: roadmap Milestone 7 (the plan's process with its events, the overview with what needs the operator), Milestone 10 (ended work); Use Cases, UC-PLN-1 (the plan's process), UC-PLN-4 (what runs, across projects), and the ended work of UC-CTL-1; the overview, the plan view, and ended work in both clients of `pm-mcp-clients`. Where: pm-workflow (the node states derived from the plan's history, the composed process as a tree); pm-core (titles and names, the start of the current phase); pm-runtime (the resources and the events per scope). The package file is the brief; this spec adds what the orchestrator observed and the constraints of the epic.

## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/process-and-overview.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated at 710da61, after the specification moved into this repository).

1. Implements Runtime Model § The Plan's Process as a Structure, § Events of the Process, § What Needs the Operator, at a Glance, § The Overview Resource and Its Events, § Events per Scope, § Ended Work, State Catalogue § Running Work, and Store Schemas § Titles and Names (`PM-ARCH-11`).
2. The process: `GET /api/v1/plans/{plan_id}/process` answers the composed process of the instance in force as a tree (the plan, its phases, every workflow's states in their order, every call expanded to the leaves), never DSL text; each node with its `node_id`, its kind, and its state derived from the plan's history of `phase-sub-workflows` (`done`, `running`, `planned`, `waiting_for_operator`, `failed`, `not_taken`, `not_in_plan` with its prune reason), how often it ran, its entry and exit times, a leaf's job or tasks with a link to their activity (`worker-activity`); the path to the current position; the context (project, epic, activity, `needs_operator`, tokens so far with split and coverage); the `instance_version`.
3. The events of the process: on the plan's stream `state.entered`, `state.left`, `subworkflow.called`, `subworkflow.returned`, `step.opened`, `step.closed`, `instance.changed`, `activity.changed`, and `tokens.changed` (at most once per metrics event interval), each with its `node_id`.
4. What needs the operator: a `needs_operator` summary per plan, epic, and project, derived on read and never stored, with its count and per item its kind and link, in the closed order `decision`, `waiver_request`, `operator_wait`, the holds (`integrity_pause`, `stalled`, `condition`, `held`, `landing_failed`), `findings`, `setup` (a project's setup repair or a step its onboarding owes); an epic's summary including its plans', a project's its plans', epics', and workspace scope's; a change of a summary announced on the runtime-wide stream as `needs_operator.changed`.
5. Running work and the overview: active plans and epics and a project's running work as the state catalogue defines them; `GET /api/v1/projects` (the projects with running work, each with its `display_name`, `needs_operator`, and counts) and `GET /api/v1/projects/{project_id}` (its active plans with title, phase, activity, `created_at`, `phase_entered_at`, task progress, `needs_operator`, `off_track`, and epic; its active epics; its open entries; its running short landings); titles and names as the stores keep them.
6. Events per scope: the streams `GET /api/v1/events`, `/plans/{plan_id}/events`, `/epics/{epic_id}/events`, `/projects/{project_id}/workspace/events`, `/machine/events`, and `/entries/{entry_id}/events`, one envelope (`event`, `id`, `data` with `at`, `scope`, and the payload of the closed event catalogue, never a model-facing representation or a secret), the resume by `Last-Event-ID` within the event buffer and otherwise one `reset`, no subscription kept; the runtime-wide stream announcing every scope's start and end, every change of running work, and every change of a `needs_operator` summary; an entry's stream announcing the scope it starts and ending, a project onboarding's entry announcing at `project.enrol` the workspace stream to follow; a stream on a web listener checked against its device secret on connect and ended by a revoked device, a closed listener, or the runtime's stop with `stream.closed` and its reason.
7. Ended work: `GET /api/v1/projects/{project_id}/ended`, newest first, paged by a cursor, each plan with its outcome (`merged`, `no_change`, `handed_back`, `returned`, `cancelled`, `abandoned`, `released`, `aborted`), reason, and end time, each epic with its `terminal_reason`; each resolving read-only from its archive to its process and history as it ended, its completion report, decisions, re-planning records, and events; a deleted one left out and resolving to its tombstone.
8. Where the stream and the resources of the runtime exist only in a first form (the runtime-wide stream of Milestone 2), this package brings them to the closed catalogue and envelope; the envelope and the event catalogue as the clients' contract are `pm-api`'s in `pm-mcp-clients` (roadmap Milestone 2), this package their runtime side; the transport of the streams on the web listener is the product's.
9. Schema parity: the members of the status store the specification added (`title`, `created_at`, `phase_entered_at`, `pause`, `terminal_reason`, `setup_aspect`, `onboard_run`, `onboard_steps`) are in its codec, the ones of the packages before this one included, and `WorkflowStatusSchemaParityTest.membersEqualSchema` is re-enabled.

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): The process of a fixture plan answers every node with its derived state, also `failed`, `not_taken`, and `not_in_plan` with its reason, the path to its position, and its context, after a restart as before; each change of a node is announced on the plan's stream with its `node_id`; the `needs_operator` summary lists each kind in its order, `setup` included, is never stored, and each change of it is announced with `needs_operator.changed`; the overview lists exactly the projects with running work and their active plans, epics, entries, and short landings; every stream carries the one envelope, resumes from `Last-Event-ID` within the buffer and sends `reset` otherwise, and a web stream ends with `stream.closed` and its reason when its device is revoked, its listener closed, or the runtime stopped; ended work lists each outcome newest first by cursor, resolves an ended plan read-only from its archive, and a deleted one to its tombstone; `WorkflowStatusSchemaParityTest.membersEqualSchema` runs without `@Disabled` and passes.

- **Checklist for `pm-workflow` code (found by PLAN-69, 2026-10-18).** (1) A refusal added for a workflow-DSL construct has exactly one owner, the parser or the verifier; every verifier refusal fixture is a tree the parser accepts, and fixture-count success criteria are written after that split. (2) A new pre-parsing layer in front of the lexer accepts at least what the lexer accepts (line endings, whitespace); add a CRLF twin fixture for every new line-oriented reader. (3) Where a printer and a reader must agree on a lexical rule, the printer calls the reader's predicate. (4) A completeness test over an enum derives the expected set from the enum at run time. (5) An exception to an invariant for one caller is scoped to that caller, with a refusing fixture from every other caller that reaches the check. (6) When a concept is generalised (start state to starting states), sweep every diagnostic message that names the old one.
- **Checklist for durable multi-step protocols in `pm-runtime` and `core.store` (found by PLAN-74, 2026-10-18; four review rounds each found another unguarded transition of the credential-store move).** (1) A resume decides from the pair (record phase, state actually recorded), never from the phase alone, and a guard added to one phase branch gets its symmetric guard on every other branch; enumerate (phase x recorded state x writer) with a test per cell before the first review. (2) While a durable in-progress record exists, every other writer of the state it switches is refused under the same lock, and an authorization is verified against durable state, never asserted by the caller. (3) The record that drives recovery is deleted last, after every side effect (audit event, clean-up after a failure) is durable. (4) A decision with side effects (which store, which key) is decided once and persisted, never re-derived per call from a probe that can flap. (5) A copy-then-switch move has a test with a pre-populated target (an overlapping key and a target-only key) and a failure injected after the copy; the pre-existing-target hazard is checked for every kind of object the protocol copies (entries, integrity key, sidecars) in the same task. (6) A store's temp-file namespace is disjoint from its legal entry-name namespace. (7) A fix of a state-machine transition lists the specification section, the ADR, the Javadoc and the test that state it among its affected files and updates them in the same commit; ADR and test are checked against the specification text, not against the code they document. (8) The invariants of a multi-write protocol are stated per crash window, not per phase. (9) A test that asserts a file location derives the expected path from the production layout independently, never from the object under test. (10) A native binding is loaded once; only what can change at runtime (a lock state) is probed per operation.
- **Facts about `pm-runtime` (found by PLAN-20, 2026-10-18).** (1) FFM downcall descriptors (`MacSecurity`, `Posix`, the `kill(2)` descriptor of `KillBinding` and `ProcessGroupSignals`) are registered in `pm-runtime/src/main/resources/META-INF/native-image/de.planmarshall/pm-runtime-credentials/reachability-metadata.json`, not in the `plan-marshall-mcp` assembly; verify a location claim with a path query, not with a Javadoc sentence. (2) `ProjectRecovery` is public and wired by the `pm-mcp-server` assembly in `plan-marshall-mcp`: a change of its factory methods needs a source-compatible overload or a named follow-up in `plan-marshall-mcp` after the SNAPSHOT deploy; the outline says which. (3) Signal numbers differ between Linux and macOS: a table of signal names for a job killed by a signal is chosen per OS, never taken from the Linux table. (4) In `pm-runtime`, suppress a Sonar rule inline at the declaration with a stated reason beside it where the design requires the flagged shape (seen: S2142, S3077, S6539, S1075), rather than reshaping the design or excluding the rule for the file.
- **Log messages: the holder of your module, next free identifier** (operator decision 2026-10-13; pm-mcp-core #64, ADR `doc/adr/005-one-log-message-holder-per-module.adoc`). A new INFO, WARN or ERROR record goes into the holder of the module whose production code logs it, at the next free identifier of its level (INFO 1-99, WARN 100-199, ERROR 200-299, the same ranges in every holder); for this plan: `WorkflowLogMessages` (`de.planmarshall.workflow.log`, prefix `PM_WORKFLOW`); `CoreLogMessages` (`de.planmarshall.core.log`, prefix `PM_CORE`); `RuntimeLogMessages` (`de.planmarshall.runtime.log`, prefix `PM_RUNTIME`). A record two modules log goes to the lower one (`pm-core`); a module logs only through its own holder and `CoreLogMessages`. There are no workstream blocks and nothing is renumbered. A provider's first record creates its holder `de.planmarshall.provider.<x>.log.<X>LogMessages` (prefix `PM_PROVIDER_<X>`) with a `package-info.java`, and adds the prefix to `KNOWN_PREFIXES` in `CoreLogMessagesTest`. The same identifiers go into `doc/LogMessages.adoc` of this repository, under the holder's section.
