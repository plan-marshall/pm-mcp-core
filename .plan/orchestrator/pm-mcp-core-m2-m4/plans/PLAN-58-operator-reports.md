## Objective

Implement the work package `operator-reports` of `doc/plans/operator-reports.adoc`: reports to the operator. Serves: roadmap Milestone 7 (the terminal client with the reports to the operator); Runtime Model § Reports to the Operator; Use Cases, UC-PLN-6; the reports, their list, and a plan's completion report in both clients of `pm-mcp-clients`. Where: pm-core (the report record and its store in the machine scope, the read state, the catalogue of kinds and subjects); pm-workflow (the server transitions that write a report); pm-runtime (the endpoints, the event stream `reports`, the representation with its `detail` link, a plan's completion report as a view of an ended plan). The package file is the brief; this spec adds what the orchestrator observed and the constraints of the epic.

**Parked:** waits on packages that are not done: `operator-wait-and-machine-scope`, `plan-and-epic-control`, `epic-auto`, `decision-engine`, `start-from-clients`, `token-usage`, `worker-activity`. Re-ground this spec before emitting it.

## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/operator-reports.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated at 710da61, after the specification moved into this repository).

1. Implements Runtime Model § Reports to the Operator with its completion report of a plan, the journal of Store Schemas § Report Journal, and the identifier `report_id` (`PM-WF-21`).
2. A report: a record the runtime writes for the operator when something happened that the operator should know without having to act; it never asks anything, and what blocks stays what it is (decisions, waits, holds: `needs_operator`). Its fields: id (`rep-NNNN`, never reused); `from` (the project, or `machine` for a report of the machine itself, and the epic and the plan where there are, each with its link); `kind`; `subject` (one line, a text of the catalogue with its parameters, in the client's language); a short body; `created_at` (when it happened) and `sent_at` (when the clients could see it, later than `created_at` where it was written after a restart); `read_at` with the channel and device that read it; `detail`, the link to the detailed report where there is one.
3. The catalogue: closed, first version `plan.started` (by the operator, an epic, or a recipe; a plan an epic starts gives this one report, its `from` naming the epic), `plan.ended` (with its outcome, one of the closed list of plan outcomes of the terminal-state catalogue as `plan-and-epic-control` extends it), `plan.revised` (a re-planning or a chat's revision), `plan.hold_released`, `epic.launchable` (automatic off, a row became launchable), `epic.waits_overlap`, `epic.overlap_found`, `epic.recut_applied`, `epic.through`, `epic.ended`, `project.onboarded`, `project.released`, `source.flagged`, `decision.closed_elsewhere`, `task.off_track` (the server took `yield` or `restart` on a worker off track), and `system.cleanup_due`, whose source is `cleanup`'s; each kind with its subject text in English and German and its `detail`.
4. The sources: each kind written once for the server transition that makes it true, appended after that transition has committed and outside its transaction, a report a committed transition still owed at a stop written at the next start from its scope's transaction journal: the start and the end of a plan and its outcomes and holds (`plan-and-epic-control`), a revision (re-planning and `chat`), a row launchable with automatic off, its overlap wait and the overlap found, an applied recut, and the epic through (`epic-auto`), the end of an epic and the release of a project (`plan-and-epic-control`), a project onboarded (`project-onboarding-unit`), a screened source flagged (`start-from-clients`), a decision closed in another channel (`decision-engine`), a worker off track (`worker-activity`), the cleanup due (`cleanup.check`, `cleanup`); no report is written outside the catalogue.
5. The store: one append-only journal of the machine scope (`operator-wait-and-machine-scope`), `<PM_MCP_BASE>/state/reports.jsonl` under its own leaf lock, with the events `report` (a new report), `read` (its `read_at`, channel, and device), and `deleted` (read reports the cleanup deleted, with the cleanup job and `last_report_id`, the highest id ever allocated), the current state of a report folded from them; the next id follows the highest id of the journal, which a `deleted` event carries, so no id is reused; unread reports never expire; read ones are kept until the cleanup of the machine deletes them by a journaled rewrite of the journal under its lock that drops their `report` and `read` events and appends one `deleted` event, the one rewrite of the journal (UC-SYS-3, built by `cleanup`).
6. Read: a report is read when the operator marks it read (one, or all shown) or opens its detail; read is per machine, so a report read on one device is read on all, with the channel and device recorded; marking a report read twice changes nothing.
7. The endpoints: `GET /api/v1/reports?state=unread` (unread only, newest first, grouped by project, then epic and plan; the count of unread reports; `?state=all` paging through the read ones as well), `POST /api/v1/reports/{report_id}/read`, `POST /api/v1/reports/read` with the ids the client showed (only those, never one that arrived since), the event stream `GET /api/v1/reports/events` that announces `report.sent` and `report.read` with the ids to every open client; the representation with `from`'s links and the `detail` link, addressed through `entity-addressing`; the same acts as operator verbs.
8. The details: a plan's completion report `GET /api/v1/plans/{plan_id}/completion`, new as a view of an ended plan built on its archive entry and its records (outcome, reason, its pull request, deliverables done, re-planning records, decisions taken, its tokens, duration); the epic's completion review and the landing record as they are; a subject that has been archived opens from the archive; opening a detail through a report's `detail` link marks the report read.
9. The completion report's Tokens section (Client Profiles § Tokens, Not Money): usage only, every figure the unified one of `token-usage` with its split (input, output) and from its one derivation: the plan's total with the wall time; by phase; by role (a role pins harness, model, and effort); by model and harness; the waste, the tokens of the tasks of spend class `no_product` and of the retries and remediations by cause class; the coverage, the tasks unmeasured with their reasons and a total over them shown as a floor. No cost (recorded, never shown), no limit, no budget line, no projection, and no comparison with one: the plan has none (`token-usage` removes them).

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): A test per kind of the catalogue shows its source transition writing exactly one report with `from`, subject, `created_at`, `sent_at`, and `detail`, and no transition writing a kind outside it; a report written after a restart carries a later `sent_at`; the list returns unread reports only, newest first and grouped, with their count; marking one read, marking the ids shown read (not one that arrived since), and opening the detail each set `read_at` with channel and device, once, and the read state is the same on every device; the journal folds to the same state after a restart, also after a cleanup's rewrite, and the next id after a rewrite follows `last_report_id`; the event stream announces a new and a read report; a plan's completion report shows outcome, reason, pull request, deliverables, re-planning records, decisions, and duration for each outcome, also for an archived plan, with its Tokens section by phase, role, model and harness, waste, and coverage, a total over unmeasured tasks shown as a floor and no cost, limit, or budget comparison in it; no report ever waits for the operator.
