## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/cleanup.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated by cleanup at 99971f3 from the package file).

1. Implements Timeouts § One Cleanup with the residue rules of § Retention & Rotation, the steps of Runtime Lifecycle § Periodic Sweep (residue, disk use, the cleanup's check), Store Schemas § Cleanup Journal, the tombstones of Filesystem Layout § Plan & Epic Archives, and `unenrolled_at` of Store Schemas § Project Store Identity (`PM-ARCH-10`). The use case's step names `cleanup.*` are operator acts over the local API, no workflow unit (State Catalogue § Operator Flows That Are Not Workflow Units).
2. Two classes: work history goes only through the cleanup, after the operator decided a cleanup job: an archived plan with its whole archive entry (records, history, decisions, transcripts, metrics rows, and the workspace's metrics rows of the plan), an archived epic's machine store `epics/<epic_id>/` (its archived ledger in the repository stays), the logs of ended work (the logs of ended jobs and the worker logs of archived plans; a job record stays with its plan), read reports, and the machine store of an unenrolled project, whole. The technical residue goes by itself in the periodic sweep, each removal in a transaction of its scope with one audit record, by evidence, never asked: the pending entry directory of an entry already started or abandoned, the workspace scope's technical rows past `retention.workspace_rows_days` (a plan's metrics rows excepted), retired lesson bodies, ended queue task records, stale daemon store directories, the job-private directories of ended jobs, stale leases, temporary files and caches, and rotated audit files, with `pm-operator sweep` listing the next pass and running it with `--apply`; the sweep also measures the machine store's size for `runtime.disk.machine_store`, and each pass keeps its counts per step and its instant for the cleanup view.
3. The settings: `cleanup.plans_after_days`, `cleanup.epics_after_days`, `cleanup.logs_after_days`, `cleanup.reports_after_days` (read reports), `cleanup.unenrolled_after_days`, each `30` by default, at least `7`, or `never`, and `cleanup.remind_after_days` (`3`, at least `1`); the machine values in `machine-config.json` `cleanup` (set from the machine's settings, `machine-onboarding-unit`), a project's own value of the first three in its `local-config.json` `cleanup`, which wins for its items (`project-onboarding-unit`); a value out of range refused with its reason. An item is due when its end (its archiving, its reading, the project's unenrolment) is older than its duration. Never due: live work, a pending entry, an unread report, an archived plan with an undischarged owed item, a kept item, and an item of an open or postponed job.
4. The check: the first pass of the periodic sweep after the start and the first of each new UTC day compute the items due; when there are items not yet in a job and no job is open or postponed, they form one new job, fixed from then on, and the runtime writes one report `system.cleanup_due` (`from` the machine) with the counts per kind, whose detail is the job (the kind is in the catalogue of `operator-reports`); items that become due meanwhile wait for the next job.
5. The journal: `<PM_MCP_BASE>/state/cleanup.jsonl`, append-only, of the machine scope under its own leaf lock, with the events `job`, `decided`, `deleted`, `delete_failed`, `kept`, and `released`; a job's state (`open`, `postponed`, `deleting`, `done`) and the kept list the fold of its events; a job's id `cln-NNNN`, store-assigned and never reused; a cleanup job starts no process and has no job record.
6. The decisions, operator acts of either client: _delete_ deletes the checked items and keeps the unchecked ones for good; _remind_ deletes nothing and, after the reminder, opens a new job of the same items with a new report; _ignore_ deletes nothing and keeps every item for good. The first decision of a job is the one taken; a later one is answered with it, unchanged. A released kept item is due again at the next check.
7. Deleting: item by item, each in its own journaled transaction with one audit record `cleanup_deleted` (category `store`; kind, id, project, `job_id`, channel, size, `audit-trail`): an archived plan's `archive/<plan_id>/` replaced by the tombstone `archive/<plan_id>/deleted.json` (its id, title, outcome, end, `deleted_at`, `job_id`), so its id is never allocated again, ended work leaves it out, and its reports' detail resolves to deleted with the date; an archived epic's `epics/<epic_id>/` likewise by `epics/<epic_id>/deleted.json`; read reports by a journaled rewrite of `state/reports.jsonl` under its lock that drops their `report` and `read` events and appends one `deleted` event with the `job_id` and `last_report_id`, so their ids are never reused (the journal, its events and its fold, built by `operator-reports`); an unenrolled project's store as a whole. Before a plan's metrics rows go, what it used is added to the usage aggregate `<PM_MCP_BASE>/state/usage-aggregate.jsonl` (per day, project, model, and role, in tokens with their coverage), from which `runtime-metrics` fills the system view's figures at the start, so no figure goes down. A delete cut by a stop goes on after the next start from the journal. A plan's branch, its pull request, and the remote are never touched, nor an epic's archived ledger in the repository.
8. Unenrolment: `pm-operator project unenrol` (or the act of the web app) records `unenrolled_at` in the store's `store.json`; enrolling again removes it; the store is a cleanup item from then on, never residue.
9. The endpoints: `GET /api/v1/system/cleanup` (the open or postponed job with its summary, the counts per kind and the space it frees, its items by project each linked read-only to its ended view, the durations with their sources, the count of kept items, and what the sweep removed and when), `POST /api/v1/system/cleanup/{job_id}/delete` (with the unchecked items), `…/remind`, `…/ignore`, `GET` and `PUT /api/v1/system/cleanup/kept` (an item left out released); each write privileged, accepted from `pm-operator` (`pm-operator cleanup [--delete|--remind|--ignore] [--yes]`) and from a paired device, audited with its channel; the job and its items addressed through `entity-addressing`; the runtime-wide stream announcing `cleanup.job_opened`, `cleanup.decided`, `cleanup.progress`, and `cleanup.done`.
10. Recurring waivers are counted over the `waiver_waived` audit records of the last 90 days, whatever has been deleted since.

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): Nothing of the work history is deleted without a decided job, shown by a test that passes every duration with no job decided; with a fixed clock the check at start and the daily one each write one report of kind `system.cleanup_due` for the items newly due and none while nothing is, never one per item, and never offer an unread report, a pending entry, a kept item, or live work; a project's own duration wins over the machine's and `never` keeps its kind; delete removes exactly the checked items of each kind, keeps the unchecked ones for good, leaves the branch, the pull request, and the archived ledger in the repository untouched, leaves a tombstone so the id is never reused, writes one `cleanup_deleted` audit record per item, and is resumed by the recovery after a stop in the middle; an item that fails is recorded `delete_failed` and offered again; a duration below 7 days or a reminder below 1 is refused, and a project's own value is accepted for plans, epics, and logs only; an archived plan that owes an item is never due; the check runs on the first sweep pass after start and the first of each UTC day only; an item that became due after the job was created is not deleted with it; remind brings the same items back as a new job after its days, ignore keeps them, and a kept item released is offered again; a decided job answers a second decision with its decision; the token figures of the system view are the same before and after a deletion and after a restart, read from the usage aggregate; the journal folds to the same jobs and kept list after a restart; a report whose subject was deleted shows it deleted; each residue kind of the periodic sweep is removed by its evidence with one audit record, an open pending entry never, and `pm-operator sweep` lists the next pass; each run of the sweep keeps its counts, shown in the cleanup view; every endpoint answers both channels, audited with its channel. **Sonar:** the SonarCloud search of the pull request (`pullRequest=<N>&resolved=false`) lists no open issue other than one dismissed with its reason, and its quality gate is green, before the pull request is reported ready.
