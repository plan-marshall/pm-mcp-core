## Deliverables

The bullets of `doc/plans/runtime-control.adoc` § What, in its words (regenerated by cleanup at 99971f3 from the package file).

1. Implements Runtime Lifecycle § Drain, Stop & Upgrade (the operator's stop, restart, update check, and upgrade, with the lifecycle and version states), the consent kind `check_updates` of Store Schemas § Consent Before Acting, and the version state and the upgrade's result of § Machine State Records (`PM-ARCH-7`, `PM-DIST-1`).
2. The lifecycle state: `GET /api/v1/status` carries the version and the lifecycle state: `running`; `draining` (who asked it: `operator`, `signal`, `service_manager`; whether it can be withdrawn; what follows, `exit` or `restart`; when the drain timeout ends); `stopping`; `upgrading` with its step; beside it the version state (the newest version known and when it was checked, a failed check with its reason, whether the runtime can upgrade this installation, the release notes at `GET /api/v1/system/release-notes`) and an upgrade's last result.
3. Stop: `POST /api/v1/operator/runtime/stop` with `when` (`drain`, the default, or `now`) and `then` (`exit`, the default, or `restart`). `drain` is the drain of `runtime-lifecycle` with its participants; `now` is that drain with its wait cut to zero: what still runs is stopped and settled as after the drain timeout (tasks offered again after the next start, jobs recorded `killed`, worker generations fenced), so the recovery resumes it; a stop with `now` during a drain cuts that drain's wait. The answer names what runs, so the client can ask its confirmation with it.
4. Withdrawing a drain: `DELETE /api/v1/operator/runtime/stop` withdraws a drain the operator asked while its wait runs, the runtime admitting jobs and offering tasks again; once it terminates what runs, the withdrawal is refused `not_withdrawable`; a drain a signal started cannot be withdrawn. `pm-operator drain` stays a signal for a runtime in any state.
5. Restart: with `then: restart` the runtime finishes its drain, starts its successor detached through the on-demand start procedure of `pm-api`, and exits, releasing the singleton lock the successor waits for; under the service unit it exits with `75` (`EX_TEMPFAIL`) instead, on which the unit restarts the service. A stopped runtime is started only from the terminal client (on demand, or `pm-operator runtime start`).
6. Update check: `POST /api/v1/operator/runtime/upgrade/check`, and, while `check_updates` is remembered, the runtime itself at most once a day (the update check interval), asks the organisation's registry of `PM-DIST-1` for the newest release of the machine's platform under that consent; nothing about the machine is sent beyond the request. The installed version is a local read without consent. The registry credential and the form of a release are an open question of the documentation repository (`registry-credential`); the package blocks on it for the check and the fetch.
7. Upgrade: `POST /api/v1/operator/runtime/upgrade` with the version and `when`: fetch, verify the build attestation and the archive against its digest (`release_unattested` otherwise), unpack into a new versioned installation directory, rewrite an installed service unit, record the upgrade (`from`, `to`, step `switching`), switch both `PATH` links, then stop with `then: restart`. `DELETE /api/v1/operator/runtime/upgrade` withdraws it until the links are switched, then `not_withdrawable`. A failure before the switch changes nothing and is the result with its reason; a successor that does not reach its authenticated round trip within the runtime start timeout is terminated, the links and the unit switched back, and the previous version started; the starting runtime settles the record (`runtime.upgraded`, or `runtime.upgrade_failed` with the reason). The previous directory stays as the way back until the next upgrade. An installation not laid out in versioned directories with the two links, or reached through `PM_MCP_HOME`, is refused `upgrade_not_possible` with its reason. Pre-1.0: no store migration beyond what the specification defines.
8. The acts: privileged operator acts under `/api/v1/operator/`, accepted from `pm-operator` and from a paired device, audited with their channel, and announced on the runtime-wide stream (the events of the Event Catalogue), so every open client sees the runtime draining, stopping, or upgrading.

**Done when** (the package's own acceptance): With a killed-runtime fixture and fixture jobs and workers: a stop now ends every job and fences every worker at once and the next start resumes each plan from its last committed state; a drain lets the running task and job finish and starts nothing new; a withdrawn drain leaves everything running; a drain turned into a stop now behaves like a stop now; a restart comes back on the same installation without a terminal; a drain a signal started and a drain past its wait are refused `not_withdrawable`; the update check runs only with `check_updates` given, never asks again while it is remembered, and runs by itself at most once a day; an upgrade from a staged release switches to the new directory and resumes the running work; a failure before and one after the switch each leave the previous version running with the reason; a release without a valid build attestation is refused `release_unattested`, an archive that does not match the digest the attestation states is refused, and the registry's `sha256` is shown only, never used to verify the archive; an installation without versioned directories is refused `upgrade_not_possible`; every act is audited with its channel, and every open event stream sees the runtime stopping. **Sonar:** the SonarCloud search of the pull request (`pullRequest=<N>&resolved=false`) lists no open issue other than one dismissed with its reason, and its quality gate is green, before the pull request is reported ready.
